# 22.아가 (AMP)



## 1장

**1:1** ¶ The <sup>①</sup>Song of Songs [the best of songs], which is Solomon’s.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Some theologians believe the Song of Solomon to be a collection of songs, but it is more generally understood to be a sort of drama or lyric poem celebrating the wholesomeness of a growing love that leads to maturity in marriage. The ancient rabbis understood it as an allegory of the relationship between God and Israel, and viewed the details as symbolic.  
**1:2** ¶ <sup>①</sup><i>(The Shulammite Bride)</i><br>“May he kiss me with the kisses of his mouth!” [Solomon arrives, she turns to him, saying,]<br>“For your love is better than wine.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>The parenthetical headings indicate that the speakers are not from the Hebrew text nor the Septuagint, but reflect an ancient tradition which appears in some manuscripts.  
**1:3** “The aroma of your oils is fragrant <i>and</i> pleasing;<br>Your name is perfume poured out;<br>Therefore the maidens love you.  
**1:4** “Draw me away with you <i>and</i> let us run <i>together</i>!<br>Let the king bring me into his chambers.” <pb/> <sup>①</sup><i>(The Chorus)</i><br>“We will rejoice and be glad in you;<br>We will remember <i>and</i> extol your love more [sweet and fragrant] than wine.<br>Rightly do they love you.”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>The purpose of the chorus is to echo and expand the sentiments of the bride and her bridegroom. The members of the chorus are not always known, but have been variously identified as “daughters of Jerusalem,” “daughters of Zion,” “ladies in waiting,” “friends” or “relatives” of the bride.  
**1:5** ¶ <i>(The Shulammite Bride)</i><br>“I am deeply tanned but lovely,<br>O daughters of Jerusalem,<br>[I am dark] like the tents of [the Bedouins of] Kedar,<br>Like the [beautiful] curtains of Solomon.  
**1:6** “Do not gaze at me because I am deeply tanned,<br>[I have worked in] the sun; it has left its mark on me.<br>My mother’s sons were angry with me;<br>They made me keeper of the vineyards,<br>But my own vineyard (my complexion) I have not kept.”  
**1:7** “Tell me, O you whom my soul loves,<br>Where do you pasture <i>your flock</i>,<br>Where do you make it lie down at noon?<br>For why should I be like one who is veiled<br>Beside the flocks of your companions?”  
**1:8** ¶ <i>(The Bridegroom)</i><br>“If you do not know [where your lover is],<br>O you fairest among women,<br>Run along, follow the tracks of the flock,<br>And pasture your young goats<br>By the tents of the shepherds.  
**1:9** ¶ “To me, my love, you are like<br>My [favorite] mare among the chariots of Pharaoh.  
**1:10** “Your cheeks are lovely with ornaments,<br>Your neck with strings of jewels.”  
**1:11** ¶ <i>(The Chorus)</i><br>“We will make for you chains <i>and</i> ornaments of gold,<br>[Studded] with beads of silver.”  
**1:12** ¶ <i>(The Shulammite Bride)</i><br>“While the king was at his table,<br>My perfume (Solomon) sent forth [his] fragrance [surrounding me].  
**1:13** “My beloved is to me like a pouch of myrrh<br>Which lies all night between my breasts.  
**1:14** “My beloved is to me a cluster of henna flowers<br>In the [fragrant] vineyards of <sup>①</sup>Engedi.”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>An oasis on the western side of the Dead Sea.  
**1:15** ¶ <i>(The Bridegroom)</i><br>“Behold, how beautiful you are, my darling,<br>Behold, how beautiful you are!<br>Your eyes are dove’s eyes.”  
**1:16** ¶ <i>(The Shulammite Bride)</i><br>“Behold, how fair <i>and</i> handsome you are, my beloved;<br><i>And</i> so delightful!<br>Our arbor is green <i>and</i> luxuriant.  
**1:17** “The beams of our houses are cedars,<br>Our rafters <i>and</i> panels are cypresses.  

## 2장

**2:2** ¶ <i>(The Bridegroom)</i><br>“Like the lily among the thorns,<br>So are you, my darling, among the maidens.”  
**2:3** ¶ <i>(The Shulammite Bride)</i><br>“Like an apple tree [rare and welcome] among the trees of the forest,<br>So is my beloved among the young men!<br>In his shade I took great delight and sat down,<br>And his fruit was sweet <i>and</i> delicious to my palate.  
**2:4** “He has brought me to his banqueting place,<br>And his banner over me is love [waving overhead to protect and comfort me].  
**2:5** “Sustain me with raisin cakes,<br>Refresh me with apples,<br>Because I am sick with love.  
**2:6** “Let his left hand be under my head<br>And his right hand embrace me.”  
**2:7** ¶ <i>(The Bridegroom)</i><br>“I command that you take an oath, O daughters of Jerusalem,<br>By the gazelles or by the does of the field [which run free],<br>That you do not rouse nor awaken my love<br>Until she pleases.”  
**2:8** ¶ <i>(The Shulammite Bride)</i><br>“Listen! My beloved!<br>Behold, he comes,<br>Climbing on the mountains,<br>Leaping <i>and</i> running on the hills!  
**2:9** “My beloved is like a gazelle or a young stag.<br>Behold, he is standing behind our wall,<br>He is looking through the windows,<br>He is gazing through the lattice.  
**2:10** ¶ “My beloved speaks and says to me,<br>‘Arise, my love, my fair one,<br>And come away.  
**2:11** ‘For behold, the winter is past,<br>The rain is over and gone.  
**2:12** ‘The flowers appear on the earth once again;<br>The time for singing has come,<br>And the voice of the turtledove is heard in our land.  
**2:13** ‘The fig tree has budded <i>and</i> ripens her figs,<br>And the vines are in blossom and give forth their fragrance.<br>Arise, my love, my fair one,<br>And come away [to climb the rocky steps of the hillside].’”  
**2:14** ¶ <i>(The Bridegroom)</i><br>“O my dove, [here] in the clefts in the rock,<br>In the sheltered <i>and</i> secret place of the steep pathway,<br>Let me see your face,<br>Let me hear your voice;<br>For your voice is sweet,<br>And your face is lovely.”  
**2:15** ¶ <i>(The Chorus)</i><br>“Catch the foxes for us,<br>The little foxes that spoil <i>and</i> ruin the vineyards [of love],<br>While our vineyards are in blossom.”  
**2:16** ¶ <i>(The Shulammite Bride)</i><br>“My beloved is mine and I am his;<br>He pastures <i>his flock</i> among the lilies.  
**2:17** “Until the cool of the day when the shadows flee away,<br>Return quickly, my beloved, and be like a gazelle<br>Or a young stag on the mountains of Bether [which separate us].”

**5:13** “His cheeks are like a bed of balsam,<br>Banks of sweet, fragrant herbs.<br>His lips are lilies<br>Dripping <i>sweet-scented</i> myrrh.

**5:15** “His legs are [strong and steady] pillars of alabaster<br>Set upon pedestals of fine gold.<br>His appearance is like Lebanon,<br>Stately <i>and</i> choice as the cedars.
