# 3.레위기 (EXB)



## 1장

**1:1** The Burnt Offering The Lord ·called to [summoned] Moses and spoke to him from the Meeting Tent, saying,  
**1:2** “Tell the ·people [sons; children] of Israel: ‘When you bring an offering [of livestock] to the Lord, bring as your offering an animal from the herd or flock.  
**1:3** “‘If the offering is a whole burnt offering from the herd, it must be a male ·that has nothing wrong with it [without blemish/defect]. The person must take the animal to the entrance of the Meeting Tent so that the Lord will accept the offering.  
**1:4** He must ·put [lay] his hand on the animal’s head [to identify with the animal that served as a substitute], and ·the Lord will accept it to remove the person’s sin so he will belong to God [it will be acceptable as atonement/expiation for him].  
**1:5** He must ·kill [slaughter] the young bull before the Lord, and Aaron’s sons, the priests, must bring its blood and ·sprinkle [dash] it on all sides of the altar at the entrance to the Meeting Tent.  
**1:6** After that he will skin the ·animal [whole burnt offering] and cut it into pieces.  
**1:7** The [sons of Aaron, the] priests, when they have ·put [arranged] wood and fire on the altar,  
**1:8** [the sons of Aaron, the priests,] are to ·lay [arrange] the head, the ·fat [suet], and other pieces on the wood that is on the fire of the altar.  
**1:9** The animal’s inner organs and legs must be washed with water. Then the priest must ·burn all the animal’s parts [turn it into smoke] on the altar. It is a whole burnt offering, an offering made by fire, and its smell is pleasing to the Lord.  
**1:10** “‘If the ·burnt offering [offering; gift] is a sheep or a goat from the flock [acceptable if the worshiper could not afford a bull], it must be a male ·that has nothing wrong with it [without blemish/defect].  
**1:11** The person must ·kill [slaughter] the animal on the north side of the altar before the Lord, and Aaron’s sons, the priests, must ·sprinkle [dash] its blood on all sides of the altar.  
**1:12** The person must cut the animal into pieces, and the priest must ·lay [arrange] them, with the head and ·fat [suet], on the wood that is on the fire of the altar.  
**1:13** The person must wash the animal’s inner organs and legs with water, and then the priest must ·burn all its parts [turn it into smoke] on the altar. It is a whole burnt offering, an offering made by fire, and its smell is pleasing to the Lord.  
**1:14** “‘If the ·whole burnt offering [offering; gift] for the Lord is a bird [presumably because the worshiper could not afford an animal from the herd or flock], it must be a dove or a young pigeon.  
**1:15** The priest will bring it to the altar and ·pull [wring] off its head, which he will ·burn [turn into smoke] on the altar; its blood must be drained out on the side of the altar.  
**1:16** The priest must remove the bird’s crop [a part of its digestive system] and its contents and throw them on the east side of the altar, where the ashes are.  
**1:17** Then he must tear the bird open by its wings without dividing it into two parts. The priest must ·burn the bird [turn it into smoke] on the altar, on the wood which is on the fire. It is a whole burnt offering, an offering made by fire, and its smell is pleasing to the Lord.  

## 2장

**2:1** The Grain Offering “‘When anyone offers a ·grain [gift; tribute; this offering of grain, called a gift offering, was not considered an atonement for sin] offering to the Lord, that ·offering [gift] must be made from ·fine [choice] flour. The person must pour oil on it, put ·incense [frankincense] on it,  
**2:2** and then take it to Aaron’s sons, the priests. The priest must take a handful of the ·fine [choice] flour and oil and all the incense, and ·burn it [turn it into smoke] on the altar as a memorial portion. It is an offering made by fire, and its smell is pleasing to the Lord.  
**2:3** The rest of the ·grain [gift; tribute] offering will belong to Aaron and the priests; it is a most ·holy [sacred] part of the offerings made by fire to the Lord.  
**2:4** “‘If you bring a ·grain [gift; tribute] offering that was baked in the oven, it must be made from ·fine [choice] flour. It may be ·loaves made without yeast [unleavened cakes] and mixed with oil, or it may be ·wafers made without yeast [unleavened wafers] that have oil ·poured [spread] over them.  
**2:5** If your grain offering is cooked on a griddle, it must be made, ·without yeast [unleavened], of ·fine [choice] flour mixed with oil.  
**2:6** ·Crumble it [Break it in pieces] and pour oil over it; it is a ·grain [gift; tribute] offering.  
**2:7** If your ·grain [gift; tribute] offering is cooked in a pan, it must be made from ·fine [choice] flour and oil.  
**2:8** Bring the ·grain [gift; tribute] offering made ·of these things [or in any of these ways] to the Lord. Give it to the priest, and he will take it to the altar.  
**2:9** He will take out the memorial portion from the ·grain [gift; tribute] offering and ·burn it [turn it into smoke] on the altar, as an offering made by fire. Its smell is pleasing to the Lord.  
**2:10** The rest of the ·grain [gift; tribute] offering belongs to Aaron and the priests. It is a most ·holy [sacred] part of the offerings made to the Lord by fire.  
**2:11** “‘Every ·grain [gift; tribute] offering you bring to the Lord must be made without ·yeast [leaven], because you must not ·burn [turn into smoke] any ·yeast [leaven] or honey [both of them ferment in fire, a form of decay] in an offering made by fire to the Lord.  
**2:12** You may bring yeast and honey to the Lord as ·an offering [gift] from the first harvest, but they must not be ·burned [raised up] on the altar as a pleasing smell.  
**2:13** You must also put salt on all your ·grain [gift; tribute] offerings. Salt stands for your ·agreement [covenant; treaty] with God that will last forever; do not leave salt out of your ·grain [gift; tribute] offering. You must add salt to all your offerings [salt would endure the fire, thereby representing the eternal covenant].  
**2:14** “‘If you bring a ·grain [gift; tribute] offering from the first harvest to the Lord, bring crushed heads of new grain ·roasted [parched] in the fire.  
**2:15** Put oil and ·incense [frankincense] on it; it is a ·grain [gift; tribute] offering.  
**2:16** The priest will ·burn [turn into smoke] the memorial portion of the crushed grain and oil, with the ·incense [frankincense] on it. It is an offering by fire to the Lord.  

## 3장

**3:1** The Fellowship Offering “‘If a person’s ·fellowship [or peace; well-being] offering [demonstrated fellowship because the meat was to be eaten by the worshipers] to the Lord is from the herd, it may be a male or female, but it must ·have nothing wrong with it [be unblemished].  
**3:2** The person must ·put [lay] his hand on the animal’s head and ·kill [slaughter] it at the entrance to the Meeting Tent. Then Aaron’s sons, the priests, must ·sprinkle [dash] the blood on all sides of the altar.  
**3:3** From the ·fellowship [or peace; well-being] offering he must make a sacrifice by fire to the Lord. He must offer the fat of the animal’s inner organs (both the fat that is in them and that covers them),  
**3:4** both kidneys with the fat that is on them near the ·lower back muscle [loins], and the ·best part [appendage] of the liver, which he will remove with the kidneys.  
**3:5** Then the priests will ·burn these parts [turn these into smoke] on the altar, on the whole burnt offering that is on the wood of the fire. It is an offering made by fire, and its smell is pleasing to the Lord.  
**3:6** “‘If a person’s ·fellowship [or peace; well-being] offering to the Lord is ·a lamb or a goat [from the flock], it may be a male or female, but it must ·have nothing wrong with it [be unblemished].  
**3:7** If he offers a lamb, he must bring it before the Lord  
**3:8** and ·put [lay] his hand on its head. Then he must ·kill [slaughter] the animal in front of the Meeting Tent, and the ·priests [sons of Aaron] must ·sprinkle [dash] its blood on all sides of the altar.  
**3:9** From the ·fellowship [or peace; well-being] offering the person must make a sacrifice by fire to the Lord. He must bring the fat, the whole fat tail cut off close to the backbone, the fat of the inner organs (both the fat that is in them and that covers them),  
**3:10** both kidneys with the fat that is on them, near the ·lower back muscle [loins], and the ·best part [appendage] of the liver, which he will remove with the kidneys.  
**3:11** Then the priest will ·burn these parts [turn them into smoke] on the altar as food; it will be an offering made by fire to the Lord.  
**3:12** “‘If a person’s ·offering [gift] is a goat, he must offer it before the Lord  
**3:13** and ·put [lay] his hand on its head. Then he must ·kill [slaughter] it in front of the Meeting Tent, and the priests must ·sprinkle [dash] its blood on all sides of the altar.  
**3:14** From this ·offering [gift] the person must make a sacrifice by fire to the Lord. He must offer all the fat of the goat’s inner organs (both the fat that is in them and that covers them),  
**3:15** both kidneys with the fat that is on them near the ·lower back muscle [loins], and the ·best part [appendage] of the liver, which he will remove with the kidneys.  
**3:16** The priest will ·burn these parts [turn them into smoke] on the altar as food. It is an offering made by fire, and its smell is pleasing to the Lord. All the fat belongs to the Lord [see].  
**3:17** “‘This ·law [statute; ordinance; requirement] will continue for people ·from now on [forever], ·wherever you live [in all your habitations/settlements]: You must not eat any fat [perhaps considered the location of an animal’s strength] or ·blood [the source of life;].’”  

## 4장

**4:1** The Sin Offering The Lord said to Moses,  
**4:2** “Tell the ·people [sons; children] of Israel this: ‘When a person sins ·by accident [unintentionally; inadvertently; in contrast to a deliberate sin] ·and does some things the Lord has commanded not to be done [in any of the Lord’s commandments/regulations], ·that person must do these things [and does any one of them]:  
**4:3** “‘If the ·appointed [anointed] priest sins so that he brings guilt on the people, then he must offer a young bull to the Lord, ·one that has nothing wrong with it [unblemished], as a ·sin [or purification; the offering would purify the offerer of the effects of sin] offering for the sin he has done.  
**4:4** He will bring the bull to the entrance of the Meeting Tent in front of the Lord, ·put [lay] his hand on its head, and ·kill [slaughter] it before the Lord.  
**4:5** Then the ·appointed [anointed] priest must bring some of the bull’s blood into the Meeting Tent.  
**4:6** The priest is to dip his finger into the blood and ·sprinkle [dash] it seven times before the Lord in front of the curtain of the ·Most Holy Place [sanctuary].  
**4:7** The priest must also put some of the blood on the ·corners [horns] of the altar of incense that stands before the Lord in the Meeting Tent. The rest of the blood he must pour out at the ·bottom [base; foundation] of the altar of burnt offering, which is at the entrance of the Meeting Tent.  
**4:8** He must remove all the fat from the bull of the ·sin [or purification] offering—the fat on and around the inner organs,  
**4:9** both kidneys with the fat that is on them near the ·lower back muscle [loins], and the ·best part [appendage] of the liver which he will remove with the kidneys.  
**4:10** (He must do this in the same way the fat is removed from the bull of the ·fellowship [peace; well-being] offering.) Then the priest must ·burn [turn into smoke] the animal parts on the altar of burnt offering.  
**4:11** But the priest must carry off the skin of the bull and all its meat, along with the rest of the bull—its head, legs, intestines, and other inner organs.  
**4:12** He must take it outside the camp to the special clean [in a ritual sense] place where the ashes are poured out. He must burn it on a wood fire on the pile of ashes.  
**4:13** “‘If the whole ·nation [congregation; assembly] of Israel sins ·accidentally [inadvertently; unintentionally] ·without knowing it [and the matter is hidden from the eyes of the assembly/crowd] and does something the Lord has commanded not to be done, they are guilty.  
**4:14** When they learn about the sin they have done, they must offer a young bull as a ·sin [or purification] offering and bring it before the Meeting Tent.  
**4:15** The elders of the ·group of people [congregation; assembly] must ·put [lay] their hands on the bull’s head before the Lord, and it must be ·killed [slaughtered] before the Lord.  
**4:16** Then the ·appointed [anointed] priest must bring some of the bull’s blood into the Meeting Tent.  
**4:17** Dipping his finger in the blood, he must ·sprinkle [dash] it seven times before the Lord in front of the curtain.  
**4:18** Then he must put some of the blood on the ·corners [horns] of the altar that is before the Lord in the Meeting Tent. The priest must pour out the rest of the blood at the ·bottom [base; foundation] of the altar of burnt offering, which is at the entrance to the Meeting Tent.  
**4:19** He must remove all the fat from the animal and ·burn it [turn it into smoke] on the altar;  
**4:20** he will do the same thing with this bull that he did with the first bull of the ·sin [or purification] offering. In this way the priest ·removes the sins of the people so they will belong to the Lord and [atones for them and they will] be forgiven.  
**4:21** Then the priest must carry the bull outside the camp and burn it, just as he did with the first bull. This is the ·sin [or purification] offering for the whole ·community [assembly; crowd].  
**4:22** “‘If a ·ruler [leader; prince] sins ·by accident [inadvertently; unintentionally] and does something the Lord his God has commanded must not be done, he is guilty.  
**4:23** When he learns about his sin, he must bring a male goat that ·has nothing wrong [is unblemished] with it as his ·offering [gift].  
**4:24** The ·ruler [leader; prince] must ·put [lay] his hand on the goat’s head and ·kill [slaughter] it in the place where they ·kill [slaughter] the whole burnt offering before the Lord; it is a ·sin [purification] offering.  
**4:25** The priest must take some of the blood of the ·sin [purification] offering on his finger and put it on the ·corners [horns] of the altar of burnt offering. He must pour out the rest of the blood at the ·bottom [base; foundation] of the altar of burnt offering.  
**4:26** He must ·burn [turn into smoke] all the goat’s fat on the altar in the same way he ·burns [turns into smoke] the fat of the ·fellowship [peace; well-being] offerings. In this way the priest ·removes the ruler’s sin so he belongs to the Lord, and the Lord will forgive him [makes atonement for him and he is forgiven].  
**4:27** “‘If any ·person in the community [ordinary/private/lay person; of the people of the land] sins ·by accident [inadvertently; unintentionally] and does something which the Lord has commanded must not be done, he is guilty.  
**4:28** When the person learns about his sin, he must bring a female goat ·that has nothing wrong with it [unblemished] as an ·offering [gift] for his sin.  
**4:29** He must ·put [lay] his hand on the animal’s head and ·kill [slaughter] it at the place of the whole burnt offering.  
**4:30** Then the priest must take some of the goat’s blood on his finger and put it on the ·corners [horns] of the altar of burnt offering. He must pour out the rest of the goat’s blood at the ·bottom [base; foundation] of the altar.  
**4:31** Then the priest must remove all the goat’s fat in the same way the fat is removed from the ·fellowship [peace; well-being] offerings. He must ·burn it [turn it into smoke] on the altar as a smell pleasing to the Lord. In this way the priest ·will remove that person’s sin so he will belong to the Lord, and the Lord will forgive him [makes atonement for him and he is forgiven].  
**4:32** “‘If this person brings a lamb as his ·offering [gift] for sin, he must bring a female ·that has nothing wrong with it [unblemished].  
**4:33** He must ·put [lay] his hand on the animal’s head and ·kill [slaughter] it as a ·sin [or purification] offering in the place where the whole burnt offering is ·killed [slaughtered].  
**4:34** The priest must take some of the blood from the sin offering on his finger and put it on the ·corners [horns] of the altar of burnt offering. He must pour out the rest of the lamb’s blood at the ·bottom [base; foundation] of the altar.  
**4:35** Then the priest must remove all the lamb’s fat in the same way that the lamb’s fat is removed from the ·fellowship [peace; well-being] offerings. He must ·burn the pieces on the altar [turn them into smoke] on top of the offerings made by fire for the Lord. In this way the priest ·will remove that person’s sins so he will belong to the Lord, and the Lord will forgive him [makes atonement for him and he is forgiven].  

## 5장

**5:1** Special Types of Accidental Sins “‘If a person ·is ordered to tell in court [hears a public oath and he has been a witness of] what he has seen or what he knows and he does not ·tell the court [make it known], he is guilty of sin.  
**5:2** “‘Or someone might touch something unclean, such as the dead body of an unclean wild animal or an unclean farm animal or an unclean crawling animal. Even if he does not know that he touched it, he will still be unclean and guilty of sin.  
**5:3** “‘Someone might touch human uncleanness—anything that makes someone unclean—and not know it. But when he learns about it, he will be guilty.  
**5:4** “‘Or someone might make a promise before the Lord ·without thinking [rashly]. It might be a [rash] promise to do something bad or something good; it might be about anything. Even if he forgets about it, when he remembers, he will be guilty.  
**5:5** “‘When anyone is guilty of any of these things, he must ·tell how he sinned [confess his sin].  
**5:6** He must bring an offering to the Lord as a penalty for sin; it must be a female lamb or goat from the flock. The priest will ·perform the acts to remove that person’s sin so he will belong to the Lord [make atonement for his sin].  
**5:7** “‘But if the person cannot afford a lamb, he must bring two ·doves [turtledoves] or two young pigeons to the Lord as the penalty for his sin. One bird must be for a ·sin [or purification] offering, and the other must be for a whole burnt offering.  
**5:8** He must bring them to the priest, who will first offer the one for the ·sin [or purification] offering. He will ·pull [wring] the bird’s head from its neck, but he will not ·pull it completely off [sever it].  
**5:9** He must ·sprinkle [dash] the blood from the ·sin [purification] offering on the side of the altar, and then he must pour the rest of the blood at the ·bottom [base; foundation] of the altar; it is a ·sin [or purification] offering.  
**5:10** Then the priest must offer the second bird as a whole burnt offering, as the ·law [regulation] says. In this way the priest ·will remove the person’s sin so he will belong to the Lord, and the Lord will forgive him [makes atonement for him and he is forgiven].  
**5:11** “‘If the person cannot afford two ·doves [turtledoves] or two pigeons, he must bring about ·two quarts of [one-tenth of an ephah of] ·fine [choice] flour as an ·offering for sin [purification offering]. He must not put oil or ·incense [frankincense] on the flour, because it is a ·sin [or purification] offering.  
**5:12** He must bring the flour to the priest. The priest will take a handful of the flour as a memorial offering and burn it on the altar on top of the offerings made by fire to the Lord; it is a ·sin [or purification] offering.  
**5:13** In this way the priest ·will remove the person’s sins so he will belong to the Lord, and the Lord will forgive him [makes atonement for him and he will be forgiven]. What is left of the sin offering belongs to the priest, like the ·grain [gift; tribute] offering.’”  
**5:14** The Penalty Offering The Lord said to Moses,  
**5:15** “If a person ·accidentally [inadvertently; unintentionally] sins and ·does something against [is unfaithful/disloyal toward] the holy things of the Lord, he must bring from the flock a male sheep ·that has nothing wrong with it [unblemished]. This will be his ·penalty [guilt; reparation] offering to the Lord. Its value in silver must be correct as set by the Holy Place measure. It is a ·penalty [guilt; reparation] offering.  
**5:16** That person must pay for the sin he did against the holy thing, adding one-fifth to its value. Then he must give it all to the priest. In this way the priest will ·remove the person’s sin so he will belong to the Lord [make atonement for him], by using the male sheep as the ·penalty [guilt; reparation] offering. And ·the Lord will forgive the person [he will be forgiven].  
**5:17** “If a person sins and does something the Lord has commanded not to be done, even if he does not know it, he is still guilty. He is responsible for his sin.  
**5:18** He must bring the priest a male sheep from the flock, one that ·has nothing wrong with it [is unblemished] and that is worth the correct amount. It will be a ·penalty [guilt; reparation] offering. Though the person sinned without knowing it, with this offering the priest will ·remove the sin so the person will belong to the Lord, and the Lord will forgive him [make atonement for him and he will be forgiven].  
**5:19** The person is guilty of doing wrong, so he must give the ·penalty [guilt; reparation] offering to the Lord.”  

## 6장

**6:1** The Lord said to Moses,  
**6:2** “A person might sin against the Lord ·by doing one of these sins [and be unfaithful/disloyal]: He might lie about ·what happened to something he was taking care of for someone else [a deposit], or ·he might lie about a promise he made [a pledge]. He might steal something or cheat someone.  
**6:3** He might find something that had been lost and then lie about it. He might make a promise before the Lord about something and not mean it, or he might do some other sin.  
**6:4** If he does any of these things, he is guilty of sin. He must bring back whatever he stole or whatever he took by cheating. He must bring back the ·thing he took care of for someone else [deposit]. He must bring back what he found and lied about  
**6:5** or what he made a false promise about. He must pay the full price plus an extra one-fifth of the value of what he took. He must give the money to the true owner on the day he brings his ·penalty [guilt; reparation] offering.  
**6:6** He must bring his penalty to the priest—a male sheep from the flock, ·one that does not have anything wrong with it [unblemished] and that is worth the correct amount. It will be a ·penalty [guilt; reparation] offering to the Lord.  
**6:7** Then the priest will ·perform the acts to remove that person’s sin so he will belong to the Lord, and the Lord will forgive him [make atonement for him and he will be forgiven] for the sins that made him guilty.”  
**6:8** The Whole Burnt Offering The Lord said to Moses,  
**6:9** “Give this command to Aaron and ·the priests [his sons]: ‘These are the ·teachings [laws; instruction] about the whole burnt offering : The burnt offering must stay on the [hearth of the] altar all night until morning, and the altar’s fire must be kept burning.  
**6:10** The priest must put on his linen robe and linen underclothes next to his body. Then he will remove the ashes from the burnt offering on the altar and put them beside the altar.  
**6:11** Then he must take off those clothes and put on others and carry the ashes outside the camp to a special clean [in a ritual sense] place.  
**6:12** But the fire must be kept burning on the altar; it must not be ·allowed to go out [extinguished]. The priest must put more firewood on the altar every morning, place the whole burnt offering on the fire, and ·burn [turn into smoke] the fat of the ·fellowship [or peace; well-being] offerings.  
**6:13** The fire must be kept burning on the altar ·all the time [perpetually]; it must not ·go out [be extinguished].  
**6:14** The Grain Offering “‘These are the ·teachings [laws; instructions] about the ·grain [gift; tribute] offering : The ·priests [sons of Aaron] must bring it to the Lord in front of the altar.  
**6:15** The priest must take a handful of ·fine [choice] flour, with the oil and all of the ·incense [frankincense] on it, and ·burn the grain offering [turn it into smoke] on the altar as a memorial offering to the Lord. Its smell is pleasing to him.  
**6:16** Aaron and ·the priests [his sons] may eat what is left, but it must be eaten ·without yeast [unleavened] in a holy place. They must eat it in the courtyard of the Meeting Tent.  
**6:17** It must not be ·cooked [baked] with ·yeast [leaven]. I have given it as their ·share [portion] of the offerings made to me by fire; it is most holy, like the ·sin [or purification] offering and the ·penalty [guilt; reparation] offering.  
**6:18** Any male descendant of Aaron may eat it as his ·share of the offerings [perpetual due; decree] made to the Lord by fire, ·and this will continue from now on [throughout your generations]. Whatever touches these offerings shall become holy.’”  
**6:19** The Lord said to Moses,  
**6:20** “This is the ·offering [gift] Aaron and ·the priests [his sons] must bring to the Lord on the day they ·appoint Aaron as high priest [anoint him]: They must bring ·two quarts [one-tenth of an ephah] of ·fine [choice] flour for a ·continual [perpetual] ·grain [gift; tribute] offering, half of it in the morning and half in the evening.  
**6:21** The ·fine [choice] flour must be mixed with oil and cooked on a griddle. Bring it when it is well ·mixed [or soaked]. Present the ·grain [gift; tribute] offering that is ·broken into pieces [or partly baked; or folded], and it will be a smell that is pleasing to the Lord.  
**6:22** One of the priests ·appointed [anointed] to take Aaron’s place as high priest must make the ·grain [gift; tribute] offering. It is a ·rule [statute; ordinance; regulation] forever that the ·grain [gift; tribute] offering must be completely burned to the Lord.  
**6:23** Every grain offering made by a priest must be completely ·burned [turned into smoke]; it must not be eaten.”  
**6:24** The Sin Offering The Lord said to Moses,  
**6:25** “Tell Aaron and ·the priests [his sons]: ‘These are the ·teachings [laws; instructions] about the ·sin [or purification] offering: The ·sin [or purification] offering must be ·killed [slaughtered] in front of the Lord in the same place the whole burnt offering is ·killed [slaughtered]; it is most holy.  
**6:26** The priest who offers the ·sin [or purification] offering must eat it in a holy place, in the courtyard of the Meeting Tent.  
**6:27** Whatever touches the meat of the ·sin [or purification] offering ·must be [or will become] ·holy [consecrated; set apart], and if the blood is ·sprinkled [spattered] on any clothes, you must wash them in a holy place.  
**6:28** The clay pot the meat is ·cooked [boiled] in must be broken, or if a bronze pot is used, it must be scrubbed and rinsed with water.  
**6:29** Any male in a priest’s family may eat the offering; it is most holy.  
**6:30** But if the blood of the ·sin [or purification] offering is taken into the Meeting Tent and used to ·remove sin [make atonement] in the Holy Place, that ·sin [or purification] offering must be burned with fire. It must not be eaten.  

## 7장

**7:1** The Penalty Offering “‘These are the ·teachings [laws; instructions] about the ·penalty [guilt; reparation] offering, which is most holy:  
**7:2** The ·penalty [guilt; reparation] offering must be ·killed [slaughtered] where the whole burnt offering is ·killed [slaughtered]. Then the priest must ·sprinkle [dash] its blood on all sides of the altar.  
**7:3** He must offer all the fat from the ·penalty [guilt; reparation] offering—the fat tail, the fat that covers the inner organs,  
**7:4** both kidneys with the fat that is on them near the ·lower back muscle [loins], and the ·best part [appendage] of the liver, which is to be removed with the kidneys.

**13:1** Rules About Skin Diseases The Lord said to Moses and Aaron,  
**13:2** “Someone might have on ·his skin [the skin of his body; and so throughout the passage] a swelling or a ·rash [eruption] or a bright spot. If the sore looks like ·a harmful skin disease [leprosy; the term does not refer to modern leprosy (Hansen’s disease), but to various skin disorders], the person must be brought to Aaron the priest or to one of Aaron’s sons, the priests.  
**13:3** The priest must ·look at [examine] the sore on the person’s skin. If the hair in the sore has become white, and the sore seems deeper than the person’s skin, it is a harmful skin disease. When he has finished ·looking at [examining] the person, the priest must announce that the person is unclean [in a ritual sense; the principle of “wholeness” was disturbed by running sores or by a skin disease that partially covered the body].  
**13:4** “If there is a white spot on a person’s skin, but the spot does not seem deeper than the skin, and if the hair from the spot has not turned white, the priest must ·separate [confine; quarantine] that person from other people for seven days.  
**13:5** On the seventh day the priest must ·look at [examine] the person again. If he sees that the sore has not changed and it has not spread on the skin, the priest must keep the person ·separated [confined; quarantined] for seven more days.  
**13:6** On the seventh day the priest must ·look at [examine] the person again. If the sore has faded and has not spread on the skin, the priest must announce that the person is clean [in a ritual sense]. The sore is only a ·rash [eruption]. The person must wash his clothes, and he will become clean again.  
**13:7** “But if the ·rash [eruption] spreads again after the priest has announced him clean [in a ritual sense], the person must come again to the priest.  
**13:8** The priest must ·look at [examine] him, and if the ·rash [eruption] has spread on the skin, the priest must announce that the person is unclean; it is a harmful skin disease.  
**13:9** “If a person has a harmful skin disease, he must be brought to the priest,  
**13:10** and the priest must ·look at [examine] him. If there is a white swelling in the skin, and the hair has become white, and the skin looks raw in the swelling,  
**13:11** it is a harmful skin disease. It is one he has had for a long time. The priest must announce that the person is unclean [in a ritual sense]. He will not need to ·separate [confine; quarantine] that person from other people, because everyone already knows that the person is unclean.  
**13:12** “If the skin disease spreads all over a person’s body, covering his skin from his head to his feet, as far as the priest can see, the priest must ·look at [examine] the person’s whole body.  
**13:13** If the priest sees that the disease covers the whole body and has turned all of the person’s skin white, he must announce that the person is clean [in a ritual sense; he was clean because he was whole, uniformly covered by skin disease].  
**13:14** “But when the person has ·an open sore [raw flesh], he is unclean [in a ritual sense; because there was a break in the surface of his body, he was no longer whole].  
**13:15** When the priest sees the ·open sore [raw flesh], he must announce that the person is unclean. The open sore is not clean; it is a harmful skin disease.  
**13:16** If the ·open sore [raw flesh] becomes white again, the person must come to the priest.  
**13:17** The priest must ·look at [examine] him, and if the ·sores have [raw flesh has] become white, the priest must announce that the person with the ·sores [raw flesh] is clean. Then he will be clean.  
**13:18** “Someone may have a boil on his skin that is healed.  
**13:19** If in the place where the boil was, there is a white swelling or a bright red spot, this place on the skin must be shown to the priest.  
**13:20** And the priest must ·look at [examine] it. If the spot seems deeper than the skin and the hair on it has become white, the priest must announce that the person is unclean [in a ritual sense]. The spot is a harmful skin disease that has broken out from inside the boil.  
**13:21** But if the priest looks at the spot and there are no white hairs in it and the spot is not deeper than the skin and it has faded, the priest must ·separate [confine; quarantine] the person from other people for seven days.  
**13:22** If the spot spreads on the skin, the priest must announce that the person is unclean; it is a disease that will spread.  
**13:23** But if the bright spot does not spread or change, it is only the scar from the old boil. Then the priest must announce that the person is clean.  
**13:24** “When a person gets a burn on his skin, if the ·open sore [raw flesh] becomes white or red,  
**13:25** the priest must ·look at [examine] it. If the white spot seems deeper than the skin and the hair at that spot has become white, it is a harmful skin disease. The disease has broken out in the burn, and the priest must announce that the person is unclean [in a ritual sense]. It is a harmful skin disease.  
**13:26** But if the priest ·looks at [examines] the spot and there is no white hair in the bright spot, and the spot is no deeper than the skin and has faded, the priest must ·separate [confine; quarantine] the person from other people for seven days.  
**13:27** On the seventh day the priest must ·look at [examine] him again. If the spot has spread on the skin, the priest must announce that the person is unclean. It is a harmful skin disease.  
**13:28** But if the bright spot has not spread on the skin but has faded, it is the swelling from the burn. The priest must announce that the person is clean, because the spot is only a scar from the burn.  
**13:29** “When a man or a woman gets a sore on the scalp or on the ·chin [or cheek; or beard],  
**13:30** a priest must ·look at [examine] the sore. If it seems deeper than the skin and the hair around it is thin and yellow, the priest must announce that the person is unclean [in a ritual sense]. It is an ·itch [scaly patch; perhaps eczema or ringworm], a harmful skin disease of the head or ·chin [or cheek; or beard].  
**13:31** But if the priest ·looks at [examines] it and it does not seem deeper than the skin and there is no black hair in it, the priest must ·separate [confine; quarantine] the person from other people for seven days.  
**13:32** On the seventh day the priest must ·look at [examine] the ·sore [itch; scaly patch]. If it has not spread, and there are no yellow hairs growing in it, and the ·sore [itch; scaly patch] does not seem deeper than the skin,  
**13:33** the person must shave himself, but he must not shave the ·sore place [itch; scaly patch]. The priest must ·separate [confine; quarantine] that person from other people for seven more days.  
**13:34** On the seventh day the priest must ·look at [examine] the ·sore [itch; scaly patch]. If it has not spread on the skin and it does not seem deeper than the skin, the priest must announce that the person is clean [in a ritual sense]. So the person must wash his clothes and become clean.  
**13:35** But if the ·sore [itch; scaly patch] spreads on the skin after the person has become clean,  
**13:36** the priest must ·look at [examine] him again. If the sore has spread on the skin, the priest doesn’t need to look for the yellowish hair; the person is unclean.  
**13:37** But if the priest thinks the sore has stopped spreading, and black hair is growing in it, the ·sore [itch; scaly patch] has healed. The person is clean, and the priest must announce that he is clean.  
**13:38** “When a man or a woman has white spots on the skin,  
**13:39** a priest must ·look at [examine] them. If the spots on the skin are dull white, the disease is only a harmless rash. That person is clean [in a ritual sense].  
**13:40** “When anyone loses hair from his head and is bald, he is clean [in a ritual sense].  
**13:41** If he loses hair from the front of his head and has a bald forehead, he is clean.  
**13:42** But if there is a red-white sore on his bald head or forehead, it is a skin disease breaking out in those places.  
**13:43** A priest must ·look at [examine] that person. If the swelling of the sore on his bald head or forehead is red-white, like a skin disease that spreads,  
**13:44** that person has a skin disease. He is unclean. The priest must announce that the person is unclean because of the sore on his head.  
**13:45** “If a person has a skin disease that spreads, he must warn other people by shouting, ‘Unclean, unclean [in a ritual sense]!’ His clothes must be torn at the seams, he must let his hair stay uncombed, and he must cover his ·mouth [upper lip].  
**13:46** That person will be unclean the whole time he has the disease; he is unclean. He must live alone outside the camp.

**13:48** (either woven or knitted), or of leather, or something made from leather.  
**13:49** If the mildew in the clothing, leather, or woven or knitted material is green or red, it is a spreading mildew. It must be shown to the priest.

**13:54** he must order the people to wash that piece of leather or cloth. Then he must ·separate [confine; quarantine] the clothing for seven more days.

**13:56** “But when the priest ·looks at [examines] that piece of leather or cloth, the mildew might have faded after the piece has been washed. Then the priest must tear the mildew out of the piece of leather or cloth (either woven or knitted).  
**13:57** But if the mildew comes back to that piece of leather or cloth (either woven or knitted), the mildew is spreading. And whatever has the mildew must be burned with fire.  
**13:58** When the cloth (either woven or knitted) or the leather is washed and the mildew is gone, it must be washed again; then it will be clean [in a ritual sense].  
**13:59** “These are the ·teachings [laws; instructions] about mildew on pieces of cloth (either woven or knitted) or leather, to decide if they are clean or unclean [in a ritual sense].”
