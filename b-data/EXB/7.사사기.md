# 7.사사기 (EXB)



## 1장

**1:1** Judah Fights the Canaanites After Joshua died, the ·Israelites [sons/children of Israel] asked the ·Lord [or Yahweh; the translation “Lord” (all caps) represents the divine name YHWH, usually pronounced “Yahweh”], “Who will ·be first to go and [lead the] fight for us against the Canaanites?”  
**1:2** The Lord said to them, “·The tribe of Judah [Judah] will go. [Look; Behold] I have ·handed the land over to them [given the land into his hand].”  
**1:3** ·The men of Judah [Judah] said to ·the men of Simeon, their relatives [his brother Simeon], “Come and help us fight the Canaanites for our ·land [allotment]. If you do, we will go and help you fight for your ·land [allotment].” So ·the men of Simeon [Simeon] went with them [Simeon’s land lay within Judah].  
**1:4** When Judah attacked, the Lord handed over the Canaanites and the Perizzites to them, and they defeated ten thousand men at the city of Bezek.  
**1:5** ·There [At Bezek] they found Adoni-Bezek [the ruler of the city], and fought him. The men of Judah defeated the Canaanites and the Perizzites,  
**1:6** but Adoni-Bezek ran away. The men of Judah chased him, and when they caught him, they cut off his thumbs and big toes [such mutilation was common in the ancient Near East, rendering a king unfit for military service or priestly functions].  
**1:7** Adoni-Bezek said, “Seventy kings whose thumbs and big toes had been cut off used to eat scraps that fell from my table. Now God has paid me back for what I did to them.” The men of Judah took Adoni-Bezek to Jerusalem, and he died there.  
**1:8** Then the ·men [sons] of Judah fought against Jerusalem and captured it. They ·attacked with their swords [struck it with the edge of the sword] and burned the city [a temporary conquest; David later captured the city].  
**1:9** Later, they went down to fight the Canaanites who lived in the mountains, in the ·dry [or hill] country ·to the south [in the Negev], and in the ·western hills [or lowland].  
**1:10** ·The men of Judah [Judah] went to fight against the Canaanites in the city of Hebron (which used to be called Kiriath Arba ). And they defeated Sheshai, Ahiman, and Talmai.  
**1:11** Caleb and His Daughter Then they left there and went to fight against the people living in Debir. (In the past Debir had been called Kiriath Sepher.)  
**1:12** Before attacking the city, Caleb said, “I will give Acsah, my daughter, as a wife to the man who attacks and captures the city of Kiriath Sepher.”  
**1:13** Othniel son of Kenaz, Caleb’s younger brother, captured the city, so Caleb gave his daughter Acsah to Othniel to be his wife.  
**1:14** When Acsah came to ·Othniel [him; could be Othniel or Caleb], she ·told him to ask [or asked] her father for a field. When she got down from her donkey, Caleb asked her, “What do you want?”   
**1:15** Acsah answered him, “·Do me a special favor [Give me a blessing]. Since you have given me land in ·southern Canaan [the Negev], also give me springs of water.” So Caleb gave her the upper and lower springs.  
**1:16** Fights with the Canaanites The ·Kenite people, who were from the family of [sons/descendants of the Kenite] Moses’ father-in-law, left the city of palm trees [Jericho]. They went with the men of Judah to the ·Desert [Wilderness] of Judah to live with them there in ·southern Judah [the Negev] near the city of Arad.  
**1:17** The men of Judah and the men of Simeon, their ·relatives [brothers], defeated the Canaanites who lived in Zephath. They ·completely destroyed the city [devoted it to the Lord for destruction], so they called it Hormah [sounds like Hebrew for “total destruction”].  
**1:18** The men of Judah captured Gaza, Ashkelon, Ekron [Philistine cities along the coast], and the lands around them.  
**1:19** The Lord was with the men of Judah. They took the land in the ·mountains [hill country], but they could not force out the people living on the plain, because they had iron chariots [wooden chariots with iron fittings].  
**1:20** As Moses had promised, Hebron was given to Caleb, and Caleb forced out the three sons of Anak.  
**1:21** But the people of Benjamin could not ·make the Jebusite people leave [drive out the Jebusites from] Jerusalem. Since that time the Jebusites have lived with the ·Benjaminites [sons of Benjamin] in Jerusalem.  
**1:22** The ·men [house] of Joseph went to fight against the city of Bethel, and the Lord was with them.  
**1:23** They sent some spies to Bethel (which used to be called Luz).  
**1:24** The spies saw a man coming out of the city and said to him, “Show us a way into the city, and we will ·be kind to [have mercy on; or reward] you.”  
**1:25** So the man showed them the way into the city. The men of Joseph ·attacked with swords the people in Bethel [struck the city with the edge of the sword], but they let the man and his family go free.  
**1:26** He went to the land where the Hittites lived [in Syria, north of Israel] and built a city. He named it Luz, which it is called even today.  
**1:27** The people of Manasseh did not ·force [drive] out the inhabitants of the cities of Beth Shan, Taanach, Dor, Ibleam, Megiddo, nor the small towns around them, because the Canaanites were determined to stay there.  
**1:28** Later, the Israelites grew strong and forced the Canaanites ·to work as slaves [into forced labor], but they did not ·make all the Canaanites leave their land [drive them out completely].  
**1:29** The people of Ephraim did not ·force [drive] out all of the Canaanites living in Gezer. So the Canaanites continued to live in Gezer with the people of Ephraim.  
**1:30** The people of Zebulun did not force out the Canaanites living in the cities of Kitron and Nahalol. They stayed and lived with the people of Zebulun, but Zebulun ·made them work as slaves [forced them into hard labor].  
**1:31** The people of Asher did not ·force [drive out] the Canaanites from the cities of Acco, Sidon, Ahlab, Aczib, Helbah, Aphek, and Rehob [cities on the Mediterranean Sea north of Carmel and inland from the coast].  
**1:32** Since the people of Asher did not ·force [drive] them out, the Canaanites continued to live with them.  
**1:33** The people of Naphtali did not ·force [drive] out the people of the cities of Beth Shemesh and Beth Anath [the region east of Asher]. So they continued to live with the Canaanites in those cities, and the Canaanites ·worked as slaves [did forced labor].  
**1:34** The Amorites forced the Danites back into the ·mountains [hill country] and would not let them come down to live in the plain.  
**1:35** The Amorites were determined to stay in Mount Heres, Aijalon, and Shaalbim. But when the Israelites grew stronger, they made the Amorites ·work as slaves [do forced labor].  
**1:36** The land of the Amorites was from ·Scorpion [or Akrabbim; Hebrew for “scorpion”] ·Pass [or Ascent; south of the Dead Sea] to Sela and beyond.  

## 2장

**2:1** The Angel of the Lord at Bokim The ·angel [messenger] of the Lord [an angelic spokesperson for God, sometimes identified with the Lord himself] went up from Gilgal to Bokim and said, “I brought you up from Egypt and led you to the land I promised to give your ancestors. I said, ‘I will never break my ·agreement [covenant] with you.  
**2:2** But you must not make an ·agreement [covenant] with the people who live in this land. You must ·destroy [tear down] their altars.’ But you did not obey me. How could you do this [What is this you have done]?  
**2:3** ·Now I tell you [or Then I told you], ‘I will not ·force [drive] out the people in this land. They will ·be your enemies [or ensnare you; or be thorns in your side], and their gods will be a ·trap [snare] for you.’”  
**2:4** After the angel gave ·Israel [the sons/children of Israel] this message from the Lord, they ·cried loudly [lifted up their voices and wept].  
**2:5** So they named the place Bokim [“weeping ones”]. There they offered sacrifices to the Lord.  
**2:6** Joshua Dies After Joshua ·dismissed [sent away] the people, the ·Israelites [sons/children of Israel] went to take possession of the land that they had been given.  
**2:7** The people ·served [worshiped; remained faithful to] the Lord during the lifetime of Joshua and during the lifetimes of the elders who ·lived after [outlived] Joshua and who had seen what great things the Lord had done for Israel.  
**2:8** Joshua son of Nun, the servant of the Lord, died at the age of one hundred ten.  
**2:9** They buried him in ·his own land [the territory of his inheritance] at Timnath Heres [also known as Timnath Serah] in the ·mountains [hill country] of Ephraim, north of Mount Gaash.  
**2:10** The People Disobey After ·those people [the whole generation] ·had died [were gathered to their fathers/ancestors], ·their children [another generation] grew up and did not know the Lord or what he had done for Israel.  
**2:11** So ·they [the sons/children of Israel] did ·what the Lord said was wrong [evil in the eyes/sight of the Lord], and they worshiped the ·Baal idols [Baals; Baal was the main god of the Canaanites, but had many local manifestations].  
**2:12** They ·quit following [abandoned] the Lord, the God of their ancestors who had brought them out of Egypt. They began to worship the gods of the people who lived around them, and that made the Lord angry.  
**2:13** The Israelites ·quit following [abandoned] the Lord and worshiped Baal and Ashtoreth.  
**2:14** The Lord was angry with the people of Israel, so he ·handed them over to [gave them into the hand of] ·robbers [raiders; plunderers] who took their possessions. He ·let their enemies who lived around them defeat them [sold them into the hand of their enemies around them]; they could not ·protect themselves [resist/stand before them].  
**2:15** When the Israelites went out to fight, ·they always lost, because the Lord was not with them [the hand of the Lord was against them for harm/evil]. The Lord had sworn to them this would happen. So the Israelites ·suffered very much [were in great distress].  
**2:16** God Chooses Judges Then the Lord ·chose leaders called [raised up] ·judges [leaders; not courtroom judges, but leaders who guided the nation through difficult times, sometimes as military commanders], ·who saved the Israelites from [to deliver them from the hand of] the ·robbers [raiders; plunderers].  
**2:17** But the Israelites did not listen to their ·judges [leaders]. They ·were not faithful to God but [prostituted themselves to and] worshiped other gods instead. Their ancestors had ·obeyed [walked in the way/path of] the Lord’s commands, but they quickly turned away and did not obey.  
**2:18** Whenever the Lord sent ·judges [leaders] to save the Israelites from their enemies, he was with that ·judge [leader] and rescued the people during that ·judge’s [leader’s] lifetime. The Lord ·felt sorry for them [took pity on them; or relented] when they cried for help because of those who ·hurt [oppressed and afflicted] them.  
**2:19** But when the ·judges [leaders] died, the Israelites ·again sinned [returned to their corrupt ways] and worshiped other gods. They became worse than their ancestors. The Israelites were very stubborn and refused to change their evil ways.  
**2:20** So the ·Lord became angry with [anger of the Lord burned against] the Israelites. He said, “These people have ·broken [violated] the ·agreement [covenant] I made with their ancestors. They have not listened to me.  
**2:21** I will no longer ·defeat [drive out before them] the nations who were left when Joshua died.  
**2:22** I will use them to test Israel, to see if Israel will keep ·the Lord’s commands [the way of the Lord to walk in it] as their ancestors did.”  
**2:23** In the past the Lord had permitted those nations to stay in the land. He did not quickly ·force [drive] them out or ·help Joshua’s army defeat them [give them into the hand of Joshua].

**3:13** Eglon got the ·Ammonites and the Amalekites [sons/descendants of Ammon and Amalek] to join him. Then he attacked Israel and took the city of palm trees [Jericho].  
**3:14** So the ·people [sons; children] of Israel were ·ruled by [subject to] Eglon king of Moab for eighteen years.  
**3:15** When the people cried to the Lord, he ·sent someone to save [raised up a rescuer/deliverer for] them. He was Ehud, son of Gera from the people of Benjamin, who was ·left-handed [bound in the right hand]. Israel sent Ehud to give Eglon king of Moab the ·payment [tribute money] he demanded.  
**3:16** Ehud made himself a sword with two edges, ·about eighteen inches [a cubit; the distance between the elbow and the tip of the fingers] long, and he tied it to his right hip under his clothes.  
**3:17** Ehud gave Eglon king of Moab the ·payment [tribute money] he demanded. Now Eglon was a very fat man [Eglon means “fat calf”].  
**3:18** After he had given Eglon the ·payment [tribute money], Ehud ·sent away [dismissed] the people who had carried it.  
**3:19** When he passed the ·statues [images; idols] near Gilgal, he turned around [Ehud returned to Eglon’s palace and sought a private audience with the king] and said to Eglon, “I have a secret message for you, King Eglon.” The king said, “·Be quiet [Silence; or Give us privacy]!” Then he sent all of his servants out of the room.

**3:23** Then he went out ·of the room [or to the porch/vestibule; or through the latrine] and closed and locked the doors behind him.

**3:30** So that day Moab was ·forced to be under the rule of Israel [subdued/made subject that day under the hand of Israel], and there was ·peace [rest] in the land for eighty years.  
**3:31** Shamgar, the Judge After Ehud, Shamgar son of Anath saved Israel. Shamgar killed six hundred Philistines with ·a sharp stick used to guide oxen [an oxgoad].
