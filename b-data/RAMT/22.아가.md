# 22.아가 (RAMT)



## 1장

**1:1** The Song of Songs, which is Solomon’s.  
**1:2** Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth,<br>for your loving is better than wine.  
**1:3** For fragrance your oils are goodly,<br>poured oil is your name.<br>And so the young women love you.  
**1:4** Draw me after you, let us run.<br>The king has brought me to his chamber.<br>Let us be glad and rejoice in you.<br>Let us extol your loving beyond wine.<br>Rightly do they love you.  
**1:5** I am dark but desirable,<br>O daughters of Jerusalem,<br>like the tents of Kedar,<br>like Solomon’s curtains.  
**1:6** Do not look on me for being dark,<br>for the sun has glared on me.<br>My mother’s sons were incensed with me,<br>they made me a keeper of the vineyards.<br>My own vineyard I have not kept.  
**1:7** Tell me, whom I love so,<br>where you pasture your flock at noon,<br>lest I go straying<br>after the flocks of your companions.  
**1:8** —If you do not know, O fairest of women,<br>go out in the tracks of the sheep<br>and graze your goats<br>by the shepherd’s shelters.  
**1:9** To my mare among Pharaoh’s chariots<br>I likened you, my friend.  
**1:10** Your cheeks are lovely with looped earrings,<br>your neck with beads.  
**1:11** Earrings of gold we will make for you<br>with silver filigree.  
**1:12** While the king was on his couch<br>my nard gave off its scent.  
**1:13** A sachet of myrrh is my lover to me,<br>all night between my breasts.  
**1:14** A cluster of henna, my lover to me,<br>in the vineyards of Ein-Gedi.  
**1:15** O you are fair, my friend,<br>O you are fair, your eyes are doves.  
**1:16** —O you are fair, my lover, you are sweet,<br>our bed is verdant, too.  
**1:17** Our house’s beams are cedar,<br>our rafters evergreens.  

## 2장

**2:1** I am the rose of Sharon,<br>the lily of the valley.  
**2:2** —Like a lily among the thorns<br>so is my friend among the young women.  
**2:3** —Like a quince tree among the trees of the forest<br>so is my lover among the young men.<br>In its shade I delighted to sit<br>and its fruit was sweet to my taste  
**2:4** He has brought me to the house of wine<br>and his banner over me is love.  
**2:5** Stay me up with raisin cakes,<br>cushion me with quinces,<br>for I am in a swoon of love.  
**2:6** His left hand beneath my head,<br>his right hand embracing me.  
**2:7** I make you swear, O daughters of Jerusalem<br>by the deer or the gazelles of the field,<br>that you shall not rouse not stir love<br>until it pleases.  
**2:8** Hark! Oh, my lover is coming,<br>bounding over the mountains,<br>leaping over the hills.  
**2:9** My lover is like a deer<br>or like a stag.<br>Oh, he stands behind our wall<br>peering through the windows,<br>peeping through the crannies.  
**2:10** My lover spoke out and said to me:<br>“Arise my friend, my fair one, go.  
**2:11** For, look, the winter has passed<br>the rain has gone away.  
**2:12** Buds can be seen in the land,<br>the nightingale’s season has come<br>and the turtledove’s voice is heard in our land.  
**2:13** The fig tree has put forth its green fruit<br>and the vines in blossom waft fragrance.<br>Arise and go, my friend,<br>my fair one, go forth.”  
**2:14** My dove in the rock’s crevices,<br>in the hollow of the cliff,<br>show me how you look,<br>let me hear your voice,<br>for your voice is sweet<br>and your look desirable.  
**2:15** Seize us the foxes,<br>the little foxes,<br>despoiling the vineyards,<br>but our vineyards are in bloom.  
**2:16** My lover is mine and I am his,<br>who grazes among the lilies.  
**2:17** Until morning’s breeze blows<br>and the shadows flee,<br>turn round, be like a deer, my love,<br>or like a gazelle<br>on the cloven mountains.  

## 3장

**3:1** On my couch at night<br>I sought him I love so.<br>I sought him but did not find him.  
**3:2** Let me rise and go round the town,<br>in the streets and in the square.<br>Let me seek him I love so.<br>I sought him but did not find him.  
**3:3** The watchmen who go round the town found me.<br>“Have you seen him I love so?”  
**3:4** I had barely passed on from them<br>when I found him I love so.<br>I held him and did not let go<br>till I brought him to my mother’s house,<br>and to the chamber of her who conceived me.  
**3:5** I make you swear, O daughters of Jerusalem,<br>by the deer or by the gazelles of the field,<br>that you shall not stir nor rouse<br>love until it pleases.  
**3:6** Who is this coming up from the desert<br>like a pillar of smoke<br>perfumed with myrrh and frankincense<br>from all the merchant’s powders?  
**3:7** Look, Solomon’s bed—<br>sixty warriors round it<br>of the warriors of Israel.  
**3:8** All of them wielding the sword<br>trained in battle,<br>each with his sword on his thigh<br>out of terror in the nights.  
**3:9** A palanquin did King Solomon make<br>from Lebanon wood.<br>Its posts he made of silver,<br>its padding gold,<br>its curtains crimson,<br>its inside paved with love<br>by the daughters of Jerusalem.  
**3:10** Go out and behold, O daughters of Zion,<br>King Solomon in the diadem<br>with which his mother crowned him<br>on his wedding day,<br>on the day of his heart’s rejoicing.  

## 4장

**4:1** O you are fair, my friend,<br>O you are fair.<br>Your eyes are doves<br>through the screen of your tresses.<br>Your hair is like a herd of goats<br>that have swept down from Mount Gilead.  
**4:2** Your teeth like a flock of matched ewes<br>that have come up from the washing,<br>all of them alike,<br>and none has lost its young.  
**4:3** Like a scarlet thread, your lips,<br>and your tongue—desire.<br>Like cut pomegranate your cheekbones<br>through the screen of your tresses.  
**4:4** Like the tower of David your neck<br>built gloriously.<br>A thousand shields are hung on it,<br>all the warriors’ bucklers.  
**4:5** Your two breasts are like two fawns,<br>twins of a gazelle,<br>that graze among the lilies.  
**4:6** Till morning’s breeze blows<br>and the shadows flee,<br>I will go to the mountain of myrrh<br>and to the hill of frankincense  
**4:7** You are wholly fair, my friend,<br>there is no blemish in you.  
**4:8** With me from Lebanon, bride,<br>with me from Lebanon come.<br>Gaze from the peak of Amanah,<br>from the peak of Senir and Hermon,<br>from the lions’ dens,<br>from the leopards’ mountains.  
**4:9** You have captured my heart, my sister, bride.<br>You have captured my heart with one glance of your eyes,<br>with one bead of your necklace.  
**4:10** How beautiful your loving, my sister, bride,<br>how much better your loving than wine,<br>and the scent of your unguents than all perfumes.  
**4:11** Nectar your lips drip, bride,<br>honey and milk are under your tongue,<br>and the scent of your robes<br>like Lebanon’s scent.  
**4:12** A locked garden, my sister, bride,<br>a locked well, a sealed spring.  
**4:13** Your branches, an orchard of pomegranates<br>with luscious fruit,<br>henna and spikenard,  
**4:14** spikenard and saffron,<br>cane and cinnamon<br>with every tree of frankincense,<br>myrrh and aloes<br>with every choice perfume.  
**4:15** A garden spring,<br>a garden of fresh water<br>and streams from Lebanon.  
**4:16** —Arise, O north, and come, O south,<br>blow on my garden, let its perfumes flow,<br>Let my lover come to his garden<br>and eat its luscious fruit.  

## 5장

**5:1** —I have come to my garden, my sister, bride,<br>I have gathered my myrrh with my perfume,<br>I have eaten my honeycomb with my honey,<br>I have drunk my wine with my milk.<br>—Eat, friends, and drink,<br>be drunk with loving.  
**5:2** I was asleep but my heart was awake:<br>Hark! my lover knocks.<br>—Open for me, my sister, my friend,<br>my dove, my perfect one.<br>For my head is drenched with dew,<br>my locks with the drops of the night.  
**5:3** —I have put off my gown,<br>how can I don it?<br>I have bathed my feet,<br>how can I besmirch them?  
**5:4** My lover pulled back his hand from the latch,<br>and my heart raced within me.  
**5:5** I rose to open for my lover.<br>My hands dripped myrrh<br>and my fingers liquid myrrh,<br>over the handles of the bolt.  
**5:6** I opened for my lover,<br>but my lover had slipped off, was gone,<br>my breath left me when he spoke.<br>I sought him but did not find him.<br>I called him but he did not answer.  
**5:7** The watchmen who go round the town found me.<br>They struck me, they wounded me.<br>They pulled my veil from me,<br>the watchmen of the walls.  
**5:8** I make you swear, O daughters of Jerusalem,<br>should you find my lover, what shall you tell him?<br>that I am in a swoon of love.  
**5:9** —How is your lover more than another,<br>O fairest among women?<br>How is your lover more than another,<br>that thus you make us vow?  
**5:10** —My lover is shining white and ruddy,<br>standing out among ten thousand.  
**5:11** His head is purest gold,<br>his locks are curls<br>black as a raven.  
**5:12** His eyes are like doves<br>by streams of water<br>bathing in milk,<br>dwelling by a pool.  
**5:13** His cheeks are like beds of spices<br>sprouting aromatic scents.<br>His lips are lilies,<br>dripping liquid myrrh.  
**5:14** His arms are coils of gold<br>inset with ruby.<br>His loins are fine-wrought ivory,<br>with sapphire inlaid.  
**5:15** His thighs are ivory pillars<br>set on pedestals of gold.<br>Like Lebanon his look,<br>he is choice as the cedars.  
**5:16** His mouth is sweetest drink,<br>all of him, delight.<br>This is my lover and this is my friend,<br>O daughters of Jerusalem.  

## 6장

**6:1** Where has your lover gone,<br>O fairest among women.<br>Where has your lover turned<br>that we might seek him with you?  
**6:2** —My lover has gone down to his garden,<br>to the spice beds,<br>to graze in the garden<br>and to gather lilies.  
**6:3** I am my lover’s and my lover is mine,<br>who grazes among the lilies.  
**6:4** —You are fair, my friend, as Tirzah,<br>lovely as Jerusalem,<br>daunting as what looms on high.  
**6:5** Turn away your eyes from me,<br>for they have overwhelmed me.<br>Your hair is like a herd of goats<br>that have swept down from Mount Gilead.  
**6:6** Your teeth are like a flock of ewes<br>that have come up from the washing,<br>all of them alike<br>and none has lost its young.  
**6:7** Like cut pomegranate your cheekbones<br>through the screen of your tresses.  
**6:8** Sixty are there queens<br>and eighty concubines<br>and young women beyond number.  
**6:9** Just one is my dove, my pure one,<br>just one to her mother,<br>dazzling to her who bore her.<br>The girls saw her and called her happy,<br>queens and concubines, and they praised her.  
**6:10** Who is this espied like the dawn,<br>fair as the moon,<br>dazzling as the sun,<br>daunting as what looms on high?  
**6:11** To the walnut garden I went down<br>to see the buds of the brook,<br>to see if the vine had blossomed,<br>if the pomegranate trees were in flower.  
**6:12** I scarcely knew myself,<br>she set me in the noblest chariot.  

## 7장

**7:1** Turn back, turn back, O Shulamite,<br>turn back, that we may behold you.<br>—Why should you behold the Shulamite<br>in the dance of the double rows?  
**7:2** —How fair your feet in sandals,<br>O daughter of a nobleman.<br>The curves of your thighs like wrought rings,<br>the handiwork of a master.  
**7:3** Your navel a crescent bowl,<br>let mixed wine never lack!<br>Your belly a mound of wheat<br>hedged about with lilies.  
**7:4** Your two breasts like two fawns,<br>twins of a gazelle.  
**7:5** Your neck like an ivory tower,<br>your eyes like pools in Heshbon<br>by the gate of the town of grandees.<br>Your nose like the tower of Lebanon<br>looking out toward Damascus.  
**7:6** Your head upon you like Mount Carmel,<br>and the locks of your head are purple.<br>A king is caught in the tangle.  
**7:7** How fair you are, how sweet,<br>O Love, among delights!  
**7:8** Your stature was like a palm tree<br>and your breasts were like the clusters.  
**7:9** I thought: I will climb the palm,<br>I will grasp its stalks,<br>and let your breasts be like grape clusters,<br>and the scent of your breath like quince,  
**7:10** and your mouth like goodly wine.<br>—It flows to my lover smoothly,<br>stirring sleepers’ lips to speak.  
**7:11** I am my lover’s<br>and for me his desire.  
**7:12** Come my lover,<br>Let us go out to the field,<br>spend the night in the henna.<br>There will I give my love to you.  
**7:13** Let us rise early in the vineyards.<br>We shall see if the vine is in flower,<br>if the blossoms have opened,<br>if the pomegranate trees have budded.<br>There I will give my loving to you.  
**7:14** The mandrakes give off fragrance<br>and at our door all luscious fruit,<br>fresh picked and stored as well,<br>I have laid up for you, my love.  

## 8장

**8:1** Would that you were a brother to me,<br>suckling my mother’s breasts.<br>I would find you in the street, would kiss you,<br>and they would show no scorn for me.  
**8:2** I would lead you, I would bring you<br>to my mother’s house, she would teach me.<br>I would give you spiced wine to drink,<br>from my pomegranate wine.  
**8:3** His left hand beneath my head,<br>his right hand embracing me.  
**8:4** I make you swear, O daughters of Jerusalem,<br>that you shall not rouse nor stir love<br>until it pleases.  
**8:5** Who is this coming up from the desert<br>leaning on her lover?<br>Under the quince tree I roused you.<br>There your mother conceived you,<br>there she who bore you conceived you.  
**8:6** Set me as a seal on your heart,<br>as a seal on your arm.<br>For strong as death is love,<br>fierce as Sheol is jealousy.<br>Its sparks are fiery sparks,<br>a fearsome flame.  
**8:7** Many waters cannot<br>put out love<br>nor rivers sweep it away.<br>Should a man give<br>all the wealth of his house for love,<br>they would surely scorn him.  
**8:8** We have a little sister<br>and she has no breasts.<br>What shall we do for our sister<br>on the day she is spoken for?  
**8:9** If she is a wall,<br>we will build on her a silver turret.<br>If she is a door,<br>we will besiege her with cedar boards.  
**8:10** —I am a wall<br>and my breasts are like towers.<br>Then I was in his eyes<br>like a town that finds peace.  
**8:11** A vineyard Solomon had<br>in the Vale of Wealth.<br>He gave the vineyard to the keepers:<br>each would get from its fruit<br>a thousand silver shekels.  
**8:12** My vineyard is my own.<br>You can have the thousand, Solomon,<br>and two hundred for the keepers of its fruit.  
**8:13** You who dwell in the garden,<br>friends listen for your voice.<br>Let me hear it.  
**8:14** —Flee my lover and be like a deer<br>or like a gazelle<br>on the spice mountains.
