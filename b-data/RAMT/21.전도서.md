# 21.전도서 (RAMT)



## 1장

**1:1** The words of Qohelet son of David, king in Jerusalem.  
**1:2** Merest breath, said Qohelet, merest breath. All is mere breath.  
**1:3** What gain is there for man in all his toil that he toils under the sun.  
**1:4** A generation goes and a generation comes, but the earth endures forever.  
**1:5** The sun rises and the sun sets, and to its place it glides, there it rises.  
**1:6** It goes to the south and swings round to the north, round and round goes the wind, and on its rounds the wind returns.  
**1:7** All the rivers go to the sea, and the sea is not full.<br>To the place that the rivers go, there they return to go.  
**1:8** All things are weary. A man cannot speak. The eye is not sated with seeing, nor the ear filled with hearing.  
**1:9** That which was is that which will be, and that which was done is that which will be done, and there is nothing new under the sun.  
**1:10** There is a thing of which one would say, “See this, it is new.” It already has been in the eons that were before us.  
**1:11** There is no remembrance of the first things nor of the last things that will be. They will have no remembrance with those who will be in the latter time.  
**1:12** I, Qohelet, have been king over Israel in Jerusalem.  
**1:13** And I set my heart to inquire and seek through wisdom of all that is done under the sun—it is an evil business that God gave to the sons of man to busy themselves with.  
**1:14** I have seen all the deeds that are done under the sun, and, look, all is mere breath, and herding the wind.  
**1:15** The crooked cannot turn straight nor can the lack be made good.  
**1:16** I spoke to my heart, saying: As for me, look, I increased and added wisdom beyond all who were before me over Jerusalem, and my heart has seen much wisdom and knowledge.  
**1:17** And I set my heart to know wisdom and to know revelry and folly, for this, too, is herding the wind.  
**1:18** For in much wisdom is much worry, and he who adds wisdom adds pain.  

## 2장

**2:1** I said in my heart, “Come, now, let me pour out wine in merriment and enjoy good things.” And, look, this, too, is mere breath.  
**2:2** To mirth I said, “Wild reveling,” and to merriment, “What does it do?”  
**2:3** I sought in my heart to ply my body with wine, while my heart acted with wisdom, not grasping folly, until I might see what is good for the sons of man that they should do under the heavens in the number of the days of their lives.  
**2:4** I made me great works. I built myself houses, I planted for myself vineyards.  
**2:5** I made for myself gardens and orchards and planted in them every kind of fruit tree.  
**2:6** I made for myself pools of water from which to water a wood growing trees.  
**2:7** I bought male slaves and slavegirls, and had home-born slaves, too. Also many herds of cattle and sheep did I have, more than all who were before me in Jerusalem.  
**2:8** I gathered for myself both silver and gold, and the treasure of kings and provinces. I got myself men and women singers and the pleasures of humankind, and many a concubine.  
**2:9** And I grew great and added more than all who were before me in Jerusalem. Still my wisdom stayed with me.  
**2:10** And all that my eyes sought I did not withhold from them, nor did I deny my heart any merriment—for my heart was merry—from all my toil, and this was my share from all my toil.  
**2:11** And I turned about in all my deeds that my hands had done and in the toil that I had toiled to do, and, look, all was mere breath and herding the wind, and there was no gain under the sun.  
**2:12** And I turned to see wisdom and revelry and folly, for what is the man  
**2:13** who comes after the king, that which he has already done? And I saw that wisdom surpasses folly as light surpasses darkness.  
**2:14** The wise man has eyes in his head, and the fool goes in darkness. Yet I, too, knew that a single fate befalls them all.  
**2:15** And I said in my heart, “Like the fate of the fool, it will befall me, too, and so why have I become so wise?” And I said in my heart that this, too, is mere breath.  
**2:16** For there is no remembrance of the wise, as with the fool, forever. Since in the days to come, all will be forgotten. Yes, the wise dies like the fool!  
**2:17** And I hated life, for all that was done under the sun was evil to me, for all is mere breath and herding the wind.  
**2:18** And I hated all things got from my toil that I had toiled under the sun, that I should leave it to the man who will come after me.  
**2:19** And who knows whether he will be wise or a fool, and he will have power over all that was got from my toil for which I toiled and grew wise under the sun. This, too, is mere breath.  
**2:20** And I turned round to make my heart despair over all the toil that I had toiled under the sun.  
**2:21** For there is a man whose toil is in wisdom and knowledge and skill, and to a man who did not toil for it he will give away his share. This, too, is mere breath and a grievous evil.  
**2:22** For what does a man have from all his toil and from his heart’s care that he toils under the sun?  
**2:23** For all his days are pain, and worry is his business. At night, as well, his heart does not rest. This, too, is mere breath.  
**2:24** There is nothing better for a man than to eat and drink and sate himself with good things through his toil. This, too, have I seen, for it is from God’s hand.  
**2:25** For who will eat and who will feel, save me?  
**2:26** For to the man who seems good before Him He has given wisdom and knowledge and merriment, but to the offender He has given the business of amassing and taking in to give to him who seems good before God. This, too, is mere breath and herding the wind.  

## 3장

**3:1** Everything has a season, and a time for every matter under the heavens.  
**3:2** A time to be born and a time to die.<br>A time to plant and a time to uproot what is planted.  
**3:3** A time to kill and a time to heal.<br>A time to rip down and a time to build.  
**3:4** A time to weep and a time to laugh.<br>A time to mourn and a time to dance.  
**3:5** A time to fling stones and a time to gather stones in.<br>A time to embrace and a time to pull back from embracing.  
**3:6** A time to seek and a time to lose.<br>A time to keep and a time to fling away.  
**3:7** A time to tear and a time to sew.<br>A time to keep silent and a time to speak.  
**3:8** A time to love and a time to hate.<br>A time for war and a time for peace.  
**3:9** What gain is there for him who does in what he toils?  
**3:10** I have seen the business that God has given to the sons of man with which to busy themselves.  
**3:11** Everything He has done aptly in its time. Eternity, too, He has put in their heart, without man’s grasping at all what it is God has done from beginning to end.  
**3:12** I know that there is nothing good in it but to be merry and to partake of good things in his life.  
**3:13** And also every man who eats and drinks and enjoys good things in all his toil—this is a gift from God.  
**3:14** I know that whatever God does will be forever. One cannot add to it and one cannot take away from it. And God has acted also that they should fear Him.  
**3:15** That which was already has been, and what is to be already has been, and God seeks out the pursued.  
**3:16** And further did I see under the sun: the place of judgment—wickedness there, and the place of justice—the wicked there.  
**3:17** I said in my heart: The just man and the wicked God will judge, for there is a time for every matter, and every deed He assesses.  
**3:18** I said in my heart in regard to the sons of man, God has sifted them out to show them they are but beasts.  
**3:19** For the fate of the sons of man and the fate of the beast is a single fate. As one dies so dies the other, and all have a single spirit, and man’s advantage over the beast is naught, for everything is mere breath.  
**3:20** Everything goes to a single place. Everything was from the dust, and everything goes back to the dust.  
**3:21** Who knows whether man’s spirit goes upward and the beast’s spirit goes down to the earth?  
**3:22** And I saw that nothing is better than that man should rejoice in his works, for that is his share, for who can bring him to see what will be after him?  

## 4장

**4:1** And I went back and saw all the oppression that is done under the sun: the tears of the oppressed who have none to console them, and from the hand of their violent oppressors there is none to console them.  
**4:2** And I praised the dead, who have already died, more than the living, who are still alive.  
**4:3** And better than both is he who has not yet been, who has not seen the evil deeds that are done under the sun.  
**4:4** And I saw all the toil and all the skilled deeds—that it is a man’s envy of his fellow. This, too, is mere breath and herding the wind.  
**4:5** The fool hugs his hands and eats his own flesh.  
**4:6** Better a palmful of ease than two handfuls of toil and herding the wind.  
**4:7** And I went back and saw mere breath under the sun.  
**4:8** There is one without a second, neither son nor brother he has. And there is no end to his toil, nor is his eye sated with wealth: “And for whom do I toil and deprive myself of good things?” This, too, is mere breath and an evil business.  
**4:9** Two are better than one, for they get good reward for their toil.  
**4:10** For if one should fall, the other will lift up his friend. But if the one alone should fall, there is no other to lift him up.  
**4:11** If two lie together, they are warm, but as for one, how will he be warm?  
**4:12** And if one should attack him, the two will stand against him. And the triple cord will not quickly be snapped.  
**4:13** Better a poor but wise boy than an old and foolish king who no longer knows how to be wary.  
**4:14** For from the prison-house he came out to be king, for in his kingship, too, the impoverished man was born.  
**4:15** I have seen all the living who go about under the sun alongside the next boy who will stand in his place.  
**4:16** There is no end to all the people, to all before whom he stood, nor would the ones who came later be happy with him. For this, too, is mere breath and herding the wind.  
**4:17** Watch your step when you go to the house of God, for understanding is more favored than the offering of sacrifice by fools, for they do not know even how to do evil.  

## 5장

**5:1** Be not rash with your mouth, and let your heart not hurry to utter a word before God. For God is in the heavens and you are on earth. Therefore let your words be few.  
**5:2** For a dream comes with much business and the fool’s voice with much talk.  
**5:3** When you make a vow to God, do not delay to fulfill it, for there is no pleasure in fools. What you vow, fulfill.  
**5:4** Better that you do not vow than that you vow and do not fulfill.  
**5:5** Do not let your mouth make your body offend, nor say before God that it was a mistake. Why should God rage over your voice and ruin your handiwork?  
**5:6** For in many dreams are mere breath and much talk. Instead, fear God.  
**5:7** If you see the oppression of the poor and the perversion of justice and right in the province, be not amazed at the matter, for he who is high has a higher one watching him, and still higher ones over them.  
**5:8** And the gain of the land is in everything—a king is subject to the field.  
**5:9** He who loves money will not be sated with money, and he who loves wealth will have no crop. This, too, is mere breath.  
**5:10** As bounty multiplies, those who consume it multiply, and what is the benefit for its owner except what his eyes see?  
**5:11** Sweet is the worker’s sleep, whether he eats little or much. And the rich man’s surfeit does not let him sleep.  
**5:12** There is a blighting evil I have seen under the sun: wealth kept for its owner for his harm.  
**5:13** And that wealth is lost in a bad business, and he begets a son with nothing in his hand.  
**5:14** As he came out from his mother’s womb, naked will he return to go as he came, and nothing will he bear off from his toil that he brings in his hand.  
**5:15** And this, too, is a blighting evil: as he came, so he goes, and what gain does he have that he should toil for the wind?  
**5:16** All his days, too, he goes in darkness, with much worry and illness and rage.  
**5:17** Look, I have seen what is good: it is fit to eat and to drink and enjoy good things in all his toil that he toils under the sun in the number of the days of his life that God gave him, for that is his share.  
**5:18** Also the man whom God has given wealth and possessions and whom He has empowered to enjoy it and bear off his share and delight in his toil—this is God’s gift.  
**5:19** For not much will he recall the days of his life, for God makes him busy with his heart’s delight.  

## 6장

**6:1** There is an evil that I have seen under the sun, and it is heavy on humankind:  
**6:2** a man whom God gives wealth and possessions and honor, and he lacks nothing for himself of all he desires, and God does not grant him to enjoy it, for a stranger will enjoy it. This is mere breath and an evil sickness.  
**6:3** If a man begot a hundred children and lived many years, and many were the days of his life, he might yet not be sated with good things, and even a burial he might not have. I said: better than he is the stillborn.  
**6:4** For in mere breath did it come, and into darkness it goes, and in darkness its name is covered.  
**6:5** The very sun it did not see or know—more ease for it than for him.  
**6:6** And were he to live a thousand years twice over, yet good things he did not enjoy—does not everything go to a single place?  
**6:7** All a man’s toil is for his own mouth, yet his appetite will not be filled.  
**6:8** For what advantage has the wise over the fool? What good is it for the poor man to know how to get round among the living?  
**6:9** Better what the eyes see than desire going round. This, too, is mere breath and herding the wind.  
**6:10** What was has already been called by name and is known, as he is man and cannot deal with one more powerful than he.  
**6:11** For there are many words that increase mere breath; what is the advantage for man?  
**6:12** For who knows what is good for man in life, in his days of mere breath, for he spends them like a shadow? Who can tell man what will be after him under the sun?  

## 7장

**7:1** Better a good name than good oil, and the day of death than the day one is born.  
**7:2** Better to go to the house of mourning than to go to the house of carousing. For that is the end of all men—let the living take it to heart.  
**7:3** Better worry than merriment, for by a scowl the heart is gladdened.  
**7:4** The wise men’s heart is in the house of mourning, and the heart of fools in the house of mirth.  
**7:5** Better to hear the rebuke of the wise than a man hearing the song of fools.  
**7:6** For like the sound of thorns beneath the pot, so is the laughter of the fool. And this, too, is mere breath.  
**7:7** For oppression’s profit drives the wise man wild, and a bribe destroys the mind.  
**7:8** Better a thing’s end than its beginning; better patience than haughtiness.  
**7:9** Do not be rash in your mood to be angry, for anger rests in the lap of fools.  
**7:10** Say not: What has happened, that the days gone by were better than these? For you asked not about this in wisdom.  
**7:11** Better wisdom with a legacy, it is an advan tage to those who see the sun.  
**7:12** For in wisdom’s shade is money’s shade, and the gain of wisdom’s knowledge keeps its possessors alive.  
**7:13** See God’s work, for who can straighten what He has made crooked?  
**7:14** On the day of good luck, enjoy the good, and on the day of evil, see: one against the other God has set, so that man find nothing after him.  
**7:15** Everything have I seen in my days of mere breath: the righteous perishing in his righteousness, and the wicked living a long life in his evil.  
**7:16** Do not be overrighteous and do not be overwise. Why should you be dumbfounded?  
**7:17** Don’t be overwicked and don’t be a fool. Why should you die before your time?  
**7:18** It is good that you seize this, and from the other as well do not take back your hand, for he who fears God will get out of them all.  
**7:19** Wisdom is stronger for the wise than ten rulers who are in the town.  
**7:20** For there is no man righteous on earth who does good and will not offend.  
**7:21** To all the words, too, that they speak, do not pay heed, that you hear not your servant reviling you.  
**7:22** For many times, too, your heart has known, that you, too, have reviled others.  
**7:23** All this I tried out through wisdom. I said, Let me grow wise, and it was far away from me.  
**7:24** Far away that which was and deep, deep—who can find it?  
**7:25** I turned round in my heart to know and to inquire and seek wisdom and reckoning and to know foolish wickedness and mad folly.  
**7:26** And I find woman more bitter than death. For she is all snares, and nets her heart, and fetters her arms. He who is good before God will escape her, and an offender will be trapped by her.  
**7:27** See, this have I found, said Qohelet: one by one to find a reckoning.  
**7:28** Further I sought and did not find—one man in a thousand I found, and a woman among all these I did not find.  
**7:29** Except, see this I found: that God made men upright, but they sought many reckonings.  

## 8장

**8:1** Who is like the wise man, and who knows a word’s solution? A man’s wisdom lights up his face, and the impudence of his face transforms it.  
**8:2** Keep a king’s utterance as though it concerned a vow to God.  
**8:3** Do not be hasty. From his presence you should go. Do not persist in a bad business.  
**8:4** For whatever he desires, he may do, since a king’s word is power, and who can say to him, “What are you doing?”  
**8:5** He who keeps a command will know no evil thing, and the time of judgment a wise heart knows.  
**8:6** For every happening has a time of judgment, for man’s evil is heavy upon him.  
**8:7** For one knows not what will be, for what will be—who can tell of it?  
**8:8** No man has power over the wind, to shut in the wind, and there is no power over the day of death, and there is no sending away from war, and wickedness will not make those who do it escape.  
**8:9** All this have I seen and set my heart to all that is done under the sun, a time when man holds power over man to his harm.  
**8:10** And so have I seen the wicked brought to the grave, and from a holy place they went forth, while those who did right were forgotten in the town. This, too, is mere breath.  
**8:11** The sentence for an evil act is not carried out swiftly. Therefore the heart of the sons of men brims over within them to do evil.  
**8:12** For the offender does evil a hundredfold and lives a long life, though I know, too, that it will be well with those who fear God, who fear His presence.  
**8:13** And it will not be well with the wicked, and like a shadow, he will not live long, as he does not fear God’s presence.  
**8:14** There is a thing of mere breath that is done on the earth—there are righteous to whom it befalls as though they did wickedly, and there are wicked to whom it befalls as though they did righteously. I said that this, too, is mere breath.  
**8:15** And I praised merriment, for there is nothing better for man under the sun than to eat and to drink and to make merry, and that will attend him in all his toil in the days of his life that God gives him under the sun.  
**8:16** When I set my heart to know wisdom and to see the business that is done on earth, day and night my eyes saw no sleep.  
**8:17** And I have seen every deed of God, that man cannot grasp the deed which is done under the sun, inasmuch as man toils to seek and cannot grasp it. And even if the wise means to know, he will not be able to grasp it.  

## 9장

**9:1** For on all this I set my heart to sort out all this—that the righteous and the wise and their acts are in God’s hand. Neither hatred nor love does man know. All before them is mere breath.  
**9:2** As all have a single fate, the righteous and the wicked, the good and the bad, and the clean and the unclean, and he who offers sacrifice and he who does not sacrifice, the good and the offender, he who vows and he who fears the vow.  
**9:3** This is the evil in all that is done under the sun, for all have a single fate, and also the heart of the sons of man is full of evil, and mad revelry in their heart while they live, and afterward—off to the dead.  
**9:4** For he who is joined to the living knows one sure thing: that a live dog is better than a dead lion.  
**9:5** For the living know that they will die, and the dead know nothing, and they no longer have recompense, for their memory is forgotten.  
**9:6** Their love and their hatred as well, their jealousy, too, are already lost, and they no longer have any share forever in all that is done under the sun.  
**9:7** Go, eat your bread with rejoicing and drink your wine with a merry heart, for God has already been pleased by your deeds.  
**9:8** At every season let your garments be white, and let oil on your head not be lacking.  
**9:9** Enjoy life with a woman whom you love all your days of mere breath that have been given to you under the sun, all your days of mere breath, for that is your share in life and in your toil that you toil under the sun.  
**9:10** All that your hand manages to do with your strength, do, for there is no doing nor reckoning nor knowledge nor wisdom in Sheol where you are going.  
**9:11** I returned to see under the sun that not to the swift is the race and not to the mighty, the battle, nor to the wise, bread, nor to the discerning, wealth, nor to those who know, favor, for a time of mishap will befall them all.  
**9:12** Nor does man know his time, like fish caught in an evil net and like birds held in a trap, like them the sons of man are ensnared by an evil time when it suddenly falls upon them.  
**9:13** Wisdom, too, have I seen under the sun, and it is great in my eyes.  
**9:14** There was a little town, and few people within it, and a great king came against it and went round it and built against it great siege-works.  
**9:15** And there was found within it a poor wise man, and that person saved the town through his wisdom, but no one recalled that poor man.  
**9:16** I said: Better wisdom than might, but the poor man’s wisdom is scorned and his words are unheard.  
**9:17** The words of the wise gently said are heard more than the shout of the ruler among fools.  
**9:18** Better wisdom than weapons, yet a single offender destroys much good.  

## 10장

**10:1** A dead fly makes the perfumer’s oil chalice stink. Heavier than weighty wisdom is a bit of folly.  
**10:2** A wise man’s mind is at his right, and the fool’s mind at his left.  
**10:3** Even when the fool walks on the road, his mind is absent, and it says to all, he is a fool.  
**10:4** If the ruler’s mood goes against you, do not leave your place, for calmness puts great offenses to rest.  
**10:5** There is an evil I have seen under the sun, a true error that comes forth from the person in power.  
**10:6** Folly is set on great heights, and the rich dwell down below.  
**10:7** I have seen slaves on horses and noblemen walking like slaves on the ground.  
**10:8** He who digs a pit will fall in it, and he who breaches a wall, a snake will bite him.  
**10:9** He who moves stones will be hurt by them, and he who splits wood is endangered by it.  
**10:10** If the iron is dull and he has not honed its edge, he will exert great effort. And the advantage of skill is wisdom.  
**10:11** If a snake bites with no snake-charm, there is no advantage for the expert of incantations.  
**10:12** The words of a wise man’s mouth bring favor, and the lips of a fool bring him ruin.  
**10:13** The first of the words of his mouth are folly, and the last of them evil revelry.  
**10:14** And the fool speaks many words. Man knows not what will be, and what will be after him, who can tell him?  
**10:15** The toil of a fool wears him out, so that he knows not how to go to town.  
**10:16** Woe to you, land, whose king is a lackey, and your princes dine in the morning.  
**10:17** Happy are you, land, whose king is a noble, and your princes dine in fit time, in manliness, not in drunkenness.  
**10:18** Through sloth the roof-beam sags, and through slack hands the house leaks.  
**10:19** For food is set out for merriment and wine that gladdens the living. And money keeps everyone busy.  
**10:20** Even on your couch revile not a king, and in your sleeping chambers revile not a rich man, for the fowl of the heavens will carry the sound and the wingèd thing will tell the word.  

## 11장

**11:1** Send out your bread upon the waters, for in the long course of time you will find it.  
**11:2** Give a share to seven and even to eight, for you know not what evil will be on earth.  
**11:3** If the clouds fill, they will empty out rain on the earth. And if a tree falls in the south or the north, the place where the tree falls, there it will be.  
**11:4** He who watches the wind will not plant, and who gazes on clouds will not harvest.  
**11:5** As you know not the path of the life-breath into the limbs within the full womb, so you know not the deeds of God, Who does everything.  
**11:6** In the morning plant your seed and at evening let your hand not rest, for you know not which will be fit, this one or that, or whether both be equally good.  
**11:7** And light is sweet, and it is good for the eyes to see the sun.  
**11:8** Should a man live many years, let him rejoice in all of them, and let him recall the days of darkness, for they will be many. Whatever comes is mere breath.  
**11:9** Rejoice, young man, in your youth, and let your heart be merry in the days of your prime, and go about in the ways of your heart and what your eyes see. But know that for all these God will bring you to judgment.  
**11:10** And remove worry from your heart, and take evil away from your flesh, for youth and the time of vigor are mere breath.  

## 12장

**12:1** And recall your Creator in the days of your prime, until the days of evil come, and the years arrive, when you will say, “I have no delight in them.”  
**12:2** Until the sun goes dark, and the light and the moon and the stars, and the clouds come back after the rain.  
**12:3** On the day that the guards of the house will quake<br>and the stalwart men be twisted,<br>and the maids who grind grow idle, for they are now few,<br>and those who look from the casements go dark.  
**12:4** And the double doors close in the market<br>as the sound of the mill sinks down,<br>and the sound of the bird arises,<br>and all the songstresses are bowed.  
**12:5** Of the very height they are afraid,<br>and terror is in the road.<br>And the almond blossoms,<br>and the locust tree is laden,<br>and the caper fruit falls apart.<br>For man is going to his everlasting house,<br>and the mourners turn round in the market.  
**12:6** Until the silver cord is snapped,<br>and the golden bowl is smashed,<br>and the pitcher is broken against the well,<br>and the jug smashed at the pit.  
**12:7** And dust returns to the earth as it was,<br>and the life-breath returns to God Who gave it.  
**12:8** Merest breath, said Qohelet. All is mere breath.  
**12:9** And more than being wise, Qohelet further taught knowledge to the people and weighed and searched out and framed many maxims.  
**12:10** Qohelet sought to find apt words and wrote honestly words of truth.  
**12:11** The words of the wise are like goads and like nails driven in—from the composers of collections, given from a certain shepherd.  
**12:12** And more than these, my son, beware: of making many books there is no end, and much chatter is a weariness of the flesh.  
**12:13** The last word, all being heard: fear God and keep His commands, for that is all humankind.  
**12:14** Since every deed will God bring to judgment, for every hidden act, be it good or evil.
