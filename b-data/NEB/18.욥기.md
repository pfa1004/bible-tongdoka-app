# 18.욥기 (NEB)



## 1장

**1:1**  THERE LIVED IN THE LAND OF UZ a man of blameless and upright life named Job, who feared God and set his face against wrongdoing.  
**1:2** He had seven sons and three daughters;  
**1:3** and he owned seven thousand sheep and three thousand camels, five hundred yoke of oxen and five hundred asses, with a large number of slaves. Thus Job was the greatest man in all the East.   
**1:4** Now his sons used to foregather and give, each in turn, a feast in his own house; and they used to send and invite their three sisters to eat and drink with them.  
**1:5** Then, when a round of feasts was finished, Job sent for his children and sanctified them, rising early in the morning and sacrificing a whole-offering for each of them; for he thought that they might somehow have sinned against God and committed blasphemy in their hearts. This he always did.   
**1:6** The day came when the members of the court of heaven took their places in the presence of the LORD, and Satan was there among them.  
**1:7** The LORD asked him where he had been. 'Ranging over the earth', he said, 'from end to end.'  
**1:8** Then the LORD asked Satan, 'Have you considered my servant Job? You will find no one like him on earth, a man of blameless and upright life, who fears God and sets his face against wrongdoing.'  
**1:9** Satan answered the LORD, 'Has not Job good reason to be God-fearing?  
**1:10** Have you not hedged him round on every side with your protection, him and his family and all his possessions? Whatever he does you have blessed, and his herds have increased beyond measure.  
**1:11** But stretch out your hand and touch all that he has, and then he will curse you to your face.'  
**1:12** Then the LORD said to Satan, 'So be it. All that he has is in your hands; only Job himself you must not touch.' And Satan left the LORD's presence.  
**1:13**  When the day came that Job's sons and daughters were eating and drinking in the eldest brother's house,  
**1:14** a messenger came running to Job and said, 'The oxen were ploughing and the asses were grazing near them,  
**1:15** when the Sabaeans swooped down and carried them off, after putting the herdsmen to the sword; and I am the only one to escape and tell the tale.'  
**1:16** While he was still speaking, another messenger arrived and said, 'God's fire flashed from heaven. It struck the sheep and the shepherds and burnt them up; and I am the only one to escape and tell the tale.'  
**1:17** While he was still speaking, another arrived and said, 'The Chaldaeans, three bands of them, have made a raid on the camels and carried them off', after putting the drivers to the sword; and I am the only one to escape and tell the tale.'  
**1:18** While this man was speaking, yet another arrived and said, 'Your sons and daughters were eating and drinking in the eldest brother's house,  
**1:19** when suddenly a whirlwind swept across from the desert and struck the four corners of the house, and it fell on the young people and killed them; and I am the only one to escape and tell the tale.'  
**1:20** At this Job stood up and rent his cloak; then he shaved his head and fell prostrate on the ground,  
**1:21** saying: Naked I came from the womb, naked I shall return whence I came. The LORD gives and the LORD takes away; blessed be the name of the LORD.  
**1:22**  Throughout all this Job did not sin; he did not charge God with unreason.  

## 2장

**2:1**  Once again the day came when the members of the court of heaven took their places in the presence of the LORD, and Satan was there among them.  
**2:2** The LORD asked him where he had been. 'Ranging over the earth', he said, 'from end to end.'  
**2:3** Then the LORD asked Satan, 'Have you considered my servant Job? You will find no one like him on earth, a man of blameless and upright life, who fears God and sets his face against wrongdoing. You incited me to ruin him without a cause, but his integrity is still unshaken.'  
**2:4** Satan answered the LORD, 'Skin for skin! There is nothing the man will grudge to save himself.  
**2:5** But stretch out your hand and touch his bone and his flesh, and see if he will not curse you to your face.'   
**2:6** Then the LORD said to Satan, 'So be it. He is in your hands; but spare his life.'  
**2:7** And Satan left the LORD's presence, and he smote Job with running sores from head to foot,  
**2:8** so that he took a piece of a broken pot to scratch himself as he sat among the ashes.  
**2:9** Then his wife said to him, 'Are you still unshaken in your integrity? Curse God and die!'  
**2:10** But he answered, 'You talk as any wicked fool of a woman might talk. If we accept good from God, shall we not accept evil?' Throughout all this, Job did not utter one sinful word.  
**2:11**  When Job's three friends, Eliphaz of Teman, Bildad of Shuah, and Zophar of Naamah, heard of all these calamities which had overtaken him, they left their homes and arranged to come and condole with him and comfort him.  
**2:12** But when they first saw him from a distance, they did not recognize him; and they wept aloud, rent their cloaks and tossed dust into the air over their heads.  
**2:13** For seven days and seven nights they sat beside him on the ground, and none of them said a word to him; for they saw that his suffering was very great.  

## 3장

**3:1**  (1-2) After this Job broke silence and cursed the day of his birth:   
**3:2**   
**3:3**  Perish the day when I was born and the night which said, 'A man is conceived'!  
**3:4** May that day turn to darkness; may God above not look for it, nor light of dawn shine on it.  
**3:5** May blackness sully it, and murk and gloom, cloud smother that day, swift darkness eclipse its sun.  
**3:6** Blind darkness swallow up that night; count it not among the days of the year, reckon it not in the cycle of the months.  
**3:7** That night, may it be barren for ever, no cry of joy be heard in it.  
**3:8** Cursed be it by those whose magic binds even the monster of the deep, who are ready to tame Leviathan himself with spells.  
**3:9** May no star shine out in its twilight; may it wait for a dawn that never comes, nor ever see the eyelids of the morning,  
**3:10** because it did not shut the doors of the womb that bore me and keep trouble away from my sight.  
**3:11** Why was I not still-born, why did I not die when I came out of the womb?  
**3:12** Why was I ever laid on my mother's knees or put to suck at her breasts? (16) Why was I not hidden like an untimely birth, like an infant that has not lived to see the light?  
**3:13** For then I should be lying in the quiet grave, asleep in death, at rest,  
**3:14** with kings and their ministers who built themselves palaces,  
**3:15** with princes rich in gold who filled their houses with silver.  
**3:16**   
**3:17** There the wicked man chafes no more, there the tired labourer rests;  
**3:18** the captive too finds peace there and hears no taskmaster's voice;  
**3:19** high and low are there, even the slave, free from his master.  
**3:20** Why should the sufferer be born to see the light? Why is life given to men who find it so bitter?  
**3:21** They wait for death but it does not come, they seek it more eagerly than hidden treasure.  
**3:22** They are glad when they reach the tomb, and when they come to the grave they exult.  
**3:23** Why should a man be born to wander blindly, hedged in by God on every side?  
**3:24** My sighing is all my food, and groans pour from me in a torrent.  
**3:25** Every terror that haunted me has caught up with me, and all that I feared has come upon me.  
**3:26** There is no peace of mind nor quiet for me; I chafe in torment and have no rest.  

## 4장

**4:1**  Then Eliphaz the Temanite began:   
**4:2**  If one ventures to speak with you, will you lose patience? For who could hold his tongue any longer?  
**4:3** Think how once you encouraged those who faltered, how you braced feeble arms,  
**4:4** how a word from you upheld the stumblers and put strength into weak knees.  
**4:5** But now that adversity comes upon you, you lose patience; it touches you, and you are unmanned.  
**4:6** Is your religion no comfort to you? Does your blameless life give you no hope?  
**4:7** For consider, what innocent man has ever perished? Where have you seen the upright destroyed?  
**4:8** This I know, that those who plough mischief and sow trouble reap as they have sown;  
**4:9** they perish at the blast of God and are shrivelled by the breath of his nostrils.  
**4:10** The roar of the lion, the whimpering of his cubs, fall silent; the teeth of the young lions are broken;  
**4:11** the lion perishes for lack of prey and the whelps of the lioness are abandoned.  
**4:12** A word stole into my ears, and they caught the whisper of it;  
**4:13** in the anxious visions of the night, when a man sinks into deepest sleep,  
**4:14** terror seized me and shuddering; the trembling of my body frightened me.  
**4:15** A wind brushed my face and made the hairs bristle on my flesh;  
**4:16** and a figure stood there whose shape I could not discern, an apparition loomed before me, and I heard the sound of a low voice:  
**4:17** 'Can mortal man be more righteous than God, or the creature purer than his Maker?  
**4:18** If God mistrusts his own servants and finds his messengers at fault,  
**4:19** how much more those that dwell in houses whose walls are clay, whose foundations are dust, which can be crushed like a bird's nest  
**4:20** or torn down between dawn and dark, how much more shall such men perish outright and unheeded,  
**4:21**  die, without ever finding wisdom?'  

## 5장

**5:1**  Call if you will; is there any to answer you? To which of the holy ones will you turn?  
**5:2** The fool is destroyed by his own angry passions, and the end of childish resentment is death.  
**5:3** I have seen it for myself: a fool uprooted, his home in sudden ruin about him ,  
**5:4** his children past help, browbeaten in court with none to save them. Their rich possessions are snatched from them;  
**5:5** what they have harvested others hungrily devour; the stronger man seizes it from the panniers, panting, thirsting for their wealth.  
**5:6** Mischief does not grow out of the soil nor trouble spring from the earth;  
**5:7** man is born to trouble, as surely as birds fly upwards.  
**5:8** For my part, I would make my petition to God and lay my cause before him,  
**5:9** who does great and unsearchable things, marvels without number.  
**5:10** He gives rain to the earth. and sends water on the fields;  
**5:11** he raises the lowly to the heights, the mourners are uplifted by victory;  
**5:12** he frustrates the plots of the crafty, and they win no success,  
**5:13** he traps the cunning in their craftiness, and the schemers' plans are thrown into confusion.  
**5:14** In the daylight they run into darkness, and grope at midday as though it were night.  
**5:15** He saves the destitute from their greed, and the needy from the grip of the strong;  
**5:16** so the poor hope again, and the unjust are sickened.  
**5:17** Happy the man whom God rebukes! therefore do not reject the discipline of the Almighty.  
**5:18** For, though he wounds, he will bind up; the hands that smite will heal.  
**5:19** You may meet disaster six times, and he will save you; seven times, and no harm shall touch you.  
**5:20** In time of famine he will save you from death, in battle from the sword.  
**5:21** You will be shielded from the lash of slander , and when violence comes you need not fear.  
**5:22** You will laugh at violence and starvation and have no need to fear wild beasts;  
**5:23** for you have a covenant with the stones to spare your fields, and the weeds have been constrained to leave you at peace.  
**5:24** You will know that all is well with your household, you will look round your home and find nothing amiss;  
**5:25** you will know, too, that your descendants will be many and your offspring like grass, thick upon the earth.  
**5:26** You will come in sturdy old age to the grave as sheaves come in due season to the threshing-floor.  
**5:27** We have inquired into all this, and so it is; this we have heard, and you may know it for the truth.  

## 6장

**6:1**  Then Job answered:  
**6:2**  O that the grounds for my resentment might be weighed, and my misfortunes set with them on the scales!  
**6:3** For they would outweigh the sands of the sea: what wonder if my words are wild?   
**6:4** The arrows of the Almighty find their mark in me, and their poison soaks into my spirit; God's onslaughts wear me away.  
**6:5** Does the wild ass bray when he has grass or the ox low when he has fodder?  
**6:6** Can a man eat tasteless food unseasoned with salt, or find any flavour in the juice of mallows?  
**6:7** Food that should nourish me sticks in my throat, and my bowels rumble with an echoing sound.  
**6:8** O that I might have my request, that God would grant what I hope for:  
**6:9** that he would be pleased to crush me, to snatch me away with his hand and cut me off!  
**6:10** For that would bring me relief, and in the face of unsparing anguish I would leap for joy .  
**6:11** Have I the strength to wait? What end have I to expect, that I should be patient?  
**6:12** Is my strength the strength of stone, or is my flesh bronze?  
**6:13** Oh how shall I find help within myself? The power to aid myself is put out of my reach.  
**6:14** Devotion is due from his friends to one who despairs and loses faith in the Almighty;  
**6:15** but my brothers have been treacherous as a mountain stream, like the channels of streams that run dry,  
**6:16** which turn dark with ice or are hidden with piled-up snow;  
**6:17** or they vanish the moment they are in spate, dwindle in the heat and are gone.  
**6:18** Then the caravans, winding hither and thither, go up into the wilderness and perish ;  
**6:19** the caravans of Tema look for their waters, travelling merchants of Sheba hope for them;  
**6:20** but they are disappointed, for all their confidence, they reach them only to be balked.  
**6:21** So treacherous have you now been to me : you felt dismay and were afraid.  
**6:22** Did I ever say, 'Give me this or that; open your purses to save my life;  
**6:23** rescue me from my enemy; ransom me out of the hands of ruthless men'?  
**6:24** Tell me plainly, and I will listen in silence; show me where I have erred.  
**6:25** How harsh are the words of the upright man! What do the arguments of wise men prove?  
**6:26** Do you mean to argue about words or to sift the utterance of a man past hope?  
**6:27** Would you assail an orphan ? Would you hurl yourselves on a friend?  
**6:28** So now, I beg you, turn and look at me: am I likely to lie to your faces?  
**6:29** Think again, let me have no more injustice; think again, for my integrity is in question.  
**6:30** Do I ever give voice to injustice? Does my sense not warn me when my words are wild?  

## 7장

**7:1**  Has not man hard service on earth, and are not his days like those of a hired labourer,  
**7:2** like those of a slave longing for the shade or a servant kept waiting for his wages?  
**7:3** So months of futility are my portion troubled nights are my lot.  
**7:4** When I lie down, I think, 'When will it be day that I may rise?' When the evening grows long and I lie down, I do nothing but toss till morning twilight.  
**7:5** My body is infested with worms, and scabs cover my skin .  
**7:6** My days are swifter than a shuttle and come to an end as the thread runs out .  
**7:7** Remember, my life is but a breath of wind; I shall never again see good days.  
**7:8** Thou wilt behold me no more with a seeing eye; under thy very eyes I shall disappear.  
**7:9** As clouds break up and disperse, so he that goes down to Sheol never comes back;  
**7:10** he never returns home again, and his place will know him no more .  
**7:11** But I will not hold my peace; I will speak out in the distress of my mind and complain in the bitterness of my soul.  
**7:12** Am I the monster of the deep, am I the sea-serpent, that thou settest a watch over me?  
**7:13** When I think that my bed will comfort me, that sleep will relieve my complaining,  
**7:14** thou dost terrify me with dreams and affright me with visions.  
**7:15** I would rather be choked outright; I would prefer death to all my sufferings.  
**7:16** I am in despair, I would not go on living; leave me alone, for my life is but a vapour.  
**7:17** What is man that thou makest much of him and turnest thy thoughts towards him,  
**7:18** only to punish him morning by morning or to test him every hour of the day?  
**7:19** Wilt thou not look away from me for an instant? Wilt thou not let me be while I swallow my spittle?  
**7:20** If I have sinned, how do I injure thee, thou watcher of the hearts of men? Why hast thou made me thy butt, and why have I become thy target?  
**7:21** Why dost thou not pardon my offence and take away my guilt? But now I shall lie down in the grave; seek me, and I shall not be.  

## 8장

**8:1**  Then Bildad the Shuhite began:  
**8:2**  How long will you say such things, the long-winded ramblings of an old man?  
**8:3** Does God pervert judgement? Does the Almighty pervert justice?  
**8:4** Your sons sinned against him, so he left them to be victims of their own iniquity.  
**8:5** If only you will seek God betimes and plead for the favour of the Almighty,  
**8:6** if you are innocent and upright, then indeed will he watch over you and see your just intent fulfilled.  
**8:7** Then, though your beginnings were humble, your end will be great.  
**8:8** Inquire now of older generations and consider the experience of their fathers;  
**8:9** for we ourselves are of yesterday and are transient; our days on earth are a shadow.  
**8:10** Will not they speak to you and teach you and pour out the wisdom of their hearts?  
**8:11** Can rushes grow where there is no marsh? Can reeds flourish without water?  
**8:12** While they are still in flower and not ready to cut , they wither earlier than any green plant.  
**8:13** Such is the fate of all who forget God; the godless man's life-thread breaks off;  
**8:14** his confidence is gossamer, and the ground of his trust a spider's web.  
**8:15** He leans against his house but it does not stand; he clutches at it but it does not hold firm.  
**8:16** His is the lush growth of a plant in the sun, pushing out shoots over the garden;  
**8:17** but its roots become entangled in a stony patch and run against a bed of rock.  
**8:18** Then someone uproots it from its place, which disowns it and says, 'I have never known you.'  
**8:19** That is how its life withers away, and other plants spring up from the earth.  
**8:20** Be sure, God will not spurn the blameless man, nor will he grasp the hand of the wrongdoer.  
**8:21** He will yet fill your mouth with laughter, and shouts of joy will be on your lips;  
**8:22** your enemies shall be wrapped in confusion, and the tents of the wicked shall vanish away.  

## 9장

**9:1**  Then Job answered:   
**9:2**  Indeed this I know for the truth, that no man can win his case against God.  
**9:3** If a man chooses to argue with him, God will not answer one question in a thousand .  
**9:4** He is wise, he is powerful; what man has stubbornly resisted him and survived?  
**9:5** It is God who moves mountains, giving them no rest, turning them over in his wrath;  
**9:6** who makes the earth start from its place so that its pillars are convulsed;  
**9:7** who commands the sun's orb not to rise and shuts up the stars under his seal;  
**9:8** who by himself spread out the heavens and trod on the sea-monster's back ;  
**9:9** who made Aldebaran and Orion, the Pleiades and the circle of the southern stars;  
**9:10** who does great and unsearchable things, marvels without number.  
**9:11** He passes by me, and I do not see him; he moves on his way undiscerned by me;  
**9:12** if he hurries on, who can bring him back? Who will ask him what he does?  
**9:13** God does not turn back his wrath; the partisans of Rahab lie prostrate at his feet.  
**9:14** How much less can I answer him or find words to dispute with him?  
**9:15** Though I am right, I get no answer, though I plead with my accuser for mercy.  
**9:16** If I summoned him to court and he responded, I do not believe that he would listen to my plea—  
**9:17** for he bears hard upon me for a tri?e and rains blows on me without cause;  
**9:18** he leaves me no respite to recover my breath but fills me with bitter thoughts.  
**9:19** If the appeal is to force, see how strong he is; if to justice, who can compel him to give me a hearing?  
**9:20** Though I am right, he condemns me out of my own mouth; though I am blameless, he twists my words.  
**9:21** Blameless, I say; of myself I reek nothing, I hold my life cheap.  
**9:22** But it is all one; therefore I say, 'He destroys blameless and wicked alike.'  
**9:23** When a sudden flood brings death, he mocks the plight of the innocent.  
**9:24** The land is given over to the power of the wicked, and the eyes of its judges are blindfold   
**9:25** My days have been swifter than a runner, they have slipped away and seen no prosperity;  
**9:26** they have raced by like reed-built skiffs, swift as vultures swooping on carrion.  
**9:27** If I think, 'I will forget my griefs, I will show a cheerful face and smile',  
**9:28** I tremble in every nerve ; I know that thou wilt not hold me innocent.  
**9:29** If I am to be accounted guilty, why do I labour in vain?  
**9:30** Though I wash myself with soap or cleanse my hands with lye,  
**9:31** thou wilt thrust me into the mud and my clothes will make me loathsome.  
**9:32** He is not a man as I am, that I can answer him or that we can confront one another in court.  
**9:33** If only there were one to arbitrate between us and impose his authority on us both,  
**9:34** so that God might take his rod from my back, and terror of him might not come on me suddenly.  
**9:35** I would then speak without fear of him; for I know I am not what I am thought to be.  

## 10장

**10:1**  I am sickened of life; I will give free rein to my griefs, I will speak out in bitterness of soul.  
**10:2** I will say to God, 'Do not condemn me, but tell me the ground of thy complaint against me.  
**10:3** Dost thou find any advantage in oppression, in spurning the fruit of all thy labour and smiling on the policy of wicked men?  
**10:4** Hast thou eyes of flesh or dost thou see as mortal man sees?  
**10:5** Are thy days as those of a mortal or thy years as the life of a man,  
**10:6** that thou lookest for guilt in me and dost seek in me for sin,  
**10:7** though thou knowest that I am guiltless and have none to save me from thee?  
**10:8** 'Thy hands gave me shape and made me; and dost thou at once turn and destroy me?  
**10:9** Remember that thou didst knead me like clay; and wouldst thou turn me back into dust?  
**10:10** Didst thou not pour me out like milk and curdle me like cheese,  
**10:11** clothe me with skin and flesh and knit me together with bones and sinews?  
**10:12** Thou hast given me life and continuing favour, and thy providence has watched over my spirit.  
**10:13** Yet this was the secret purpose of thy heart, and I know that this was thy intent:  
**10:14** that, if I sinned, thou wouldst be watching me and wouldst not acquit me of my guilt.  
**10:15** If I indeed am wicked, the worse for me! If I am righteous, even so I may lift up my head ;  
**10:16** if I am proud as a lion, thou dost hunt me down and dost confront me again with marvellous power;  
**10:17** thou dost renew thy onslaught upon me, and with mounting anger against me bringest fresh forces to the attack.  
**10:18** Why didst thou bring me out of the womb?  
**10:19** O that I had ended there and no eye had seen me, that I had been carried from the womb to the grave and were as though I had not been born.  
**10:20** Is not my life short and fleeting? Let me be, that I may be happy for a moment,  
**10:21** before I depart to a land of gloom, a land of deep darkness, never to return,  
**10:22** a land of gathering shadows, of deepening darkness, lit by no ray of light , dark upon dark.'  

## 11장

**11:1**  Then Zophar the Naamathite began:  
**11:2**  Should this spate of words not be answered? Must a man of ready tongue be always right?  
**11:3** Is your endless talk to reduce men to silence? Are you to talk nonsense and no one rebuke you?  
**11:4** You claim that your opinions are sound; you say to God, 'I am spotless in thy sight.'  
**11:5** But if only he would speak and open his lips to talk with you,  
**11:6** and expound to you the secrets of wisdom, for wonderful are its effects! {Know then that God exacts from you less than your sin deserves.}  
**11:7** Can you fathom the mystery of God, can you fathom the perfection of the Almighty?  
**11:8** It is higher than heaven; you can do nothing. It is deeper than Sheol; you can know nothing.  
**11:9** Its measure is longer than the earth and broader than the sea.  
**11:10** If he passes by, he may keep secret his passing; if he proclaims it, who can turn him back?  
**11:11** He surely knows which men are false, and when he sees iniquity, does he not take note of it ?  
**11:12** Can a fool grow wise? can a wild ass's foal be born a man?  
**11:13** If only you had directed your heart rightly and spread out your hands to pray to him!  
**11:14** If you have wrongdoing in hand, thrust it away; let no iniquity make its home with you.  
**11:15** Then you could hold up your head without fault, a man of iron, knowing no fear.  
**11:16** Then you will forget your trouble; you will remember it only as flood-waters that have passed;  
**11:17** life will be lasting, bright as noonday, and darkness will be turned to morning.  
**11:18** You will be confident, because there is hope; sure of protection, you will lie down in confidence  
**11:19** ; great men will seek your favour.  
**11:20** Blindness will fall on the wicked; the ways of escape are closed to them, and their hope is despair.  

## 12장

**12:1**  Then Job answered:  
**12:2**  No doubt you are perfect men and absolute wisdom is yours!  
**12:3** But I have sense as well as you; in nothing do I fall short of you; what gifts indeed have you that others have not?  
**12:4** Yet I am a laughing-stock to my friend— a laughing-stock, though I am innocent and blameless, one that called upon God, and he answered .  
**12:5** Prosperity and ease look down on misfortune, on the blow that fells the man who is already reeling,  
**12:6** while the marauders' tents are left undisturbed and those who provoke God live safe and sound   

## 13장

**13:1**  All this I have seen with my own eyes, with my own ears I have heard it, and understood it.  
**13:2** What you know, I also know; in nothing do I fall short of you.  
**13:3** But for my part I would speak with the Almighty and am ready to argue with God,  
**13:4** while you like fools are smearing truth with your falsehoods, stitching a patchwork of lies, one and all.  
**13:5** Ah, if you would only be silent and let silence be your wisdom!  
**13:6** Now listen to my arguments and attend while I put my case.  
**13:7** Is it on God's behalf that you speak so wickedly, or in his defence that you allege what is false?  
**13:8** Must you take God's part, or put his case for him?
