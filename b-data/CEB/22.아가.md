# 22.아가 (CEB)



## 1장

**1:1** The Song of Songs, which is for Solomon.  
**1:2**  If only he would give me some of his kisses . . .<br>Oh, your loving is sweeter than wine!<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Identification of speakers here and throughout the Song is hypothetical and in several cases uncertain.  
**1:3** Your fragrance is sweet;<br>your very name is perfume.<br>That’s why the young women love you.  
**1:4** Take me along with you; let’s run!<br>My king has brought me into his chambers, saying,<br>“Let’s exult and rejoice in you.<br>Let’s savor your loving more than wine.<br>No wonder they all love you!”  
**1:5** Dark am I, and lovely, daughters of Jerusalem—<br>like the black tents of the Kedar nomads,<br>like the curtains of Solomon’s palace.  
**1:6** Don’t stare at me because I’m darkened<br>by the sun’s gaze.<br>My own brothers were angry with me.<br>They made me a caretaker of the vineyards—<br>but I couldn’t care for my own vineyard.  
**1:7** Tell me, you whom I love with all my heart—<br>where do you pasture your flock,<br>where do you rest them at noon?—<br>so I don’t wander around with the flocks of your companions.  
**1:8** If you don’t know your way,<br>most beautiful of women,<br>then follow the tracks of the herds<br>and graze your little goats<br>by the tents of the shepherds.  
**1:9** I picture you, my dearest,<br>as a mare among Pharaoh’s chariots!  
**1:10** Lovely are your cheeks, adorned with ear hoops;<br>your neck, with beads.  
**1:11** Let’s make hoops of gold beaded with silver for you!  
**1:12** With my king close by,<br>my perfume filled the air.  
**1:13** A sachet of myrrh is my love to me,<br>lying all night between my breasts.  
**1:14** A cluster of henna flowers is my love to me<br>in the desert gardens of En-gedi.  
**1:15** Look at you—so beautiful, my dearest!<br>Look at you—so beautiful! Your eyes are doves!  
**1:16** Look at you—so beautiful, my love!<br>Yes, delightful! Yes, our bed is lush and green!  
**1:17** The ceilings of our chambers are cedars;<br>our rafters, cypresses.  

## 2장

**2:1** I’m a rose of the Sharon plain,<br>a lily of the valleys.  
**2:2** Like a lily among thornbushes,<br>so is my dearest among the young women.  
**2:3** Like an apple tree among the wild trees,<br>so is my lover among the young men.<br>In his shade I take pleasure in sitting,<br>and his fruit is sweet to my taste.  
**2:4** He has brought me to the house of wine;<br>his banner raised over me is love.  
**2:5** Sustain me with raisin cakes,<br>strengthen me with apples,<br>for I’m weak with love!  
**2:6** His left arm is beneath my head,<br>his right embraces me.  
**2:7** Make a solemn pledge, daughters of Jerusalem,<br>by the gazelles or the wild deer:<br>Don’t rouse, don’t arouse love<br>until it desires.  
**2:8** Listen! It’s my lover: here he comes now,<br>leaping upon the mountains,<br>bounding over the hills.  
**2:9** My lover is like a gazelle<br>or a young stag.<br>Here he stands now,<br>outside our wall,<br>peering through the windows,<br>peeking through the lattices.  
**2:10** My lover spoke and said to me,<br>“Rise up, my dearest,<br>my fairest, and go.  
**2:11** Here, the winter is past;<br>the rains have come and gone.  
**2:12** Blossoms have appeared in the land;<br>the season of singing<sup>①</sup> has arrived,<br>and the sound of the turtledove is heard in our land.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>pruning</i>  
**2:13** The green fruit is on the fig tree,<br>and the grapevines in bloom are fragrant.<br>Rise up,<sup>①</sup> my dearest,<br>my fairest, and go.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>LXX and Kethib add <i>go;</i> but Qere, DSS, Vulg, Syr, and Tg lack the verb; cf <a name='B:260 2:10'>2:10.  
**2:14** My dove—in the rock crevices,<br>hidden in the cliff face—<br>let me catch sight of you;<br>let me hear your voice!<br>The sound of your voice is sweet,<br>and the sight of you is lovely.”  
**2:15** Catch foxes for us—<br>those little foxes<br>that spoil vineyards,<br>now that our vineyards are in bloom!  
**2:16** I belong to my lover and he belongs to me—<br>the one grazing among the lilies.  
**2:17** Before the day breeze blows<br>and the shadows flee,<br>turn about, my love; be like a gazelle<br>or a young stag<br>upon the jagged mountains.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>upon the mountains of Bether;</i> cf <a name='B:260 8:14'>8:14 <i>mountains of spice</i>  

## 3장

**3:1** Upon my bed, night after night,<br>I looked for the one whom I love with all my heart.<br>I looked for him but couldn’t find him.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>LXX adds <i>I called him, but he didn't answer me;</i> cf <a name='B:260 5:6'>5:6.  
**3:2** “I will rise now and go all around the city,<br>through the streets and the squares.<br>I will look for the one whom I love with all my heart.”<br>I looked for him but couldn’t find him.  
**3:3** The guards found me,<br>those who make their rounds in the city.<br>“The one whom I love with all my heart—<br>have you seen him?”  
**3:4** No sooner did I depart from them<br>than I found the one whom I love with all my heart.<br>I held on to him and now I won’t let him go,<br>until I’ve brought him to my mother’s house,<br>to the chamber of the one who conceived me.  
**3:5** I place you under oath, daughters of Jerusalem,<br>by the gazelles or the wild deer:<br>don’t rouse, don’t arouse love<br>until it desires.  
**3:6** Who is this, coming up from the wilderness,<br>like pillars of smoke?<br>She is perfumed with myrrh and frankincense,<br>selected from all the spice merchant’s powders.  
**3:7** Picture Solomon’s bed—<br>sixty heroic men round about it,<br>all from the heroes of Israel,  
**3:8** all of them skilled with the sword,<br>expert in warfare,<br>each with his sword ready at his thigh<br>against terrors that come by night.  
**3:9** King Solomon made a canopied couch for himself<br>from the trees of Lebanon.  
**3:10** Its pillars he made of silver,<br>its covering, cloth of gold,<br>its cushions, royal purple;<br>its interior inlaid with love.<br>Daughters of Jerusalem,  
**3:11** go forth!<br>Look, daughters of Zion—<br>on King Solomon wearing the crown<br>with which his mother crowned him<br>on the day of his wedding,<br>on the day of his heart’s joy.  

## 4장

**4:1** Look at you—so beautiful, my dearest!<br>Look at you—so beautiful! Your eyes are doves<br>behind the veil of your hair!<br>Your hair is like a flock of goats<br>as they stream down Mount Gilead.  
**4:2** Your teeth are like newly shorn ewes<br>as they come up from the washing pool—<br>all of them perfectly matched,<br>not one of them lacks its twin.  
**4:3** Like a crimson ribbon are your lips;<br>when you smile, it is lovely.<br>Like a slice of pomegranate is the curve of your face<br>behind the veil of your hair.  
**4:4** Like David’s tower is your neck,<br>splendidly built!<br>A thousand shields are hung upon it—<br>all the weapons of the warriors.  
**4:5** Your two breasts are like two fawns,<br>twins of a gazelle doe,<br>that graze among the lilies.  
**4:6** Before the day breeze blows<br>and the shadows flee,<br>I will be off to the mountain of myrrh,<br>to the hill of frankincense.  
**4:7** You are utterly beautiful, my dearest;<br>there’s not a single flaw in you.  
**4:8** Come down with me from Lebanon, my bride—<br>if only you would come down with me from Lebanon.<br>Descend from the peak of Amana,<br>from the peaks of Senir and Hermon,<br>from the lions’ dens,<br>from the mountain lairs of leopards.  
**4:9** You have captured my heart, my sister,<sup>①</sup> my bride!<br>You have captured my heart with one glance from your eyes,<br>with one strand of your necklace.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Sister</i> here and below is a common term in ancient love poetry; it doesn't imply blood relation.  
**4:10** How beautiful is your loving, my sister, my bride!<br>Your loving is so much better than wine,<br>and your fragrance better than any perfume!  
**4:11** Sweetness drops from your lips, my bride;<br>honey and milk are under your tongue,<br>and the fragrance of your garments<br>is like the fragrance of Lebanon.  
**4:12** An enclosed garden is my sister, my bride;<br>an enclosed pool, a sealed spring.  
**4:13** Your limbs are an orchard of pomegranates<br>with all kinds of luscious fruit,<br>henna, and spices:  
**4:14** nard and saffron,<br>sweet cane and cinnamon,<br>with all scented woods,<br>myrrh, and aloes,<br>with the very choicest perfumes!  
**4:15** You are a garden spring, a well of fresh water,<br>streams from Lebanon.  
**4:16** Stir, north wind, and come, south wind!Blow upon my garden;<br>let its perfumes flow! <i><sup>①</sup></i> </pb><br>Let my love come to his garden;<br>let him eat its luscious fruit!  

## 5장

**5:1** I have come to my garden, my sister, my bride!<br>I have gathered my myrrh and my spices.<br>I have eaten my honeycomb with my honey;<br>I have drunk my wine and my milk.<br>Eat, dear friends!<br>Drink and get drunk on love!  
**5:2** I was sleeping, but my heart was awake.<br>A sound! My love is knocking: <i><sup>①</sup></i> </pb><br>“Open for me, my sister, my dearest,<br>my dove, my perfect one!<br>My head is soaked with dew,<br>my hair, with the night mists.”  
**5:3** “I have taken off my tunic—<br>why should I put it on again?<br>I have bathed my feet—<br>why should I get them dirty?”  
**5:4** My love put his hand in through the latch hole,<br>and my body ached for him.  
**5:5** I rose; I went to open for my love,<br>and my hands dripped myrrh,<br>my fingers, liquid myrrh,<br>over the handles of the lock.  
**5:6** I went and opened for my love,<br>but my love had turned, gone away.<br>I nearly died when he turned away.<br>I looked for him but couldn’t find him.<br>I called out to him, but he didn’t answer me.  
**5:7** They found me—the guards<br>who make their rounds in the city.<br>They struck me, bruised me.<br>They took my shawl away from me,<br>those guards of the city walls!  
**5:8** I place you under oath, daughters of Jerusalem:<br>If you find my love, what should you tell him?<br>That I’m weak with love!  
**5:9** How is your lover different from any other lover,<br>you who are the most beautiful of women?<br>How is your lover different from any other lover,<br>that you make us swear a solemn pledge?  
**5:10** My lover is radiant and ruddy;<br>he stands out among ten thousand!  
**5:11** His head is finest gold;<br>his wavy hair, black as a raven.  
**5:12** His eyes are like doves<br>by channels of water.<br>They are bathing in milk,<br>sitting by brimming pools.  
**5:13** His cheeks are like fragrant plantings,<br>towers of spices.<br>His lips are lilies<br>dripping liquid myrrh.  
**5:14** His arms are gold cylinders<br>studded with jewels.<br>His belly is smooth ivory<br>encrusted with sapphires.  
**5:15** His thighs are pillars of whitest stone<br>set on pedestals of gold.<br>His appearance—like Lebanon,<br>stately, like the cedars.  
**5:16** His mouth is everything sweet,<br>every bit of him desirable.<br>This is my love, this my dearest,<br>daughters of Jerusalem!  

## 6장

**6:1** Which way did your lover go,<br>you who are the most beautiful of women?<br>Which way did your lover turn,<br>that we may look for him along with you?  
**6:2** My lover has gone down to his garden,<br>to the fragrant plantings,<br>to graze in the gardens,<br>to gather the lilies.  
**6:3** I belong to my lover and my lover belongs to me—<br>the one grazing among the lilies.  
**6:4** You are as beautiful, my dearest, as Tirzah,<br>as lovely as Jerusalem,<br>formidable as those lofty sights.  
**6:5** Turn your eyes away from me,<br>for they overwhelm me!<br>Your hair is like a flock of goats<br>as they stream down from Gilead.  
**6:6** Your teeth are like a flock of ewes<br>as they come up from the washing pool—<br>all of them perfectly matched,<br>not one of them lacks its twin.  
**6:7** Like a slice of pomegranate is the curve of your face<br>behind the veil of your hair.  
**6:8** There may be sixty queens<br>and eighty secondary wives,<br>young women beyond counting,  
**6:9** but my dove, my perfect one, is one of a kind.<br>To her mother she’s the only one,<br>radiant to the one who bore her.<br>Young women see her and declare her fortunate;<br>queens and secondary wives praise her.  
**6:10** Who is this, gazing down like the morning star,<br>beautiful as the full moon,<br>radiant as the sun,<br>formidable as those lofty sights?  
**6:11** To the nut grove I went down<br>to look upon the fresh growth in the valley,<br>to see whether the vine was in flower,<br>whether the pomegranates had bloomed.  
**6:12** I hardly knew myself;<br>she had set me in an official’s chariot!<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>I hardly knew what happened; my passion set me in an official's chariot!</i> LXX, Vulg <i>Aminadab's chariots;</i> Heb uncertain

## 7장

**7:1** How graceful are your sandaled feet,<br>willing woman!<br>The smooth curves of your thighs—like fine jewelry,<br>the work of an artist’s hands!  
**7:2** Your navel, cupped like the full moon—<br>may it never lack spiced wine!<br>Your belly is a mound of winnowed wheat<br>edged with lilies.  
**7:3** Your two breasts are like two fawns,<br>twins of a gazelle doe;  
**7:4** your neck, like a tower of ivory;<br>your eyes, pools in Heshbon,<br>by the gate of that lordly city.<sup>①</sup><br>Your profile is like the tower of Lebanon,<br>looking out toward Damascus.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>by the gate of Bath-rabbim</i>  
**7:5** Your head crowns you like Mount Carmel,<br>and your hair, braided in royal purple—<br>a king is bound by the tresses!  
**7:6** You are so beautiful, so lovely—<br>my love, delightful one!<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>With Syr and Aquila <i>daughter of delights;</i> MT <i>love in delights</i> or <i>love with every charm</i>  
**7:7** Your stately form resembles a date palm,<br>and your breasts are like clustered fruit.  
**7:8** I say, “I will climb the palm tree;<br>I will hold its fruit!”<br>May your breasts be now<br>like grape clusters,<br>and the scent of your breath like apples!  
**7:9** Your palate is like excellent wine . . . <i><sup>①</sup></i> </pb><br>. . . flowing smoothly for my love,<br>gliding through the lips and teeth.<sup>②</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>LXX, Syr, Vulg; MT <i>through the lips of those who sleep;</i> Heb uncertain  
**7:10** I belong to my lover,<br>and his longing is only for me.  
**7:11** Come, my love:<br>Let’s go out to the field<br>and rest all night among the flowering henna.  
**7:12** Let’s set out early for the vineyards.<br>We will see if the vines have budded<br>and the blossoms opened,<br>see if the pomegranates have bloomed.<br>There I’ll give my loving to you.  
**7:13** The mandrakes give off their scent,<br>and at our doorways is every delicacy—<br>fresh or ripened—<br>my love, I have kept them hidden for you.

**8:2** I would lead you, I would bring you<br>to my mother’s house;<br>she would teach me what to do.<sup>①</sup><br>I would give you spiced wine to drink,<br>some of my fresh pomegranate juice.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>you would teach me;</i> LXX, Syr <i>to my mother's house, and to the chamber of the one who conceived me</i> (cf <a name='B:260 3:4'>3:4)

**8:4** Make a solemn pledge,<br>daughters of Jerusalem,<br>never to rouse, never to arouse love<br>until it desires.  
**8:5** Who is this coming up from the wilderness<br>leaning against her lover? <i><sup>①</sup></i> </pb><br>Under the apple tree I aroused you—<br>there, where your mother labored with you,<br>there where, laboring, she bore you.
