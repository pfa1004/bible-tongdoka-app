# 10.사무엘하 (CEB)



## 1장

**1:1** After Saul’s death, when David had returned from defeating the Amalekites, he stayed in Ziklag two days.  
**1:2** On the third day, a man showed up from Saul’s camp with his clothes torn and dirt on his head. When he reached David, he fell to the ground, bowing low out of respect.  
**1:3** “Where have you come from?” David asked him. “I’ve escaped from the Israelite army!” he answered.  
**1:4** “What’s the report?” David asked him. “Tell me!” The man answered, “The troops fled from the battle! Many of the soldiers have fallen and died. What’s more, Saul and his son Jonathan have also died!”  
**1:5** “How do you know,” David asked the young man who brought the news, “that Saul and his son Jonathan are dead?”  
**1:6** The young man who brought the news replied, “I just happened to be on Mount Gilboa and Saul was there, leaning on his spear, with chariots and horsemen closing in on him.  
**1:7** He turned around and saw me, then he called to me. ‘Yes, sir,’ I answered.  
**1:8** ‘Who are you?’ he asked, and I told him, ‘I’m an Amalekite.’  
**1:9** He said to me, ‘Please come over here and kill me, because convulsions have come over me but I’m still alive.’<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Syr, Tg; Heb uncertain  
**1:10** So I went over to him and killed him, because I knew he couldn’t survive after being wounded like that. I took the crown that was on his head and the bracelet that was on his arm, and I’ve brought them here to you, my master.”  
**1:11** Then David grabbed his clothes and ripped them—and all his soldiers did the same.  
**1:12** They mourned and cried and fasted until evening for Saul, his son Jonathan, the LORD’s army, and the whole house of Israel, because they had died by the sword.  
**1:13** “Where are you from?” David asked the young man who brought him the news. “I’m the son of an immigrant,” he answered. “An Amalekite.”  
**1:14** Then David said to him, “How is it that you weren’t afraid to raise your hand and destroy the LORD’s anointed?”  
**1:15** Then David called for one of the young servants. “Come here!” he said. “Strike him down!” So the servant struck the Amalekite down, and he died.  
**1:16** “Your blood is on your own head,” David said to the Amalekite, “because your own mouth testified against you when you admitted, ‘I killed the LORD’s anointed.’”  
**1:17** Then David sang this funeral song<sup>①</sup> for Saul and his son Jonathan.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>lament</i>  
**1:18** David ordered everyone in Judah to learn the Song of the Bow.<sup>①</sup> (In fact, it is written in the scroll from Jashar.)<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb lacks <i>Song.</i>  
**1:19** Oh, no, Israel! Your prince<sup>①</sup> lies dead on your heights.<sup>②</sup><br>Look how the mighty warriors have fallen!<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>gazelle</i> or <i>splendor</i> or <i>splendid one</i> <sup>②</sup>Correction  
**1:20** Don’t talk about it in Gath;<br>don’t bring news of it to Ashkelon’s streets,<br>or else the Philistines’ daughters will rejoice;<br>the daughters of the uncircumcised will celebrate.  
**1:21** You hills of Gilboa!<br>Let there be no dew or rain on you,<br>and no fields yielding grain offerings.<sup>①</sup><br>Because it was there that the mighty warrior’s<sup>②</sup> shield was defiled—<br>the shield of Saul!—never again anointed with oil.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb uncertain, perhaps <i>bountiful fields</i>; alternatively, with LXX<sup>L</sup>, <i>fields of death</i>, or with correction <i>and no springs from the deep</i> <sup>②</sup>Or <i>warriors’</i> (plural)  
**1:22** Jonathan’s bow never wavered from the blood of the slain,<br>from the gore of the warriors.<br>Never did Saul’s sword return empty.  
**1:23** Saul and Jonathan! So well loved, so dearly cherished!<br>In their lives and in their deaths they were never separated.<br>They were faster than eagles,<br>stronger than lions!  
**1:24** Daughters of Israel, weep over Saul!<br>He dressed you in crimson with jewels;<br>he decorated your clothes with gold jewelry.  
**1:25** Look how the mighty warriors have fallen in the midst of battle!<br>Jonathan lies dead on your heights.  
**1:26** I grieve for you, my brother Jonathan!<br>You were so dear to me!<br>Your love was more amazing to me<sup>①</sup> than the love of women.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>your love</i> (or <i>care</i>; cf <a name='B:90 18:1'>1 Sam 18:1, <a name='B:90 18:3'>3; <a name='B:90 20:17'>20:17) <i>for me was more amazing</i>  
**1:27** Look how the mighty warriors have fallen!<br>Look how the weapons of war have been destroyed!  

## 2장

**2:1** Some time later, David questioned the LORD, “Should I go to one of the towns in Judah?” “Yes, go,” the LORD told him. “Which one should I go to?” David asked. “To Hebron,” the LORD replied.  
**2:2** So David went there, along with his two wives: Ahinoam from Jezreel and Abigail, Nabal’s widow, from Carmel.  
**2:3** David also took the soldiers who were with him, each with his family, and they lived in the towns around Hebron.  
**2:4** Then the people of Judah came to Hebron and anointed David king over the house of Judah. When David was informed that it was the people of Jabesh-gilead who had buried Saul,  
**2:5** he sent messengers to the people of Jabesh-gilead. “The LORD bless you,” he said to them, “for doing this loyal deed for your master Saul by burying him.  
**2:6** May the LORD now show you loyal love and faithfulness. I myself will also reward you because you did this.  
**2:7** So now take courage and be brave—yes, your master Saul is dead, but the house of Judah has anointed me king over them.”  
**2:8** Meanwhile, Abner, Ner’s son, the commander of Saul’s army, had taken Ishbosheth,<sup>①</sup> Saul’s son, and brought him over to Mahanaim.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Ishbosheth means <i>man of shame; shame</i> (Heb <i>bosheth</i>) may be a deliberate alteration from <i>Baal</i> (cf Esh-baal, <i>man of Baal</i> in <a name='B:130 8:33'>1 Chron 8:33; <a name='B:130 9:39'>9:39; see also <a name='B:100 4:4'>2 Sam 4:4); one manuscript of LXX<sup>L</sup> reads <i>Ishbaal</i>.  
**2:9** There he made him king over Gilead, the Geshurites,<sup>①</sup> Jezreel, Ephraim, and Benjamin—over all Israel.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Syr, Vulg; MT <i>Ashurites</i> or <i>Assyrians;</i> cf Tg, LXX<sup>L</sup>, <a name='B:70 1:32'>Judg 1:32 <i>Asherites</i>  
**2:10** Saul’s son Ishbosheth was 40 years old when he became king over Israel, and he ruled for two years. The house of Judah, however, followed David.  
**2:11** The amount of time David ruled in Hebron over the house of Judah totaled seven and a half years.  
**2:12** Abner, Ner’s son, along with the soldiers of Ishbosheth, Saul’s son, left Mahanaim to go to Gibeon.  
**2:13** Joab, Zeruiah’s son, and David’s soldiers also came out and confronted them at the pool of Gibeon. One group sat on one side of the pool; the other sat on the opposite side of the pool.  
**2:14** Abner said to Joab, “Let’s have the young men fight in a contest<sup>①</sup> before us.” “All right,” Joab said, “let’s do it.”<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>come forward and play</i> or <i>compete</i>  
**2:15** So the men came forward and were counted as they passed by: twelve for Benjamin and Ishbosheth, Saul’s son; and twelve of David’s soldiers.  
**2:16** Each man grabbed his opponent by the head and stuck<sup>①</sup> his sword into his opponent’s side so that they both fell dead together. That’s why that place is called The Field of Daggers,<sup>②</sup> which is located in Gibeon.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb lacks <i>stuck</i>. <sup>②</sup>Heb <i>Helkath-hazzurim</i>  
**2:17** A fierce battle took place that day, and Abner and the Israelite troops were defeated by David’s soldiers.  
**2:18** Now Zeruiah’s three sons were present at the battle: Joab, Abishai, and Asahel. Asahel was as fast as a gazelle in an open field.  
**2:19** Asahel went after Abner, staying completely focused in his pursuit of Abner.  
**2:20** Abner looked behind him and said, “Is that you, Asahel?” “Yes, it’s me,” Asahel answered.  
**2:21** “Break off your pursuit!” Abner told him. “Fight one of the young warriors and take his gear for yourself!” But Asahel wouldn’t stop chasing him.  
**2:22** So Abner repeated himself to Asahel: “Stop chasing me. Why should I kill you? How could I look your brother Joab in the face?”  
**2:23** But Asahel wouldn’t turn back, so Abner hit him in the stomach with the back end of his spear. But the spear went through Asahel’s back. He fell down and died right there. Everyone who came to the place where Asahel had fallen and died just stood there,  
**2:24** but Joab and Abishai went after Abner. The sun was setting when they came to the hill of Ammah, which faces Giah on the road to the Gibeon wilderness.  
**2:25** The Benjaminites rallied behind Abner, forming a single unit. Then they took their positions on the top of a hill.  
**2:26** Abner yelled down to Joab, “Must the sword keep killing forever? Don’t you realize that this will end bitterly? How long before you order the troops to stop chasing their brothers?”  
**2:27** “As surely as God lives,” Joab replied, “if you hadn’t just said that, the soldiers would have continued after their brothers until morning.”  
**2:28** Joab blew the trumpet,<sup>①</sup> and all the soldiers stopped. They didn’t pursue Israel anymore, nor did they continue to fight.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb <i>shofar</i>  
**2:29** Abner and his men then marched all night through the wilderness, crossing the Jordan River and marching all morning<sup>①</sup> until they got to Mahanaim.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb uncertain  
**2:30** Joab, meanwhile, returned from pursuing Abner and assembled the troops. Nineteen of David’s soldiers were counted missing in addition to Asahel.  
**2:31** But David’s soldiers had defeated the Benjaminites, killing three hundred sixty of Abner’s soldiers.  
**2:32** They took Asahel and buried him in his father’s tomb in Bethlehem. Then Joab and his men marched all night. When daylight came, they were in Hebron.  

## 3장

**3:2** David’s sons were born in Hebron. His oldest son was Amnon, by Ahinoam from Jezreel;  
**3:3** the second was Chileab, by Abigail, Nabal’s widow from Carmel; the third was Absalom, by Maacah,<sup>①</sup> who was the daughter of Geshur’s King Talmai;<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>son of</i>; also twice in <a name='B:100 3:4'>3:4

**3:5** and the sixth was Ithream, by David’s wife Eglah. These are David’s sons that were born in Hebron.  
**3:6** Throughout the war between Saul’s house and David’s house, Abner was gaining power in Saul’s house.

**3:15** Ishbosheth then sent for Michal and took her from her husband Paltiel, Laish’s son.  
**3:16** Her husband went with her all the way to Bahurim, crying as he followed her. “Go home!” Abner told him. So he went home.  
**3:17** Abner then sent word to Israel’s elders. “You’ve wanted David to be your king for some time now,” he said.

**3:20** When Abner, along with twenty others, reached David at Hebron, David threw a celebration for Abner and his men.  
**3:21** Then Abner said to David, “Please let me get going so I can assemble all Israel for my master the king. Then they can make a covenant with you, and you will rule over everything your heart<sup>①</sup> desires.” At that, David sent Abner off in peace.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>soul</i>

**3:29** May it fall upon the head of Joab and his entire family tree! May Joab’s family never be without someone with a discharge or a skin disease,<sup>①</sup> someone who uses a crutch,<sup>②</sup> someone who dies by the sword, or someone who is hungry!”<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Traditionally <i>leprosy</i>, a term used for several different skin diseases <sup>②</sup>Or <i>who holds a spindle</i>

**3:31** Then David ordered Joab and all the troops who were with him, “Tear your clothes and put on funeral clothes! Mourn for Abner!” King David himself walked behind the body.  
**3:32** They buried Abner in Hebron. The king wept loudly at Abner’s grave. All the troops cried too.  
**3:33** Then the king sang this funeral song<sup>①</sup> for Abner:<br>“Should Abner have died like a fool dies?<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>lament</i>

**3:35** Then all the soldiers came to urge David to eat something while it was still day, but David swore, “May God deal harshly with me and worse still if I eat bread or anything else before the sun goes down.”  
**3:36** All the troops took notice of this and were pleased by it. Indeed, everything that the king did pleased them.  
**3:37** So on that day all the troops and all Israel knew that it wasn’t the king’s idea to kill Abner, Ner’s son.  
**3:38** The king told his soldiers, “Don’t you know that a prince and a great man in Israel has fallen today?  
**3:39** And today, though I am the anointed king, I am weak. These men, Zeruiah’s sons, are too strong for me.<sup>①</sup> May the LORD repay the one who does evil according to the evil they did!”<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>more ruthless than me;</i> DSS (4QSam<sup>a</sup>) lacks this clause.

**5:5** He ruled over Judah for seven and a half years in Hebron. He ruled thirty-three years over all Israel and Judah in Jerusalem.

## 6장

**6:1** Once again David assembled the select warriors of Israel, thirty thousand strong.  
**6:2** David and all the troops who were with him set out for Baalah, which is Kiriath-jearim of Judah,<sup>①</sup> to bring God’s chest up from there—the chest that is called by the name<sup>②</sup> of the LORD of heavenly forces, who sits enthroned on the winged creatures.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>DSS (4QSam<sup>a</sup>), <a name='B:130 13:6'>1 Chron 13:6; MT <i>from Baale-judah</i> <sup>②</sup>MT repeats <i>name,</i> but <a name='B:130 13:6'>1 Chron 13:6 omits one of these and LXX reads the first as <i>there</i>.
