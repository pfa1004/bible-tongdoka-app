## 1장

**1:1** These are the words of Nehemiah, Hacaliah’s son. In the month of Kislev,<sup>①</sup> in the twentieth year,<sup>②</sup> while I was in the fortress city of Susa,<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>November–December <sup>②</sup>Of Artaxerxes  
**1:2** Hanani, one of my brothers, came with some other men from Judah. I asked them about the Jews who had escaped and survived the captivity, and about Jerusalem.  
**1:3** They told me, “Those in the province who survived the captivity are in great trouble and shame! The wall around Jerusalem is broken down, and its gates have been destroyed by fire!”  
**1:4** When I heard this news, I sat down and wept. I mourned for days, fasting and praying before the God of heaven.  
**1:5** I said:<br>“LORD God of heaven, great and awesome God, you are the one who keeps covenant and is truly faithful to those who love you and keep your commandments.  
**1:6** Let your ear be attentive and your eyes open to hear the prayer of your servant, which I now pray before you night and day for your servants, the people of Israel. “I confess the sins of the people of Israel, which we have committed against you. Both I and my family have sinned.  
**1:7** We have wronged you greatly. We haven’t kept the commandments, the statutes, and the ordinances that you commanded your servant Moses.  
**1:8** “Remember the word that you gave to your servant Moses when you said, ‘If you are unfaithful, I will scatter you among the peoples.  
**1:9** But if you return to me and keep my commandments by really doing them, then, even though your outcasts live<sup>①</sup> under distant skies, I will gather them from there and bring them to the place that I have chosen as a dwelling for my name.’<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb lacks <i>live.</i>  
**1:10** They are your servants and your people. They are the ones whom you have redeemed by your great power and your strong hand.  
**1:11** “LORD, let your ear be attentive to the prayer of your servant and to the prayer of your servants who delight in honoring your name. Please give success to your servant today and grant him favor in the presence of this man!” <i>Cupbearer’s plea</i> </pb>  At that time, I was a cupbearer to the king.  

## 2장

**2:1** In the month of Nisan,<sup>①</sup> in the twentieth year of King Artaxerxes, the king was about to be served wine. I took the wine and gave it to the king. Since I had never seemed sad in his presence,<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>March–April  
**2:2** the king asked me, “Why do you seem sad? Since you aren’t sick, you must have a broken heart!” I was very afraid  
**2:3** and replied, “May the king live forever! Why shouldn’t I seem sad when the city, the place of my family’s graves, is in ruins and its gates destroyed by fire?”  
**2:4** The king asked, “What is it that you need?” I prayed to the God of heaven  
**2:5** and replied, “If it pleases the king, and if your servant has found favor with you, please send me to Judah, to the city of my family’s graves so that I may rebuild it.”  
**2:6** With the queen sitting beside him, the king asked me, “How long will you be away and when will you return?” So it pleased the king to send me, and I told him how long I would be gone.  
**2:7** I also said to him, “If it pleases the king, may letters be given me addressed to the governors of the province Beyond the River to allow me to travel to Judah.  
**2:8** May the king also issue a letter to Asaph the keeper of the king’s forest, directing him to supply me with timber for the beams of the temple fortress gates, for the city wall, and for the house in which I will live.” The king gave me what I asked, for the gracious power of my God was with me.  
**2:9** So I went to the governors of the province<sup>①</sup> Beyond the River and gave them the king’s letters. The king had sent officers of the army and cavalry with me.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb lacks <i>of the province.</i>  
**2:10** When Sanballat the Horonite and Tobiah the Ammonite official heard this, they were very angry that someone had come to seek the welfare of the people of Israel.  
**2:11** When I reached Jerusalem and had been there for three days,  
**2:12** I set out at night, taking only a few people with me. I didn’t tell anyone what my God was prompting me to do for Jerusalem, and the only animal I took was the one I rode.  
**2:13** I went out by night through the Valley Gate past the Dragon’s Spring to the Dung Gate so that I could inspect the walls of Jerusalem that had been broken down, as well as its gates, which had been destroyed by fire.  
**2:14** Then I went on to the Spring Gate and to the King’s Pool. Since there was no room for the animal on which I was riding to pass,  
**2:15** I went up by way of the valley by night and inspected the wall. Then I turned back and returned by entering through the Valley Gate.  
**2:16** The officials didn’t know where I had gone or what I was doing. I hadn’t yet told the Jews, the priests, the officials, the officers, or the rest who were to do the work.  
**2:17** So I said to them, “You see the trouble that we’re in: Jerusalem is in ruins, and its gates are destroyed by fire! Come, let’s rebuild the wall of Jerusalem so that we won’t continue to be in disgrace.”  
**2:18** I told them that my God had taken care of me, and also told them what the king had said to me. “Let’s start rebuilding!” they said, and they eagerly began the work.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>they strengthened their hands for the good.</i>  
**2:19** But when Sanballat the Horonite, Tobiah the Ammonite official, and Geshem the Arab heard about it, they mocked and made fun of us. “What are you doing?” they asked. “Are you rebelling against the king?”  
**2:20** “The God of heaven will give us success!” I replied. “As God’s servants, we will start building. But you will have no share, right, or claim in Jerusalem.”

## 4장

**4:1** <sup>①</sup> When Sanballat heard that we were building the wall, he became angry and raged. He mocked the Jews,<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>3:33 in Heb  
**4:2** saying in the presence of his associates and the army of Samaria: “What are those feeble Jews doing? Will they restore things themselves? Will they offer sacrifices? Will they finish it in a day? Will they revive the stones from the piles of rubble, even though they are burned?”  
**4:3** Tobiah the Ammonite, who was beside him, added: “If even a fox climbs on whatever they build, their wall of stones will crumble.”  
**4:4** Listen, God; we are despised! Turn their insults to us<sup>①</sup> back on their heads and make them like plunder in a captive land.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb lacks <i>to us.</i>  
**4:5** Don’t forgive their iniquity or blot out their sins from your sight. They have thrown insults at the builders!  
**4:6** We continued to build the wall. All of it was joined together, and it reached half of its intended height because the people were eager to work.  
**4:7** <sup>①</sup> But when Sanballat, Tobiah, the Arabs, the Ammonites, and the people of Ashdod heard that the work on the walls was progressing and the gaps were being closed, they were very angry.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><a name='B:160 4:1'>4:1 in Heb  
**4:8** They plotted together to come and fight against Jerusalem and to create a disturbance in it.  
**4:9** So we prayed to our God and set a guard as protection against them day and night.  
**4:10** But in Judah it was said,<br>“The carrier’s strength is failing,<br>for there is too much rubble.<br>We are unable to rebuild the wall!”  
**4:11** Meanwhile, our enemies were saying: “Before they know or see anything, we can be in their midst and start to kill them. We’ll stop the work!”  
**4:12** Now the Jews who were living near them came and said to us again and again,<sup>①</sup> “You must return to us!”<sup>②</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>ten times from all sides</i> <sup>②</sup>Heb uncertain  
**4:13** So I took up a position in the lowest parts of the space behind the wall in an open area.<sup>①</sup> Then I stationed the people by families, and they had their swords, spears, and bows.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb uncertain  
**4:14** After reviewing this, I stood up and said to the officials, the officers, and the rest of the people, “Don’t be afraid of them! Remember that the LORD is great and awesome! Fight for your families, your sons, your daughters, your wives, and your houses!”  
**4:15** Then our enemies heard that we had found out and that God had spoiled their plans. So we all returned to doing our own work on the wall.

**4:21** So we continued the work, with half of them holding spears from dawn until dusk.  
**4:22** I also said to the people at that time, “Let every man and his servant spend the night in Jerusalem so that we can guard during the night and work during the day.”  
**4:23** Neither I nor my relatives, nor my servants, nor my bodyguards took off our clothes, even when they sent for water.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb uncertain  

## 5장

**5:1** Then there was a great protest of the people and their wives against their fellow Jews.  
**5:2** Some said, “With our sons and daughters we are many, and we all need grain to eat and stay alive.”  
**5:3** Others said, “We have to mortgage our fields, our vineyards, and our houses in order to get grain during the famine.”  
**5:4** Still others said, “We have had to borrow money against our fields and vineyards in order to pay the king’s tax.”  
**5:5** “We are of the same flesh and blood as our kin, and our children are the same as theirs. Yet we are just about to force our sons and daughters into slavery, and some of our daughters are already slaves! There is nothing we can do since our fields and vineyards now belong to others.”  
**5:6** I was very angry when I heard their protest and these complaints.  
**5:7** After thinking it over, I brought charges against the officials and the officers. I told them, “You are all taking interest from your own people!” I also called for a large assembly in order to deal with them.  
**5:8** “To the best of our ability,” I said to them, “we have bought back our Jewish kin who had been sold to other nations. But now you are selling your own kin, who must then be bought back by us!” At this they were silent, unable to offer a response.  
**5:9** So I continued, “What you are doing isn’t good! Why don’t you walk in the fear of our God? This will prevent the taunts of the nations that are our enemies!  
**5:10** I myself, along with my family and my servants, am lending them money and grain. But let’s stop charging this interest!  
**5:11** Give it back to them, right now. Return their fields, their vineyards, their olive orchards, and their houses. And give back the interest on money, grain, wine, and oil that you are charging them.”  
**5:12** They replied, “We’ll return everything, and we won’t charge anything else.<sup>①</sup> We’ll do what you’ve asked.” So I called the priests and made them swear to do what they had promised.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb lacks <i>everything…anything else.</i>  
**5:13** I also shook out the fold of my robe, saying, “So may God shake out everyone from their house and property if they don’t keep this promise. So may they be shaken out and emptied!” The whole assembly said, “Amen,” and praised the LORD. And the people did as they had promised.  
**5:14** In addition, from the time that I was appointed to be their governor in the land of Judah (that is, from the twentieth to the thirty-second year of King Artaxerxes for a total of twelve years), neither I nor my family ate from the governor’s food allowance.  
**5:15** The earlier governors who had come before me laid heavy burdens on the people. They took food and wine from them as well as<sup>①</sup> forty shekels of silver. Even their servants oppressed the people. But because I was God-fearing, I didn’t behave in this way.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb uncertain  
**5:16** Instead, I devoted myself to the work on this wall. We acquired no land, and all my servants were gathered there for the work.  
**5:17** One hundred fifty Jews and officials, along with those who came to us from the surrounding nations, gathered around my table.  
**5:18** One ox, six choice sheep, and birds were prepared each day. Every ten days there was a large amount of wine. Yet even with this I didn’t ask for the governor’s food allowance because of the heavy burden the people had to carry.  
**5:19** Remember in my favor, my God, all that I’ve done for this people!  

## 6장

**6:1** Now when Sanballat, Tobiah, Geshem the Arab, and the rest of our enemies heard that I had rebuilt the wall and that there were no gaps left in it (although I hadn’t yet hung the doors in the gates),  
**6:2** Sanballat and Geshem sent me this message: “Come, let’s meet together in one of the villages<sup>①</sup> in the plain of Ono.” But they wanted to harm me,<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>LXX, Vulg; MT <i>at Hakkephirim</i>  
**6:3** so I sent messengers to tell them, “I’m doing important work, so I can’t come down. Why should the work stop while I leave it to come down to you?”  
**6:4** They sent me a message like this four times, and every time I gave them a similar reply.  
**6:5** But the fifth time, Sanballat sent his servant to me in the same way, except that now he carried an open letter.  
**6:6** It stated:<br>It is reported among the nations and confirmed by Geshem<sup>①</sup> that you and the Jews intend to rebel. This is why you are rebuilding the wall. According to these reports, you intend to become their king.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>Gashmu</i>  
**6:7** You have also appointed prophets to make this announcement about you in Jerusalem: There is a king in Judah! Now, the king will hear of these reports, so come; let’s talk together.  
**6:8** So I sent him this reply: “Nothing that you say has happened. You are simply inventing this.”  
**6:9** All of them were trying to make us afraid, saying, “They will be discouraged, and the work won’t get finished.” But now, God, strengthen me!  
**6:10** Later I went to see Shemaiah, Delaiah’s son and Mehetabel’s grandson, who was confined to his house, and he said:<br>“Let’s meet together in God’s house,<br>inside the temple itself.<br>Let’s shut the doors of the temple,<br>for they are coming to kill you;<br>they are coming to kill you tonight!”  
**6:11** But I replied, “Should someone like me run away? Who like me would go into the temple to save his life? I won’t go in!”  
**6:12** Then I realized that God hadn’t sent him at all but that he spoke this prophecy against me because Tobiah and Sanballat had hired him.  
**6:13** He was hired to frighten me and to make me sin by acting in this way. Then they could give me a bad name and discredit me.  
**6:14** My God, remember these deeds of Tobiah and Sanballat! Also remember Noadiah the prophetess and the rest of the prophets who have been trying to frighten me.  
**6:15** So the wall was finished on the twenty-fifth day of the month of Elul.<sup>①</sup> It took fifty-two days.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>August–September  
**6:16** When our enemies heard about this, all of the nations around us were afraid and their confidence was greatly shaken. They knew that this work was completed with the help of our God.  
**6:17** In addition, in those days the officials of Judah sent many letters to Tobiah, and Tobiah’s letters were coming to them.  
**6:18** Many in Judah were bound to him by solemn pledge because he was the son-in-law of Shecaniah, Arah’s son, and his son Jehohanan had married the daughter of Meshullam, Berechiah’s son.  
**6:19** They also kept talking about his good deeds in my presence and then reported back to him what I said. In addition, Tobiah sent letters to intimidate me.

## 13장

**13:1** On that day, when the scroll from Moses was being read to the people, they found written in it that no Ammonite or Moabite should ever enter God’s assembly.  
**13:2** This is because they hadn’t met the Israelites with food and water but instead hired Balaam against them to curse them. Yet our God turned the curse into a blessing.  
**13:3** When the people heard this law, they separated out from Israel all those of mixed descent.  
**13:4** Now before this, however, Eliashib the priest, who was appointed to be in charge of the storerooms of our God’s house and who was related to Tobiah,  
**13:5** prepared a large room for Tobiah to use. This was the room where they had previously kept the grain offering, the incense, and the equipment, together with the tenth-part gifts of grain, wine, and oil. These items were for the Levites, singers, and gatekeepers as well as the portions for the priests.  
**13:6** I wasn’t in Jerusalem while this was happening because I had gone to Babylon’s King Artaxerxes in the thirty-second year of the king. After some time, I asked the king’s permission  
**13:7** and returned to Jerusalem. That was when I saw the wrong that Eliashib had done on behalf of Tobiah by preparing him a room in the courtyards of God’s house.  
**13:8** I was very angry and threw all of Tobiah’s household furniture out of the room.  
**13:9** Then I gave orders that the rooms be purified, and I put back the temple equipment, along with the grain offering and the incense.  
**13:10** I also found out that the Levites hadn’t been given their portions, so they and the singers who did the work had gone back to their fields.  
**13:11** So I scolded the officials, asking, “Why is God’s house being neglected?” I gathered them together and set them in their stations.  
**13:12** Then all Judah brought the tenth-part gifts of the grain, wine, and oil into the storehouses.  
**13:13** I appointed the priest Shelemiah, the scribe Zadok, and Pedaiah of the Levites to be in charge over the storehouses. I also appointed Hanan, Zaccur’s son and Mattaniah’s grandson, as their assistant. These men were considered trustworthy, and their task was to hand out shares to their colleagues.  
**13:14** Remember me, my God, concerning this. Don’t erase my good deeds that I have done for my God’s house and for its services.  
**13:15** In those days I saw people in Judah using the winepresses on the Sabbath. They were also collecting piles of grain and loading them on donkeys, as well as wine, grapes, figs, and every kind of load, and then bringing them to Jerusalem on the Sabbath. I warned them at that time against selling food.  
**13:16** In addition, people from Tyre who lived in the city were bringing in fish and all kinds of merchandise and selling them to the people of Judah on the Sabbath. This happened in Jerusalem itself!  
**13:17** So I scolded the officials of Judah: “What is this evil thing that you are doing?” I asked. “You are making the Sabbath impure!  
**13:18** This is just what your ancestors did, and God brought all this evil upon us and upon this city. And now you are bringing more wrath upon Israel by making the Sabbath impure!”  
**13:19** So when it began to grow dark at the gates of Jerusalem before the Sabbath, I gave orders that the doors should be shut. I also ordered that they shouldn’t be reopened until after the Sabbath. To make sure that no load would come into the city<sup>①</sup> on the Sabbath, I stationed some of my own men at the gates.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb lacks <i>the city.</i>  
**13:20** Once or twice the traders and sellers of all kinds of merchandise spent the night outside Jerusalem.  
**13:21** But I warned them: “Why are you spending the night by the wall? If you do that again, I will lay hands on you!” At that point, they stopped coming on the Sabbath.  
**13:22** I also commanded the Levites to purify themselves and to come and guard the gates in order to keep the Sabbath day holy. Remember this also in my favor, my God, and spare me according to the greatness of your mercy.  
**13:23** Also in those days I saw Jews who had married women of Ashdod, Ammon, and Moab.  
**13:24** Half of their children spoke the language of Ashdod or the language of various peoples; they couldn’t speak the language of Judah.  
**13:25** So I scolded them and cursed them, and beat some of them, and pulled out their hair. I also made them swear a solemn pledge in the name of God, saying, “You won’t give your daughters to their sons in marriage, or take their daughters in marriage for your sons or yourselves.  
**13:26** Didn’t Israel’s King Solomon sin on account of such women? Among the many nations there was no king like him. He was well loved by his God, and God made him king over all Israel. Yet foreign wives led even him into sin!  
**13:27** Should we then listen to you and do all this great evil, acting unfaithfully toward our God by marrying foreign women?”  
**13:28** Now one of the sons of Joiada son of the high priest Eliashib was a son-in-law of Sanballat the Horonite. So I chased him away from me.  
**13:29** Remember them, my God, because they have defiled the priesthood and the covenant of the priests and the Levites!  
**13:30** So I purified them of everything foreign and established the services of the priests and Levites with specific duties for each person.  
**13:31** I also provided for the wood offering at appointed times as well as for the early produce. Remember me, my God, for good.
