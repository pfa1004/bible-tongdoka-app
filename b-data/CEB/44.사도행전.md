## 1장

**1:1** Theophilus, the first scroll I wrote concerned everything Jesus did and taught from the beginning,  
**1:2** right up to the day when he was taken up into heaven. Before he was taken up, working in the power of the Holy Spirit, Jesus instructed the apostles he had chosen.  
**1:3** After his suffering, he showed them that he was alive with many convincing proofs. He appeared to them over a period of forty days, speaking to them about God’s kingdom.  
**1:4** While they were eating together, he ordered them not to leave Jerusalem but to wait for what the Father had promised. He said, “This is what you heard from me:  
**1:5** John baptized with water, but in only a few days you will be baptized with the Holy Spirit.”  
**1:6** As a result, those who had gathered together asked Jesus, “Lord, are you going to restore the kingdom to Israel now?”  
**1:7** Jesus replied, “It isn’t for you to know the times or seasons that the Father has set by his own authority.  
**1:8** Rather, you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you, and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem, in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth.”  
**1:9** After Jesus said these things, as they were watching, he was lifted up and a cloud took him out of their sight.  
**1:10** While he was going away and as they were staring toward heaven, suddenly two men in white robes stood next to them.  
**1:11** They said, “Galileans, why are you standing here, looking toward heaven? This Jesus, who was taken up from you into heaven, will come in the same way that you saw him go into heaven.”  
**1:12** Then they returned to Jerusalem from the Mount of Olives, which is near Jerusalem—a sabbath day’s journey away.  
**1:13** When they entered the city, they went to the upstairs room where they were staying. Peter, John, James, and Andrew; Philip and Thomas; Bartholomew and Matthew; James, Alphaeus’ son; Simon the zealot; and Judas, James’ son—  
**1:14** all were united in their devotion to prayer, along with some women, including Mary the mother of Jesus, and his brothers.  
**1:15** During this time, the family of believers was a company of about one hundred twenty persons. Peter stood among them and said,  
**1:16** “Brothers and sisters, the scripture that the Holy Spirit announced beforehand through David had to be fulfilled. This was the scripture concerning Judas, who became a guide for those who arrested Jesus.  
**1:17** This happened even though he was one of us and received a share of this ministry.”  
**1:18** <n><sup>①</sup></n> (In fact, he bought a field with the payment he received for his injustice. Falling headfirst, he burst open in the middle and all his intestines spilled out.  
**1:19** This became known to everyone living in Jerusalem, so they called that field in their own language Hakeldama, or “Field of Blood.”)  
**1:20** “It is written in the Psalms scroll,<br><i>Let his home become deserted and let there be no one living in it;</i><sup>①</sup>  and<br><i>Give his position of leadership to another.</i><sup>②</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><a name='B:230 69:25'>Ps 69:25 <sup>②</sup><a name='B:230 109:8'>Ps 109:8  
**1:21** “Therefore, we must select one of those who have accompanied us during the whole time the Lord Jesus lived among us,  
**1:22** beginning from the baptism of John until the day when Jesus was taken from us. This person must become along with us a witness to his resurrection.”  
**1:23** So they nominated two: Joseph called Barsabbas, who was also known as Justus, and Matthias.  
**1:24** They prayed, “Lord, you know everyone’s deepest thoughts and desires. Show us clearly which one you have chosen from among these two  
**1:25** to take the place of this ministry and apostleship, from which Judas turned away to go to his own place.”  
**1:26** When they cast lots, the lot fell on Matthias. He was added to the eleven apostles.  

## 2장

**2:1** When Pentecost Day arrived, they were all together in one place.  
**2:2** Suddenly a sound from heaven like the howling of a fierce wind filled the entire house where they were sitting.  
**2:3** They saw what seemed to be individual flames of fire alighting on each one of them.  
**2:4** They were all filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in other languages as the Spirit enabled them to speak.  
**2:5** There were pious Jews from every nation under heaven living in Jerusalem.  
**2:6** When they heard this sound, a crowd gathered. They were mystified because everyone heard them speaking in their native languages.  
**2:7** They were surprised and amazed, saying, “Look, aren’t all the people who are speaking Galileans, every one of them?  
**2:8** How then can each of us hear them speaking in our native language?  
**2:9** Parthians, Medes, and Elamites; as well as residents of Mesopotamia, Judea, and Cappadocia, Pontus and Asia,  
**2:10** Phrygia and Pamphylia, Egypt and the regions of Libya bordering Cyrene; and visitors from Rome (both Jews and converts to Judaism),  
**2:11** Cretans and Arabs—we hear them declaring the mighty works of God in our own languages!”  
**2:12** They were all surprised and bewildered. Some asked each other, “What does this mean?”  
**2:13** Others jeered at them, saying, “They’re full of new wine!”  
**2:14** Peter stood with the other eleven apostles. He raised his voice and declared, “Judeans and everyone living in Jerusalem! Know this! Listen carefully to my words!  
**2:15** These people aren’t drunk, as you suspect; after all, it’s only nine o’clock in the morning!  
**2:16** Rather, this is what was spoken through the prophet Joel:  
**2:17** <i>In the last days, God says,</i><br><i>I will pour out my Spirit on all people.</i><br><i>Your sons and daughters will prophesy.</i><br><i>Your young will see visions.</i><br><i>Your elders will dream dreams.</i>  
**2:18** <i>Even upon my servants, men and women,</i><br><i>I will pour out my Spirit in those days,</i><br><i>and they will prophesy.</i>  
**2:19** <i>I will cause wonders to occur in the heavens above</i><br><i>and signs on the earth below,</i><br><i>blood and fire and a cloud of smoke.</i>  
**2:20** <i>The sun will be changed into darkness,</i><br><i>and the moon will be changed into blood,</i><br><i>before the great and spectacular day of the Lord comes.</i>  
**2:21** <i>And everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved.</i><sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><a name='B:360 2:28-32'>Joel 2:28–32  
**2:22** “Fellow Israelites, listen to these words! Jesus the Nazarene was a man whose credentials God proved to you through miracles, wonders, and signs, which God performed through him among you. You yourselves know this.  
**2:23** In accordance with God’s established plan and foreknowledge, he was betrayed. You, with the help of wicked men, had Jesus killed by nailing him to a cross.  
**2:24** God raised him up! God freed him from death’s dreadful grip, since it was impossible for death to hang on to him.  
**2:25** David says about him,<br><i>I foresaw that the Lord was always with me;</i><br><i>because he is at my right hand I won’t be shaken.</i>  
**2:26** <i>Therefore, my heart was glad</i><br><i>and my tongue rejoiced.</i><br><i>Moreover, my body will live in hope,</i>  
**2:27** <i>because you won’t abandon me to the grave,</i><br><i>nor permit your holy one to experience decay.</i>  
**2:28** <i>You have shown me the paths of life;</i><br><i>your presence will fill me with happiness.</i><sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><a name='B:230 16:8-11'>Ps 16:8–11  
**2:29** “Brothers and sisters, I can speak confidently about the patriarch David. He died and was buried, and his tomb is with us to this very day.  
**2:30** Because he was a prophet, he knew that God promised him with a solemn pledge to seat one of his descendants on his throne.  
**2:31** Having seen this beforehand, David spoke about the resurrection of Christ, that <i>he wasn’t abandoned to the grave, nor did his body experience decay</i>.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><a name='B:230 16:10'>Ps 16:10  
**2:32** This Jesus, God raised up. We are all witnesses to that fact.  
**2:33** He was exalted to God’s right side and received from the Father the promised Holy Spirit. He poured out this Spirit, and you are seeing and hearing the results of his having done so.  
**2:34** David didn’t ascend into heaven. Yet he says,<br><i>The Lord said to my Lord, ‘Sit at my right side,</i>

**2:36** “Therefore, let all Israel know beyond question that God has made this Jesus, whom you crucified, both Lord and Christ.”  
**2:37** When the crowd heard this, they were deeply troubled. They said to Peter and the other apostles, “Brothers, what should we do?”  
**2:38** Peter replied, “Change your hearts and lives. Each of you must be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins. Then you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.  
**2:39** This promise is for you, your children, and for all who are far away—as many as the Lord our God invites.”  
**2:40** With many other words he testified to them and encouraged them, saying, “Be saved from this perverse generation.”  
**2:41** Those who accepted Peter’s message were baptized. God brought about three thousand people into the community on that day.  
**2:42** The believers devoted themselves to the apostles’ teaching, to the community, to their shared meals, and to their prayers.  
**2:43** A sense of awe came over everyone. God performed many wonders and signs through the apostles.  
**2:44** All the believers were united and shared everything.  
**2:45** They would sell pieces of property and possessions and distribute the proceeds to everyone who needed them.  
**2:46** Every day, they met together in the temple and ate in their homes. They shared food with gladness and simplicity.  
**2:47** They praised God and demonstrated God’s goodness to everyone. The Lord added daily to the community those who were being saved.  

## 3장

**3:1** Peter and John were going up to the temple at three o’clock in the afternoon, the established prayer time.  
**3:2** Meanwhile, a man crippled since birth was being carried in. Every day, people would place him at the temple gate known as the Beautiful Gate so he could ask for money from those entering the temple.  
**3:3** When he saw Peter and John about to enter, he began to ask them for a gift.  
**3:4** Peter and John stared at him. Peter said, “Look at us!”  
**3:5** So the man gazed at them, expecting to receive something from them.  
**3:6** Peter said, “I don’t have any money, but I will give you what I do have. In the name of Jesus Christ the Nazarene, rise up and walk!”  
**3:7** Then he grasped the man’s right hand and raised him up. At once his feet and ankles became strong.  
**3:8** Jumping up, he began to walk around. He entered the temple with them, walking, leaping, and praising God.  
**3:9** All the people saw him walking and praising God.  
**3:10** They recognized him as the same one who used to sit at the temple’s Beautiful Gate asking for money. They were filled with amazement and surprise at what had happened to him.  
**3:11** While the healed man clung to Peter and John, all the people rushed toward them at Solomon’s Porch, completely amazed.  
**3:12** Seeing this, Peter addressed the people: “You Israelites, why are you amazed at this? Why are you staring at us as if we made him walk by our own power or piety?  
**3:13** The God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob—the God of our ancestors—has glorified his servant Jesus. This is the one you handed over and denied in Pilate’s presence, even though he had already decided to release him.  
**3:14** You rejected the holy and righteous one, and asked that a murderer be released to you instead.  
**3:15** You killed the author of life, the very one whom God raised from the dead. We are witnesses of this.  
**3:16** His name itself has made this man strong. That is, because of faith in Jesus’ name, God has strengthened this man whom you see and know. The faith that comes through Jesus gave him complete health right before your eyes.  
**3:17** “Brothers and sisters, I know you acted in ignorance. So did your rulers.  
**3:18** But this is how God fulfilled what he foretold through all the prophets: that his Christ would suffer.  
**3:19** Change your hearts and lives! Turn back to God so that your sins may be wiped away.  
**3:20** Then the Lord will provide a season of relief from the distress of this age and he will send Jesus, whom he handpicked to be your Christ.  
**3:21** Jesus must remain in heaven until the restoration of all things, about which God spoke long ago through his holy prophets.  
**3:22** Moses said, <i>The Lord your God will raise up from your own people a prophet like me. Listen to whatever he tells you.</i>  
**3:23** <i>Whoever doesn’t listen to that prophet will be totally cut off from the people.</i><sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><a name='B:50 18:15'>Deut 18:15, <a name='B:50 18:19'>19  
**3:24** All the prophets who spoke—from Samuel forward—announced these days.  
**3:25** You are the heirs of the prophets and the covenant that God made with your ancestors when he told Abraham, <i>Through your descendants, all the families on earth will be blessed.</i><sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><a name='B:10 22:18'>Gen 22:18; <a name='B:10 26:4'>26:4  
**3:26** After God raised his servant, he sent him to you first—to bless you by enabling each of you to turn from your evil ways.”  

## 4장

**4:1** While Peter and John were speaking to the people, the priests, the captain of the temple guard, and the Sadducees confronted them.  
**4:2** They were incensed that the apostles were teaching the people and announcing that the resurrection of the dead was happening because of Jesus.  
**4:3** They seized Peter and John and put them in prison until the next day. (It was already evening.)  
**4:4** Many who heard the word became believers, and their number grew to about five thousand.  
**4:5** The next day the leaders, elders, and legal experts gathered in Jerusalem,  
**4:6** along with Annas the high priest, Caiaphas, John, Alexander, and others from the high priest’s family.  
**4:7** They had Peter and John brought before them and asked, “By what power or in what name did you do this?”  
**4:8** Then Peter, inspired by the Holy Spirit, answered, “Leaders of the people and elders,  
**4:9** are we being examined today because something good was done for a sick person, a good deed that healed him?  
**4:10** If so, then you and all the people of Israel need to know that this man stands healthy before you because of the name of Jesus Christ the Nazarene—whom you crucified but whom God raised from the dead.  
**4:11** This Jesus is the stone you builders rejected; he has become the cornerstone!  
**4:12** Salvation can be found in no one else. Throughout the whole world, no other name has been given among humans through which we must be saved.”  
**4:13** The council was caught by surprise by the confidence with which Peter and John spoke. After all, they understood that these apostles were uneducated and inexperienced. They also recognized that they had been followers of Jesus.  
**4:14** However, since the healed man was standing with Peter and John before their own eyes, they had no rebuttal.  
**4:15** After ordering them to wait outside, the council members began to confer with each other.  
**4:16** “What should we do with these men? Everyone living in Jerusalem is aware of the sign performed through them. It’s obvious to everyone and we can’t deny it.  
**4:17** To keep it from spreading further among the people, we need to warn them not to speak to anyone in this name.”  
**4:18** When they called Peter and John back, they demanded that they stop all speaking and teaching in the name of Jesus.  
**4:19** Peter and John responded, “It’s up to you to determine whether it’s right before God to obey you rather than God.  
**4:20** As for us, we can’t stop speaking about what we have seen and heard.”  
**4:21** They threatened them further, then released them. Because of public support for Peter and John, they couldn’t find a way to punish them. Everyone was praising God for what had happened,  
**4:22** because the man who had experienced this sign of healing was over 40 years old.  
**4:23** After their release, Peter and John returned to the brothers and sisters and reported everything the chief priests and elders had said.  
**4:24** They listened, then lifted their voices in unison to God, “Master, you are the one who created the heaven, the earth, the sea, and everything in them.  
**4:25** You are the one who spoke by the Holy Spirit through our ancestor David, your servant:<br><i>Why did the Gentiles rage</i>,<br><i>and the peoples plot in vain?</i>  
**4:26** <i>The kings of the earth took their stand</i><br><i>and the rulers gathered together as one</i><br><i>against the Lord and against his Christ.</i><sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>anointed one;</i> <a name='B:230 2:1-2'>Ps 2:1–2  
**4:27** Indeed, both Herod and Pontius Pilate, with Gentiles and Israelites, did gather in this city against your holy servant Jesus, whom you anointed.  
**4:28** They did what your power and plan had already determined would happen.  
**4:29** Now, Lord, take note of their threats and enable your servants to speak your word with complete confidence.  
**4:30** Stretch out your hand to bring healing and enable signs and wonders to be performed through the name of Jesus, your holy servant.”  
**4:31** After they prayed, the place where they were gathered was shaken. They were all filled with the Holy Spirit and began speaking God’s word with confidence.  
**4:32** The community of believers was one in heart and mind. None of them would say, “This is mine!” about any of their possessions, but held everything in common.  
**4:33** The apostles continued to bear powerful witness to the resurrection of the Lord Jesus, and an abundance of grace was at work among them all.  
**4:34** There were no needy persons among them. Those who owned properties or houses would sell them, bring the proceeds from the sales,  
**4:35** and place them in the care and under the authority of the apostles. Then it was distributed to anyone who was in need.  
**4:36** Joseph, whom the apostles nicknamed Barnabas (that is, “one who encourages”), was a Levite from Cyprus.  
**4:37** He owned a field, sold it, brought the money, and placed it in the care and under the authority of the apostles.  

## 5장

**5:1** However, a man named Ananias, along with his wife Sapphira, sold a piece of property.  
**5:2** With his wife’s knowledge, he withheld some of the proceeds from the sale. He brought the rest and placed it in the care and under the authority of the apostles.  
**5:3** Peter asked, “Ananias, how is it that Satan has influenced you to lie to the Holy Spirit by withholding some of the proceeds from the sale of your land?  
**5:4** Wasn’t that property yours to keep? After you sold it, wasn’t the money yours to do with whatever you wanted? What made you think of such a thing? You haven’t lied to other people but to God!”  
**5:5** When Ananias heard these words, he dropped dead. Everyone who heard this conversation was terrified.  
**5:6** Some young men stood up, wrapped up his body, carried him out, and buried him.  
**5:7** About three hours later, his wife entered, but she didn’t know what had happened to her husband.  
**5:8** Peter asked her, “Tell me, did you and your husband receive this price for the field?” She responded, “Yes, that’s the amount.”  
**5:9** He replied, “How could you scheme with each other to challenge the Lord’s Spirit? Look! The feet of those who buried your husband are at the door. They will carry you out too.”  
**5:10** At that very moment, she dropped dead at his feet. When the young men entered and found her dead, they carried her out and buried her with her husband.  
**5:11** Trepidation and dread seized the whole church and all who heard what had happened.  
**5:12** The apostles performed many signs and wonders among the people. They would come together regularly at Solomon’s Porch.  
**5:13** No one from outside the church dared to join them, even though the people spoke highly of them.  
**5:14** Indeed, more and more believers in the Lord, large numbers of both men and women, were added to the church.  
**5:15** As a result, they would even bring the sick out into the main streets and lay them on cots and mats so that at least Peter’s shadow could fall on some of them as he passed by.  
**5:16** Even large numbers of persons from towns around Jerusalem would gather, bringing the sick and those harassed by unclean spirits. Everyone was healed.  
**5:17** The high priest, together with his allies, the Sadducees, was overcome with jealousy.  
**5:18** They seized the apostles and made a public show of putting them in prison.  
**5:19** An angel from the Lord opened the prison doors during the night and led them out. The angel told them,  
**5:20** “Go, take your place in the temple, and tell the people everything about this new life.”  
**5:21** Early in the morning, they went into the temple as they had been told and began to teach. When the high priest and his colleagues gathered, they convened the Jerusalem Council, that is, the full assembly of Israel’s elders. They sent word to the prison to have the apostles brought before them.  
**5:22** However, the guards didn’t find them in the prison. They returned and reported,  
**5:23** “We found the prison locked and well-secured, with guards standing at the doors, but when we opened the doors we found no one inside!”  
**5:24** When they received this news, the captain of the temple guard and the chief priests were baffled and wondered what might be happening.  
**5:25** Just then, someone arrived and announced, “Look! The people you put in prison are standing in the temple and teaching the people!”  
**5:26** Then the captain left with his guards and brought the apostles back. They didn’t use force because they were afraid the people would stone them.  
**5:27** The apostles were brought before the council where the high priest confronted them:  
**5:28** “In no uncertain terms, we demanded that you not teach in this name. And look at you! You have filled Jerusalem with your teaching. And you are determined to hold us responsible for this man’s death.”  
**5:29** Peter and the apostles replied, “We must obey God rather than humans!  
**5:30** The God of our ancestors raised Jesus from the dead—whom you killed by hanging him on a tree.  
**5:31** God has exalted Jesus to his right side as leader and savior so that he could enable Israel to change its heart and life and to find forgiveness for sins.  
**5:32** We are witnesses of such things, as is the Holy Spirit, whom God has given to those who obey him.”  
**5:33** When the council members heard this, they became furious and wanted to kill the apostles.  
**5:34** One council member, a Pharisee and teacher of the Law named Gamaliel, well-respected by all the people, stood up and ordered that the men be taken outside for a few moments.  
**5:35** He said, “Fellow Israelites, consider carefully what you intend to do to these people.  
**5:36** Some time ago, Theudas appeared, claiming to be somebody, and some four hundred men joined him. After he was killed, all of his followers scattered, and nothing came of that.  
**5:37** Afterward, at the time of the census, Judas the Galilean appeared and got some people to follow him in a revolt. He was killed too, and all his followers scattered far and wide.  
**5:38** Here’s my recommendation in this case: Distance yourselves from these men. Let them go! If their plan or activity is of human origin, it will end in ruin.  
**5:39** If it originates with God, you won’t be able to stop them. Instead, you would actually find yourselves fighting God!” The council was convinced by his reasoning.  
**5:40** After calling the apostles back, they had them beaten. They ordered them not to speak in the name of Jesus, then let them go.  
**5:41** The apostles left the council rejoicing because they had been regarded as worthy to suffer disgrace for the sake of the name.  
**5:42** Every day they continued to teach and proclaim the good news that Jesus is the Christ, both in the temple and in houses.  

## 6장

**6:1** About that time, while the number of disciples continued to increase, a complaint arose. Greek-speaking disciples accused the Aramaic-speaking disciples because their widows were being overlooked in the daily food service.  
**6:2** The Twelve called a meeting of all the disciples and said, “It isn’t right for us to set aside proclamation of God’s word in order to serve tables.  
**6:3** Brothers and sisters, carefully choose seven well-respected men from among you. They must be well-respected and endowed by the Spirit with exceptional wisdom. We will put them in charge of this concern.  
**6:4** As for us, we will devote ourselves to prayer and the service of proclaiming the word.”  
**6:5** This proposal pleased the entire community. They selected Stephen, a man endowed by the Holy Spirit with exceptional faith, Philip, Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon, Parmenas, and Nicolaus from Antioch, a convert to Judaism.  
**6:6** The community presented these seven to the apostles, who prayed and laid their hands on them.  
**6:7** God’s word continued to grow. The number of disciples in Jerusalem increased significantly. Even a large group of priests embraced the faith.  
**6:8** Stephen, who stood out among the believers for the way God’s grace was at work in his life and for his exceptional endowment with divine power, was doing great wonders and signs among the people.  
**6:9** Opposition arose from some who belonged to the so-called Synagogue of Former Slaves. Members from Cyrene, Alexandria, Cilicia, and Asia entered into debate with Stephen.  
**6:10** However, they couldn’t resist the wisdom the Spirit gave him as he spoke.  
**6:11** Then they secretly enticed some people to claim, “We heard him insult Moses and God.”  
**6:12** They stirred up the people, the elders, and the legal experts. They caught Stephen, dragged him away, and brought him before the Jerusalem Council.  
**6:13** Before the council, they presented false witnesses who testified, “This man never stops speaking against this holy place and the Law.  
**6:14** In fact, we heard him say that this man Jesus of Nazareth will destroy this place and alter the customary practices Moses gave us.”  
**6:15** Everyone seated in the council stared at Stephen, and they saw that his face was radiant, just like an angel’s.  

## 7장

**7:1** The high priest asked, “Are these accusations true?”  
**7:2** Stephen responded, “Brothers and fathers, listen to me. Our glorious God appeared to our ancestor Abraham while he was still in Mesopotamia, before he settled in Haran.  
**7:3** God told him, ‘Leave your homeland and kin, and go to the land that I will show you.’<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><a name='B:10 12:1'>Gen 12:1  
**7:4** So Abraham left the land of the Chaldeans and settled in Haran. After Abraham’s father died, God had him resettle in this land where you now live.  
**7:5** God didn’t give him an inheritance here, not even a square foot of land. However, God did promise to give the land as his possession to him and to his descendants, even though Abraham had no child.  
**7:6** God put it this way: <i>His descendants will be strangers in a land that belongs to others, who will enslave them and abuse them for four hundred years.</i><sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><a name='B:10 15:13'>Gen 15:13  
**7:7** <i>And I will condemn the nation they serve as slaves,</i> God said, <i>and afterward they will leave</i><sup>①</sup> that land and serve me in this place.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><a name='B:10 15:14'>Gen 15:14  
**7:8** God gave him the covenant confirmed through circumcision. Accordingly, eight days after Isaac’s birth, Abraham circumcised him. Isaac did the same with Jacob, and Jacob with the twelve patriarchs.  
**7:9** “Because the patriarchs were jealous of Joseph, they sold him into slavery in Egypt. God was with him, however,  
**7:10** and rescued him from all his troubles. The grace and wisdom he gave Joseph were recognized by Pharaoh, king of Egypt, who appointed him ruler over Egypt and over his whole palace.  
**7:11** A famine came upon all Egypt and Canaan, and great hardship came with it. Our ancestors had nothing to eat.  
**7:12** When Jacob heard there was grain in Egypt, he sent our ancestors there for the first time.  
**7:13** During their second visit, Joseph told his brothers who he was, and Pharaoh learned about Joseph’s family.  
**7:14** Joseph sent for his father Jacob and all his relatives—seventy-five in all—and invited them to live with him.

**12:2** He had James, John’s brother, killed with a sword.  
**12:3** When he saw that this pleased the Jews, he arrested Peter as well. This happened during the Festival of Unleavened Bread.  
**12:4** He put Peter in prison, handing him over to four squads of soldiers, sixteen in all, who guarded him. He planned to charge him publicly after the Passover.  
**12:5** While Peter was held in prison, the church offered earnest prayer to God for him.  
**12:6** The night before Herod was going to bring Peter’s case forward, Peter was asleep between two soldiers and bound with two chains, with soldiers guarding the prison entrance.  
**12:7** Suddenly an angel from the Lord appeared and a light shone in the prison cell. After nudging Peter on his side to awaken him, the angel raised him up and said, “Quick! Get up!” The chains fell from his wrists.  
**12:8** The angel continued, “Get dressed. Put on your sandals.” Peter did as he was told. The angel said, “Put on your coat and follow me.”  
**12:9** Following the angel, Peter left the prison. However, he didn’t realize the angel had actually done all this. He thought he was seeing a vision.  
**12:10** They passed the first and second guards and came to the iron gate leading to the city. It opened for them by itself. After leaving the prison, they proceeded the length of one street, when abruptly the angel was gone.  
**12:11** At that, Peter came to his senses and remarked, “Now I’m certain that the Lord sent his angel and rescued me from Herod and from everything the Jewish people expected.”  
**12:12** Realizing this, he made his way to Mary’s house. (Mary was John’s mother; he was also known as Mark.) Many believers had gathered there and were praying.  
**12:13** When Peter knocked at the outer gate, a female servant named Rhoda went to answer.  
**12:14** She was so overcome with joy when she recognized Peter’s voice that she didn’t open the gate. Instead, she ran back in and announced that Peter was standing at the gate.  
**12:15** “You’ve lost your mind!” they responded. She stuck by her story with such determination that they began to say, “It must be his guardian angel.”  
**12:16** Meanwhile, Peter remained outside, knocking at the gate. They finally opened the gate and saw him there, and they were astounded.  
**12:17** He gestured with his hand to quiet them down, then recounted how the Lord led him out of prison. He said, “Tell this to James and the brothers and sisters.” Then he left for another place.  
**12:18** The next morning the soldiers were flustered about what had happened to Peter.  
**12:19** Herod called for a thorough search. When Peter didn’t turn up, Herod interrogated the guards and had them executed. Afterward, Herod left Judea in order to spend some time in Caesarea.  
**12:20** Herod had been furious with the people of Tyre and Sidon for some time. They made a pact to approach him together, since their region depended on the king’s realm for its food supply. They persuaded Blastus, the king’s personal attendant, to join their cause, then appealed for an end to hostilities.  
**12:21** On the scheduled day Herod dressed himself in royal attire, seated himself on the throne, and gave a speech to the people.  
**12:22** Those assembled kept shouting, over and over, “This is a god’s voice, not the voice of a mere human!”  
**12:23** Immediately an angel from the Lord struck Herod down, because he didn’t give the honor to God. He was eaten by worms and died.  
**12:24** God’s word continued to grow and increase.

## 16장

**16:2** The brothers and sisters in Lystra and Iconium spoke well of him.

**16:23** When Paul and Silas had been severely beaten, the authorities threw them into prison and ordered the jailer to secure them with great care.  
**16:24** When he received these instructions, he threw them into the innermost cell and secured their feet in stocks.

**16:26** All at once there was such a violent earthquake that it shook the prison’s foundations. The doors flew open and everyone’s chains came loose.  
**16:27** When the jailer awoke and saw the open doors of the prison, he thought the prisoners had escaped, so he drew his sword and was about to kill himself.

**16:34** He brought them into his home and gave them a meal. He was overjoyed because he and everyone in his household had come to believe in God.  
**16:35** The next morning the legal authorities sent the police to the jailer with the order “Release those people.”  
**16:36** So the jailer reported this to Paul, informing him, “The authorities sent word that you both are to be released. You can leave now. Go in peace.”  
**16:37** Paul told the police, “Even though we are Roman citizens, they beat us publicly without first finding us guilty of a crime, and they threw us into prison. And now they want to send us away secretly? No way! They themselves will have to come and escort us out.”  
**16:38** The police reported this to the legal authorities, who were alarmed to learn that Paul and Silas were Roman citizens.  
**16:39** They came and consoled Paul and Silas, escorting them out of prison and begging them to leave the city.  
**16:40** Paul and Silas left the prison and made their way to Lydia’s house where they encouraged the brothers and sisters. Then they left Philippi.  

## 17장

**17:1** Paul and Silas journeyed through Amphipolis and Apollonia, then came to Thessalonica, where there was a Jewish synagogue.  
**17:2** As was Paul’s custom, he entered the synagogue and for three Sabbaths interacted with them on the basis of the scriptures.  
**17:3** Through his interpretation of the scriptures, he demonstrated that the Christ had to suffer and rise from the dead. He declared, “This Jesus whom I proclaim to you is the Christ.”  
**17:4** Some were convinced and joined Paul and Silas, including a larger number of Greek God-worshippers and quite a few prominent women.  
**17:5** But the Jews became jealous and brought along some thugs who were hanging out in the marketplace. They formed a mob and started a riot in the city. They attacked Jason’s house, intending to bring Paul and Silas before the people.  
**17:6** When they didn’t find them, they dragged Jason and some believers before the city officials. They were shouting, “These people who have been disturbing the peace throughout the empire have also come here.  
**17:7** What is more, Jason has welcomed them into his home. Every one of them does what is contrary to Caesar’s decrees by naming someone else as king: Jesus.”  
**17:8** This provoked the crowd and the city officials even more.  
**17:9** After Jason and the others posted bail, they released them.  
**17:10** As soon as it was dark, the brothers and sisters sent Paul and Silas on to Beroea. When they arrived, they went to the Jewish synagogue.  
**17:11** The Beroean Jews were more honorable than those in Thessalonica. This was evident in the great eagerness with which they accepted the word and examined the scriptures each day to see whether Paul and Silas’ teaching was true.  
**17:12** Many came to believe, including a number of reputable Greek women and many Greek men.  
**17:13** The Jews from Thessalonica learned that Paul also proclaimed God’s word in Beroea, so they went there too and were upsetting and disturbing the crowds.  
**17:14** The brothers and sisters sent Paul away to the seacoast at once, but Silas and Timothy remained at Beroea.  
**17:15** Those who escorted Paul led him as far as Athens, then returned with instructions for Silas and Timothy to come to him as quickly as possible.  
**17:16** While Paul waited for them in Athens, he was deeply distressed to find that the city was flooded with idols.

**17:20** You’ve told us some strange things and we want to know what they mean.”  
**17:21** <n><sup>①</sup></n> (They said this because all Athenians as well as the foreigners who live in Athens used to spend their time doing nothing but talking about or listening to the newest thing.)

## 18장

**18:2** There he found a Jew named Aquila, a native of Pontus. He had recently come from Italy with his wife Priscilla because Claudius had ordered all Jews to leave Rome. Paul visited with them.  
**18:3** Because they practiced the same trade, he stayed and worked with them. They all worked with leather.  
**18:4** Every Sabbath he interacted with people in the synagogue, trying to convince both Jews and Greeks.  
**18:5** Once Silas and Timothy arrived from Macedonia, Paul devoted himself fully to the word, testifying to the Jews that Jesus was the Christ.  
**18:6** When they opposed and slandered him, he shook the dust from his clothes in protest and said to them, “You are responsible for your own fates! I’m innocent! From now on I’ll go to the Gentiles!”

**18:27** When he wanted to travel to Achaia, the brothers and sisters encouraged him and wrote to the disciples so they would open their homes to him. Once he arrived, he was of great help to those who had come to believe through grace.

## 19장

**19:5** After they listened to Paul, they were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus.

**19:31** Even some officials of the province of Asia, who were Paul’s friends, sent word to him, urging him not to risk going into the theater.

## 20장

## 21장

## 23장
