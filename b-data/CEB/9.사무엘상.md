# 9.사무엘상 (CEB)



## 1장

**1:1** Now there was a certain man from Ramathaim, a Zuphite<sup>①</sup> from the highlands of Ephraim, whose name was Elkanah. He was from the tribe of Ephraim, and he was the son of Jeroham son of Elihu son of Tohu son of Zuph.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>LXX; MT <i>Ramathaim-zophim</i>  
**1:2** Elkanah had two wives, one named Hannah and the other named Peninnah. Peninnah had children, but Hannah didn’t.  
**1:3** Every year this man would leave his town to worship and sacrifice to the LORD of heavenly forces in Shiloh, where Eli’s two sons Hophni and Phinehas were the LORD’s priests.  
**1:4** Whenever he sacrificed, Elkanah would give parts of the sacrifice to his wife Peninnah and to all her sons and daughters.  
**1:5** But he would give only one part of it to Hannah, though he loved her, because the LORD had kept her from conceiving.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb uncertain; Syr <i>But he would give a double portion to Hannah, because he loved her, though the LORD had kept her from conceiving.</i>  
**1:6** And because the LORD had kept Hannah from conceiving, her rival would make fun of her mercilessly, just to bother her.  
**1:7** So that is what took place year after year. Whenever Hannah went to the Lord’s house, Peninnah would make fun of her. Then she would cry and wouldn’t eat anything.  
**1:8** “Hannah, why are you crying?” her husband Elkanah would say to her. “Why won’t you eat? Why are you<sup>①</sup> so sad? Aren’t I worth more to you than ten sons?”<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>your heart</i>  
**1:9** One time, after eating and drinking in Shiloh, Hannah got up and presented herself before the LORD.<sup>①</sup> (Now Eli the priest was sitting in the chair by the doorpost of the LORD’s temple.)<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>LXX; MT lacks <i>presented herself before the LORD.</i>  
**1:10** Hannah was very upset and couldn’t stop crying as she prayed to the LORD.  
**1:11** Then she made this promise: “LORD of heavenly forces, just look at your servant’s pain and remember me! Don’t forget your servant! Give her a boy! Then I’ll give him to the LORD for his entire life. No razor will ever touch his head.”  
**1:12** As she kept praying before the LORD, Eli watched her mouth.  
**1:13** Now Hannah was praying in her heart; her lips were moving, but her voice was silent, so Eli thought she was drunk.  
**1:14** “How long will you act like a drunk? Sober up!” Eli told her.  
**1:15** “No sir!” Hannah replied. “I’m just a very sad woman. I haven’t had any wine or beer but have been pouring out my heart to the LORD.  
**1:16** Don’t think your servant is some good-for-nothing woman. This whole time I’ve been praying out of my great worry and trouble!”  
**1:17** Eli responded, “Then go in peace. And may the God of Israel give you what you’ve asked from him.”  
**1:18** “Please think well of me, your servant,” Hannah said. Then the woman went on her way, ate some food, and wasn’t sad any longer.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>LXX; MT lacks <i>sad.</i>  
**1:19** They got up early the next morning and worshipped the LORD. Then they went back home to Ramah. Elkanah had sex with his wife Hannah, and the LORD remembered her.  
**1:20** So in the course of time, Hannah conceived and gave birth to a son. She named him Samuel, which means “I asked the LORD for him.”<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Samuel means <i>God has heard</i> but here is connected to the Heb verb <i>to ask.</i>  
**1:21** When Elkanah and all his household went up to make the annual sacrifice and keep his solemn promise,  
**1:22** Hannah didn’t go. “I’ll bring the boy when he is weaned,” she told her husband, “so he can be presented to the LORD and stay there permanently. I will offer him as a nazirite forever.”<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>DSS (4QSam<sup>a</sup>); MT lacks <i>I will offer… forever.</i>  
**1:23** “Do what seems best to you,” said her husband Elkanah. “Stay here until you’ve weaned him. But may the LORD bring to pass what you’ve<sup>①</sup> promised.” So the woman stayed home and nursed her son until she had weaned him.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>LXX, DSS (4QSam<sup>a</sup>); MT <i>he</i>  
**1:24** When he had been weaned and was still very young,<sup>①</sup> Hannah took him, along with a three-year-old bull,<sup>②</sup> an ephah<sup>③</sup> of flour, and a jar of wine, and brought him to the LORD’s house at Shiloh.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>LXX, DSS (4QSam<sup>a</sup>), Syr; MT <i>three bulls</i> <sup>②</sup>An ephah was approximately twenty quarts. <sup>③</sup>Or <i>and the boy was a boy</i>; Heb uncertain  
**1:25** They slaughtered the bull, then brought the boy to Eli.  
**1:26** “Excuse me, sir!” Hannah said. “As surely as you live, sir, I am the woman who stood here next to you, praying to the LORD.  
**1:27** I prayed for this boy, and the LORD gave me what I asked from him.  
**1:28** So now I give this boy back to the LORD. As long as he lives, he is given to the LORD.” Then they worshipped there before the LORD.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Some Heb manuscripts, Syr, Vulg; MT he (Eli?) <i>worshipped</i>; DSS (4QSam<sup>a</sup>) <i>and she</i> (Hannah) <i>left him there and worshipped the LORD</i>.

But now—this is what the LORD declares: I’ll do no such thing! No. I honor those who honor me, and whoever despises me will be cursed.

**2:32** You’ll see trouble in my dwelling place, though all will go well for Israel.<sup>①</sup> But there will never be an old person in your family tree.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb uncertain; LXX and DSS (4QSam<sup>a</sup>) omit <a name='B:90 2:31'>2:31<i>b</i>-32<i>a</i>.

## 3장

**3:1** Now the boy Samuel was serving the LORD under Eli. The LORD’s word was rare at that time, and visions weren’t widely known.  
**3:2** One day Eli, whose eyes had grown so weak he was unable to see, was lying down in his room.  
**3:3** God’s lamp hadn’t gone out yet, and Samuel was lying down in the LORD’s temple, where God’s chest<sup>①</sup> was.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Traditionally <i>ark</i>  
**3:4** The LORD called to Samuel. “I’m here,” he said.  
**3:5** Samuel hurried to Eli and said, “I’m here. You called me?” “I didn’t call you,” Eli replied. “Go lie down.” So he did.  
**3:6** Again the LORD called Samuel, so Samuel got up, went to Eli, and said, “I’m here. You called me?” “I didn’t call, my son,” Eli replied. “Go and lie down.”  
**3:7** <n><sup>①</sup></n> (Now Samuel didn’t yet know the LORD, and the LORD’s word hadn’t yet been revealed to him.)  
**3:8** A third time the LORD called Samuel. He got up, went to Eli, and said, “I’m here. You called me?” Then Eli realized that it was the LORD who was calling the boy.  
**3:9** So Eli said to Samuel, “Go and lie down. If he calls you, say, ‘Speak, LORD. Your servant is listening.’” So Samuel went and lay down where he’d been.  
**3:10** Then the LORD came and stood there, calling just as before, “Samuel, Samuel!” Samuel said, “Speak. Your servant is listening.”  
**3:11** The LORD said to Samuel, “I am about to do something in Israel that will make the ears of all who hear it tingle!  
**3:12** On that day, I will bring to pass against Eli everything I said about his household—every last bit of it!<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>the beginning and the end</i>  
**3:13** I told him that I would punish his family forever because of the wrongdoing he knew about—how his sons were cursing God,<sup>①</sup> but he wouldn’t stop them.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>LXX; MT <i>to themselves</i>, one of several intentional scribal corrections to avoid the phrase <i>cursing God</i>  
**3:14** Because of that I swore about Eli’s household that his family’s wrongdoing will never be reconciled by sacrifice or by offering.”  
**3:15** Samuel lay there until morning, then opened the doors of the LORD’s house. Samuel was afraid to tell the vision to Eli.  
**3:16** But Eli called Samuel, saying: “Samuel, my son!” “I’m here,” Samuel said.  
**3:17** “What did he say to you?” Eli asked. “Don’t hide anything from me. May God deal harshly with you and worse still if you hide from me a single word from everything he said to you.”  
**3:18** So Samuel told him everything and hid nothing from him. “He is the LORD, ” Eli said. “He will do as he pleases.”  
**3:19** So Samuel grew up, and the LORD was with him, not allowing any of his words to fail.  
**3:20** All Israel from Dan to Beer-sheba knew that Samuel was trustworthy as the LORD’s prophet.  
**3:21** The LORD continued to appear at Shiloh because the LORD revealed himself to Samuel at Shiloh through the LORD’s own word.  

## 4장

**4:1** And Samuel’s word went out to all Israel. <i>The Philistines capture God’s chest</i> </pb> In those days the Philistines gathered for war against Israel,<sup>①</sup> so Israel went out to engage the Philistines in war. Israel camped at Ebenezer, while the Philistines camped at Aphek.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>LXX; MT lacks <i>In those days… against Israel</i>.  
**4:2** The Philistines readied themselves to fight Israel. When the battle was joined, Israel was defeated by the Philistines, who killed about four thousand men on the battlefield.  
**4:3** When the troops returned to the camp, Israel’s elders said, “Why did the LORD defeat us today before the Philistines? Let’s bring the chest containing the LORD’s covenant from Shiloh so it can go with us and save us from our enemies’ power.”  
**4:4** So the people sent to Shiloh and brought from there the chest containing the covenant of the LORD of heavenly forces, who sits enthroned on the winged heavenly creatures.<sup>①</sup> Eli’s two sons Hophni and Phinehas were there with the chest containing God’s covenant.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb <i>cherubim</i>  
**4:5** When the chest containing the LORD’s covenant entered the camp, all Israel let out such a loud shout that the ground shook.  
**4:6** When the Philistines heard the sound of that shout, they asked, “What is that loud shouting in the Hebrew camp about?” When they learned that the LORD’s chest had come into the camp,  
**4:7** the Philistines were afraid and said, “A god has come into that camp! We’re doomed,” they said, “because nothing like this has ever happened before.  
**4:8** We’re doomed! Who will deliver us from the grip of these powerful deities? They are the same gods who struck the Egyptians in the desert with every kind of wound.  
**4:9** Pull yourselves together and act like men, Philistines! Otherwise, you’ll serve the Hebrews like they’ve been serving you. Act like men and fight!”  
**4:10** So the Philistines fought. Israel was defeated, and everyone fled to their homes. It was a massive defeat: thirty thousand Israelite foot soldiers fell,  
**4:11** God’s chest was taken, and Eli’s two sons Hophni and Phinehas died.  
**4:12** That very day, a Benjaminite ran from the battle to Shiloh. His clothes were torn, and dirt was on his head.  
**4:13** When he got there, Eli was sitting in a chair beside the road, waiting because he was nervous about God’s chest. The man arrived and gave the news to the city, and the whole city cried out.  
**4:14** Eli heard the sound of the cry and said, “What’s all this noise about?” The man hurriedly went and told Eli the news.  
**4:15** <n><sup>①</sup></n> (Now Eli was 98 years old, and his eyes stared straight ahead, unable to see.)  
**4:16** The man told Eli, “I’m the one who just came from the battle. I fled from the battle today.” “What’s the report, my son?” Eli asked.  
**4:17** The messenger answered, “Israel has fled from the Philistines. The army has suffered a massive defeat. Also, your own two sons Hophni and Phinehas have died, and God’s chest has been taken!”  
**4:18** At the mention of God’s chest, Eli fell backward off the chair beside the gate. His neck broke, and he died because he was an old man and overweight. Eli had judged Israel for forty years.  
**4:19** Now Eli’s daughter-in-law, Phinehas’ wife, was pregnant and about to give birth. When she heard the news that God’s chest had been captured and that her father-in-law and her husband had died, she doubled over and gave birth because her labor pains overwhelmed her.  
**4:20** As she was about to die, the women standing by helping her said, “Don’t be afraid. You’ve given birth to a son!” But she didn’t answer or pay them any attention.  
**4:21** She named the boy Ichabod,<sup>①</sup> saying, “The glory has left Israel,” referring to the capture of God’s chest and the death of her father-in-law and her husband.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Meaning <i>Where is the glory?</i>  
**4:22** “The glory has left Israel because God’s chest has been taken,” she said.  

## 5장

## 6장

**6:3** They replied, “If you are returning the chest of Israel’s God, don’t send it back empty, but be sure to return a guilt offering to him.<sup>①</sup> Then you will be healed, and it will become clear to you why God’s hand hasn’t left you alone.”<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>be sure to return it with a compensation offering</i>.  
**6:4** “What compensation offering should we return to him?” they asked. **6:7** So get a new cart ready along with two nursing cows that have never been yoked before. Harness the cows to the cart, but take any of their calves that are following back home.  
**6:8** Next, take the LORD’s chest and put it in the cart. Set the gold items that you are giving God as a compensation offering in a box next to the chest. Then send it on its way.  
**6:9** Then watch what happens: If the cart goes up the road to its own territory toward Beth-shemesh, then Israel’s God has brought this great disaster on us. If the cart goes another way, then we’ll know that it wasn’t God’s hand that struck us. It happened to us randomly.”

## 29장

**29:1** The Philistines assembled all their forces at Aphek, and the Israelites camped by the spring in Jezreel.  
**29:2** As the Philistine rulers went out marching in units of hundreds and thousands, David and his soldiers were in the rear with Achish.  
**29:3** “Who are these Hebrews?” the Philistine commanders asked. “That’s David,” Achish told them, “the servant of Israel’s King Saul. He’s been with me a year or so now. I haven’t found anything wrong with him from the day he defected until now.”  
**29:4** But the Philistine commanders were angry with Achish. “Send the man home!” they told Achish. “He can go back to the place you gave him, but he won’t go with us into battle. ## 30장

**30:1** Three days later, David and his soldiers reached Ziklag. The Amalekites had raided the arid southern plain and Ziklag. They had attacked Ziklag and burned it down,  
**30:2** taking the women and everyone in the city prisoner, whether young or old.<sup>①</sup> They hadn’t killed anyone but carried them off and went on their way.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>LXX; MT lacks <i>and everyone in the city</i>.

**30:4** David and the troops with him broke into tears and cried until they could cry no more.  
**30:5** David’s two wives had been captured as well: Ahinoam from Jezreel and Abigail, Nabal’s widow from Carmel.  
**30:6** David was in deep trouble because the troops were talking about stoning him. Each of the soldiers was deeply distressed about their sons and daughters. But David found strength in the LORD his God.

**30:10** David and four hundred men continued the pursuit, while two hundred men stayed there, too exhausted to cross the Besor ravine.  
**30:11** They found an Egyptian in the countryside and brought him to David. They gave him bread, and he ate, and they gave him water to drink.

**30:15** “Can you guide me to this raiding party?” David asked him. “Make a pledge to me by God that you won’t kill me or hand me over to my master,” the boy said, “and I will guide you to the raiding party.”

**31:1** When the Philistines attacked the Israelites, the Israelites ran away from the Philistines, and many fell dead on Mount Gilboa.  
**31:2** The Philistines overtook Saul and his sons, and they killed his sons Jonathan, Abinadab, and Malchishua.  
**31:3** The battle was fierce around Saul. When the archers located him, they wounded him badly.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Correction; LXX <i>wounded in the belly</i>

**31:5** When the armor-bearer saw that Saul was dead, he also impaled himself on his sword and died with Saul.  
**31:6** So Saul, his three sons, his armor-bearer, and all his soldiers died together that day.  
**31:7** When the Israelites across the valley and across the Jordan learned that the Israelite army had fled and that Saul and his sons were dead, they abandoned their towns and fled. So the Philistines came and occupied the towns.  
**31:8** The next day, when the Philistines came to strip the dead, they found Saul and his three sons lying dead on Mount Gilboa.  
**31:9** They cut off Saul’s head and stripped off his armor, and then sent word throughout Philistine territory, carrying the good news to their gods’ temples and to their people.  
**31:10** They put Saul’s armor in the temple of Astarte, and hung his body on the wall of Beth-shan.  
**31:11** But when all the people of Jabesh-gilead heard what the Philistines had done to Saul,  
**31:12** the bravest of their men set out, traveled all night long, and took the bodies of Saul and his sons off the wall of Beth-shan. Then they went back to Jabesh, where they burned them.  
**31:13** Then they took their bones and buried them under the tamarisk tree at Jabesh, and they fasted seven days.
