# 21.전도서 (CEB)



## 1장

**1:1** The words of the Teacher of the Assembly,<sup>①</sup> David’s son, king in Jerusalem:<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>Gatherer</i> or <i>Convener</i> or <i>Assembler</i> (Heb <i>Qoheleth);</i> see also <a name='B:250 1:2'>1:2, <a name='B:250 1:12'>12; <a name='B:250 7:27'>7:27; <a name='B:250 12:8'>12:8, <a name='B:250 12:9'>9, <a name='B:250 12:10'>10.  
**1:2** Perfectly pointless,<sup>①</sup> says the Teacher, perfectly pointless.<br>Everything is pointless.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>meaningless</i> or <i>vapor</i> or <i>puff of air</i> (Heb <i>hebel</i>, which often occurs in the book)  
**1:3** What do people gain from all the hard work<br>that they work so hard at under the sun?  
**1:4** A generation goes, and a generation comes,<br>but the earth remains as it always has.  
**1:5** The sun rises, the sun sets;<br>it returns panting to the place where it dawns.  
**1:6** The wind blows to the south,<br>goes around to the north;<br>around and around blows the wind;<br>the wind returns to its rounds again.  
**1:7** All streams flow to the sea,<br>but the sea is never full;<br>to the place where the rivers flow,<br>there they continue to flow.  
**1:8** All words<sup>①</sup> are tiring;<br>no one is able to speak.<br>The eye isn’t satisfied with seeing,<br>neither is the ear filled up by hearing.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>things</i>  
**1:9** Whatever has happened—that’s what will happen again;<br>whatever has occurred—that’s what will occur again.  There’s nothing new under the sun.  
**1:10** People may say about something: “Look at this! It’s new!” But it was already around for ages before us.  
**1:11** There’s no remembrance of things in the past, nor of things to come in the future. Neither will there be any remembrance among those who come along in the future.  
**1:12** I am the Teacher. I was king over Israel in Jerusalem.  
**1:13** I applied my mind to investigate and to explore by wisdom all that happens under heaven. It’s an unhappy obsession that God has given to human beings.  
**1:14** When I observed all that happens under the sun, I realized that everything is pointless, a chasing after wind.  
**1:15** What’s crooked can’t be straightened;<br>what isn’t there can’t be counted.  
**1:16** I said to myself, Look here, I have grown much wiser than any who ruled over Jerusalem before me. My mind has absorbed great wisdom and knowledge.  
**1:17** But when I set my mind to understand wisdom, and also to understand madness and folly, I realized that this too was just wind chasing.  
**1:18** Remember:<br>In much wisdom is much aggravation;<br>the more knowledge, the more pain.  

## 2장

**2:1** I said to myself,<sup>①</sup> Come, I will make you<sup>②</sup> experience pleasure; enjoy what is good! But this too was pointless!<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>in my heart; mind</i> <sup>②</sup>Or <i>the self</i> (or <i>heart; mind</i>)  
**2:2** Merriment, I thought, is madness; pleasure, of no use at all.  
**2:3** I tried cheering myself with wine and by embracing folly—with wisdom still guiding me—until I might see what is really worth doing in the few days that human beings have under heaven.  
**2:4** I took on great projects: I built houses for myself, planted vineyards for myself.  
**2:5** I made gardens and parks for myself, planting every kind of fruit tree in them.  
**2:6** I made reservoirs for myself to water my lush groves.  
**2:7** I acquired male servants and female servants; I even had slaves born in my house. I also had great herds of cattle and sheep, more than any who preceded me in Jerusalem.  
**2:8** I amassed silver and gold for myself, the treasures of kings and provinces. I acquired male and female singers for myself, along with every human luxury, treasure chests galore!<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>many secondary wives</i>  
**2:9** So I became far greater than all who preceded me in Jerusalem. Moreover, my wisdom stood by me.  
**2:10** I refrained from nothing that my eyes desired. I refused my heart no pleasure. Indeed, my heart found pleasure from the results of my hard work; that was the reward from all my hard work.  
**2:11** But when I surveyed all that my hands had done, and what I had worked so hard to achieve, I realized that it was pointless—a chasing after wind. Nothing is to be gained under the sun.  
**2:12** My reflections then turned to wisdom, madness, and folly. What can the king’s heir do but what has already been done?  
**2:13** I saw that wisdom is more beneficial than folly, as light is more beneficial than darkness.  
**2:14** The wise have eyes in their head,<br>but fools walk around in darkness.  But I also realized that the same fate happens to both of them.  
**2:15** So I thought to myself, What happens to the fool will also happen to me. So why have I been so very wise? I said to myself, This too is pointless.  
**2:16** There is no eternal memory of the wise any more than the foolish,<sup>①</sup> because everyone is forgotten before long. How can the wise die just like the fool?<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>The wise and the foolish alike are never remembered.</i>  
**2:17** So I hated life, because the things that happen under the sun were troublesome to me. Definitely, everything is pointless—just wind chasing.  
**2:18** I hated the things I worked so hard for here under the sun, because I will have to leave them to someone who comes after me.  
**2:19** And who knows whether that one will be wise or foolish? Either way, that person will have control over the results of all my hard work and wisdom here under the sun. That too is pointless.  
**2:20** I then gave myself up to despair, as I thought about all my laborious hard work under the sun,  
**2:21** because sometimes those who have worked hard with wisdom, knowledge, and skill must leave the results of their hard work as a possession to those who haven’t worked hard for it. This too is pointless—it’s a terrible wrong.  
**2:22** I mean, What do people get for all their hard work and struggles under the sun?  
**2:23** All their days are pain, and their work is aggravation; even at night, their hearts don’t find rest. This too is pointless.  
**2:24** There’s nothing better for human beings than to eat, drink, and experience pleasure in their hard work. I also saw that this is from God’s hand—  
**2:25** Who can eat and find enjoyment otherwise?—  
**2:26** because God gives wisdom, knowledge, and joy to those who please God. But to those who are offensive,<sup>①</sup> God gives the task of hoarding and accumulating, but only so as to give it all to those who do please God. This too is pointless and a chasing after wind.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>to those who sin</i>  

## 3장

**3:1** There’s a season for everything<br>and a time for every matter under the heavens:  
**3:2** a time for giving birth and a time for dying,<br>a time for planting and a time for uprooting what was planted,  
**3:3** a time for killing and a time for healing,<br>a time for tearing down and a time for building up,  
**3:4** a time for crying and a time for laughing,<br>a time for mourning and a time for dancing,  
**3:5** a time for throwing stones and a time for gathering stones,<br>a time for embracing and a time for avoiding embraces,  
**3:6** a time for searching and a time for losing,<br>a time for keeping and a time for throwing away,  
**3:7** a time for tearing and a time for repairing,<br>a time for keeping silent and a time for speaking,  
**3:8** a time for loving and a time for hating,<br>a time for war and a time for peace.  
**3:9** What do workers gain from all their hard work?  
**3:10** I have observed the task that God has given human beings.  
**3:11** God has made everything fitting in its time, but has also placed eternity in their hearts, without enabling them to discover what God has done from beginning to end.  
**3:12** I know that there’s nothing better for them but to enjoy themselves and do what’s good while they live.  
**3:13** Moreover, this is the gift of God: that all people should eat, drink, and enjoy the results of their hard work.  
**3:14** I know that whatever God does will last forever; it’s impossible to add to it or take away from it. God has done this so that people are reverent before him.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>to inspire awe before the divine</i>  
**3:15** Whatever happens has already happened, and whatever will happen has already happened before. And God looks after what is driven away.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>God seeks out what is pursued,</i> or <i>God seeks what has gone by,</i> or <i>God seeks the pursued;</i> Heb uncertain  
**3:16** I saw something else under the sun: in the place of justice, there was wickedness; and in the place of what was right, there was wickedness again!  
**3:17** I thought to myself, God will judge both righteous and wicked people, because there’s a time for every matter and every deed.  
**3:18** I also thought, Where human beings are concerned, God tests them to show them that they are but animals  
**3:19** because human beings and animals share the same fate. One dies just like the other—both have the same life-breath. Humans are no better off than animals because everything is pointless.  
**3:20** All go to the same place:<br>all are from the dust;<br>all return to the dust.  
**3:21** Who knows if a human being’s life-breath rises upward while an animal’s life-breath descends into the earth?  
**3:22** So I perceived that there was nothing better for human beings but to enjoy what they do because that’s what they’re allotted in life. Who, really, is able to see what will happen in the future?  

## 4장

**4:1** When I next observed all the oppressions that take place under the sun, I saw the tears of the oppressed—and they have no one to comfort them. Their oppressors wield power—but they have no one to comfort them.  
**4:2** So I declare that the dead, who have already died, are more fortunate than the living, who are still alive.  
**4:3** But happier than both are those who have never existed, who haven’t witnessed the terrible things that happen under the sun.  
**4:4** I also observed that people work hard and become good at what they do only out of mutual envy. This too is pointless, just wind chasing.  
**4:5** Fools fold their hands and eat their own flesh.  
**4:6** But better is resting with one handful<br>than working hard for two fistfuls and chasing after wind.  
**4:7** Next, I saw under the sun something else that was pointless:  
**4:8** There are people who are utterly alone, with no companions, not even a child or a sibling. Yet they work hard without end, never satisfied with their wealth. So for whom am I working so hard and depriving myself of enjoyment? This too is pointless and a terrible obsession.  
**4:9** Two are better than one because they have a good return for their hard work.  
**4:10** If either should fall, one can pick up the other. But how miserable are those who fall and don’t have a companion to help them up!  
**4:11** Also, if two lie down together, they can stay warm. But how can anyone stay warm alone?  
**4:12** Also, one can be overpowered, but two together can put up resistance. A three-ply cord doesn’t easily snap.  
**4:13** A poor but wise youth is better than an old and foolish king, who no longer listens to advice.  
**4:14** He emerged from prison to become king, even though during his rule a poor child<sup>①</sup> is born.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Possibly the youth of <a name='B:250 4:13'>4:13; or <i>He emerged from prison to become king, even though he was born poor in the kingdom.</i>  
**4:15** I saw all who live and walk under the sun following the next youth who would rise to take his place.  
**4:16** There was no counting the number of people he ruled, but those who came later aren’t happy with him. This too is pointless and a chasing after wind.  

## 5장

**5:1** Watch<sup>①</sup> your steps when you go to God’s house. It’s more acceptable to listen than to offer the fools’ sacrifice—they have no idea that they’re acting wrongly.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><a name='B:250 4:17'>4:17 in Heb  
**5:2** Don’t<sup>①</sup> be quick with your mouth or say anything hastily before God, because God is in heaven, but you are on earth. Therefore, let your words be few.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><a name='B:250 5:1'>5:1 in Heb  
**5:3** Remember:<br>Dreams come with many cares,<br>and the voice of fools with many words.  
**5:4** When you make a promise to God, fulfill it without delay because God has no pleasure in fools. Fulfill what you promise.  
**5:5** Better not to make a promise than to make a promise without fulfilling it.  
**5:6** Don’t let your mouth make a sinner of you, and don’t say to the messenger: “It was a mistake!” Otherwise, God may become angry at such talk and destroy what you have accomplished.  
**5:7** Remember:<br>When dreams multiply,<br>so do pointless thoughts and excessive speech.<br>Therefore, fear God.  
**5:8** If you witness the poor being oppressed or the violation of what is just and right in some territory, don’t be surprised because a high official watches over another, and yet others stand over them.  
**5:9** But the land’s yield should be for everyone if the field is cultivated.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Correction; Heb uncertain; or <i>The land's advantage in everything is this: a king for a plowed field.</i>  
**5:10** The money lover isn’t satisfied with money; neither is the lover of wealth satisfied with income. This too is pointless.  
**5:11** When good things flow, so do those who consume them. But what do owners benefit from such goods, except to feast their eyes on them?  
**5:12** Sweet is the worker’s sleep, whether there’s a lot or little to eat; but the excess of the wealthy won’t let them sleep.  
**5:13** I have seen a sickening tragedy under the sun: people hoard their wealth to their own detriment.  
**5:14** Then that wealth is lost in a bad business venture so that when they have children, they are left with nothing.  
**5:15** Just as they came from their mother’s womb naked, naked they’ll return, ending up just like they started. All their hard work produces nothing—nothing they can take with them.  
**5:16** This too is a sickening tragedy: they must pass on just as they arrived. What then do they gain from working so hard for wind?  
**5:17** What’s more, they constantly eat in darkness, with much aggravation, grief, and anger.  
**5:18** This is the one good thing I’ve seen: it’s appropriate for people to eat, drink, and find enjoyment in all their hard work under the sun during the brief lifetime that God gives them because that’s their lot in life.  
**5:19** Also, whenever God gives people wealth and riches and enables them to enjoy it, to accept their place in the world<sup>①</sup> and to find pleasure in their hard work—all this is God’s gift.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>portion in life,</i> as in <a name='B:250 5:18'>5:18  
**5:20** Indeed, people shouldn’t brood too much over the days of their lives because God gives an answer in their hearts’ joy.  

## 6장

**6:1** I saw a tragedy under the sun, and it weighs heavily upon humanity.  
**6:2** God may give some people plenty of wealth, riches, and glory so that they lack nothing they desire. But God doesn’t enable them to enjoy it; instead, a stranger enjoys it. This is pointless and a sickening tragedy.  
**6:3** Some people may have one hundred children and live a long life. But no matter how long they live, if they aren’t content with life’s good things, I say that even a stillborn child with no grave is better off than they are.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Correction; Heb puts the lack of burial site with those who do not enjoy life's good things.  
**6:4** Because that child arrives pointlessly, then passes away in darkness. Darkness covers its name.  
**6:5** It hasn’t seen the sun or experienced anything. But it has more peace than those  
**6:6** who live a thousand years twice over but don’t enjoy life’s good things. Isn’t everyone heading to the same destination?  
**6:7** All the hard work of humans is for the mouth, but the appetite is never full.  
**6:8** What advantage do the wise have over the foolish? Or what do the poor gain by knowing how to conduct themselves before the living?  
**6:9** It’s better to enjoy what’s at hand than to have an insatiable appetite. This too is pointless, just wind chasing.  
**6:10** Whatever happens has already been designated, and human beings are fully known. They can’t contend with the one who is stronger than they are.  
**6:11** Because the more words increase, the more everything is pointless. What do people gain by it?  
**6:12** Because who knows what’s good for human beings during life, during their brief pointless life, which will pass away like a shadow? Who can say what the future holds for people under the sun?  

## 7장

**7:1** A good name is better than fine oil,<br>and the day of death better than the birthday.  
**7:2** It is better to go to a house in mourning<br>than to a house party,<br>because that is everyone’s destiny;<br>and the living should take it to heart.  
**7:3** Aggravation is better than merriment<br>because a sad face may lead to a glad heart.  
**7:4** The wise heart is in the house that mourns,<br>but the foolish heart is in the house that rejoices.  
**7:5** It is better to obey the reprimand of the wise<br>than to listen to the song of fools,  
**7:6** because the fool’s merriment<br>is like nettles crackling under a kettle.<br>That too is pointless.  
**7:7** Oppression turns the wise into fools;<br>a bribe corrupts the heart.  
**7:8** The end of something is better than its beginning.<br>Patience is better than arrogance.  
**7:9** Don’t be too quick to get angry<br>because anger lives in the fool’s heart.  
**7:10** Don’t ask, “How is it that the former days were better than these?”<br>because it isn’t wise to ask this.  
**7:11** Wisdom is as good as<sup>①</sup> an inheritance—<br>an advantage for those who see the sun.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>Wisdom is good with</i>  
**7:12** Wisdom’s protection is like the protection of money;<br>the advantage of knowledge is that wisdom preserves the lives of its possessors.  
**7:13** Consider God’s work! Who can straighten what God has made crooked?  
**7:14** When times are good, enjoy the good; when times are bad, consider: God has made the former as well as the latter so that people can’t discover anything that will come to be after them.  
**7:15** I have seen everything in my pointless lifetime: the righteous person may die in spite of their righteousness; then again, the wicked may live long in spite of their wickedness.  
**7:16** Don’t be too righteous or too wise, or you may be dumbfounded.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>destroyed</i>  
**7:17** Don’t be too wicked and don’t be a fool, or you may die before your time.  
**7:18** It’s good that you take hold of one of these without letting go of the other because the one who fears God will go forth with both.  
**7:19** Wisdom makes a wise person stronger than ten rulers who are in a city.  
**7:20** Remember: there’s no one on earth so righteous as to do good only and never make a mistake.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>and never sin</i>  
**7:21** Don’t worry about all the things people say, so you don’t hear your servant cursing you.  
**7:22** After all, you know that you’ve often cursed others yourself!  
**7:23** I tested all of this by wisdom. I thought, I will be wise, but it eluded me.  
**7:24** All that happens is elusive and utterly unfathomable. Who can grasp it?  
**7:25** I turned my mind to know, to investigate, and to seek wisdom, along with an account of things, to know that wickedness is foolishness and folly is madness.  
**7:26** I found one woman more bitter than death: she who is a trap, her heart a snare, her hands shackles. Anyone who pleases God escapes her, but a sinner is trapped by her.  
**7:27** See, this is what I found, says the Teacher, examining one matter after another to account for things.  
**7:28** But there’s something that I constantly searched for but couldn’t find: I found one man among a thousand, but I couldn’t find a woman among any of these.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb uncertain  
**7:29** See, this alone I found: God made human beings straightforward, but they search for many complications.  

## 8장

**8:1** Who is wise? And who knows the meaning of anything?<br>A person’s wisdom brightens the expression;<br>it changes the hardness of someone’s face.  
**8:2** Keep<sup>①</sup> the king’s command<br>as you would keep a solemn pledge.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Correction; Heb <i>I (say?) keep</i>  
**8:3** Don’t be dismayed; leave his presence.<br>Don’t linger in a harmful situation<br>because he can do whatever he wants!  
**8:4** Because the king’s word has authority,<br>no one can say to him, “What are you doing?”  
**8:5** Whoever keeps a command will meet no harm, and the wise heart knows the right time and the right way  
**8:6** because there’s a right time and right way for every matter. But human misfortunes are overwhelming  
**8:7** because no one knows what will happen, and no one can say when something might happen.  
**8:8** No one has control over the life-breath,<sup>①</sup> to retain it, and there’s no control over the day of death. There’s no release from war, and wickedness won’t deliver those who practice it.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>wind</i>  
**8:9** I observed all of this as I paid attention to all that happens under the sun. Sometimes people exercise power over each other to their detriment.  
**8:10** Then I saw the wicked brought to their graves, with people processing from a holy place,<sup>①</sup> while those who had lived honestly were neglected in the city. This too is pointless.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>temple</i>  
**8:11** The condemnation for wicked acts isn’t carried out quickly; that’s why people dare to do evil.  
**8:12** Wrongdoers may commit a hundred crimes but still live long lives. But I also know that it will go well for those who fear God, for those who are reverent before God.  
**8:13** But it will not go well for the wicked; they won’t live long at all because they aren’t reverent before God.  
**8:14** Here’s another thing that happens on earth that is pointless: the righteous get what the wicked deserve, and the wicked get what the righteous deserve. I say that this too is pointless.  
**8:15** So I commend enjoyment because there’s nothing better for people to do under the sun but to eat, drink, and be glad. This is what will accompany them in their hard work, during the lifetime that God gives under the sun.  
**8:16** Then I set my mind to know wisdom and to observe the business that happens on earth, even going without sleep day and night  
**8:17** I observed all the work of God—that no one can grasp what happens under the sun. Those who strive to know can’t grasp it. Even the wise who are set on knowing are unable to grasp it.  

## 9장

**9:1** So I considered all of this carefully, examining all of it: The righteous and the wise and their deeds are in God’s hand, along with both love and hate. People don’t know anything that’s ahead of them.  
**9:2** Everything is the same for everyone. The same fate awaits the righteous and the wicked, the good and the bad,<sup>①</sup> the pure and the impure, those who sacrifice and those who don’t sacrifice. The good person is like the wrongdoer; the same holds for those who make solemn pledges and those who are afraid to swear.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>LXX  
**9:3** This is the sad thing about all that happens under the sun: the same fate awaits everyone. Moreover, the human heart is full of evil; people’s minds are full of madness while they are alive, and afterward they die.  
**9:4** Whoever is among the living can be certain about this. A living dog is definitely better off than a dead lion,  
**9:5** because the living know that they will die. But the dead know nothing at all. There is no more reward for them; even the memory of them is lost.  
**9:6** Their love and their hate, as well as their zeal, are already long gone. They will never again have a stake in all that happens under the sun.  
**9:7** Go, eat your food joyfully and drink your wine happily because God has already accepted what you do.  
**9:8** Let your garments always be white; don’t run short of oil for your head.  
**9:9** Enjoy life with your dearly loved spouse all the days of your pointless life that God<sup>①</sup> gives you under the sun—all the days of your pointless life!<sup>②</sup>—because that’s your part to play<sup>③</sup> in this life and in your hard work under the sun.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>he</i> or <i>that are given you</i> <sup>②</sup>This phrase is missing in some LXX sources, Syr, and Tg. <sup>③</sup>Or <i>portion</i>  
**9:10** Whatever you are capable of doing, do with all your might because there’s no work, thought, knowledge, or wisdom in the grave,<sup>①</sup> which is where you are headed.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>underworld;</i> Heb <i>Sheol</i>  
**9:11** I also observed under the sun that the race doesn’t always go to the swift, nor the battle to the mighty, nor food to the wise, nor wealth to the intelligent, nor favor to the knowledgeable, because accidents can happen to anyone.  
**9:12** People most definitely don’t know when their time will come. Like fish tragically caught in a net or like birds trapped in a snare, so are human beings caught in a time of tragedy that suddenly falls to them.  
**9:13** I also observed the following example of wisdom under the sun—it impressed me greatly:  
**9:14** There was a small town with only a few residents. A mighty king came against it, surrounded it, and waged a terrible war against it.  
**9:15** Now there lived in that town a poor but wise man who saved everyone by his wisdom. But no one remembered that poor man.  
**9:16** So I thought, Wisdom is better than might, but the wisdom of commoners is despised and their words aren’t heeded.  
**9:17** The calm words of the wise are better heeded than the racket caused by a ruler among fools.  
**9:18** Wisdom is better than weapons of war, but one incompetent person destroys much good.  

## 10장

**10:1** As dead flies spoil the perfumer’s oil,<br>so a little folly outweighs wisdom and honor.  
**10:2** The mind of the wise tends toward the right,<br>but the mind of the fool toward the left.  
**10:3** Fools lack all sense even when they walk down the street;<br>they show everyone that they are fools.  
**10:4** If a ruler’s temper rises against you,<br>don’t leave your post, because calmness alleviates great offenses.  
**10:5** There’s an evil that I have seen under the sun: the kind of mistake that comes from people in power.  
**10:6** Fools are appointed to high posts, while the rich sit in lowly positions.  
**10:7** I have seen slaves on horseback, while princes walk on foot like slaves.  
**10:8** Whoever digs a pit may fall into it,<br>and whoever breaks through a wall<br>may be bitten by a snake.  
**10:9** Whoever quarries stones may be injured by them;<br>whoever splits logs<br>may be endangered by them.  
**10:10** If an ax is dull<br>and one doesn’t sharpen it first,<br>then one must exert more force.<br>It’s profitable to be skillful and wise.  
**10:11** If a snake bites before it’s charmed,<br>then there’s no profit<br>for the snake charmer.  
**10:12** Words from a wise person’s mouth are beneficial, but fools are devoured by their own lips.  
**10:13** Fools start out talking foolishness and end up speaking awful nonsense.  
**10:14** Fools talk too much! No one knows what will happen; no one can say what will happen in the future.  
**10:15** The hard work of fools tires them out because they don’t even know the way to town!  
**10:16** Too bad for you, land,<br>whose king is a boy<br>and whose princes feast in the morning.  
**10:17** Happy is the land<br>whose king is dignified<br>and whose princes feast at the right time for energy,<br>not for drunkenness.  
**10:18** Through laziness, the roof sags;<br>through idle hands, the house leaks.  
**10:19** Feasts are made for laughter,<br>wine cheers the living,<br>and money answers everything.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>money is everyone's answer.</i>  
**10:20** Don’t curse a king even in private; don’t curse the rich in your bedroom, because a bird could carry your voice; some winged creature could report what you said!  

## 11장

**11:1** Send your bread out on the water because, in the course of time, you may find it again.  
**11:2** Give a portion to seven people, even to eight: you don’t know what disaster may come upon the land.  
**11:3** If clouds fill up, they will empty out rain on the earth. If a tree falls, whether to the south or to the north, wherever it falls, there it will lie.  
**11:4** Those who watch the wind blow will never sow, and those who observe the clouds will never reap.  
**11:5** Just as you don’t understand what the life-breath does in the fetus<sup>①</sup> inside a pregnant woman’s womb, so you can’t understand the work of God, who makes everything happen.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Correction with Tg; MT <i>like the bones</i>  
**11:6** Scatter your seed in the morning, and in the evening don’t be idle because you don’t know which will succeed, this one or that, or whether both will be equally good.  
**11:7** Sweet is the light, and it’s pleasant for the eyes to see the sun.  
**11:8** Even those who live many years should take pleasure in them all. But they should be mindful that there will also be many dark days. Everything that happens is pointless.  
**11:9** Rejoice, young person, while you are young! Your heart should make you happy in your prime. Follow your heart’s inclinations and whatever your eyes see, but know this: God will call you to account for all of these things.  
**11:10** Remove anxiety from your heart, banish pain from your body, because youth and the dawn of life are pointless too.  

## 12장

**12:1** Remember your creator in your prime,<br>before the days of trouble arrive,<br>and those years, about which you’ll say, “I take no pleasure in these”—  
**12:2** before the sun and the light grow dark, the moon and the stars too,<br>before the clouds return after the rain;  
**12:3** on the day when the housekeepers tremble and the strong men stoop;<br>when the women who grind stop working because they’re so few,<br>and those who look through the windows grow dim;  
**12:4** when the doors to the street are shut,<br>when the sound of the mill fades,<br>the sound of the bird rises,<br>and all the singers come down low;  
**12:5** when people are afraid of things above<br>and of terrors along the way;<br>when the almond tree blanches, the locust droops,<br>and the caper-berry comes to nothing;<sup>①</sup><br>when the human goes to the eternal abode,<br>with mourners all around in the street;<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb uncertain  
**12:6** before the silver cord snaps and the gold bowl shatters;<br>the jar is broken at the spring and the wheel is crushed at the pit;  
**12:7** before dust returns to the earth as it was before<br>and the life-breath returns to God who gave it.  
**12:8** Perfectly pointless, says the Teacher, everything is pointless.  
**12:9** Additionally: Because the Teacher was wise, he constantly taught the people knowledge. He listened and investigated. He composed many proverbs.  
**12:10** The Teacher searched for pleasing words, and he wrote truthful words honestly.  
**12:11** The words of the wise are like iron-tipped prods;<br>the collected sayings of the masters<br>are like nails fixed firmly by a shepherd.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>fixed by one shepherd</i>  
**12:12** Be careful, my child, of anything beyond them!  There’s no end to the excessive production of scrolls. Studying too much wearies the body.  
**12:13** So this is the end of the matter; all has been heard. Worship God and keep God’s commandments because this is what everyone must do.  
**12:14** God will definitely bring every deed to judgment, including every hidden thing, whether good or bad.
