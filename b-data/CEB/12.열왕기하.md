# 12.열왕기하 (CEB)



## 1장

**1:1** After Ahab died, Moab rebelled against Israel.  
**1:2** Ahaziah fell out the window of his second-story room in Samaria and was hurt. He sent messengers, telling them, “Go to Ekron’s god Baal-zebub, and ask if I will recover from this injury.”  
**1:3** But the LORD’s messenger said to Elijah from Tishbe, “Go, intercept the messengers of Samaria’s king, and ask them, ‘Is it because there’s no God in Israel that you are going to question Ekron’s god Baal-zebub?  
**1:4** This is what the LORD says: You will never get out of the bed you are lying in; you will die for sure!’” So Elijah set off.  
**1:5** The messengers returned to Ahaziah. He said to them, “Why have you come back?”  
**1:6** They said to him, “A man met us and said, ‘Go back to the king who sent you. Say to him, This is what the LORD says: Is it because there’s no God in Israel that you’ve come to question Ekron’s god Baal-zebub? Because of this, you will never get out of the bed you are lying in; you will die for sure!’”  
**1:7** Ahaziah said to them, “Describe the man who met you and said these things.”  
**1:8** They said to him, “He wore clothes made of hair<sup>①</sup> with a leather belt around his waist.” Ahaziah said, “That was Elijah from Tishbe.”<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>He was a hairy man</i>.  
**1:9** So Ahaziah sent out a commander with fifty soldiers. The commander met up with Elijah while he was sitting on a hilltop. The commander said, “Man of God, the king says, ‘Come down!’”  
**1:10** Elijah replied to the commander of the fifty soldiers, “If I really am a man of God, may fire come down from the sky and burn up you and your fifty soldiers.” Then fire came down from the sky and burned up the commander and his fifty soldiers.  
**1:11** Ahaziah then sent another commander with fifty soldiers. The commander said to Elijah, “Man of God, this is what the king says: ‘Hurry and come down!’”  
**1:12** Elijah said to them, “If I really am a man of God, may fire come down from the sky and burn up you and your fifty soldiers.” Then God’s fire came down from the sky and burned up the commander and his fifty soldiers.  
**1:13** For a third time Ahaziah sent a commander with fifty soldiers. So the third commander arrived. He kneeled before Elijah and begged him, “Man of God! Please have some regard for my life and the lives of these fifty soldiers who are your servants.  
**1:14** Look, fire came from the sky and burned up the two earlier commanders and their troops of fifty soldiers. Please have regard for my life.”  
**1:15** Then the LORD’s messenger said to Elijah, “Go down with him. Don’t be afraid of him.” So Elijah set out to go with him to the king.  
**1:16** Elijah said to the king: “This is what the LORD says: Why did you send messengers to question Ekron’s god Baal-zebub? Is there no God in Israel whose word you could seek? Because of this, you won’t ever get out of the bed you are lying in; you’ll die for sure!”  
**1:17** So Ahaziah died in agreement with the LORD’s word that Elijah had spoken. Because Ahaziah had no son, Joram<sup>①</sup> became king after him in the second year of Judah’s King Jehoram, who was Jehoshaphat’s son.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb <i>Jehoram</i>; the king’s name is variously spelled in either long <i>Jehoram</i> or short <i>Joram</i> form.  
**1:18** The rest of Ahaziah’s deeds, aren’t they written in the official records of Israel’s kings?  

## 2장

**2:1** Now the LORD was going to take Elijah up to heaven in a windstorm, and Elijah and Elisha were leaving Gilgal.  
**2:2** Elijah said to Elisha, “Stay here, because the LORD has sent me to Bethel.” But Elisha said, “As the LORD lives and as you live, I won’t leave you.” So they went down to Bethel.  
**2:3** The group of prophets from Bethel came out to Elisha. These prophets said to Elisha, “Do you know that the LORD is going to take your master away from you today?” Elisha said, “Yes, I know. Don’t talk about it!”  
**2:4** Elijah said, “Elisha, stay here, because the LORD has sent me to Jericho.” But Elisha said, “As the LORD lives and as you live, I won’t leave you.” So they went to Jericho.  
**2:5** The group of prophets from Jericho approached Elisha and said to him, “Do you know that the LORD is going to take your master away from you today?” He said, “Yes, I know. Don’t talk about it!”  
**2:6** Elijah said to Elisha, “Stay here, because the LORD has sent me to the Jordan.” But Elisha said, “As the LORD lives and as you live, I won’t leave you.” So both of them went on together.  
**2:7** Fifty members from the group of prophets also went along, but they stood at a distance. Both Elijah and Elisha stood beside the Jordan River.  
**2:8** Elijah then took his coat, rolled it up, and hit the water. Then the water was divided in two! Both of them crossed over on dry ground.  
**2:9** When they had crossed, Elijah said to Elisha, “What do you want me to do for you before I’m taken away from you?” Elisha said, “Let me have twice your spirit.”  
**2:10** Elijah said, “You’ve made a difficult request. If you can see me when I’m taken from you, then it will be yours. If you don’t see me, it won’t happen.”  
**2:11** They were walking along, talking, when suddenly a fiery chariot and fiery horses appeared and separated the two of them. Then Elijah went to heaven in a windstorm.  
**2:12** Elisha was watching, and he cried out, “Oh, my father, my father! Israel’s chariots and its riders!” When he could no longer see him, Elisha took hold of his clothes and ripped them in two.  
**2:13** Then Elisha picked up the coat that had fallen from Elijah. He went back and stood beside the banks of the Jordan River.  
**2:14** He took the coat that had fallen from Elijah and hit the water. He said, “Where is the LORD, Elijah’s God?” And when he hit the water, it divided in two! Then Elisha crossed over.  
**2:15** The group of prophets from Jericho saw him from a distance. They said, “Elijah’s spirit has settled on Elisha!” So they came out to meet him, bowing down before him.  
**2:16** “Look,” they told him, “there are fifty strong men among us, your servants. Please let them go and search for your master. Perhaps the LORD’s spirit has picked him up and put him down on some mountain or in some valley.” Elisha said, “Don’t send them.”  
**2:17** They insisted until he became embarrassed and said, “Okay, send them.” So they sent fifty men who searched for three days. But they couldn’t find Elijah.  
**2:18** When these men returned to Elisha, who was staying in Jericho, he said to them, “Didn’t I tell you not to go?”  
**2:19** The citizens said to Elisha, “As you can see, sir, this city is in a good location, but the water is bad, and the land causes miscarriages.”  
**2:20** He said, “Bring me a new bowl, and put some salt in it.” They did so.  
**2:21** Elisha then went out and threw salt into the spring. He said, “This is what the LORD has said: I have purified this water. It will no longer cause death and miscarriage.”  
**2:22** The water has stayed pure right up to this very day, in agreement with the word that Elisha spoke.  
**2:23** Elisha went up from there to Bethel. As he was going up the road, some young people came out of the city. They mocked him: “Get going, Baldy! Get going, Baldy!”  
**2:24** Turning around, Elisha looked at them and cursed them in the LORD’s name. Then two bears came out of the woods and mangled forty-two of the youths.  
**2:25** From there Elisha went to Mount Carmel and then back to Samaria.  

## 3장

**3:1** Joram,<sup>①</sup> Ahab’s son, became king of Israel in Samaria in the eighteenth year of Jehoshaphat, Judah’s king. He ruled for twelve years.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb <i>Jehoram</i> (also in <a name='B:120 3:6'>3:6); the king’s name is variously spelled in either long <i>Jehoram</i> or short <i>Joram</i> form.  
**3:2** He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes, but he wasn’t as bad as his father and mother. He removed the sacred pillar of Baal that his father had made.  
**3:3** But he nevertheless clung to the sins that Jeroboam, Nebat’s son, had caused Israel to commit. He didn’t deviate from them.  
**3:4** Now Moab’s King Mesha kept sheep. He would pay Israel’s king one hundred thousand lambs and the wool from one hundred thousand rams.  
**3:5** But when Ahab died, Moab’s king rebelled against Israel’s king.  
**3:6** So King Joram set out from Samaria at once. He prepared all Israel for war.  
**3:7** He sent word to Judah’s King Jehoshaphat, “Moab’s king has rebelled against me. Will you go with me to fight against Moab?” Jehoshaphat responded, “Yes, I’ll go. We’ll fight as one: you and I, our troops and our horses.”  
**3:8** “Which road should we take?” Joram asked. Jehoshaphat responded, “The road that goes through the Edomite wilderness.”  
**3:9** So Israel’s and Judah’s kings set out with the king of Edom. They marched around for seven days until there was no water left for the army or for the animals with them.  
**3:10** Israel’s king said, “This is terrible! Has the LORD brought us three kings together only to hand us over to Moab?”  
**3:11** Jehoshaphat said, “Isn’t there any prophet of the LORD around, so we could question the LORD through him?” One of the servants of Israel’s king answered, “Elisha, Shaphat’s son, is here. He used to pour water on Elijah’s hands.”  
**3:12** Jehoshaphat said, “He has the LORD’s word!” So Israel’s king and Jehoshaphat and Edom’s king went down to see Elisha.  
**3:13** Elisha said to Israel’s king, “What do we have to do with each other? Go to your father’s or mother’s prophets.” Then Israel’s king said to him, “Don’t say that, because it is the LORD who has brought us three kings together—but only to hand us over to Moab!”  
**3:14** Elisha said, “I swear by the life of the LORD of heavenly forces, the one I stand before and serve, if I didn’t care about Judah’s King Jehoshaphat, I wouldn’t notice you or even look at you!  
**3:15** Now bring me a musician.” While the musician played, the LORD’s power came over Elisha.  
**3:16** He said, “This is what the LORD says: This valley will be filled with pools.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>LXX, Vulg <i>Fill this valley with ditches</i>.  
**3:17** This is what the LORD says: You won’t see any wind or rain, but that valley will be full of water. Then you’ll be able to drink—you, your cattle, and your animals.  
**3:18** This is easy for the LORD to do. He will also hand Moab over to you.  
**3:19** You will then attack every fort and every grand city, cutting down all the good trees, stopping up all the springs, and ruining the good fields with stones.”  
**3:20** The next morning, at the time to offer the grain offering, water came flowing from the direction of Edom. The land filled up with water.  
**3:21** Now all the Moabites had heard how these kings had come to fight against them. So all who were able to fight were summoned, and they took up positions along the border.  
**3:22** They got up early in the morning as the sun’s rays shone on the water. The Moabites saw the water from a distance. It looked as red as blood.  
**3:23** They said, “It’s blood! The kings must have fought each other and killed themselves! Now get the plunder, Moab!”  
**3:24** But when they entered Israel’s camp, the Israelites rose up and attacked the Moabites. The Moabites fled from them. Israel moved forward, striking the Moabites down as they went.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb uncertain  
**3:25** Then the Israelites destroyed the Moabite cities. Each Israelite threw a stone on every piece of good land until it was covered. They stopped up every spring and cut down every good tree. Only Kir-hareseth remained with its stone wall intact,<sup>①</sup> but then stone throwers<sup>②</sup> surrounded it and attacked it.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb uncertain <sup>②</sup>Heb uncertain  
**3:26** Moab’s king saw that he was losing the battle. So he took seven hundred soldiers with him, each with sword in hand, to break through to Edom’s king. But they failed.  
**3:27** Then he took his oldest son, who was to succeed him as king, and he offered him on the wall as an entirely burned offering. As a result, outrage was expressed by Israel. So they pulled back from Moab’s king and returned to their own country.  

## 4장

**4:1** Now there was a woman who had been married to a member of a group of prophets. She appealed to Elisha, saying, “My husband, your servant, is dead. You know how he feared the LORD. But now someone he owed money to has come to take my two children away as slaves.”  
**4:2** Elisha said to her, “What can I do for you? Tell me what you still have left in the house.” She said, “Your servant has nothing at all in the house except a small jar of oil.”  
**4:3** He said, “Go out and borrow containers from all your neighbors. Get as many empty containers as possible.  
**4:4** Then go in and close the door behind you and your sons. Pour oil into all those containers. Set each one aside when it’s full.”  
**4:5** She left Elisha and closed the door behind her and her sons. They brought her containers as she kept on pouring.  
**4:6** When she had filled the containers, she said to her son, “Bring me another container.” He said to her, “There aren’t any more.” Then the oil stopped flowing,  
**4:7** and she reported this to the man of God. He said, “Go! Sell the oil and pay your debts. You and your sons can live on what remains.”  
**4:8** One day Elisha went to Shunem. A rich woman lived there. She urged him to eat something, so whenever he passed by, he would stop in to eat some food.  
**4:9** She said to her husband, “Look, I know that he is a holy man of God and he passes by regularly.  
**4:10** Let’s make a small room on the roof. We’ll set up a bed, a table, a chair, and a lamp for him there. Then when he comes to us, he can stay there.”  
**4:11** So one day Elisha came there, headed to the room on the roof, and lay down.  
**4:12** He said to his servant Gehazi, “Call this Shunammite woman.” Gehazi called her, and she stood before him.  
**4:13** Elisha then said to Gehazi, “Say to her, ‘Look, you’ve gone to all this trouble for us. What can I do for you? Is there anything I can say on your behalf to the king or to the commander of the army?’” She said, “I’m content to live at home with my own people.”  
**4:14** Elisha asked, “So what can be done for her?” Gehazi said, “Well, she doesn’t have a son, and her husband is old.”  
**4:15** Elisha said, “Call her.” So Gehazi called her, and she stood at the door.  
**4:16** Elisha said, “About this time next year, you will be holding a son in your arms.” But she said, “No, man of God, sir; don’t lie to your servant.”  
**4:17** But the woman conceived and gave birth to a son at about the same time the next year. This was what Elisha had promised her.  
**4:18** The child grew up. One day he ran to his father, who was with the harvest workers.  
**4:19** He said to his father, “Oh, my head! My head!” The father said to a young man, “Carry him to his mother.”  
**4:20** So he picked up the boy and brought him to his mother. The boy sat on her lap until noon. Then he died.  
**4:21** She went up and laid him down on the bed for the man of God. Then she went out and closed the door.  
**4:22** She called her husband and said, “Send me one of the young men and one of the donkeys so that I can hurry to the man of God and come back.”  
**4:23** Her husband said, “Why are you going to him today? It’s not a new moon or sabbath.” She said, “Don’t worry about it.”  
**4:24** She saddled the donkey, then said to her young servant, “Drive the donkey hard. Don’t let me slow down unless I tell you.”  
**4:25** So she went off and came to the man of God at Mount Carmel. As soon as the man of God saw her from a distance, he said to Gehazi his servant, “Look, it’s the Shunammite woman!  
**4:26** Run out to meet her and ask her, ‘Are things okay with you, your husband, and your child?’” She said, “Things are okay.”  
**4:27** When she got to the man of God at the mountain, she grabbed his feet. Gehazi came up to push her away, but the man of God said, “Leave her alone! She is distraught, but the LORD has hidden the reason from me and hasn’t told me why.”  
**4:28** She said, “Did I ask you for a son, sir? Didn’t I say, ‘Don’t raise my hopes’?”  
**4:29** Elisha said to Gehazi, “Get ready, take my staff, and go! If you encounter anyone, don’t stop to greet them. If anyone greets you, don’t reply. Put my staff on the boy’s face.”  
**4:30** But the boy’s mother said, “I swear by your life and by the LORD’s life, I won’t leave you!” So Elisha got up and followed her.  
**4:31** Gehazi went on ahead of them. He set the staff on the young boy’s face, but there was no sound or response. So he went back to meet Elisha and told him, “The boy didn’t wake up.”  
**4:32** Elisha came into the house and saw the boy lying dead on his bed.  
**4:33** He went in and closed the door behind the two of them. Then he prayed to the LORD.  
**4:34** He got up on the bed and lay down on top of the child, putting his mouth on the boy’s mouth, his eyes on the boy’s eyes, his hands on the boy’s hands. And as he bent over him, the child’s skin grew warm.  
**4:35** Then Elisha got down and paced back and forth in the house. Once again he got up on the bed and bent over the boy, at which point the boy sneezed<sup>①</sup> seven times and opened his eyes.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>gasped</i>; Heb uncertain  
**4:36** Elisha called for Gehazi and said, “Call the Shunammite woman.” Gehazi called her, and she came to Elisha. He told her, “Pick up your son.”  
**4:37** She came and fell at his feet, facedown on the ground. Then she picked up her son and left.  
**4:38** When Elisha returned to Gilgal, there was a famine in the land. A group of prophets was sitting before him. He said to his servant, “Put on the big pot and cook some stew for the prophets.”  
**4:39** So one of them went out to the field to gather plants; he found a wild vine and gathered wild gourds from it, filling his garment. He came and cut them up into the pot of stew, but no one knew what they were.  
**4:40** The stew was served to the men, but as they started to eat it, they cried out and said, “There is death in that pot, man of God!” They couldn’t eat it.  
**4:41** Elisha said, “Get some flour.” He threw it into the pot and said, “Serve the people so they can eat.” At that point, there was nothing bad left in the pot.  
**4:42** A man came from Baal-shalishah, bringing the man of God some bread from the early produce—twenty loaves of barley bread and fresh grain from his bag.<sup>①</sup> Elisha said, “Give it to the people so they can eat.”<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>still on its stem</i>  
**4:43** His servant said, “How can I feed one hundred men with this?” Elisha said, “Give it to the people so they can eat! This is what the LORD says: ‘Eat and there will be leftovers.’”  
**4:44** So the servant gave the food to them. They ate and had leftovers, in agreement with the LORD’s word.  

## 5장

**5:1** Naaman, a general for the king of Aram, was a great man and highly regarded by his master, because through him the LORD had given victory to Aram. This man was a mighty warrior, but he had a skin disease.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Traditionally <i>leprosy</i>, a kind of scale skin disease  
**5:2** Now Aramean raiding parties had gone out and captured a young girl from the land of Israel. She served Naaman’s wife.  
**5:3** She said to her mistress, “I wish that my master could come before the prophet who lives in Samaria. He would cure him of his skin disease.”  
**5:4** So Naaman went and told his master what the young girl from the land of Israel had said.  
**5:5** Then Aram’s king said, “Go ahead. I will send a letter to Israel’s king.” So Naaman left. He took along ten kikkars of silver, six thousand shekels of gold, and ten changes of clothing.  
**5:6** He brought the letter to Israel’s king. It read, “Along with this letter I’m sending you my servant Naaman so you can cure him of his skin disease.”  
**5:7** When the king of Israel read the letter, he ripped his clothes. He said, “What? Am I God to hand out death and life? But this king writes me, asking me to cure someone of his skin disease! You must realize that he wants to start a fight with me.”  
**5:8** When Elisha the man of God heard that Israel’s king had ripped his clothes, he sent word to the king: “Why did you rip your clothes? Let the man come to me. Then he’ll know that there’s a prophet in Israel.”  
**5:9** Naaman arrived with his horses and chariots. He stopped at the door of Elisha’s house.  
**5:10** Elisha sent out a messenger who said, “Go and wash seven times in the Jordan River. Then your skin will be restored and become clean.”  
**5:11** But Naaman went away in anger. He said, “I thought for sure that he’d come out, stand and call on the name of the LORD his God, wave his hand over the bad spot, and cure the skin disease.  
**5:12** Aren’t the rivers in Damascus, the Abana<sup>①</sup> and the Pharpar, better than all Israel’s waters? Couldn’t I wash in them and get clean?” So he turned away and proceeded to leave in anger.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>Amana</i>  
**5:13** Naaman’s servants came up to him and spoke to him: “Our father, if the prophet had told you to do something difficult, wouldn’t you have done it? All he said to you was, ‘Wash and become clean.’”  
**5:14** So Naaman went down and bathed in the Jordan seven times, just as the man of God had said. His skin was restored like that of a young boy, and he became clean.  
**5:15** He returned to the man of God with all his attendants. He came and stood before Elisha, saying, “Now I know for certain that there’s no God anywhere on earth except in Israel. Please accept a gift from your servant.”  
**5:16** But Elisha said, “I swear by the life of the LORD I serve that I won’t accept anything.” Naaman urged Elisha to accept something, but he still refused.  
**5:17** Then Naaman said, “If not, then let me, your servant, have two mule loads of earth. Your servant will never again offer entirely burned offerings or sacrifices to any other gods except the LORD.  
**5:18** But may the LORD forgive your servant for this one thing: When my master comes into Rimmon’s temple to bow down there and is leaning on my arm, I must also bow down in Rimmon’s temple. When I bow down in Rimmon’s temple, may the LORD forgive your servant for doing that.”  
**5:19** Elisha said to him, “Go in peace.” But when Naaman had gone some distance from Elisha,  
**5:20** Gehazi (who was the servant of Elisha the man of God) thought, My master let this Aramean Naaman off the hook by not accepting the gift he brought! As surely as the LORD lives, I’ll go after him and accept something from him.  
**5:21** So Gehazi pursued Naaman. Naaman saw him running after him, so he got down off his chariot to meet him. He said, “Is everything okay?”  
**5:22** Gehazi answered, “Yes, but my master sent me to say, ‘Two young men who are members of a group of prophets have just now come to me from the hills of Ephraim. Give them a kikkar of silver and two changes of clothing.’”  
**5:23** Naaman said, “By all means, take two kikkars!” He encouraged Gehazi to accept them. He tied two kikkars of silver up in two bags, along with two changes of clothes. Naaman gave them to two of his servants, and they carried them in front of Gehazi.  
**5:24** When Gehazi arrived at the elevated fortress,<sup>①</sup> he took the items from them and stored them in his house. Then he sent the servants away, and they left.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>hillside</i>; Heb uncertain  
**5:25** Gehazi then went and stood before his master. Elisha said to Gehazi, “Where did you come from, Gehazi?” “Your servant didn’t go anywhere,” Gehazi replied.  
**5:26** Elisha said to him, “Wasn’t my heart going along with you<sup>①</sup> when the man got off his chariot to meet you? Is this the time to accept silver, clothes, olive trees, vineyards, sheep, cattle, or male and female servants?<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>LXX; MT lacks <i>along with you.</i>  
**5:27** Naaman’s skin disease will now cling to you and to your descendants forever!” And Gehazi left Elisha’s presence, flaky like snow with skin disease.  

## 6장

**6:1** The members of the group of prophets said to Elisha, “Look, the place where we now live under your authority is too small for us.  
**6:2** Let’s go to the Jordan River and each get a log from there. Then we can make a place to live there.” Elisha said, “Do it!”  
**6:3** One of them said, “Please come with us, your servants.” Elisha said, “Okay, I’ll go.”  
**6:4** So he went with them. They came to the Jordan River and began cutting down trees.  
**6:5** One of them was cutting down a tree when his ax head fell into the water. He cried out, “Oh, no! Master, it was a borrowed ax!”  
**6:6** The man of God said, “Where did it fall?” He showed Elisha the place. Elisha then cut a piece of wood, threw it into the river there, and the ax head floated up.  
**6:7** “Lift it out,” Elisha said. So the man then reached out and grabbed it.  
**6:8** Aram’s king was fighting against Israel. He took counsel with his officers, saying, “I’ll camp at such-and-such a place.”  
**6:9** The man of God sent word to Israel’s king: “Beware of passing by this place because the Arameans are going down there.”  
**6:10** Then Israel’s king sent word to the place the man of God had mentioned to him. Time after time, Elisha warned the king, and the king stayed on the alert.  
**6:11** Aram’s king was extremely upset about this. He called his officers and said to them, “Tell me! Who among us is siding with Israel’s king?”  
**6:12** One of his officers said, “No one, Your Majesty! It’s Elisha the Israelite prophet who tells Israel’s king the words that you speak in the privacy of your bedroom.”  
**6:13** He said, “Go and find out where he is. Then I will send men to capture him.” They told him, “He is in Dothan.”  
**6:14** So the king sent horses and chariots there with a strong army. They came at night and surrounded the city.  
**6:15** Elisha’s servant got up early and went out. He saw an army with horses and chariots surrounding the city. His servant said to Elisha, “Oh, no! Master, what will we do?”  
**6:16** “Don’t be afraid,” Elisha said, “because there are more of us than there are of them.”  
**6:17** Then Elisha prayed, “LORD, please open his eyes that he may see.” Then the LORD opened the servant’s eyes, and he saw that the mountain was full of horses and fiery chariots surrounding Elisha.  
**6:18** The Arameans came toward him, so Elisha prayed to the LORD, “Strike this nation with blindness.” And the LORD struck them blind, just as Elisha asked.  
**6:19** Elisha said to them, “This isn’t the right road or the right city. Follow me, and I’ll lead you to the man you are looking for.” But he took them to Samaria!  
**6:20** When they arrived in Samaria, Elisha said, “LORD, open the eyes of these men so they can see.” The LORD opened their eyes, and they saw that they were right in the middle of Samaria!

## 23장

**23:1** the king sent a message, and all of Judah’s and Jerusalem’s elders gathered before him.  
**23:2** Then the king went up to the LORD’s temple, together with all the people of Judah and all the citizens of Jerusalem, the priests and the prophets, and all the people, young and old alike. There the king read out loud all the words of the covenant scroll that had been found in the LORD’s temple.  
**23:3** The king stood beside the pillar and made a covenant with the LORD that he would follow the LORD by keeping his commandments, his laws, and his regulations with all his heart and all his being in order to fulfill the words of this covenant that were written in this scroll. All of the people accepted the covenant.  
**23:4** The king then commanded the high priest Hilkiah, the second-order priests, and the doorkeepers to remove from the LORD’s temple all the religious objects made for Baal, Asherah, and all the heavenly bodies. The king burned them outside Jerusalem in the Kidron fields and took the ashes to Bethel.  
**23:5** He got rid of the pagan priests that the Judean kings had appointed to burn incense at the shrines in Judah’s cities and the areas around Jerusalem. He did the same to those who burned incense to Baal, to the sun, to the moon, to the constellations, and to all the heavenly bodies.  
**23:6** He removed the Asherah image<sup>①</sup> from the LORD’s temple, taking it to the Kidron Valley outside Jerusalem. There he burned it, ground it to dust, and threw the dust on the public graveyard.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb lacks <i>image</i>; perhaps a pole dedicated to the goddess.  
**23:7** The king tore down the shrines for the consecrated workers<sup>①</sup> that were in the LORD’s temple, where women made woven coverings<sup>②</sup> for Asherah.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Traditionally <i>cultic prostitutes</i> <sup>②</sup>Heb uncertain  
**23:8** Then Josiah brought all the priests out of Judah’s cities. From Geba to Beer-sheba, he defiled the shrines where the priests had been burning incense. He also tore down the shrines at the gates at the entrance to the gate of Joshua the city’s governor, which were on the left as one entered the city gate.  
**23:9** Although the priests of these shrines didn’t go up on the LORD’s altar in Jerusalem, they did eat unleavened bread with their fellow priests.  
**23:10** Josiah defiled the Topheth in the Ben-hinnom Valley so no one could burn their child alive in honor of the god Molech.  
**23:11** He did away with the horses that Judah’s kings had dedicated to the sun. They were kept at the entrance to the LORD’s temple near a room in the annex<sup>①</sup> that belonged to an official named Nathan-melech. Josiah set fire to the chariots that were dedicated to the sun.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb uncertain  
**23:12** The king also tore down the altars that were on the roof of Ahaz’s upper story, which had been made by the Judean kings, and he did the same with the altars that Manasseh had built in the two courtyards of the LORD’s temple. He broke them up there<sup>①</sup> and threw their dust into the Kidron Valley.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Correction; MT <i>removed them quickly</i> or <i>ran from there</i>  
**23:13** The king then defiled the shrines facing Jerusalem, south of the Mountain of Destruction. Solomon the king of Israel had built these for Ashtoreth, the monstrous Sidonian god, for Chemosh, the monstrous Moabite god, and for Milcom, the detestable Ammonite god.  
**23:14** He smashed the sacred pillars and cut down the sacred poles,<sup>①</sup> filling the places where they had been with human bones.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb <i>asherim</i>, perhaps objects devoted to the goddess Asherah

He burned the shrine, grinding it into dust. Then he burned its sacred pole.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup>Heb <i>asherah</i>, perhaps an object devoted to the goddess Asherah

**23:18** “Let it be,” Josiah said. “No one should disturb his bones.” So they left his bones untouched, along with the bones of the prophet who came from Samaria.  
**23:19** Moreover, Josiah removed all the shrines on the high hills that the Israelite kings had constructed throughout the cities of Samaria. These had made the LORD angry. Josiah did to them just what he did at Bethel.  
**23:20** He actually slaughtered on those altars all the priests of the shrines who were there, and he burned human bones on them. Then Josiah returned to Jerusalem.  
**23:21** The king commanded all the people, “Celebrate a Passover to the LORD your God following what is instructed in this scroll containing the covenant.”  
**23:22** A Passover like this hadn’t been celebrated since the days when the judges judged Israel; neither had it been celebrated during all the days of the Israelite and Judean kings.  
**23:23** But in the eighteenth year of King Josiah’s rule, this Passover was celebrated to the LORD in Jerusalem.  
**23:24** Josiah burned those who consulted dead spirits and the mediums, the household gods and the worthless idols—all the monstrous things that were seen in the land of Judah and in Jerusalem. In this way Josiah fulfilled the words of the Instruction written in the scroll that the priest Hilkiah found in the LORD’s temple.  
**23:25** There’s never been a king like Josiah, whether before or after him, who turned to the LORD with all his heart, all his being, and all his strength, in agreement with everything in the Instruction from Moses.  
**23:26** Even so, the LORD didn’t turn away from the great rage that burned against Judah on account of all that Manasseh had done to make him angry.  
**23:27** The LORD said, “I will remove Judah from my presence just as I removed Israel. I will reject this city, Jerusalem, which I chose, and this temple where I promised my name would reside.”  
**23:28** The rest of Josiah’s deeds and all that he accomplished, aren’t they written in the official records of Judah’s kings?  
**23:29** In his days, the Egyptian king Pharaoh Neco marched against the Assyrian king at the Euphrates River. King Josiah marched out to intercept him. But when Neco encountered Josiah in Megiddo, he killed the king.  
**23:30** Josiah’s servants took his body from Megiddo in a chariot. They brought him to Jerusalem and buried him in his own tomb. The people of the land took Jehoahaz, Josiah’s son, anointed him, and made him king after his father.  
**23:31** Jehoahaz was 23 years old when he became king, and he ruled for three months in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Hamutal; she was Jeremiah’s daughter and was from Libnah.  
**23:32** He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes, just as all his ancestors had done.  
**23:33** Pharaoh Neco made Jehoahaz a prisoner at Riblah in the land of Hamath, ending his rule in Jerusalem. Pharaoh Neco imposed a fine on the land totaling one hundred kikkars of silver and one kikkar of gold.  
**23:34** Pharaoh Neco made Eliakim, Josiah’s son, king after his father Josiah. Neco changed Eliakim’s name to Jehoiakim. Neco took Jehoahaz away; he later died in Egypt.  
**23:35** Jehoiakim gave Pharaoh the silver and gold, but he taxed the land in order to meet Pharaoh’s financial demands. Each person was taxed appropriately. Jehoiakim exacted silver and the gold from the land’s people in order to give it to Pharaoh Neco.  
**23:36** Jehoiakim was 25 years old when he became king, and he ruled for eleven years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Zebidah; she was Pedaiah’s daughter and was from Rumah.  
**23:37** He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes, just as all his ancestors had done.  

## 24장

**24:1** In Jehoiakim’s days, King Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon attacked. Jehoiakim had submitted to him for three years, but then Jehoiakim changed his mind and rebelled against him.  
**24:2** The LORD sent Chaldean, Aramean, Moabite, and Ammonite raiding parties against Jehoiakim, sending them against Judah in order to destroy it. This was in agreement with the word that the LORD had spoken through his servants the prophets.  
**24:3** Indeed, this happened to Judah because the LORD commanded them to be removed from his presence on account of all the sins that Manasseh had committed  
**24:4** and because of the innocent blood that he had spilled. Manasseh had filled Jerusalem with innocent blood, and the LORD didn’t want to forgive that.  
**24:5** The rest of Jehoiakim’s deeds and all that he accomplished, aren’t they written in the official records of Judah’s kings?  
**24:6** Jehoiakim lay down with his ancestors. His son Jehoiachin succeeded him as king.  
**24:7** The Egyptian king never left his country again because the Babylonian king had taken over all the territory that had previously belonged to him—from the border of Egypt to the Euphrates River.  
**24:8** Jehoiachin was 18 years old when he became king, and he ruled for three months in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Nehushta; she was Elnathan’s daughter and was from Jerusalem.  
**24:9** He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes, just as all his ancestors had done.  
**24:10** At that time, the officers of Babylon’s King Nebuchadnezzar attacked Jerusalem and laid siege to the city.  
**24:11** Babylon’s King Nebuchadnezzar himself arrived at the city while his officers were blockading it.  
**24:12** Judah’s King Jehoiachin, along with his mother, his servants, his officers, and his officials, came out to surrender to the Babylonian king. The Babylonian king took Jehoiachin prisoner in the eighth year of Jehoiachin’s rule.  
**24:13** Nebuchadnezzar also took away all the treasures of the LORD’s temple and of the royal palace. He cut into pieces all the gold objects that Israel’s King Solomon had made for the LORD’s temple, which is exactly what the LORD said would happen.  
**24:14** Then Nebuchadnezzar exiled all of Jerusalem: all the officials, all the military leaders—ten thousand exiles—as well as all the skilled workers and metalworkers. No one was left behind except the poorest of the land’s people.  
**24:15** Nebuchadnezzar exiled Jehoiachin to Babylon; he also exiled the queen mother, the king’s wives, the officials, and the land’s elite leaders from Jerusalem to Babylon.  
**24:16** The Babylonian king also exiled seven thousand warriors—each one a hero trained for battle—as well as a thousand skilled workers and metalworkers to Babylon.  
**24:17** Then the Babylonian king made Mattaniah, Jehoiachin’s uncle, succeed Jehoiachin as king. Nebuchadnezzar changed Mattaniah’s name to Zedekiah.  
**24:18** Zedekiah was 21 years old when he became king, and he ruled for eleven years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Hamutal; she was Jeremiah’s daughter and was from Libnah.  
**24:19** He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes, just as Jehoiakim had done.  
**24:20** It was precisely because the LORD was angry with Jerusalem and Judah that he thrust them out of his presence. <i>The southern kingdom falls</i> </pb> Now Zedekiah rebelled against the Babylonian king.  

## 25장

**25:1** So in the ninth year of Zedekiah’s rule, on the tenth day of the tenth month, Babylon’s King Nebuchadnezzar attacked Jerusalem with his entire army. He camped beside the city and built a siege wall all around it.  
**25:2** The city was under attack until King Zedekiah’s eleventh year.  
**25:3** On the ninth day of the month, the famine in the city got so bad that no food remained for the common people.  
**25:4** Then the enemy broke into the city. All the soldiers fled<sup>①</sup> by night using the gate between the two walls near the King’s Garden. The Chaldeans were surrounding the city, so the soldiers ran toward the desert plain.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup>LXX, cf <a name='B:300 52:7'>Jer 52:7; MT lacks <i>fled</i>.  
**25:5** But the Chaldean army chased King Zedekiah and caught up with him in the Jericho plains. His entire army deserted him.  
**25:6** So the Chaldeans captured the king and brought him back to the Babylonian king, who was at Riblah. There his punishment was determined.  
**25:7** Zedekiah’s sons were slaughtered right before his eyes. Then he was blinded, put in bronze chains, and taken off to Babylon.  
**25:8** On the seventh day of the fifth month in the nineteenth year of Babylon’s King Nebuchadnezzar, Nebuzaradan arrived at Jerusalem. He was the commander of the guard and an official of the Babylonian king.  
**25:9** He burned down the LORD’s temple, the royal palace, and all of Jerusalem’s houses. He burned down every important building.  
**25:10** The whole Chaldean army under the commander of the guard tore down the walls surrounding Jerusalem.  
**25:11** Then Nebuzaradan the commander of the guard exiled the people who were left in the city, those who had already surrendered to Babylon’s king, and the rest of the population.  
**25:12** The commander of the guard left some of the land’s poor people behind to work the vineyards and be farmers.  
**25:13** The Chaldeans shattered the bronze columns, the stands, and the bronze Sea that were in the LORD’s temple. They carried the bronze off to Babylon.  
**25:14** They also took the pots, the shovels, the wick trimmers, the dishes, and all the bronze items that had been used in the temple.  
**25:15** The commander of the guard took the fire pans and the sprinkling bowls, which were made of pure gold and pure silver.  
**25:16** The bronze in all these objects—the two pillars, the Sea, and the stands that Solomon had made for the LORD’s temple—was too heavy to weigh.  
**25:17** Each pillar was twenty-seven feet high. The bronze capital on top of the first pillar was four and a half feet high. Decorative lattices and pomegranates, all made from bronze, were around the capital. And the second pillar was decorated with lattices just like the first.  
**25:18** The commander of the guard also took away Seraiah the chief priest, Zephaniah the priest next in rank, and the three doorkeepers.  
**25:19** Of those still left in the city, Nebuzaradan took away an officer who was in charge of the army and five royal advisors who were discovered in the city. He also took away the secretary of the officer responsible for drafting the land’s people to fight, as well as sixty people who were discovered in the city.  
**25:20** Nebuzaradan the commander of the guard took all of these people and brought them to the Babylonian king at Riblah.  
**25:21** The king of Babylon struck them down, killing them in Riblah in the land of Hamath. So Judah was exiled from its land.  
**25:22** Babylon’s King Nebuchadnezzar put Gedaliah, Ahikam’s son and Shaphan’s grandson, in charge of the people he had left behind in the land of Judah.  
**25:23** All the army officers and their soldiers heard that the Babylonian king had appointed Gedaliah as governor, so they came with their men to Gedaliah at Mizpah. The officers were Ishmael, Nethaniah’s son; Johanan, Kareah’s son; Seraiah, Tanhumeth’s son who was a Netophathite; and Jaazaniah, Maacathite’s son.  
**25:24** Gedaliah made a solemn pledge to them and their soldiers, telling them, “Don’t be afraid of the Chaldean officials. Stay in the land and serve the Babylonian king, and things will go well for you.”  
**25:25** But in the seventh month, Ishmael, Nethaniah’s son and Elishama’s grandson, who was from the royal family, came with ten soldiers, and they struck Gedaliah, and he died. They also killed the Judeans and the Chaldeans who were with him at Mizpah.  
**25:26** Then all the people, young and old, along with the army officers, departed for Egypt because they were afraid of the Chaldeans.  
**25:27** In the year that Awil-merodach<sup>①</sup> became king of Babylon, he released Judah’s King Jehoiachin from prison. **25:30** At the king’s command, a regular food allowance was given to him every day for the rest of his life.
