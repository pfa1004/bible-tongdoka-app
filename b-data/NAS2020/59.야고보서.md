## 1장

**1:1**  <sup>①</sup>James, a bond-servant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ, <e>T</e>o the twelve tribes who are <sup>②</sup>dispersed abroad: Greetings.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>Jacob</i> <sup>②</sup>Lit <i>in the Dispersion</i>  
**1:2** Consider it all joy, my brothers <i>and sisters,</i> when you encounter various <sup>①</sup>trials,<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>temptations</i>  
**1:3** knowing that the testing of your faith produces <sup>①</sup>endurance.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>steadfastness</i>  
**1:4** And let <sup>①</sup>endurance have <i>its</i> perfect <sup>②</sup>result, so that you may be <sup>③</sup>perfect and complete, lacking in nothing.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>steadfastness</i> <sup>②</sup>Lit <i>work</i> <sup>③</sup>Or <i>mature</i>  
**1:5** But if any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask of God, who gives to all generously and <sup>①</sup>without reproach, and it will be given to him.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>does not reproach</i>  
**1:6** But he must ask in faith without any doubting, for the one who doubts is like the surf of the sea, driven and tossed by the wind.  
**1:7** For that person ought not to expect that he will receive anything from the Lord,  
**1:8** <i>being</i> a <sup>①</sup>double-minded man, unstable in all his ways.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>I.e., a chronic doubter  
**1:9** Now the brother <i>or sister</i> of humble <i>circumstances</i> is to glory in his high position;  
**1:10** but the rich person <i>is to glory</i> in his <sup>①</sup>humiliation, because like <sup>②</sup>flowering grass he will pass away.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>downfall</i> <sup>②</sup>Lit <i>the flower of the grass</i>  
**1:11** For the sun rises with its scorching heat and withers the grass; and its flower falls off and the beauty of its appearance is destroyed; so also the rich person, in the midst of his pursuits, will die out.  
**1:12** Blessed is a man who perseveres under trial; for once he has <sup>①</sup>been approved, he will receive the crown of life which <i>the Lord</i> has promised to those who love Him.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>passed the test</i>  
**1:13** No one is to say when he is tempted, “I am being tempted <sup>①</sup>by God”; for God cannot be tempted <sup>②</sup>by evil, and He Himself does not tempt anyone.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>from</i> <sup>②</sup>Lit <i>of evil things</i>  
**1:14** But each one is tempted when he is carried away and enticed by his own lust.  
**1:15** Then when lust has conceived, it gives birth to sin; and sin, when it has run its course, brings forth death.  
**1:16** Do not be <sup>①</sup>deceived, my beloved brothers <i>and sisters.</i><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>misled</i>  
**1:17** Every good thing given and every perfect gift is from above, coming down from the Father of lights, with whom there is no variation or <sup>①</sup>shifting shadow.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>shadow of turning</i>  
**1:18** In the exercise of His will He gave us birth by the word of truth, so that we would be <sup>①</sup>a kind of first fruits <sup>②</sup>among His creatures.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>a certain first fruits</i> <sup>②</sup>Lit <i>of</i>  
**1:19**  <sup>①</sup>You know <i>this,</i> my beloved brothers <i>and sisters.</i> Now everyone must be quick to hear, slow to speak, <i>and</i> slow to anger;<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>Know</i> this  
**1:20** for a man’s anger does not bring about the righteousness of God.  
**1:21** Therefore, ridding <i>yourselves</i> of all filthiness and <i>all</i> <sup>①</sup>that remains of wickedness, in <sup>②</sup>humility receive the word implanted, which is able to save your souls.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>abundance of malice</i> <sup>②</sup>Or <i>gentleness</i>  
**1:22** But prove yourselves doers of the word, and not just hearers who deceive themselves.  
**1:23** For if anyone is a hearer of the word and not a doer, he is like a man who looks at his <sup>①</sup>natural face in a mirror;<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>the face of his birth;</i> or <i>nature</i>  
**1:24** for <i>once</i> he has looked at himself and gone away, <sup>①</sup>he has immediately forgotten what kind of person he was.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>and he</i>  
**1:25** But one who has looked intently at the perfect law, the <i>law</i> of freedom, and has continued <i>in it,</i> not having become a forgetful hearer but <sup>①</sup>an active doer, this person will be blessed in <sup>②</sup>what he does.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>a doer of a work</i> <sup>②</sup>Lit <i>his doing</i>  
**1:26** If anyone thinks himself to be religious, yet does not <sup>①</sup>bridle his tongue but deceives his <i>own</i> heart, this person’s religion is worthless.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>control</i>  
**1:27** Pure and undefiled religion in the sight of <i>our</i> God and Father is this: to visit orphans and widows in their distress, <i>and</i> to keep oneself unstained <sup>①</sup>by the world.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>from</i>  

## 2장

**2:1** My brothers <i>and sisters,</i> do not hold your faith in our glorious Lord Jesus Christ with <i>an attitude of</i> personal favoritism.  
**2:2** For if a man comes into your <sup>①</sup>assembly with a gold ring <i>and is dressed</i> in bright clothes, and a poor man in dirty clothes also comes in,<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>synagogue</i>  
**2:3** and you <sup>①</sup>pay special attention to the one who is wearing the bright clothes, and say, “You sit here in a good <i>place,</i>” and you say to the poor man, “You stand over there, or sit down by my footstool,”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>look at</i>  
**2:4** have you not made distinctions among yourselves, and become judges with evil <sup>①</sup>motives?<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>reasonings</i>  
**2:5** Listen, my beloved brothers <i>and sisters:</i> did God not choose the poor <sup>①</sup>of this world <i>to be</i> rich in faith and heirs of the kingdom which He promised to those who love Him?<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>to the</i>  
**2:6** But you have dishonored the poor man. Is it not the rich who oppress you and <sup>①</sup>personally drag you into <sup>②</sup>court?<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>they themselves</i> <sup>②</sup>Lit <i>courts</i>  
**2:7** Do they not blaspheme the good name <sup>①</sup>by which you have been called?<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>which has been called upon you</i>  
**2:8** If, however, you are fulfilling the <sup>①</sup>royal law according to the Scripture, “YOU SHALL LOVE YOUR NEIGHBOR AS YOURSELF,” you are doing well.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>law of</i> our <i>King</i>  
**2:9** But if you show partiality, you are committing sin <i>and</i> are convicted by the Law as violators.  
**2:10** For whoever keeps the whole Law, yet stumbles in one <i>point,</i> has become guilty of all.  
**2:11** For He who said, “DO NOT COMMIT ADULTERY,” also said, “DO NOT MURDER.” Now if you do not commit adultery, but do murder, you have become a violator of the Law.  
**2:12** So speak, and so act, as those who are to be judged by <i>the</i> law of freedom.  
**2:13** For judgment <i>will be</i> merciless to one who has shown no mercy; mercy <sup>①</sup>triumphs over judgment.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>boasts against</i>  
**2:14** What use is it, my brothers <i>and sisters,</i> if someone says he has faith, but he has no works? Can <sup>①</sup>that faith save him?<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>the</i>  
**2:15** If a brother or sister is without clothing and in need of daily food,  
**2:16** and one of you says to them, “Go in peace, <sup>①</sup>be warmed and be filled,” yet you do not give them what is necessary for <i>their</i> body, what use is that?<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>warm yourselves and fill yourselves</i>  
**2:17** In the same way, faith also, if it has no works, is <sup>①</sup>dead, <i>being</i> by itself.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>dead by its own standards</i>  
**2:18** But someone <sup>①</sup>may <i>well</i> say, “You have faith and I have works; show me your faith without the works, and I will show you my faith by my works.”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>will</i>  
**2:19** You believe that <sup>①</sup>God is one. You do well; the demons also believe, and shudder.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>One early ms <i>there is one God</i>  
**2:20** But are you willing to acknowledge, you foolish person, that faith without works is useless?  
**2:21** Was our father Abraham not justified by works when he offered up his son Isaac on the altar?  
**2:22** You see that faith was working with his works, and <sup>①</sup>as a result of the works, faith was <sup>②</sup>perfected;<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>by the deeds</i> <sup>②</sup>Or <i>completed</i>  
**2:23** and the Scripture was fulfilled which says, “AND ABRAHAM BELIEVED GOD, AND IT WAS CREDITED TO HIM AS RIGHTEOUSNESS,” and he was called a friend of God.  
**2:24** You see that a person is justified by works and not by faith alone.  
**2:25** In the same way, was Rahab the prostitute not justified by works also when she received the messengers and sent them out by another way?  
**2:26** For just as the body without <i>the</i> spirit is dead, so also faith without works is dead.  

## 3장

**3:1** Do not become teachers in large numbers, my brothers, since you know that we <i>who are teachers</i> will incur a <sup>①</sup>stricter judgment.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>greater condemnation;</i> i.e., for erroneous doctrine  
**3:2** For we all stumble in many <i>ways.</i> If anyone does not stumble in <sup>①</sup>what he says, he is a perfect man, able to <sup>②</sup>rein in the whole body as well.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>word</i> <sup>②</sup>Or <i>hold in check</i>  
**3:3** Now if we put the bits into the horses’ mouths so that they will obey us, we direct their whole body as well.  
**3:4** Look at the ships too: though they are so large and are driven by strong winds, they are <i>nevertheless</i> directed by a very small rudder wherever the inclination of the pilot determines.  
**3:5** So also the tongue is a small part <i>of the body,</i> and <i>yet</i> it boasts of great things. <e>S</e>ee how great a forest is set aflame by such a small fire!  
**3:6** And the tongue is a fire, the <i>very</i> world of unrighteousness; the tongue is set among <sup>①</sup>our body’s parts as that which defiles the whole body and sets on fire the course of <i>our</i> <sup>②</sup>life, and is set on fire by <sup>③</sup>hell.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>our parts</i> <sup>②</sup>Or <i>existence, origin</i> <sup>③</sup>Gr <i>Gehenna</i>  
**3:7** For every <sup>①</sup>species of beasts and birds, of reptiles and creatures of the sea, is tamed and has been tamed by the human <sup>②</sup>race.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>nature</i> <sup>②</sup>Lit <i>nature</i>  
**3:8** But no one <i>among</i> mankind can tame the tongue; <i>it is</i> a restless evil, full of deadly poison.  
**3:9** With it we bless <i>our</i> Lord and Father, and with it we curse people, who have been made in the likeness of God;  
**3:10** from the same mouth come <i>both</i> blessing and cursing. My brothers <i>and sisters,</i> these things should not be this way.  
**3:11** Does a spring send out from the same opening <i>both</i> <sup>①</sup>fresh and bitter <i>water?</i><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>sweet</i>  
**3:12** Can a fig tree, my brothers <i>and sisters,</i> bear olives, or a vine <i>bear</i> figs? Nor <i>can</i> salt water produce <sup>①</sup>fresh.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>sweet</i>  
**3:13** Who among you is wise and understanding? Let him show by his good behavior his deeds in the gentleness of wisdom.  
**3:14** But if you have bitter jealousy and <sup>①</sup>selfish ambition in your heart, do not be arrogant and <i>so</i> lie against the truth.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>strife</i>  
**3:15** This wisdom is not that which comes down from above, but is earthly, <sup>①</sup>natural, demonic.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>unspiritual</i>  
**3:16** For where jealousy and <sup>①</sup>selfish ambition exist, <sup>②</sup>there is disorder and every evil thing.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>strife</i> <sup>②</sup>I.e., in that place  
**3:17** But the wisdom from above is first pure, then peace-loving, gentle, <sup>①</sup>reasonable, full of mercy and good fruits, <sup>②</sup>impartial, free of hypocrisy.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>willing to yield</i> <sup>②</sup>Or <i>unwavering</i>  
**3:18** And the fruit of righteousness is sown in peace <sup>①</sup>by those who make peace.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>for</i>  

## 4장

**4:1**  <sup>①</sup>What is the source of quarrels and conflicts among you? <sup>②</sup>Is the source not your pleasures that wage war in <sup>③</sup>your body’s parts?<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>From where wars and from where fightings</i> <sup>②</sup>Lit Are they <i>not from here,</i> from your <sup>③</sup>Lit <i>your parts</i>  
**4:2** You lust and do not have, <i>so</i> you commit murder. And you are envious and cannot obtain, <i>so</i> you fight and quarrel. You do not have because you do not ask.  
**4:3** You ask and do not receive, because you ask <sup>①</sup>with the wrong motives, so that you may spend <i>what you request</i> <sup>②</sup>on your pleasures.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>wrongly</i> <sup>②</sup>Lit <i>in</i>  
**4:4** You adulteresses, do you not know that friendship with the world is hostility toward God? Therefore whoever wants to be a friend of the world makes himself an enemy of God.  
**4:5** Or do you think that the Scripture says to no purpose, “ <sup>①</sup>He <sup>②</sup>jealously desires the Spirit whom He has made to dwell in us”?<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>The spirit which He has made to dwell in us lusts with envy</i> <sup>②</sup>Lit <i>desires to jealousy</i>  
**4:6** But He gives a greater grace. Therefore <i>it</i> says, “GOD IS OPPOSED TO THE PROUD, BUT GIVES GRACE TO THE HUMBLE.”  
**4:7** Submit therefore to God. But <sup>①</sup>resist the devil, and he will flee from you.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>oppose</i>  
**4:8** Come close to God and He will come close to you. Cleanse <i>your</i> hands, you sinners; and purify <i>your</i> hearts, you <sup>①</sup>double-minded.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>I.e., chronic doubters  
**4:9** Be miserable, and mourn, and weep; let your laughter be turned into mourning, and your joy into gloom.  
**4:10** Humble yourselves in the presence of the Lord, and He will exalt you.  
**4:11** Do not speak against one another, brothers <i>and sisters.</i> The one who speaks against a brother <i>or sister,</i> or judges his brother <i>or sister,</i> speaks against the law and judges the law; but if you judge the law, you are not a doer of the law but a judge <i>of it.</i>  
**4:12** There is <i>only</i> one Lawgiver and Judge, the One who is able to save and to destroy; but who are you, <sup>①</sup>judging your neighbor?<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>the one judging</i>  
**4:13** Come now, you who say, “Today or tomorrow we will go to such and such a city, and spend a year there and engage in business and make a profit.”  
**4:14**  <sup>①</sup>Yet you do not know <sup>②</sup>what your life will be like tomorrow. For you are <i>just</i> a vapor that appears for a little while, and then vanishes away.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>Who do not</i> <sup>②</sup>Or <i>what</i> will happen <i>tomorrow. What kind of life is yours?</i>  
**4:15**  <sup>①</sup>Instead, <i>you ought</i> to say, “If the Lord wills, we will live and also do this or that.”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>Instead of your saying</i>  
**4:16** But as it is, you boast in your <sup>①</sup>arrogance; all such boasting is evil.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>pretensions</i>  
**4:17** So for one who knows <i>the</i> <sup>①</sup>right thing to do and does not do it, for him it is sin.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>good</i>  

## 5장

**5:1** Come now, you rich people, weep and howl for your miseries which are coming upon you.  
**5:2** Your riches have rotted and your garments have become moth-eaten.  
**5:3** Your gold and your silver have corroded, and their corrosion will <sup>①</sup>serve as a testimony against you and will consume your flesh like fire. It is in the last days that you have stored up your treasure!<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>be for</i>  
**5:4** Behold, the pay of the laborers who mowed your fields, <i>and</i> which has been withheld by you, cries out <i>against you;</i> and the outcry of those who did the harvesting has reached the ears of the Lord <sup>①</sup>of armies.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Gr <i>sabaoth,</i> for Heb <i>tsebaoth</i>  
**5:5** You have <sup>①</sup>lived for pleasure on the earth and lived luxuriously; you have <sup>②</sup>fattened your hearts in a day of slaughter.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>led a life of self-indulgence</i> <sup>②</sup>Lit <i>nourished</i>  
**5:6** You have condemned and <sup>①</sup>put to death the righteous person; he offers you no resistance.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>murdered</i>  
**5:7** Therefore be patient, brothers <i>and sisters,</i> until the coming of the Lord. The farmer waits for the precious produce of the soil, being patient about it, until <sup>①</sup>it gets the early and late rains.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>he</i>  
**5:8** You too be patient; strengthen your hearts, for the coming of the Lord is near.  
**5:9** Do not <sup>①</sup>complain, brothers <i>and sisters,</i> against one another, so that you may not be judged; behold, the Judge is standing <sup>②</sup>right at the <sup>③</sup>door.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>groan</i> <sup>②</sup>Lit <i>before</i> <sup>③</sup>Lit <i>doors</i>  
**5:10** As an example, brothers <i>and sisters,</i> of suffering and patience, take the prophets who spoke in the name of the Lord.  
**5:11** We count those blessed who endured. You have heard of the <sup>①</sup>endurance of Job and have seen the <sup>②</sup>outcome of the Lord’s dealings, that the Lord is full of compassion and <i>is</i> merciful.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>steadfastness</i> <sup>②</sup>Lit <i>end of the Lord</i>  
**5:12** But above all, my brothers <i>and sisters,</i> do not swear, either by heaven or by earth or with any other oath; but <sup>①</sup>your yes is to be yes, and your no, no, so that you do not fall under judgment.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>yours is to be yes, yes, and no, no</i>  
**5:13** Is anyone among you suffering? <i>Then</i> he must pray. Is anyone cheerful? He is to sing praises.  
**5:14** Is anyone among you sick? <i>Then</i> he must call for the elders of the church and they are to pray over him, <sup>①</sup>anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord;<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>having anointed</i>  
**5:15** and the prayer of faith will <sup>①</sup>restore the one who is sick, and the Lord will raise him up, and if he has committed sins, <sup>②</sup>they will be forgiven him.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>save</i> <sup>②</sup>Lit <i>it</i>  
**5:16** Therefore, confess your sins to one another, and pray for one another so that you may be healed. A <sup>①</sup>prayer of a righteous person, when it is <sup>②</sup>brought about, can accomplish much.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>supplication</i> <sup>②</sup>I.e., granted by God  
**5:17** Elijah was a man with a nature like ours, and he prayed <sup>①</sup>earnestly that it would not rain, and it did not rain on the earth for three years and six months.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>with prayer</i>  
**5:18** Then he prayed again, and the sky <sup>①</sup>poured rain and the earth produced its fruit.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>gave</i>  
**5:19** My brothers <i>and sisters,</i> if anyone among you strays from the truth and someone turns him back,  
**5:20** let him know that the one who has turned a sinner from the error of his way will save his soul from death and cover a multitude of sins.
