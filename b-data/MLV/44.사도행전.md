**23:3** Then Paul said to him, God is about to beat you, the whitewashed wall. And you sit judging me according to the law, and <i>yet</i> violating the law, you are commanding me to be beaten.  
**23:4** But those who are standing beside <i>him</i> said, Are you reviling God’s high-priest?  
**23:5** And Paul said, Brethren, I did not know that he was <i>the</i> high-priest; for it has been written, ‘You will not speak evilly of a ruler of your people.’ {Exo 22:28}  
**23:6** But <i>after</i> Paul knew that the one part were Sadducees and the other Pharisees, he cried out in the council, Men, brethren, I am a Pharisee, a son of a Pharisee. I am being judged concerning the hope and resurrection of the dead.  
**23:7** But <i>after</i> he spoke this, there became a dissension from the Pharisees and the multitude was split-apart.  
**23:8** (For the Sadducees indeed say that there is no resurrection, neither messenger nor spirit, but the Pharisees are confessing both.)  
**23:9** Now there became a great outcry, and part of the scribes of the Pharisees stood up and were quarreling, saying, We are finding no evil in this man, but if a spirit or a messenger spoke to him, we should not quarrel against God.  
**23:10** Now <i>after</i> much dissension happened, the commander, was scared, lest Paul might be torn to shreds by them, <i>and</i> commanded the army to go-down and seize him from the midst of them, and lead him <i>back</i> into the encampment.  
**23:11** Now <i>when</i> the Lord stood by him the next night, he said, Paul, have courage; for as you <i>have</i> thoroughly testified concerning me in Jerusalem, so it is essential <i>for</i> you to also testify in Rome.  
**23:12** Now <i>after</i> it became day, some of the Jews made a conspiracy and they vowed <i>for</i> themselves, saying, <i>they were</i> neither to eat nor to drink until they should kill Paul.  
**23:13** Now they were more-than forty who had made this gang;  
**23:14** who came near to the high-priests and the elders and said, We vowed <i>that</i> ourselves <i>are</i> to be accursed, to taste nothing until we should kill Paul.  
**23:15** Therefore now, you°, together with the council, disclose° <i>it</i> to the commander that he should be led to you° the next-day, as <i>if</i> being about to investigate more accurately the things concerning him. Now we are ready to assassinate him before he draws near.  
**23:16** But Paul’s sister’s son, having heard of their <i>murderous</i> plot, came and entered into the encampment and reported to Paul.  
**23:17** Now Paul, having called one of the centurions, said, Lead this young-man away to the commander; for he has something to report to him.  
**23:18** Therefore indeed, he took him and led him to the commander and says, Paul the prisoner, having called me, asked me to lead this young-man to you, who has something to say to you.  
**23:19** Now the commander grabbed his hand and departed privately, and was inquiring, What is it that you have to report to me?  
**23:20** Now he said, The Jews themselves covenanted together to ask you that you should lead Paul into the <i>Jewish</i> council the next-day, as <i>if</i> being about to inquire more accurately something concerning him.  
**23:21** Therefore you, do not be persuaded by them; for more-than forty men out of them are plotting against him, who vowed <i>for</i> themselves neither to eat nor to drink until they should assassinate him, and they are ready now, waiting for the promise from you.  
**23:22** Therefore indeed, the commander dismissed the young-man, having commanded him to tell no one that you disclosed these things to me.<br>{May 25, 59 AD. Paul is sent to the political capital at Caesarea.}  
**23:23** And having called to him a certain two of the centurions, he said, Prepare° at the third hour of the night {i.e. 9:00 PM} two hundred soldiers and seventy horsemen and two hundred spearmen, that they should travel to Caesarea,  
**23:24** and animals to be present, in order that <i>after</i> they mounted Paul upon <i>one of them</i>, they might save him for Felix the governor.  
**23:25** <i>Now</i> he wrote a letter <i>in</i> this pattern containing:  
**23:26** Claudius Lysias to the most-excellent governor Felix. Hail <i>to you</i>.  
**23:27** <i>When</i> this man was taken by the Jews and being about to be assassinated by them, I stood up together with the soldiers and liberated him, having learned that he was a Roman.  
**23:28** But planning to know the accusation (because of which they were accusing him), I led him into their council;  
**23:29** whom I found to be accused concerning debates of their law, having nothing <i>in way of</i> an accusation worthy of death or of bonds.  
**23:30** But <i>after</i> it was divulged to me of a future pact by the Jews for the man, I promptly sent him to you, having also commanded his accusers to speak the things to him in front of you. Good-bye.  
**23:31** Therefore indeed, the soldiers, according to what had been commanded to them, took Paul and led him through the night to Antipatris.  
**23:32** But on the next-day, having permitted the horsemen to travel together with him, they returned to the encampment,  
**23:33** who, having entered into Caesarea, and having given over the letter to the governor, also presented Paul to him.  
**23:34** Now the governor read <i>the letter</i> and having asked: What province is he from? And having inquired: <i>he is</i> from Cilicia.  
**23:35** He was saying, I will hear you fully whenever your accusers also come. And he commanded him to be guarded in Herod’s palace.  

## 24장

**24:1** {May 30, 59 AD. Paul before Felix, a prisoner two years at Caesarea. In 59 AD Festus becomes governor of Judea.}<br>Now after five days, the high-priest Ananias with the elders and an orator came-down, someone <i>named</i> Tertullus, who disclosed to the governor <i>the charges</i> against Paul.  
**24:2** Now having been called, Tertullus began to accuse <i>Paul</i>, saying, <i>We are</i> obtaining much peace through you and public-reforms <i>are</i> happening to this nation through your forethought.  
**24:3** We are accepting it both in all things and everywhere, with all thanksgiving, most-excellent Felix.  
**24:4** But in order that I may not hinder you on more-than <i>this</i>, I plead with you by your gentleness to hear us briefly.  
**24:5** For we <i>have</i> found this man a plague, and <i>he is</i> moving <i>this</i> dissension to all the Jews throughout the inhabited-earth. And <i>he is</i> a ringleader of the sect of the Nazarenes;  
**24:6** who also attempted to profane the temple; whom we also took-hold of.  <i>And wished to be judged according to our law.</i>  
**24:7** <i>Now Lysias the commander passed by, with much violence, took Paul away out of our hands,</i>  
**24:8** <i>having commanded his accusers to come to you.</i> From whom, you will be able, having examined <i>him</i>, to fully know concerning all these things of which we are accusing him.  
**24:9** But the Jews themselves banded together in the attack, thus claiming these things to hold <i>truth</i>.  
**24:10** Now <i>after</i> the governor signaled to him to speak, Paul answered, Having known you are a judge to this nation out of many years <i>experience</i>. I am cheerfully making my defense of these things concerning myself.  
**24:11** You <i>surely</i> are able to know that it is not more-than twelve days to me from which <i>time</i> I went-up to worship in Jerusalem;  
**24:12** and they did not find me reasoning <i>angrily</i> with anyone, or making a hostile gathering of <i>the</i> crowd in the temple, nor in the synagogues, nor in the city,  
**24:13** nor are they able to present <i>things</i> concerning me<i>,</i> of which they are now accusing me.  
**24:14** But I confess this thing to you, that thus I am giving-divine service to the God of our forefathers according to The Way, which they call a sect, believing all things <i>which are</i> according to the law and which have been written in the prophets.  
**24:15** I have hope toward God, which these themselves also wait for, that there is about to be a resurrection of <i>the</i> dead, both of <i>the</i> righteous and unrighteous.  
**24:16** But I apply myself in this, having a guilt-free conscience always toward God and men.  
**24:17** Now through many years, I came doing charity and offerings to my nation;  
**24:18** in which they found me, having been purified in the temple, not with a crowd, nor with an uproar <i>with</i> some Jews from Asia.  
**24:19** It is essential <i>for</i> those to be here and to accuse <i>me</i> in front of you, if they have anything to <i>accuse</i> me.  
**24:20** Or let these <i>men</i> themselves say what wrongdoing they found in me <i>when</i> I stood in front of the council,  
**24:21** or concerning this one voice of which I cried out, standing among them. I am being judged by you° today concerning the resurrection of the dead.  
**24:22** But Felix, having heard these things and knowing more accurately concerning The Way, postponed <i>judging</i> them, <i>and</i> said, Whenever Lysias the commander comes-down, I will be investigating the things against you°.  
**24:23** And he himself commanded the centurion to guard Paul and <i>for him</i> to have relief and to forbid none of his own to attend or come near to him.  
**24:24** Now after some days, Felix came together with Drusilla, <i>his</i> wife, being a Jewess, and sent for Paul, and heard him concerning the faith in Christ.  
**24:25** But <i>while</i> reasoning concerning righteousness and self-control and the future judgment which will be, Felix became fearful, and answered, Travel on now, and <i>when</i> I <i>have</i> taken the next opportunity, I myself will call you.  
**24:26** (<i>While</i> hoping at the same time <i>that</i> monies will be given to him by Paul, that he might loose him.) Hence, he sent for him frequently, and was conversing with him.  
**24:27** But <i>after</i> two years were fulfilled, Felix received a successor, Porcius Festus, and wishing to lay up favors for himself with the Jews, Felix left Paul bound.  

## 25장

**25:1** {July, 61 AD. Paul’s appearance before Festus.}<br>Therefore after three days, having stepped into the province, Festus went-up to Jerusalem from Caesarea.  
**25:2** Now the high-priest and the foremost ones of the Jews disclosed to him <i>things</i> against Paul, and they were pleading with him,  
**25:3** asking for a favor against him, that he might send for him to <i>come to</i> Jerusalem; making a plot to assassinate him on the road.  
**25:4** Therefore indeed, Festus answered that Paul <i>is</i> to be kept in Caesarea, but he himself is about to travel out <i>there</i> shortly.  
**25:5** Therefore he says, The mighty ones among you° <i>have</i> gone-down together with <i>me</i> if there is anything <i>evil</i> in this man, let them accuse him.  
**25:6** Now having stayed among them more than ten days, he went-down to Caesarea. On the next-day having sat upon the judicial-seat, he commanded Paul to be led <i>before him</i>.  
**25:7** Now <i>after</i> he came, the Jews who had come-down from Jerusalem stood around him, bringing many and cruel accusations against Paul, which they were not strong-enough to show.  
**25:8** <i>Then Paul,</i> making his defense, <i>said</i>, Neither at the law of the Jews nor at the temple nor at Caesar, did I sin in anything.  
**25:9** But Festus, wishing to lay up a favor for himself with the Jews, answered and said to Paul, Are you willing, <i>after</i> having gone-up to Jerusalem, to be judged there concerning these things in front of me?  
**25:10** But Paul said, I am standing in front of Caesar’s judicial-seat, where it is essential for me to be judged. I did not wrong the Jews, as you also fully well know.  
**25:11** For if I indeed am wronging <i>anyone</i>, and have practiced anything worthy of death, I do not renounce to die, but if there is nothing of what these are accusing me; no one is able to grant me to them. I myself am appealing to Caesar.  
**25:12** Then Festus, having spoken together with the council, answered, You yourself have appealed <i>to be</i> in front of Caesar, <i>then</i> you will be traveling to Caesar.  
**25:13** Now <i>after</i> some days elapsed, Agrippa the King and Bernice arrived in Caesarea and greeted Festus.  
**25:14** Now as he was staying there many days, Festus submitted to the King, the things according to Paul<i>’s case</i>, saying, There is a certain man, a prisoner, who is left <i>behind</i> by Felix;  
**25:15** concerning whom, I myself came to Jerusalem, the high-priests and the elders of the Jews disclosed <i>things to me</i>, asking for a sentence against him.  
**25:16** To whom I answered, It is not a custom with Romans to grant <i>as a favor</i> any man to destruction, preferably before he who is accused, might have the accusers face to face, and might receive a chance for defense concerning the accusation.  
**25:17** Therefore, <i>after</i> they came together here, I did not postpone <i>their meeting</i>, but on the next <i>day</i>, having sat upon the judicial-seat, I commanded the man to be led <i>there</i>.  
**25:18** Concerning whom, <i>after</i> the accusers stood up, they were bringing no accusation of things <i>as</i> I was perceiving;  
**25:19** but they were having some debates with him concerning their own religion and concerning someone <i>named</i> Jesus, who had died, whom Paul was claiming to be living.  
**25:20** But I, being perplexed <i>as to</i> the debate concerning this, was saying <i>to him</i>, if he plans to travel to Jerusalem and to be judged there concerning these things.  
**25:21** But <i>after</i> Paul himself appealed to be kept for the decision of the Emperor, I commanded him to be kept until I should send him to Caesar.  
**25:22** But Agrippa said to Festus, I was also planning to hear from the man myself. Now he says, You will hear him on the next-day.  
**25:23** Therefore on the next-day, Agrippa and Bernice came with much pomp, and entered into the auditorium together with both, the commanders and men according to prominence, who are from the city, and <i>after</i> Festus commanded, Paul was led <i>before him</i>.  
**25:24** And Festus says, King Agrippa, and all who are here together with us, you° view this man, concerning whom all the multitude of the Jews petitioned me, both in Jerusalem and here, crying that it is essential for him to live no longer.  
**25:25** But I am comprehending that he has done nothing worthy of death, and I decided to send him, <i>since</i> he himself <i>had</i> also appealed to the Emperor;  
**25:26** concerning whom, I do not have any certainty <i>as what</i> to write to <i>my</i> lord. Hence, I led him in front of you° and especially in front of you, King Agrippa, that, <i>after his</i> examination <i>has</i> happened, I may have something to write.  
**25:27** For it seems unreasonable to me <i>to be</i> sending a prisoner, and not to signify the accusations against him.  

## 26장

**26:1** {Aug., AD 61. Paul before Agrippa.}<br>Now Agrippa said to Paul, It is permitted for you to speak on behalf of yourself. Then Paul stretched out his hand and was making his defense.  
**26:2** Concerning all of which I am accused by the Jews, King Agrippa, I have deemed myself fortunate today, being about to make <i>my</i> defense in front of you;  
**26:3** especially <i>because</i> you are an expert of all things according to the <i>things of the</i> Jews, both customs and debates. Hence, I beseech of you to hear me patiently.  
**26:4** Therefore my lifestyle indeed which happened from my youth up, from the beginning in my own nation <i>and</i> in Jerusalem, all the Jews know <i>this</i>.  
**26:5** If they are willing to testify, (knowing me beforehand, from the start), that I lived <i>as</i> a Pharisee according to the strictest sect of our religion.  
**26:6** And now I am standing <i>here</i>, being judged against the hope of the promise which came by God to the fathers;  
**26:7** to which our twelve tribes, intensely giving-divine service to <i>God</i> night and day, are hoping to achieve of which hope I am accused by <i>the</i> Jews, <i>O</i> king Agrippa!  
**26:8** Why is it judged <i>as</i> unbelievable with you°, if God is raising the dead?  
**26:9** Therefore I indeed thought in myself, it is essential to practice many things adverse to the name of Jesus the Nazarene.  
**26:10** I also did this in Jerusalem, and I locked up many of the holy-ones in prisons, having received authority from the high-priests, and <i>while</i> being assassinated, I brought my voting-pebble {i.e. vote} against them.  
**26:11** And punishing them often throughout all the synagogues, I was urging <i>them</i> to blaspheme, and being exceptionally enraged with them, I was persecuting <i>them</i>, insofar as <i>possible,</i> even to the cities outside <i>our nation</i>.<br>{Examples of those who are saved in the New Testament: Acts 2:14-42; 8:5-13; 8:30-39; 10:34-48; 16:13-15; 16:25-34; 18:8a; 18:8b; 19:1-7; Saul/Apostle Paul: 9:17-18, 22:6-16, 26:12-18.}  
**26:12** In which I also traveled to Damascus with the authority and commission from the high-priests.  
**26:13** On the road in <i>the</i> middle of <i>the</i> day, <i>O</i> king, I saw a light from heaven beyond the brilliance of the sun, which shone around me and those who traveled together with me.  
**26:14** Now <i>after</i> all fell down to the earth, I heard a voice speaking to me and saying in the Hebrew language, Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me? It is harsh for you to kick toward cattle-prods.  
**26:15** But I said, Who are you, Lord? But the Lord said, I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting.  
**26:16** But <i>after</i> you <i>have</i> rose up and <i>have</i> stood upon your feet; for to this <i>end</i>, I was seen by you, to assign you <i>to be</i> an attendant and a witness both of the things which you saw, and of the things which will be seen by you.  
**26:17** <i>I will be</i> liberating you from the <i>Jewish</i> people and from the Gentiles, to whom I am sending you,  
**26:18** to open their eyes, to turn them from darkness, into light, and from the authority of the Adversary to God, <i>in order for</i> them to receive forgiveness of sins, and an inheritance among those who have been made holy in faith, the <i>faith</i> in me.  
**26:19** Hence, O King Agrippa, I did not become disobedient to the heavenly vision.  
**26:20** But <i>I was</i> proclaiming first to them in Damascus, and in Jerusalem, and <i>then</i> to all the region of Judea and to the Gentiles, to repent and to turn to God, practicing works worthy of repentance.  
**26:21** Because of these things, the Jews took me in the temple and were attempting to exterminate <i>me</i>.  
**26:22** Therefore, having obtained the assistance which <i>is</i> from God, I am standing to this day, testifying both to <i>the</i> little and to <i>the</i> great, saying nothing outside of what both the prophets and Moses spoke that was about to happen.  
**26:23** If the Christ <i>was</i> ordained to suffer, if <i>the Christ was</i> first from the resurrection of the dead, <i>then</i> he is about to proclaim light to the <i>Jewish</i> people and to the Gentiles.  
**26:24** Now he made his defense <i>with</i> these things. Festus said with a loud voice, Paul, you are insane; your much scholarship is turning you into <i>the</i> insane.  
**26:25** But <i>Paul</i> says, I am not insane, most-excellent Festus, but I am speaking out <i>the</i> words of truth and self-discipline.  
**26:26** For the king knows concerning these things, with whom <i>I am</i> also speaking boldly; for I am telling: I am persuaded <i>that</i> not any of these things were eluding him; for this thing has not been done in a corner.  
**26:27** King Agrippa, Do you believe the prophets? I know that you believe.  
**26:28** But Agrippa said to Paul, Are you persuading me to become a Christian in few <i>words</i>?  
**26:29** But Paul said, I would pray to God, that both in a few <i>words</i> or in many <i>words</i>, not only you, but also all who hear me today, <i>are</i> to become such: what sort of <i>person</i> I am, except-for these bonds.  
**26:30** And <i>after Paul</i> said these things, the king and the governor and Bernice and those who were sitting together with them stood up;  
**26:31** and having departed, they were speaking to one another, saying, This man is practicing nothing worthy of death or bonds.  
**26:32** But Agrippa said to Festus, This man was able to be released, if he had not appealed to Caesar.  

## 27장

**27:1** {Aug. 21 - Nov. 1, 61 AD. Mediterranean Sea. The voyage to Rome.}<br>Now as it was decided <i>for</i> us to sail toward Italy, and they were giving to a centurion, Julius by name, from <i>the</i> Emperor’s cohort, Paul and some other prisoners.  
**27:2** Now having stepped <i>on board</i>, in an Adramyttium ship, which is about to sail throughout the places <i>along</i> Asia, we set-sail, <i>also</i> being together with us <i>was</i> Aristarchus, a Macedonian, a Thessalonian.  
**27:3** And we brought <i>the ship</i> into Sidon in another <i>day</i> and Julius treated Paul humanely and permitted <i>him</i> to obtain care <i>after</i> he traveled to <i>his</i> friends.  
**27:4** And having set-sail to sea from there, we sailed under the lee of Cyprus, because the winds were adverse.  
**27:5** And having sailed through the sea against Cilicia and Pamphylia, we came down to Myra, <i>a city</i> of Lycia.  
**27:6** And the centurion found a ship from Alexandria there sailing to Italy, and he had us step onto it.  
**27:7** Now at a considerable number of days, <i>we were</i> voyaging slowly and happened <i>to be</i> against Cnidus with difficulty; the wind <i>was</i> not permitting us <i>to land</i>. We sailed under the lee of Crete against Salmone;  
**27:8** and coasting-along it with difficulty, we came to a certain place being called Good Harbors; which was near the city <i>of</i> Lasea.  
**27:9** Now <i>after</i> a considerable <i>amount</i> of time <i>had</i> elapsed and the voyage was already dangerous, because the Fast had already passed, Paul was advising <i>them</i>,  
**27:10** saying to them, Men, I view that the voyage is about to be with disaster and much damage, not only of the load and of the ship, but also of our lives.  
**27:11** But the centurion was being persuaded by the helmsman and by the ship-owner, rather than by the things being spoken by Paul.  
**27:12** But the harbor, being unfit for a wintering, most <i>of the</i> council purposed to set-sail to sea from there, if somehow they might be able to winter, <i>after</i> they arrived in Phoenix, a harbor of Crete, <i>while</i> looking down <i>the</i> northwest and down <i>the</i> southwest.  
**27:13** Now <i>after wind</i> from <i>the</i> south blew softly, having thought to have taken-hold of the<i>ir</i> purpose, they lifted <i>the anchor</i> up and were coasting-along, closer to Crete.  
**27:14** But not long after, a typhoon wind, being called Euroclydon, was cast against her;  
**27:15** and <i>after</i> the ship was seized and it was not able to head-into the wind, having given <i>her to the wind</i>, we were being carried along.  
**27:16** Now having ran under the lee of a certain isle called Clauda, we were strong-enough to become skippers of the boat with difficulty;  
**27:17** and having lifted it up, they were using cables, undergirding the ship, and fearing lest they might fall into the <i>sandbars of</i> Syrtis, they lowered the tackling and so we were being carried along.  
**27:18** But being extremely storm-tossed, the next <i>day</i> they were making a jettison <i>of the cargo</i>;  
**27:19** and the third <i>day</i> they tossed out the tackling of the ship with their own hands.  
**27:20** But <i>while</i> neither sun nor stars are appearing upon most days and not <i>just</i> a small storm <i>was</i> laying <i>down</i> upon <i>us,</i> all our hope to be saved was furthermore being taken away.  
**27:21** Now they are without much food {i.e. almost starving} , then Paul, standing in the midst of them, said, O men, it was indeed essential <i>to have</i> obeyed me <i>and</i> to not set-sail away from Crete and <i>then</i> to gain this disaster and damage.  
**27:22** And hereafter, I am advising you° to be cheerful; for <i>there</i> will be no termination of life from <i>among</i> you°, however of the ship, <i>yes</i>.  
**27:23** For there stood beside me this night a messenger of the God, whose I am, to whom I also am giving-divine service to,  
**27:24** saying, Do not fear, Paul; it is essential <i>for</i> you to stand-before Caesar and behold, God has granted to you all those who are sailing with you.  
**27:25** Hence, be° cheerful, men; for I believe God, that it will be so according to the manner it has been spoken to me.  
**27:26** But it is essential for us to fall onto a certain island.  
**27:27** Now as it became the fourteenth night, being carried <i>to and fro</i> in the Adriatic <i>Sea</i>, in the middle of the night, the sailors were perceiving some region to be preceding them.  
**27:28** And having sounded, they found twenty fathoms, and <i>after</i> a little bit <i>of</i> an interval <i>of time</i>, having sounded again, they found fifteen fathoms.  
**27:29** And fearing lest we might fall into rough places, they tossed <i>the</i> four anchors from the stern and was praying <i>for</i> it to become day.  
**27:30** But the sailors <i>were</i> seeking to flee out of the ship and having lowered the boat into the sea in pretext, as <i>though</i> they are about to heave out anchors from the prow,  
**27:31** Paul said to the centurion and to the soldiers, If these <i>sailors</i> do not remain in the ship, you° are not able to be saved.  
**27:32** Then the soldiers cut off the ropes of the boat and permitted her to fall.  
**27:33** Now till it was about to become day, Paul was encouraging <i>them</i> all to take some nourishment, saying, Today is the fourteenth day that you° were expecting <i>to arrive</i>; you° are continuing without food, having taken nothing <i>to eat</i> for yourselves.  
**27:34** Hence I am encouraging you° to take some nourishment; for this is for your° salvation; for not a hair will be falling from the head of <i>any</i> one of you.  
**27:35** Now having said these things and having taken bread, he gave-thanks to God in the sight of all, and he broke <i>it</i> and began to eat.  
**27:36** <i>When</i> all became cheerful, they themselves also took nourishment.  
**27:37** Now we were in the ship, all two hundred and seventy-six souls.
