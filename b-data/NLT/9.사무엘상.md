# 9.사무엘상 (NLT)



## 1장

**1:1** There was a man named Elkanah who lived in Ramah in the region of Zuph<sup>①</sup> in the hill country of Ephraim. He was the son of Jeroham, son of Elihu, son of Tohu, son of Zuph, of Ephraim.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in Greek version; Hebrew reads <i>in Ramathaim-zophim;</i> compare <a name='B:90 1:19'>1:19.  
**1:2** Elkanah had two wives, Hannah and Peninnah. Peninnah had children, but Hannah did not.  
**1:3** Each year Elkanah would travel to Shiloh to worship and sacrifice to the LORD of Heaven’s Armies at the Tabernacle. The priests of the LORD at that time were the two sons of Eli—Hophni and Phinehas.  
**1:4** On the days Elkanah presented his sacrifice, he would give portions of the meat to Peninnah and each of her children.  
**1:5** And though he loved Hannah, he would give her only one choice portion<sup>①</sup> because the LORD had given her no children.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>And because he loved Hannah, he would give her a choice portion.</i> The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain.  
**1:6** So Peninnah would taunt Hannah and make fun of her because the LORD had kept her from having children.  
**1:7** Year after year it was the same—Peninnah would taunt Hannah as they went to the Tabernacle.<sup>①</sup> Each time, Hannah would be reduced to tears and would not even eat.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>the house of the LORD;</i> also in <a name='B:90 1:24'>1:24.  
**1:8** “Why are you crying, Hannah?” Elkanah would ask. “Why aren’t you eating? Why be downhearted just because you have no children? You have me—isn’t that better than having ten sons?”  
**1:9** Once after a sacrificial meal at Shiloh, Hannah got up and went to pray. Eli the priest was sitting at his customary place beside the entrance of the Tabernacle.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>the Temple of the LORD.</i>  
**1:10** Hannah was in deep anguish, crying bitterly as she prayed to the LORD.  
**1:11** And she made this vow: “O LORD of Heaven’s Armies, if you will look upon my sorrow and answer my prayer and give me a son, then I will give him back to you. He will be yours for his entire lifetime, and as a sign that he has been dedicated to the LORD, his hair will never be cut. <sup>①</sup>”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Some manuscripts add <i>He will drink neither wine nor intoxicants.</i>  
**1:12** As she was praying to the LORD, Eli watched her.  
**1:13** Seeing her lips moving but hearing no sound, he thought she had been drinking.  
**1:14** “Must you come here drunk?” he demanded. “Throw away your wine!”  
**1:15** “Oh no, sir!” she replied. “I haven’t been drinking wine or anything stronger. But I am very discouraged, and I was pouring out my heart to the LORD.  
**1:16** Don’t think I am a wicked woman! For I have been praying out of great anguish and sorrow.”  
**1:17** “In that case,” Eli said, “go in peace! May the God of Israel grant the request you have asked of him.”  
**1:18** “Oh, thank you, sir!” she exclaimed. Then she went back and began to eat again, and she was no longer sad.  
**1:19** The entire family got up early the next morning and went to worship the LORD once more. Then they returned home to Ramah. When Elkanah slept with Hannah, the LORD remembered her plea,  
**1:20** and in due time she gave birth to a son. She named him Samuel,<sup>①</sup> for she said, “I asked the LORD for him.”<br>→ <sup>①</sup><i>Samuel</i> sounds like the Hebrew term for “asked of God” or “heard by God.”  
**1:21** The next year Elkanah and his family went on their annual trip to offer a sacrifice to the LORD and to keep his vow.  
**1:22** But Hannah did not go. She told her husband, “Wait until the boy is weaned. Then I will take him to the Tabernacle and leave him there with the LORD permanently. <sup>①</sup>”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Some manuscripts add <i>I will offer him as a Nazirite for all time.</i>  
**1:23** “Whatever you think is best,” Elkanah agreed. “Stay here for now, and may the LORD help you keep your promise. <sup>①</sup>” So she stayed home and nursed the boy until he was weaned.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in Dead Sea Scrolls and Greek version; Masoretic Text reads <i>may the LORD keep his promise.</i>  
**1:24** When the child was weaned, Hannah took him to the Tabernacle in Shiloh. They brought along a three-year-old bull<sup>①</sup> for the sacrifice and a basket<sup>②</sup> of flour and some wine.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in Dead Sea Scrolls, Greek and Syriac versions; Masoretic Text reads <i>three bulls.</i> <sup>②</sup>Hebrew <i>and an ephah</i> [20 quarts or 22 liters].  
**1:25** After sacrificing the bull, they brought the boy to Eli.  
**1:26** “Sir, do you remember me?” Hannah asked. “I am the very woman who stood here several years ago praying to the LORD.  
**1:27** I asked the LORD to give me this boy, and he has granted my request.  
**1:28** Now I am giving him to the LORD, and he will belong to the LORD his whole life.” And they<sup>①</sup> worshiped the LORD there.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>he.</i>  

## 2장

**2:1** Then Hannah prayed:<br>“My heart rejoices in the LORD!<br>The LORD has made me strong.<sup>①</sup><br>Now I have an answer for my enemies;<br>I rejoice because you rescued me.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>has exalted my horn.</i>  
**2:2** No one is holy like the LORD!<br>There is no one besides you;<br>there is no Rock like our God.  
**2:3** “Stop acting so proud and haughty!<br>Don’t speak with such arrogance!<br>For the LORD is a God who knows what you have done;<br>he will judge your actions.  
**2:4** The bow of the mighty is now broken,<br>and those who stumbled are now strong.  
**2:5** Those who were well fed are now starving,<br>and those who were starving are now full.<br>The childless woman now has seven children,<br>and the woman with many children wastes away.  
**2:6** The LORD gives both death and life;<br>he brings some down to the grave<sup>①</sup> but raises others up.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>to Sheol.</i>  
**2:7** The LORD makes some poor and others rich;<br>he brings some down and lifts others up.  
**2:8** He lifts the poor from the dust<br>and the needy from the garbage dump.<br>He sets them among princes,<br>placing them in seats of honor.<br>For all the earth is the LORD’s,<br>and he has set the world in order.  
**2:9** “He will protect his faithful ones,<br>but the wicked will disappear in darkness.<br>No one will succeed by strength alone.  
**2:10** Those who fight against the LORD will be shattered.<br>He thunders against them from heaven;<br>the LORD judges throughout the earth.<br>He gives power to his king;<br>he increases the strength<sup>①</sup> of his anointed one.”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>he exalts the horn.</i>  
**2:11** Then Elkanah returned home to Ramah without Samuel. And the boy served the LORD by assisting Eli the priest.  
**2:12** Now the sons of Eli were scoundrels who had no respect for the LORD  
**2:13** or for their duties as priests. Whenever anyone offered a sacrifice, Eli’s sons would send over a servant with a three-pronged fork. While the meat of the sacrificed animal was still boiling,  
**2:14** the servant would stick the fork into the pot and demand that whatever it brought up be given to Eli’s sons. All the Israelites who came to worship at Shiloh were treated this way.  
**2:15** Sometimes the servant would come even before the animal’s fat had been burned on the altar. He would demand raw meat before it had been boiled so that it could be used for roasting.  
**2:16** The man offering the sacrifice might reply, “Take as much as you want, but the fat must be burned first.” Then the servant would demand, “No, give it to me now, or I’ll take it by force.”  
**2:17** So the sin of these young men was very serious in the LORD’s sight, for they treated the LORD’s offerings with contempt.  
**2:18** But Samuel, though he was only a boy, served the LORD. He wore a linen garment like that of a priest.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>He wore a linen ephod.</i>  
**2:19** Each year his mother made a small coat for him and brought it to him when she came with her husband for the sacrifice.  
**2:20** Before they returned home, Eli would bless Elkanah and his wife and say, “May the LORD give you other children to take the place of this one she gave to the LORD. <sup>①</sup>”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in Dead Sea Scrolls and Greek version; Masoretic Text reads <i>this one he requested of the LORD.</i>  
**2:21** And the LORD blessed Hannah, and she conceived and gave birth to three sons and two daughters. Meanwhile, Samuel grew up in the presence of the LORD.  
**2:22** Now Eli was very old, but he was aware of what his sons were doing to the people of Israel. He knew, for instance, that his sons were seducing the young women who assisted at the entrance of the Tabernacle.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>Tent of Meeting.</i> Some manuscripts lack this entire sentence.  
**2:23** Eli said to them, “I have been hearing reports from all the people about the wicked things you are doing. Why do you keep sinning?  
**2:24** You must stop, my sons! The reports I hear among the LORD’s people are not good.  
**2:25** If someone sins against another person, God<sup>①</sup> can mediate for the guilty party. But if someone sins against the LORD, who can intercede?” But Eli’s sons wouldn’t listen to their father, for the LORD was already planning to put them to death.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>the judges.</i>  
**2:26** Meanwhile, the boy Samuel grew taller and grew in favor with the LORD and with the people.  
**2:27** One day a man of God came to Eli and gave him this message from the LORD: “I revealed myself<sup>①</sup> to your ancestors when they were Pharaoh’s slaves in Egypt.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in Greek and Syriac versions; Hebrew reads <i>Did I reveal myself.</i>  
**2:28** I chose your ancestor Aaron<sup>①</sup> from among all the tribes of Israel to be my priest, to offer sacrifices on my altar, to burn incense, and to wear the priestly vest<sup>②</sup> as he served me. And I assigned the sacrificial offerings to you priests.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>your father.</i> <sup>②</sup>Hebrew <i>an ephod.</i>  
**2:29** So why do you scorn my sacrifices and offerings? Why do you give your sons more honor than you give me—for you and they have become fat from the best offerings of my people Israel!  
**2:30** “Therefore, the LORD, the God of Israel, says: I promised that your branch of the tribe of Levi<sup>①</sup> would always be my priests. But I will honor those who honor me, and I will despise those who think lightly of me.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>that your house and your father’s house.</i>  
**2:31** The time is coming when I will put an end to your family, so it will no longer serve as my priests. All the members of your family will die before their time. None will reach old age.  
**2:32** You will watch with envy as I pour out prosperity on the people of Israel. But no members of your family will ever live out their days.  
**2:33** The few not cut off from serving at my altar will survive, but only so their eyes can go blind and their hearts break, and their children will die a violent death.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in Dead Sea Scrolls and Greek version, which read <i>die by the sword;</i> Masoretic Text reads <i>die like mortals.</i>  
**2:34** And to prove that what I have said will come true, I will cause your two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, to die on the same day!  
**2:35** “Then I will raise up a faithful priest who will serve me and do what I desire. I will establish his family, and they will be priests to my anointed kings forever.  
**2:36** Then all of your surviving family will bow before him, begging for money and food. ‘Please,’ they will say, ‘give us jobs among the priests so we will have enough to eat.’”  

## 3장

**3:1** Meanwhile, the boy Samuel served the LORD by assisting Eli. Now in those days messages from the LORD were very rare, and visions were quite uncommon.  
**3:2** One night Eli, who was almost blind by now, had gone to bed.  
**3:3** The lamp of God had not yet gone out, and Samuel was sleeping in the Tabernacle<sup>①</sup> near the Ark of God.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>the Temple of the LORD.</i>  
**3:4** Suddenly the LORD called out, “Samuel!” “Yes?” Samuel replied. “What is it?”  
**3:5** He got up and ran to Eli. “Here I am. Did you call me?” “I didn’t call you,” Eli replied. “Go back to bed.” So he did.  
**3:6** Then the LORD called out again, “Samuel!” Again Samuel got up and went to Eli. “Here I am. Did you call me?” “I didn’t call you, my son,” Eli said. “Go back to bed.”  
**3:7** Samuel did not yet know the LORD because he had never had a message from the LORD before.  
**3:8** So the LORD called a third time, and once more Samuel got up and went to Eli. “Here I am. Did you call me?” Then Eli realized it was the LORD who was calling the boy.  
**3:9** So he said to Samuel, “Go and lie down again, and if someone calls again, say, ‘Speak, LORD, your servant is listening.’” So Samuel went back to bed.  
**3:10** And the LORD came and called as before, “Samuel! Samuel!” And Samuel replied, “Speak, your servant is listening.”  
**3:11** Then the LORD said to Samuel, “I am about to do a shocking thing in Israel.  
**3:12** I am going to carry out all my threats against Eli and his family, from beginning to end.  
**3:13** I have warned him that judgment is coming upon his family forever, because his sons are blaspheming God<sup>①</sup> and he hasn’t disciplined them.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in Greek version; Hebrew reads <i>his sons have made themselves contemptible.</i>  
**3:14** So I have vowed that the sins of Eli and his sons will never be forgiven by sacrifices or offerings.”  
**3:15** Samuel stayed in bed until morning, then got up and opened the doors of the Tabernacle<sup>①</sup> as usual. He was afraid to tell Eli what the LORD had said to him.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>the house of the LORD.</i>  
**3:16** But Eli called out to him, “Samuel, my son.” “Here I am,” Samuel replied.  
**3:17** “What did the LORD say to you? Tell me everything. And may God strike you and even kill you if you hide anything from me!”  
**3:18** So Samuel told Eli everything; he didn’t hold anything back. “It is the LORD’s will,” Eli replied. “Let him do what he thinks best.”  
**3:19** As Samuel grew up, the LORD was with him, and everything Samuel said proved to be reliable.  
**3:20** And all Israel, from Dan in the north to Beersheba in the south, knew that Samuel was confirmed as a prophet of the LORD.  
**3:21** The LORD continued to appear at Shiloh and gave messages to Samuel there at the Tabernacle.  

## 4장

**4:1** And Samuel’s words went out to all the people of Israel.<br>At that time Israel was at war with the Philistines. The Israelite army was camped near Ebenezer, and the Philistines were at Aphek.  
**4:2** The Philistines attacked and defeated the army of Israel, killing 4,000 men.  
**4:3** After the battle was over, the troops retreated to their camp, and the elders of Israel asked, “Why did the LORD allow us to be defeated by the Philistines?” Then they said, “Let’s bring the Ark of the Covenant of the LORD from Shiloh. If we carry it into battle with us, it<sup>①</sup> will save us from our enemies.”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>he.</i>  
**4:4** So they sent men to Shiloh to bring the Ark of the Covenant of the LORD of Heaven’s Armies, who is enthroned between the cherubim. Hophni and Phinehas, the sons of Eli, were also there with the Ark of the Covenant of God.  
**4:5** When all the Israelites saw the Ark of the Covenant of the LORD coming into the camp, their shout of joy was so loud it made the ground shake!  
**4:6** “What’s going on?” the Philistines asked. “What’s all the shouting about in the Hebrew camp?” When they were told it was because the Ark of the LORD had arrived,  
**4:7** they panicked. “The gods have<sup>①</sup> come into their camp!” they cried. “This is a disaster! We have never had to face anything like this before!<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>A god has.</i>  
**4:8** Help! Who can save us from these mighty gods of Israel? They are the same gods who destroyed the Egyptians with plagues when Israel was in the wilderness.  
**4:9** Fight as never before, Philistines! If you don’t, we will become the Hebrews’ slaves just as they have been ours! Stand up like men and fight!”  
**4:10** So the Philistines fought desperately, and Israel was defeated again. The slaughter was great; 30,000 Israelite soldiers died that day. The survivors turned and fled to their tents.  
**4:11** The Ark of God was captured, and Hophni and Phinehas, the two sons of Eli, were killed.  
**4:12** A man from the tribe of Benjamin ran from the battlefield and arrived at Shiloh later that same day. He had torn his clothes and put dust on his head to show his grief.  
**4:13** Eli was waiting beside the road to hear the news of the battle, for his heart trembled for the safety of the Ark of God. When the messenger arrived and told what had happened, an outcry resounded throughout the town.  
**4:14** “What is all the noise about?” Eli asked. The messenger rushed over to Eli,  
**4:15** who was ninety-eight years old and blind.  
**4:16** He said to Eli, “I have just come from the battlefield—I was there this very day.” “What happened, my son?” Eli demanded.  
**4:17** “Israel has been defeated by the Philistines,” the messenger replied. “The people have been slaughtered, and your two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, were also killed. And the Ark of God has been captured.”  
**4:18** When the messenger mentioned what had happened to the Ark of God, Eli fell backward from his seat beside the gate. He broke his neck and died, for he was old and overweight. He had been Israel’s judge for forty years.  
**4:19** Eli’s daughter-in-law, the wife of Phinehas, was pregnant and near her time of delivery. When she heard that the Ark of God had been captured and that her father-in-law and husband were dead, she went into labor and gave birth.  
**4:20** She died in childbirth, but before she passed away the midwives tried to encourage her. “Don’t be afraid,” they said. “You have a baby boy!” But she did not answer or pay attention to them.  
**4:21** She named the child Ichabod (which means “Where is the glory?”), for she said, “Israel’s glory is gone.” She named him this because the Ark of God had been captured and because her father-in-law and husband were dead.  
**4:22** Then she said, “The glory has departed from Israel, for the Ark of God has been captured.”  

## 5장

**5:1** After the Philistines captured the Ark of God, they took it from the battleground at Ebenezer to the town of Ashdod.  
**5:2** They carried the Ark of God into the temple of Dagon and placed it beside an idol of Dagon.  
**5:3** But when the citizens of Ashdod went to see it the next morning, Dagon had fallen with his face to the ground in front of the Ark of the LORD! So they took Dagon and put him in his place again.  
**5:4** But the next morning the same thing happened—Dagon had fallen face down before the Ark of the LORD again. This time his head and hands had broken off and were lying in the doorway. Only the trunk of his body was left intact.  
**5:5** That is why to this day neither the priests of Dagon nor anyone who enters the temple of Dagon in Ashdod will step on its threshold.  
**5:6** Then the LORD’s heavy hand struck the people of Ashdod and the nearby villages with a plague of tumors.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Greek version and Latin Vulgate read <i>tumors; and rats appeared in their land, and death and destruction were throughout the city.</i>  
**5:7** When the people realized what was happening, they cried out, “We can’t keep the Ark of the God of Israel here any longer! He is against us! We will all be destroyed along with Dagon, our god.”  
**5:8** So they called together the rulers of the Philistine towns and asked, “What should we do with the Ark of the God of Israel?” The rulers discussed it and replied, “Move it to the town of Gath.” So they moved the Ark of the God of Israel to Gath.  
**5:9** But when the Ark arrived at Gath, the LORD’s heavy hand fell on its men, young and old; he struck them with a plague of tumors, and there was a great panic.  
**5:10** So they sent the Ark of God to the town of Ekron, but when the people of Ekron saw it coming they cried out, “They are bringing the Ark of the God of Israel here to kill us, too!”  
**5:11** The people summoned the Philistine rulers again and begged them, “Please send the Ark of the God of Israel back to its own country, or it<sup>①</sup> will kill us all.” For the deadly plague from God had already begun, and great fear was sweeping across the town.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>he.</i>  
**5:12** Those who didn’t die were afflicted with tumors; and the cry from the town rose to heaven.  

## 6장

**6:1** The Ark of the LORD remained in Philistine territory seven months in all.  
**6:2** Then the Philistines called in their priests and diviners and asked them, “What should we do about the Ark of the LORD? Tell us how to return it to its own country.”  
**6:3** “Send the Ark of the God of Israel back with a gift,” they were told. “Send a guilt offering so the plague will stop. Then, if you are healed, you will know it was his hand that caused the plague.”  
**6:4** “What sort of guilt offering should we send?” they asked. And they were told, “Since the plague has struck both you and your five rulers, make five gold tumors and five gold rats, just like those that have ravaged your land.  
**6:5** Make these things to show honor to the God of Israel. Perhaps then he will stop afflicting you, your gods, and your land.  
**6:6** Don’t be stubborn and rebellious as Pharaoh and the Egyptians were. By the time God was finished with them, they were eager to let Israel go.  
**6:7** “Now build a new cart, and find two cows that have just given birth to calves. Make sure the cows have never been yoked to a cart. Hitch the cows to the cart, but shut their calves away from them in a pen.  
**6:8** Put the Ark of the LORD on the cart, and beside it place a chest containing the gold rats and gold tumors you are sending as a guilt offering. Then let the cows go wherever they want.  
**6:9** If they cross the border of our land and go to Beth-shemesh, we will know it was the LORD who brought this great disaster upon us. If they don’t, we will know it was not his hand that caused the plague. It came simply by chance.”  
**6:10** So these instructions were carried out. Two cows were hitched to the cart, and their newborn calves were shut up in a pen.  
**6:11** Then the Ark of the LORD and the chest containing the gold rats and gold tumors were placed on the cart.  
**6:12** And sure enough, without veering off in other directions, the cows went straight along the road toward Beth-shemesh, lowing as they went. The Philistine rulers followed them as far as the border of Beth-shemesh.  
**6:13** The people of Beth-shemesh were harvesting wheat in the valley, and when they saw the Ark, they were overjoyed!  
**6:14** The cart came into the field of a man named Joshua and stopped beside a large rock. So the people broke up the wood of the cart for a fire and killed the cows and sacrificed them to the LORD as a burnt offering.  
**6:15** Several men of the tribe of Levi lifted the Ark of the LORD and the chest containing the gold rats and gold tumors from the cart and placed them on the large rock. Many sacrifices and burnt offerings were offered to the LORD that day by the people of Beth-shemesh.  
**6:16** The five Philistine rulers watched all this and then returned to Ekron that same day.  
**6:17** The five gold tumors sent by the Philistines as a guilt offering to the LORD were gifts from the rulers of Ashdod, Gaza, Ashkelon, Gath, and Ekron.  
**6:18** The five gold rats represented the five Philistine towns and their surrounding villages, which were controlled by the five rulers. The large rock<sup>①</sup> at Beth-shemesh, where they set the Ark of the LORD, still stands in the field of Joshua as a witness to what happened there.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in some Hebrew manuscripts and Greek version; most Hebrew manuscripts read <i>great meadow</i> or <i>Abel-haggedolah.</i>  
**6:19** But the LORD killed seventy men<sup>①</sup> from Beth-shemesh because they looked into the Ark of the LORD. And the people mourned greatly because of what the LORD had done.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in a few Hebrew manuscripts; most Hebrew manuscripts read <i>70 men, 50,000 men.</i> Perhaps the text should be understood to read <i>the LORD killed 70 men and 50 oxen.</i>  
**6:20** “Who is able to stand in the presence of the LORD, this holy God?” they cried out. “Where can we send the Ark from here?”  
**6:21** So they sent messengers to the people at Kiriath-jearim and told them, “The Philistines have returned the Ark of the LORD. Come here and get it!”  

## 7장

**7:1** So the men of Kiriath-jearim came to get the Ark of the LORD. They took it to the hillside home of Abinadab and ordained Eleazar, his son, to be in charge of it.  
**7:2** The Ark remained in Kiriath-jearim for a long time—twenty years in all. During that time all Israel mourned because it seemed the LORD had abandoned them.  
**7:3** Then Samuel said to all the people of Israel, “If you want to return to the LORD with all your hearts, get rid of your foreign gods and your images of Ashtoreth. Turn your hearts to the LORD and obey him alone; then he will rescue you from the Philistines.”  
**7:4** So the Israelites got rid of their images of Baal and Ashtoreth and worshiped only the LORD.  
**7:5** Then Samuel told them, “Gather all of Israel to Mizpah, and I will pray to the LORD for you.”  
**7:6** So they gathered at Mizpah and, in a great ceremony, drew water from a well and poured it out before the LORD. They also went without food all day and confessed that they had sinned against the LORD. (It was at Mizpah that Samuel became Israel’s judge.)  
**7:7** When the Philistine rulers heard that Israel had gathered at Mizpah, they mobilized their army and advanced. The Israelites were badly frightened when they learned that the Philistines were approaching.  
**7:8** “Don’t stop pleading with the LORD our God to save us from the Philistines!” they begged Samuel.  
**7:9** So Samuel took a young lamb and offered it to the LORD as a whole burnt offering. He pleaded with the LORD to help Israel, and the LORD answered him.  
**7:10** Just as Samuel was sacrificing the burnt offering, the Philistines arrived to attack Israel. But the LORD spoke with a mighty voice of thunder from heaven that day, and the Philistines were thrown into such confusion that the Israelites defeated them.  
**7:11** The men of Israel chased them from Mizpah to a place below Beth-car, slaughtering them all along the way.  
**7:12** Samuel then took a large stone and placed it between the towns of Mizpah and Jeshanah.<sup>①</sup> He named it Ebenezer (which means “the stone of help”), for he said, “Up to this point the LORD has helped us!”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in Greek and Syriac versions; Hebrew reads <i>Shen.</i>  
**7:13** So the Philistines were subdued and didn’t invade Israel again for some time. And throughout Samuel’s lifetime, the LORD’s powerful hand was raised against the Philistines.  
**7:14** The Israelite villages near Ekron and Gath that the Philistines had captured were restored to Israel, along with the rest of the territory that the Philistines had taken. And there was peace between Israel and the Amorites in those days.

**25:13** “Get your swords!” was David’s reply as he strapped on his own. Then 400 men started off with David, and 200 remained behind to guard their equipment.  
**25:14** Meanwhile, one of Nabal’s servants went to Abigail and told her, “David sent messengers from the wilderness to greet our master, but he screamed insults at them.  
**25:15** These men have been very good to us, and we never suffered any harm from them. Nothing was stolen from us the whole time they were with us.  
**25:16** In fact, day and night they were like a wall of protection to us and the sheep.  
**25:17** You need to know this and figure out what to do, for there is going to be trouble for our master and his whole family. He’s so ill-tempered that no one can even talk to him!”  
**25:18** Abigail wasted no time. She quickly gathered 200 loaves of bread, two wineskins full of wine, five sheep that had been slaughtered, nearly a bushel<sup>①</sup> of roasted grain, 100 clusters of raisins, and 200 fig cakes. She packed them on donkeys<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>5 seahs</i> [36.5 liters].  
**25:19** and said to her servants, “Go on ahead. I will follow you shortly.” But she didn’t tell her husband Nabal what she was doing.  
**25:20** As she was riding her donkey into a mountain ravine, she saw David and his men coming toward her.  
**25:21** David had just been saying, “A lot of good it did to help this fellow. We protected his flocks in the wilderness, and nothing he owned was lost or stolen. But he has repaid me evil for good.  
**25:22** May God strike me and kill me<sup>①</sup> if even one man of his household is still alive tomorrow morning!”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in Greek version; Hebrew reads <i>May God strike and kill the enemies of David.</i>  
**25:23** When Abigail saw David, she quickly got off her donkey and bowed low before him.  
**25:24** She fell at his feet and said, “I accept all blame in this matter, my lord. Please listen to what I have to say.  
**25:25** I know Nabal is a wicked and ill-tempered man; please don’t pay any attention to him. He is a fool, just as his name suggests.<sup>①</sup> But I never even saw the young men you sent.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>The name <i>Nabal</i> means “fool.”  
**25:26** “Now, my lord, as surely as the LORD lives and you yourself live, since the LORD has kept you from murdering and taking vengeance into your own hands, let all your enemies and those who try to harm you be as cursed as Nabal is.  
**25:27** And here is a present that I, your servant, have brought to you and your young men.  
**25:28** Please forgive me if I have offended you in any way. The LORD will surely reward you with a lasting dynasty, for you are fighting the LORD’s battles. And you have not done wrong throughout your entire life.  
**25:29** “Even when you are chased by those who seek to kill you, your life is safe in the care of the LORD your God, secure in his treasure pouch! But the lives of your enemies will disappear like stones shot from a sling!  
**25:30** When the LORD has done all he promised and has made you leader of Israel,  
**25:31** don’t let this be a blemish on your record. Then your conscience won’t have to bear the staggering burden of needless bloodshed and vengeance. And when the LORD has done these great things for you, please remember me, your servant!”  
**25:32** David replied to Abigail, “Praise the LORD, the God of Israel, who has sent you to meet me today!  
**25:33** Thank God for your good sense! Bless you for keeping me from murder and from carrying out vengeance with my own hands.  
**25:34** For I swear by the LORD, the God of Israel, who has kept me from hurting you, that if you had not hurried out to meet me, not one of Nabal’s men would still be alive tomorrow morning.”  
**25:35** Then David accepted her present and told her, “Return home in peace. I have heard what you said. We will not kill your husband.”  
**25:36** When Abigail arrived home, she found that Nabal was throwing a big party and was celebrating like a king. He was very drunk, so she didn’t tell him anything about her meeting with David until dawn the next day.  
**25:37** In the morning when Nabal was sober, his wife told him what had happened. As a result he had a stroke,<sup>①</sup> and he lay paralyzed on his bed like a stone.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>his heart failed him.</i>  
**25:38** About ten days later, the LORD struck him, and he died.  
**25:39** When David heard that Nabal was dead, he said, “Praise the LORD, who has avenged the insult I received from Nabal and has kept me from doing it myself. Nabal has received the punishment for his sin.” Then David sent messengers to Abigail to ask her to become his wife.  
**25:40** When the messengers arrived at Carmel, they told Abigail, “David has sent us to take you back to marry him.”  
**25:41** She bowed low to the ground and responded, “I, your servant, would be happy to marry David. I would even be willing to become a slave, washing the feet of his servants!”  
**25:42** Quickly getting ready, she took along five of her servant girls as attendants, mounted her donkey, and went with David’s messengers. And so she became his wife.  
**25:43** David also married Ahinoam from Jezreel, making both of them his wives.  
**25:44** Saul, meanwhile, had given his daughter Michal, David’s wife, to a man from Gallim named Palti son of Laish.  

## 26장

**26:1** Now some men from Ziph came to Saul at Gibeah to tell him, “David is hiding on the hill of Hakilah, which overlooks Jeshimon.”  
**26:2** So Saul took 3,000 of Israel’s elite troops and went to hunt him down in the wilderness of Ziph.  
**26:3** Saul camped along the road beside the hill of Hakilah, near Jeshimon, where David was hiding. When David learned that Saul had come after him into the wilderness,  
**26:4** he sent out spies to verify the report of Saul’s arrival.  
**26:5** David slipped over to Saul’s camp one night to look around. Saul and Abner son of Ner, the commander of his army, were sleeping inside a ring formed by the slumbering warriors.  
**26:6** “Who will volunteer to go in there with me?” David asked Ahimelech the Hittite and Abishai son of Zeruiah, Joab’s brother. “I’ll go with you,” Abishai replied.  
**26:7** So David and Abishai went right into Saul’s camp and found him asleep, with his spear stuck in the ground beside his head. Abner and the soldiers were lying asleep around him.  
**26:8** “God has surely handed your enemy over to you this time!” Abishai whispered to David. “Let me pin him to the ground with one thrust of the spear; I won’t need to strike twice!”  
**26:9** “No!” David said. “Don’t kill him. For who can remain innocent after attacking the LORD’s anointed one?  
**26:10** Surely the LORD will strike Saul down someday, or he will die of old age or in battle.  
**26:11** The LORD forbid that I should kill the one he has anointed! But take his spear and that jug of water beside his head, and then let’s get out of here!”  
**26:12** So David took the spear and jug of water that were near Saul’s head. Then he and Abishai got away without anyone seeing them or even waking up, because the LORD had put Saul’s men into a deep sleep.  
**26:13** David climbed the hill opposite the camp until he was at a safe distance.  
**26:14** Then he shouted down to the soldiers and to Abner son of Ner, “Wake up, Abner!” “Who is it?” Abner demanded.  
**26:15** “Well, Abner, you’re a great man, aren’t you?” David taunted. “Where in all Israel is there anyone as mighty? So why haven’t you guarded your master the king when someone came to kill him?  
**26:16** This isn’t good at all! I swear by the LORD that you and your men deserve to die, because you failed to protect your master, the LORD’s anointed! Look around! Where are the king’s spear and the jug of water that were beside his head?”  
**26:17** Saul recognized David’s voice and called out, “Is that you, my son David?” And David replied, “Yes, my lord the king.  
**26:18** Why are you chasing me? What have I done? What is my crime?  
**26:19** But now let my lord the king listen to his servant. If the LORD has stirred you up against me, then let him accept my offering. But if this is simply a human scheme, then may those involved be cursed by the LORD. For they have driven me from my home, so I can no longer live among the LORD’s people, and they have said, ‘Go, worship pagan gods.’  
**26:20** Must I die on foreign soil, far from the presence of the LORD? Why has the king of Israel come out to search for a single flea? Why does he hunt me down like a partridge on the mountains?”  
**26:21** Then Saul confessed, “I have sinned. Come back home, my son, and I will no longer try to harm you, for you valued my life today. I have been a fool and very, very wrong.”  
**26:22** “Here is your spear, O king,” David replied. “Let one of your young men come over and get it.  
**26:23** The LORD gives his own reward for doing good and for being loyal, and I refused to kill you even when the LORD placed you in my power, for you are the LORD’s anointed one.  
**26:24** Now may the LORD value my life, even as I have valued yours today. May he rescue me from all my troubles.”  
**26:25** And Saul said to David, “Blessings on you, my son David. You will do many heroic deeds, and you will surely succeed.” Then David went away, and Saul returned home.  

## 27장

**27:1** But David kept thinking to himself, “Someday Saul is going to get me. The best thing I can do is escape to the Philistines. Then Saul will stop hunting for me in Israelite territory, and I will finally be safe.”  
**27:2** So David took his 600 men and went over and joined Achish son of Maoch, the king of Gath.  
**27:3** David and his men and their families settled there with Achish at Gath. David brought his two wives along with him—Ahinoam from Jezreel and Abigail, Nabal’s widow from Carmel.  
**27:4** Word soon reached Saul that David had fled to Gath, so he stopped hunting for him.  
**27:5** One day David said to Achish, “If it is all right with you, we would rather live in one of the country towns instead of here in the royal city.”  
**27:6** So Achish gave him the town of Ziklag (which still belongs to the kings of Judah to this day),  
**27:7** and they lived there among the Philistines for a year and four months.  
**27:8** David and his men spent their time raiding the Geshurites, the Girzites, and the Amalekites—people who had lived near Shur, toward the land of Egypt, since ancient times.  
**27:9** David did not leave one person alive in the villages he attacked. He took the sheep, goats, cattle, donkeys, camels, and clothing before returning home to see King Achish.  
**27:10** “Where did you make your raid today?” Achish would ask. And David would reply, “Against the south of Judah, the Jerahmeelites, and the Kenites.”  
**27:11** No one was left alive to come to Gath and tell where he had really been. This happened again and again while he was living among the Philistines.  
**27:12** Achish believed David and thought to himself, “By now the people of Israel must hate him bitterly. Now he will have to stay here and serve me forever!”  

## 28장

**28:1** About that time the Philistines mustered their armies for another war with Israel. King Achish told David, “You and your men will be expected to join me in battle.”  
**28:2** “Very well!” David agreed. “Now you will see for yourself what we can do.” Then Achish told David, “I will make you my personal bodyguard for life.”  
**28:3** Meanwhile, Samuel had died, and all Israel had mourned for him. He was buried in Ramah, his hometown. And Saul had banned from the land of Israel all mediums and those who consult the spirits of the dead.  
**28:4** The Philistines set up their camp at Shunem, and Saul gathered all the army of Israel and camped at Gilboa.  
**28:5** When Saul saw the vast Philistine army, he became frantic with fear.  
**28:6** He asked the LORD what he should do, but the LORD refused to answer him, either by dreams or by sacred lots<sup>①</sup> or by the prophets.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>by Urim.</i>  
**28:7** Saul then said to his advisers, “Find a woman who is a medium, so I can go and ask her what to do.” His advisers replied, “There is a medium at Endor.”  
**28:8** So Saul disguised himself by wearing ordinary clothing instead of his royal robes. Then he went to the woman’s home at night, accompanied by two of his men. “I have to talk to a man who has died,” he said. “Will you call up his spirit for me?”  
**28:9** “Are you trying to get me killed?” the woman demanded. “You know that Saul has outlawed all the mediums and all who consult the spirits of the dead. Why are you setting a trap for me?”  
**28:10** But Saul took an oath in the name of the LORD and promised, “As surely as the LORD lives, nothing bad will happen to you for doing this.”  
**28:11** Finally, the woman said, “Well, whose spirit do you want me to call up?” “Call up Samuel,” Saul replied.  
**28:12** When the woman saw Samuel, she screamed, “You’ve deceived me! You are Saul!”  
**28:13** “Don’t be afraid!” the king told her. “What do you see?” “I see a god<sup>①</sup> coming up out of the earth,” she said.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>gods.</i>  
**28:14** “What does he look like?” Saul asked. “He is an old man wrapped in a robe,” she replied. Saul realized it was Samuel, and he fell to the ground before him.  
**28:15** “Why have you disturbed me by calling me back?” Samuel asked Saul. “Because I am in deep trouble,” Saul replied. “The Philistines are at war with me, and God has left me and won’t reply by prophets or dreams. So I have called for you to tell me what to do.”  
**28:16** But Samuel replied, “Why ask me, since the LORD has left you and has become your enemy?  
**28:17** The LORD has done just as he said he would. He has torn the kingdom from you and given it to your rival, David.  
**28:18** The LORD has done this to you today because you refused to carry out his fierce anger against the Amalekites.  
**28:19** What’s more, the LORD will hand you and the army of Israel over to the Philistines tomorrow, and you and your sons will be here with me. The LORD will bring down the entire army of Israel in defeat.”  
**28:20** Saul fell full length on the ground, paralyzed with fright because of Samuel’s words. He was also faint with hunger, for he had eaten nothing all day and all night.  
**28:21** When the woman saw how distraught he was, she said, “Sir, I obeyed your command at the risk of my life.  
**28:22** Now do what I say, and let me give you a little something to eat so you can regain your strength for the trip back.”  
**28:23** But Saul refused to eat anything. Then his advisers joined the woman in urging him to eat, so he finally yielded and got up from the ground and sat on the couch.  
**28:24** The woman had been fattening a calf, so she hurried out and killed it. She took some flour, kneaded it into dough and baked unleavened bread.  
**28:25** She brought the meal to Saul and his advisers, and they ate it. Then they went out into the night.  

## 29장

**29:1** The entire Philistine army now mobilized at Aphek, and the Israelites camped at the spring in Jezreel.  
**29:2** As the Philistine rulers were leading out their troops in groups of hundreds and thousands, David and his men marched at the rear with King Achish.  
**29:3** But the Philistine commanders demanded, “What are these Hebrews doing here?” And Achish told them, “This is David, the servant of King Saul of Israel. He’s been with me for years, and I’ve never found a single fault in him from the day he arrived until today.”  
**29:4** But the Philistine commanders were angry. “Send him back to the town you’ve given him!” they demanded. “He can’t go into the battle with us. What if he turns against us in battle and becomes our adversary? Is there any better way for him to reconcile himself with his master than by handing our heads over to him?  
**29:5** Isn’t this the same David about whom the women of Israel sing in their dances,<br>‘Saul has killed his thousands,<br>and David his ten thousands’?”  
**29:6** So Achish finally summoned David and said to him, “I swear by the LORD that you have been a trustworthy ally. I think you should go with me into battle, for I’ve never found a single flaw in you from the day you arrived until today. But the other Philistine rulers won’t hear of it.  
**29:7** Please don’t upset them, but go back quietly.”  
**29:8** “What have I done to deserve this treatment?” David demanded. “What have you ever found in your servant, that I can’t go and fight the enemies of my lord the king?”  
**29:9** But Achish insisted, “As far as I’m concerned, you’re as perfect as an angel of God. But the Philistine commanders are afraid to have you with them in the battle.  
**29:10** Now get up early in the morning, and leave with your men as soon as it gets light.”  
**29:11** So David and his men headed back into the land of the Philistines, while the Philistine army went on to Jezreel.  

## 30장

**30:1** Three days later, when David and his men arrived home at their town of Ziklag, they found that the Amalekites had made a raid into the Negev and Ziklag; they had crushed Ziklag and burned it to the ground.  
**30:2** They had carried off the women and children and everyone else but without killing anyone.  
**30:3** When David and his men saw the ruins and realized what had happened to their families,  
**30:4** they wept until they could weep no more.  
**30:5** David’s two wives, Ahinoam from Jezreel and Abigail, the widow of Nabal from Carmel, were among those captured.  
**30:6** David was now in great danger because all his men were very bitter about losing their sons and daughters, and they began to talk of stoning him. But David found strength in the LORD his God.  
**30:7** Then he said to Abiathar the priest, “Bring me the ephod!” So Abiathar brought it.  
**30:8** Then David asked the LORD, “Should I chase after this band of raiders? Will I catch them?” And the LORD told him, “Yes, go after them. You will surely recover everything that was taken from you!”  
**30:9** So David and his 600 men set out, and they came to the brook Besor.  
**30:10** But 200 of the men were too exhausted to cross the brook, so David continued the pursuit with 400 men.  
**30:11** Along the way they found an Egyptian man in a field and brought him to David. They gave him some bread to eat and water to drink.  
**30:12** They also gave him part of a fig cake and two clusters of raisins, for he hadn’t had anything to eat or drink for three days and nights. Before long his strength returned.  
**30:13** “To whom do you belong, and where do you come from?” David asked him. “I am an Egyptian—the slave of an Amalekite,” he replied. “My master abandoned me three days ago because I was sick.  
**30:14** We were on our way back from raiding the Kerethites in the Negev, the territory of Judah, and the land of Caleb, and we had just burned Ziklag.”  
**30:15** “Will you lead me to this band of raiders?” David asked. The young man replied, “If you take an oath in God’s name that you will not kill me or give me back to my master, then I will guide you to them.”  
**30:16** So he led David to them, and they found the Amalekites spread out across the fields, eating and drinking and dancing with joy because of the vast amount of plunder they had taken from the Philistines and the land of Judah.  
**30:17** David and his men rushed in among them and slaughtered them throughout that night and the entire next day until evening. None of the Amalekites escaped except 400 young men who fled on camels.  
**30:18** David got back everything the Amalekites had taken, and he rescued his two wives.  
**30:19** Nothing was missing: small or great, son or daughter, nor anything else that had been taken. David brought everything back.  
**30:20** He also recovered all the flocks and herds, and his men drove them ahead of the other livestock. “This plunder belongs to David!” they said.  
**30:21** Then David returned to the brook Besor and met up with the 200 men who had been left behind because they were too exhausted to go with him. They went out to meet David and his men, and David greeted them joyfully.  
**30:22** But some evil troublemakers among David’s men said, “They didn’t go with us, so they can’t have any of the plunder we recovered. Give them their wives and children, and tell them to be gone.”  
**30:23** But David said, “No, my brothers! Don’t be selfish with what the LORD has given us. He has kept us safe and helped us defeat the band of raiders that attacked us.  
**30:24** Who will listen when you talk like this? We share and share alike—those who go to battle and those who guard the equipment.”  
**30:25** From then on David made this a decree and regulation for Israel, and it is still followed today.  
**30:26** When he arrived at Ziklag, David sent part of the plunder to the elders of Judah, who were his friends. “Here is a present for you, taken from the LORD’s enemies,” he said.  
**30:27** The gifts were sent to the people of the following towns David had visited: Bethel, Ramoth-negev, Jattir,  
**30:28** Aroer, Siphmoth, Eshtemoa,  
**30:29** Racal,<sup>①</sup> the towns of the Jerahmeelites, the towns of the Kenites,<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Greek version reads <i>Carmel.</i>  
**30:30** Hormah, Bor-ashan, Athach,  
**30:31** Hebron, and all the other places David and his men had visited.  

## 31장

**31:1** Now the Philistines attacked Israel, and the men of Israel fled before them. Many were slaughtered on the slopes of Mount Gilboa.  
**31:2** The Philistines closed in on Saul and his sons, and they killed three of his sons—Jonathan, Abinadab, and Malkishua.  
**31:3** The fighting grew very fierce around Saul, and the Philistine archers caught up with him and wounded him severely.  
**31:4** Saul groaned to his armor bearer, “Take your sword and kill me before these pagan Philistines come to run me through and taunt and torture me.” But his armor bearer was afraid and would not do it. So Saul took his own sword and fell on it.  
**31:5** When his armor bearer realized that Saul was dead, he fell on his own sword and died beside the king.  
**31:6** So Saul, his three sons, his armor bearer, and his troops all died together that same day.  
**31:7** When the Israelites on the other side of the Jezreel Valley and beyond the Jordan saw that the Israelite army had fled and that Saul and his sons were dead, they abandoned their towns and fled. So the Philistines moved in and occupied their towns.  
**31:8** The next day, when the Philistines went out to strip the dead, they found the bodies of Saul and his three sons on Mount Gilboa.  
**31:9** So they cut off Saul’s head and stripped off his armor. Then they proclaimed the good news of Saul’s death in their pagan temple and to the people throughout the land of Philistia.  
**31:10** They placed his armor in the temple of the Ashtoreths, and they fastened his body to the wall of the city of Beth-shan.  
**31:11** But when the people of Jabesh-gilead heard what the Philistines had done to Saul,  
**31:12** all their mighty warriors traveled through the night to Beth-shan and took the bodies of Saul and his sons down from the wall. They brought them to Jabesh, where they burned the bodies.  
**31:13** Then they took their bones and buried them beneath the tamarisk tree at Jabesh, and they fasted for seven days.
