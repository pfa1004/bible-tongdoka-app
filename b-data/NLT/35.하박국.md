# 35.하박국 (NLT)



## 1장

**1:1** This is the message that the prophet Habakkuk received in a vision.  
**1:2** How long, O LORD, must I call for help?<br>But you do not listen!<br>“Violence is everywhere!” I cry,<br>but you do not come to save.  
**1:3** Must I forever see these evil deeds?<br>Why must I watch all this misery?<br>Wherever I look,<br>I see destruction and violence.<br>I am surrounded by people<br>who love to argue and fight.  
**1:4** The law has become paralyzed,<br>and there is no justice in the courts.<br>The wicked far outnumber the righteous,<br>so that justice has become perverted.  
**1:5** The LORD replied,<br>“Look around at the nations;<br>look and be amazed!<sup>①</sup><br>For I am doing something in your own day,<br>something you wouldn’t believe<br>even if someone told you about it.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Greek version reads <i>Look, you mockers; / look and be amazed and die.</i> Compare <a name='B:510 13:41'>Acts 13:41.  
**1:6** I am raising up the Babylonians,<sup>①</sup><br>a cruel and violent people.<br>They will march across the world<br>and conquer other lands.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>Chaldeans.</i>  
**1:7** They are notorious for their cruelty<br>and do whatever they like.  
**1:8** Their horses are swifter than cheetahs<sup>①</sup><br>and fiercer than wolves at dusk.<br>Their charioteers charge from far away.<br>Like eagles, they swoop down to devour their prey.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>leopards.</i>  
**1:9** “On they come, all bent on violence.<br>Their hordes advance like a desert wind,<br>sweeping captives ahead of them like sand.  
**1:10** They scoff at kings and princes<br>and scorn all their fortresses.<br>They simply pile ramps of earth<br>against their walls and capture them!  
**1:11** They sweep past like the wind<br>and are gone.<br>But they are deeply guilty,<br>for their own strength is their god.”  
**1:12** O LORD my God, my Holy One, you who are eternal—<br>surely you do not plan to wipe us out?<br>O LORD, our Rock, you have sent these Babylonians to correct us,<br>to punish us for our many sins.  
**1:13** But you are pure and cannot stand the sight of evil.<br>Will you wink at their treachery?<br>Should you be silent while the wicked<br>swallow up people more righteous than they?  
**1:14** Are we only fish to be caught and killed?<br>Are we only sea creatures that have no leader?  
**1:15** Must we be strung up on their hooks<br>and caught in their nets while they rejoice and celebrate?  
**1:16** Then they will worship their nets<br>and burn incense in front of them.<br>“These nets are the gods who have made us rich!”<br>they will claim.  
**1:17** Will you let them get away with this forever?<br>Will they succeed forever in their heartless conquests?  

## 2장

**2:1** I will climb up to my watchtower<br>and stand at my guardpost.<br>There I will wait to see what the LORD says<br>and how he<sup>①</sup> will answer my complaint.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in Syriac version; Hebrew reads <i>I.</i>  
**2:2** Then the LORD said to me,<br>“Write my answer plainly on tablets,<br>so that a runner can carry the correct message to others.  
**2:3** This vision is for a future time.<br>It describes the end, and it will be fulfilled.<br>If it seems slow in coming, wait patiently,<br>for it will surely take place.<br>It will not be delayed.  
**2:4** “Look at the proud!<br>They trust in themselves, and their lives are crooked.<br>But the righteous will live by their faithfulness to God.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Greek version reads <i>If the vision is delayed, wait patiently, / for it will surely come and not delay. /</i> <sup>4 </sup><i>I will take no pleasure in anyone who turns away. / But the righteous person will live by my faith.</i> Compare <a name='B:520 1:17'>Rom 1:17; <a name='B:550 3:11'>Gal 3:11; <a name='B:650 10:37-38'>Heb 10:37-38.  
**2:5** Wealth<sup>①</sup> is treacherous,<br>and the arrogant are never at rest.<br>They open their mouths as wide as the grave,<sup>②</sup><br>and like death, they are never satisfied.<br>In their greed they have gathered up many nations<br>and swallowed many peoples.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in Dead Sea Scroll 1QpHab; other Hebrew manuscripts read <i>Wine.</i> <sup>②</sup>Hebrew <i>as Sheol.</i>  
**2:6** “But soon their captives will taunt them.<br>They will mock them, saying,<br>‘What sorrow awaits you thieves!<br>Now you will get what you deserve!<br>You’ve become rich by extortion,<br>but how much longer can this go on?’  
**2:7** Suddenly, your debtors will take action.<br>They will turn on you and take all you have,<br>while you stand trembling and helpless.  
**2:8** Because you have plundered many nations,<br>now all the survivors will plunder you.<br>You committed murder throughout the countryside<br>and filled the towns with violence.  
**2:9** “What sorrow awaits you who build big houses<br>with money gained dishonestly!<br>You believe your wealth will buy security,<br>putting your family’s nest beyond the reach of danger.  
**2:10** But by the murders you committed,<br>you have shamed your name and forfeited your lives.  
**2:11** The very stones in the walls cry out against you,<br>and the beams in the ceilings echo the complaint.  
**2:12** “What sorrow awaits you who build cities<br>with money gained through murder and corruption!  
**2:13** Has not the LORD of Heaven’s Armies promised<br>that the wealth of nations will turn to ashes?<br>They work so hard,<br>but all in vain!  
**2:14** For as the waters fill the sea,<br>the earth will be filled with an awareness<br>of the glory of the LORD.  
**2:15** “What sorrow awaits you who make your neighbors drunk!<br>You force your cup on them<br>so you can gloat over their shameful nakedness.  
**2:16** But soon it will be your turn to be disgraced.<br>Come, drink and be exposed!<sup>①</sup><br>Drink from the cup of the LORD’s judgment,<br>and all your glory will be turned to shame.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Dead Sea Scrolls and Greek and Syriac versions read <i>and stagger!</i>  
**2:17** You cut down the forests of Lebanon.<br>Now you will be cut down.<br>You destroyed the wild animals,<br>so now their terror will be yours.<br>You committed murder throughout the countryside<br>and filled the towns with violence.  
**2:18** “What good is an idol carved by man,<br>or a cast image that deceives you?<br>How foolish to trust in your own creation—<br>a god that can’t even talk!  
**2:19** What sorrow awaits you who say to wooden idols,<br>‘Wake up and save us!’<br>To speechless stone images you say,<br>‘Rise up and teach us!’<br>Can an idol tell you what to do?<br>They may be overlaid with gold and silver,<br>but they are lifeless inside.  
**2:20** But the LORD is in his holy Temple.<br>Let all the earth be silent before him.”  

## 3장

**3:1** This prayer was sung by the prophet Habakkuk <sup>①</sup>:<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew adds <i>according to shigionoth,</i> probably indicating the musical setting for the prayer.  
**3:2** I have heard all about you, LORD.<br>I am filled with awe by your amazing works.<br>In this time of our deep need,<br>help us again as you did in years gone by.<br>And in your anger,<br>remember your mercy.  
**3:3** I see God moving across the deserts from Edom,<sup>①</sup><br>the Holy One coming from Mount Paran.<sup>②</sup><br>His brilliant splendor fills the heavens,<br>and the earth is filled with his praise.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>Teman.</i> <sup>②</sup>Hebrew adds <i>selah;</i> also in <a name='B:420 3:9'>3:9, <a name='B:420 3:13'>13. The meaning of this Hebrew term is uncertain; it is probably a musical or literary term.  
**3:4** His coming is as brilliant as the sunrise.<br>Rays of light flash from his hands,<br>where his awesome power is hidden.  
**3:5** Pestilence marches before him;<br>plague follows close behind.  
**3:6** When he stops, the earth shakes.<br>When he looks, the nations tremble.<br>He shatters the everlasting mountains<br>and levels the eternal hills.<br>He is the Eternal One!<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>The ancient paths belong to him.</i>  
**3:7** I see the people of Cushan in distress,<br>and the nation of Midian trembling in terror.  
**3:8** Was it in anger, LORD, that you struck the rivers<br>and parted the sea?<br>Were you displeased with them?<br>No, you were sending your chariots of salvation!  
**3:9** You brandished your bow<br>and your quiver of arrows.<br>You split open the earth with flowing rivers.  
**3:10** The mountains watched and trembled.<br>Onward swept the raging waters.<br>The mighty deep cried out,<br>lifting its hands in submission.  
**3:11** The sun and moon stood still in the sky<br>as your brilliant arrows flew<br>and your glittering spear flashed.  
**3:12** You marched across the land in anger<br>and trampled the nations in your fury.  
**3:13** You went out to rescue your chosen people,<br>to save your anointed ones.<br>You crushed the heads of the wicked<br>and stripped their bones from head to toe.  
**3:14** With his own weapons,<br>you destroyed the chief of those<br>who rushed out like a whirlwind,<br>thinking Israel would be easy prey.  
**3:15** You trampled the sea with your horses,<br>and the mighty waters piled high.  
**3:16** I trembled inside when I heard this;<br>my lips quivered with fear.<br>My legs gave way beneath me,<sup>①</sup><br>and I shook in terror.<br>I will wait quietly for the coming day<br>when disaster will strike the people who invade us.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>Decay entered my bones.</i>  
**3:17** Even though the fig trees have no blossoms,<br>and there are no grapes on the vines;<br>even though the olive crop fails,<br>and the fields lie empty and barren;<br>even though the flocks die in the fields,<br>and the cattle barns are empty,  
**3:18** yet I will rejoice in the LORD!<br>I will be joyful in the God of my salvation!  
**3:19** The Sovereign LORD is my strength!<br>He makes me as surefooted as a deer,<sup>①</sup><br>able to tread upon the heights. (For the choir director: This prayer is to be accompanied by stringed instruments.)<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>He gives me the speed of a deer.</i>
