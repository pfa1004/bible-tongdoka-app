# 15.에스라 (NLT)



## 1장

**1:1** In the first year of King Cyrus of Persia,<sup>①</sup> the LORD fulfilled the prophecy he had given through Jeremiah.<sup>②</sup> He stirred the heart of Cyrus to put this proclamation in writing and to send it throughout his kingdom:<br>→ <sup>①</sup>The first year of Cyrus’s reign over Babylon was 538 B.C. <sup>②</sup>See <a name='B:300 25:11-12'>Jer 25:11-12; <a name='B:300 29:10'>29:10.  
**1:2** “This is what King Cyrus of Persia says: “The LORD, the God of heaven, has given me all the kingdoms of the earth. He has appointed me to build him a Temple at Jerusalem, which is in Judah.  
**1:3** Any of you who are his people may go to Jerusalem in Judah to rebuild this Temple of the LORD, the God of Israel, who lives in Jerusalem. And may your God be with you!  
**1:4** Wherever this Jewish remnant is found, let their neighbors contribute toward their expenses by giving them silver and gold, supplies for the journey, and livestock, as well as a voluntary offering for the Temple of God in Jerusalem.”  
**1:5** Then God stirred the hearts of the priests and Levites and the leaders of the tribes of Judah and Benjamin to go to Jerusalem to rebuild the Temple of the LORD.  
**1:6** And all their neighbors assisted by giving them articles of silver and gold, supplies for the journey, and livestock. They gave them many valuable gifts in addition to all the voluntary offerings.  
**1:7** King Cyrus himself brought out the articles that King Nebuchadnezzar had taken from the LORD’s Temple in Jerusalem and had placed in the temple of his own gods.  
**1:8** Cyrus directed Mithredath, the treasurer of Persia, to count these items and present them to Sheshbazzar, the leader of the exiles returning to Judah.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>Sheshbazzar, the prince of Judah.</i>  
**1:9** This is a list of the items that were returned:<br>gold basins—30<br>silver basins—1,000<br>silver incense burners<sup>①</sup>—29<br>→ <sup>①</sup>The meaning of this Hebrew word is uncertain.  
**1:10** gold bowls—30<br>silver bowls—410<br>other items—1,000  
**1:11** In all, there were 5,400 articles of gold and silver. Sheshbazzar brought all of these along when the exiles went from Babylon to Jerusalem.

## 3장

**3:1** In early autumn,<sup>①</sup> when the Israelites had settled in their towns, all the people assembled in Jerusalem with a unified purpose.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>In the seventh month.</i> The year is not specified, so it may have been during Cyrus’s first year (538 B.C.) or second year (537 B.C.). The seventh month of the ancient Hebrew lunar calendar occurred within the months of September/October 538 B.C. and October/November 537 B.C.  
**3:2** Then Jeshua son of Jehozadak<sup>①</sup> joined his fellow priests and Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel with his family in rebuilding the altar of the God of Israel. They wanted to sacrifice burnt offerings on it, as instructed in the Law of Moses, the man of God.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>Jozadak,</i> a variant spelling of Jehozadak; also in <a name='B:150 3:8'>3:8.  
**3:3** Even though the people were afraid of the local residents, they rebuilt the altar at its old site. Then they began to sacrifice burnt offerings on the altar to the LORD each morning and evening.  
**3:4** They celebrated the Festival of Shelters as prescribed in the Law, sacrificing the number of burnt offerings specified for each day of the festival.  
**3:5** They also offered the regular burnt offerings and the offerings required for the new moon celebrations and the annual festivals as prescribed by the LORD. The people also gave voluntary offerings to the LORD.  
**3:6** Fifteen days before the Festival of Shelters began,<sup>①</sup> the priests had begun to sacrifice burnt offerings to the LORD. This was even before they had started to lay the foundation of the LORD’s Temple.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>On the first day of the seventh month.</i> This day in the ancient Hebrew lunar calendar occurred in September or October. The Festival of Shelters began on the fifteenth day of the seventh month.  
**3:7** Then the people hired masons and carpenters and bought cedar logs from the people of Tyre and Sidon, paying them with food, wine, and olive oil. The logs were brought down from the Lebanon mountains and floated along the coast of the Mediterranean Sea<sup>①</sup> to Joppa, for King Cyrus had given permission for this.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>the sea.</i>  
**3:8** The construction of the Temple of God began in midspring,<sup>①</sup> during the second year after they arrived in Jerusalem. The work force was made up of everyone who had returned from exile, including Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel, Jeshua son of Jehozadak and his fellow priests, and all the Levites. The Levites who were twenty years old or older were put in charge of rebuilding the LORD’s Temple.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>in the second month.</i> This month in the ancient Hebrew lunar calendar occurred within the months of April and May 536 B.C.  
**3:9** The workers at the Temple of God were supervised by Jeshua with his sons and relatives, and Kadmiel and his sons, all descendants of Hodaviah.<sup>①</sup> They were helped in this task by the Levites of the family of Henadad.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>sons of Judah</i> (i.e., <i>bene Yehudah</i>). <i>Bene</i> might also be read here as the proper name Binnui; <i>Yehudah</i> is probably another name for Hodaviah. Compare <a name='B:150 2:40'>2:40; <a name='B:160 7:43'>Neh 7:43; <a name='B:165 5:58'>1 Esdras 5:58.  
**3:10** When the builders completed the foundation of the LORD’s Temple, the priests put on their robes and took their places to blow their trumpets. And the Levites, descendants of Asaph, clashed their cymbals to praise the LORD, just as King David had prescribed.  
**3:11** With praise and thanks, they sang this song to the LORD:<br>“He is so good!<br>His faithful love for Israel endures forever!” Then all the people gave a great shout, praising the LORD because the foundation of the LORD’s Temple had been laid.  
**3:12** But many of the older priests, Levites, and other leaders who had seen the first Temple wept aloud when they saw the new Temple’s foundation. The others, however, were shouting for joy.  
**3:13** The joyful shouting and weeping mingled together in a loud noise that could be heard far in the distance.  

## 4장

**4:1** The enemies of Judah and Benjamin heard that the exiles were rebuilding a Temple to the LORD, the God of Israel.  
**4:2** So they approached Zerubbabel and the other leaders and said, “Let us build with you, for we worship your God just as you do. We have sacrificed to him ever since King Esarhaddon of Assyria brought us here.”  
**4:3** But Zerubbabel, Jeshua, and the other leaders of Israel replied, “You may have no part in this work. We alone will build the Temple for the LORD, the God of Israel, just as King Cyrus of Persia commanded us.”  
**4:4** Then the local residents tried to discourage and frighten the people of Judah to keep them from their work.  
**4:5** They bribed agents to work against them and to frustrate their plans. This went on during the entire reign of King Cyrus of Persia and lasted until King Darius of Persia took the throne.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Darius reigned 521–486 B.C.  
**4:6** Years later when Xerxes<sup>①</sup> began his reign, the enemies of Judah wrote a letter of accusation against the people of Judah and Jerusalem.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>Ahasuerus,</i> another name for Xerxes. He reigned 486–465 B.C.  
**4:7** Even later, during the reign of King Artaxerxes of Persia,<sup>①</sup> the enemies of Judah, led by Bishlam, Mithredath, and Tabeel, sent a letter to Artaxerxes in the Aramaic language, and it was translated for the king.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Artaxerxes reigned 465–424 B.C.  
**4:8** <sup>①</sup>Rehum the governor and Shimshai the court secretary wrote the letter, telling King Artaxerxes about the situation in Jerusalem.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>The original text of <a name='B:150 4:8-6:18'>4:8–6:18 is in Aramaic.  
**4:9** They greeted the king for all their colleagues—the judges and local leaders, the people of Tarpel, the Persians, the Babylonians, and the people of Erech and Susa (that is, Elam).  
**4:10** They also sent greetings from the rest of the people whom the great and noble Ashurbanipal<sup>①</sup> had deported and relocated in Samaria and throughout the neighboring lands of the province west of the Euphrates River.<sup>②</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Aramaic <i>Osnappar,</i> another name for Ashurbanipal. <sup>②</sup>Aramaic <i>the province beyond the river;</i> also in <a name='B:150 4:11'>4:11, <a name='B:150 4:16'>16, <a name='B:150 4:17'>17, <a name='B:150 4:20'>20.  
**4:11** This is a copy of their letter:<br>“To King Artaxerxes, from your loyal subjects in the province west of the Euphrates River.  
**4:12** “The king should know that the Jews who came here to Jerusalem from Babylon are rebuilding this rebellious and evil city. They have already laid the foundation and will soon finish its walls.  
**4:13** And the king should know that if this city is rebuilt and its walls are completed, it will be much to your disadvantage, for the Jews will then refuse to pay their tribute, customs, and tolls to you.  
**4:14** “Since we are your loyal subjects<sup>①</sup> and do not want to see the king dishonored in this way, we have sent the king this information.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Aramaic <i>Since we eat the salt of the palace.</i>  
**4:15** We suggest that a search be made in your ancestors’ records, where you will discover what a rebellious city this has been in the past. In fact, it was destroyed because of its long and troublesome history of revolt against the kings and countries who controlled it.  
**4:16** We declare to the king that if this city is rebuilt and its walls are completed, the province west of the Euphrates River will be lost to you.”  
**4:17** Then King Artaxerxes sent this reply:<br>“To Rehum the governor, Shimshai the court secretary, and their colleagues living in Samaria and throughout the province west of the Euphrates River. Greetings.  
**4:18** “The letter you sent has been translated and read to me.  
**4:19** I ordered a search of the records and have found that Jerusalem has indeed been a hotbed of insurrection against many kings. In fact, rebellion and revolt are normal there!  
**4:20** Powerful kings have ruled over Jerusalem and the entire province west of the Euphrates River, receiving tribute, customs, and tolls.  
**4:21** Therefore, issue orders to have these men stop their work. That city must not be rebuilt except at my express command.  
**4:22** Be diligent, and don’t neglect this matter, for we must not permit the situation to harm the king’s interests.”  
**4:23** When this letter from King Artaxerxes was read to Rehum, Shimshai, and their colleagues, they hurried to Jerusalem. Then, with a show of strength, they forced the Jews to stop building.  
**4:24** So the work on the Temple of God in Jerusalem had stopped, and it remained at a standstill until the second year of the reign of King Darius of Persia.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>The second year of Darius’s reign was 520 B.C. The narrative started in <a name='B:150 4:1-5'>4:1-5 is resumed at verse <a name='B:150 4:24'>24.  

## 5장

**5:1** At that time the prophets Haggai and Zechariah son of Iddo prophesied to the Jews in Judah and Jerusalem. They prophesied in the name of the God of Israel who was over them.  
**5:2** Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel and Jeshua son of Jehozadak<sup>①</sup> responded by starting again to rebuild the Temple of God in Jerusalem. And the prophets of God were with them and helped them.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Aramaic <i>Jozadak,</i> a variant spelling of Jehozadak.  
**5:3** But Tattenai, governor of the province west of the Euphrates River,<sup>①</sup> and Shethar-bozenai and their colleagues soon arrived in Jerusalem and asked, “Who gave you permission to rebuild this Temple and restore this structure?”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Aramaic <i>the province beyond the river;</i> also in <a name='B:150 5:6'>5:6.  
**5:4** They also asked for<sup>①</sup> the names of all the men working on the Temple.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in one Hebrew manuscript and Greek and Syriac versions; Masoretic Text reads <i>Then we told them.</i>  
**5:5** But because their God was watching over them, the leaders of the Jews were not prevented from building until a report was sent to Darius and he returned his decision.  
**5:6** This is a copy of the letter that Tattenai the governor, Shethar-bozenai, and the other officials of the province west of the Euphrates River sent to King Darius:  
**5:7** “To King Darius. Greetings.  
**5:8** “The king should know that we went to the construction site of the Temple of the great God in the province of Judah. It is being rebuilt with specially prepared stones, and timber is being laid in its walls. The work is going forward with great energy and success.  
**5:9** “We asked the leaders, ‘Who gave you permission to rebuild this Temple and restore this structure?’  
**5:10** And we demanded their names so that we could tell you who the leaders were.  
**5:11** “This was their answer: ‘We are the servants of the God of heaven and earth, and we are rebuilding the Temple that was built here many years ago by a great king of Israel.  
**5:12** But because our ancestors angered the God of heaven, he abandoned them to King Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon,<sup>①</sup> who destroyed this Temple and exiled the people to Babylonia.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Aramaic <i>Nebuchadnezzar the Chaldean.</i>  
**5:13** However, King Cyrus of Babylon,<sup>①</sup> during the first year of his reign, issued a decree that the Temple of God should be rebuilt.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>King Cyrus of Persia is here identified as the king of Babylon because Persia had conquered the Babylonian Empire.  
**5:14** King Cyrus returned the gold and silver cups that Nebuchadnezzar had taken from the Temple of God in Jerusalem and had placed in the temple of Babylon. These cups were taken from that temple and presented to a man named Sheshbazzar, whom King Cyrus appointed as governor of Judah.  
**5:15** The king instructed him to return the cups to their place in Jerusalem and to rebuild the Temple of God there on its original site.  
**5:16** So this Sheshbazzar came and laid the foundations of the Temple of God in Jerusalem. The people have been working on it ever since, though it is not yet completed.’  
**5:17** “Therefore, if it pleases the king, we request that a search be made in the royal archives of Babylon to discover whether King Cyrus ever issued a decree to rebuild God’s Temple in Jerusalem. And then let the king send us his decision in this matter.”  

## 6장

**6:1** So King Darius issued orders that a search be made in the Babylonian archives, which were stored in the treasury.  
**6:2** But it was at the fortress at Ecbatana in the province of Media that a scroll was found. This is what it said:<br>“Memorandum:  
**6:3** “In the first year of King Cyrus’s reign, a decree was sent out concerning the Temple of God at Jerusalem. “Let the Temple be rebuilt on the site where Jews used to offer their sacrifices, using the original foundations. Its height will be ninety feet, and its width will be ninety feet.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Aramaic <i>Its height will be 60 cubits</i> [27.6 meters], <i>and its width will be 60 cubits.</i> It is commonly held that this verse should be emended to read: “Its height will be 30 cubits [45 feet or 13.8 meters], its length will be 60 cubits [90 feet or 27.6 meters], and its width will be 20 cubits [30 feet or 9.2 meters]”; compare <a name='B:110 6:2'>1 Kgs 6:2. The emendation regarding the width is supported by the Syriac version.  
**6:4** Every three layers of specially prepared stones will be topped by a layer of timber. All expenses will be paid by the royal treasury.  
**6:5** Furthermore, the gold and silver cups, which were taken to Babylon by Nebuchadnezzar from the Temple of God in Jerusalem, must be returned to Jerusalem and put back where they belong. Let them be taken back to the Temple of God.”  
**6:6** So King Darius sent this message:<br>“Now therefore, Tattenai, governor of the province west of the Euphrates River,<sup>①</sup> and Shethar-bozenai, and your colleagues and other officials west of the Euphrates River—stay away from there!<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Aramaic <i>the province beyond the river;</i> also in <a name='B:150 6:6'>6:6b, <a name='B:150 6:8'>8, <a name='B:150 6:13'>13.  
**6:7** Do not disturb the construction of the Temple of God. Let it be rebuilt on its original site, and do not hinder the governor of Judah and the elders of the Jews in their work.  
**6:8** “Moreover, I hereby decree that you are to help these elders of the Jews as they rebuild this Temple of God. You must pay the full construction costs, without delay, from my taxes collected in the province west of the Euphrates River so that the work will not be interrupted.  
**6:9** “Give the priests in Jerusalem whatever is needed in the way of young bulls, rams, and male lambs for the burnt offerings presented to the God of heaven. And without fail, provide them with as much wheat, salt, wine, and olive oil as they need each day.  
**6:10** Then they will be able to offer acceptable sacrifices to the God of heaven and pray for the welfare of the king and his sons.  
**6:11** “Those who violate this decree in any way will have a beam pulled from their house. Then they will be lifted up and impaled on it, and their house will be reduced to a pile of rubble.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Aramaic <i>a dunghill.</i>  
**6:12** May the God who has chosen the city of Jerusalem as the place to honor his name destroy any king or nation that violates this command and destroys this Temple. “I, Darius, have issued this decree. Let it be obeyed with all diligence.”  
**6:13** Tattenai, governor of the province west of the Euphrates River, and Shethar-bozenai and their colleagues complied at once with the command of King Darius.  
**6:14** So the Jewish elders continued their work, and they were greatly encouraged by the preaching of the prophets Haggai and Zechariah son of Iddo. The Temple was finally finished, as had been commanded by the God of Israel and decreed by Cyrus, Darius, and Artaxerxes, the kings of Persia.  
**6:15** The Temple was completed on March 12,<sup>①</sup> during the sixth year of King Darius’s reign.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Aramaic <i>on the third day of the month Adar,</i> of the ancient Hebrew lunar calendar. A number of events in Ezra can be cross-checked with dates in surviving Persian records and related accurately to our modern calendar. This day was March 12, 515 B.C.  
**6:16** The Temple of God was then dedicated with great joy by the people of Israel, the priests, the Levites, and the rest of the people who had returned from exile.  
**6:17** During the dedication ceremony for the Temple of God, 100 young bulls, 200 rams, and 400 male lambs were sacrificed. And 12 male goats were presented as a sin offering for the twelve tribes of Israel.  
**6:18** Then the priests and Levites were divided into their various divisions to serve at the Temple of God in Jerusalem, as prescribed in the Book of Moses.  
**6:19** On April 21<sup>①</sup> the returned exiles celebrated Passover.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>On the fourteenth day of the first month,</i> of the ancient Hebrew lunar calendar. This day was April 21, 515 B.C.; also see note on 6:15.  
**6:20** The priests and Levites had purified themselves and were ceremonially clean. So they slaughtered the Passover lamb for all the returned exiles, for their fellow priests, and for themselves.  
**6:21** The Passover meal was eaten by the people of Israel who had returned from exile and by the others in the land who had turned from their corrupt practices to worship the LORD, the God of Israel.  
**6:22** Then they celebrated the Festival of Unleavened Bread for seven days. There was great joy throughout the land because the LORD had caused the king of Assyria<sup>①</sup> to be favorable to them, so that he helped them to rebuild the Temple of God, the God of Israel.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>King Darius of Persia is here identified as the king of Assyria because Persia had conquered the Babylonian Empire, which included the earlier Assyrian Empire.  

## 7장

**7:1** Many years later, during the reign of King Artaxerxes of Persia,<sup>①</sup> there was a man named Ezra. ## 9장

**9:1** When these things had been done, the Jewish leaders came to me and said, “Many of the people of Israel, and even some of the priests and Levites, have not kept themselves separate from the other peoples living in the land. They have taken up the detestable practices of the Canaanites, Hittites, Perizzites, Jebusites, Ammonites, Moabites, Egyptians, and Amorites.  
**9:2** For the men of Israel have married women from these people and have taken them as wives for their sons. So the holy race has become polluted by these mixed marriages. Worse yet, the leaders and officials have led the way in this outrage.”  
**9:3** When I heard this, I tore my cloak and my shirt, pulled hair from my head and beard, and sat down utterly shocked.  
**9:4** Then all who trembled at the words of the God of Israel came and sat with me because of this outrage committed by the returned exiles. And I sat there utterly appalled until the time of the evening sacrifice.  
**9:5** At the time of the sacrifice, I stood up from where I had sat in mourning with my clothes torn. I fell to my knees and lifted my hands to the LORD my God.  
**9:6** I prayed,<br>“O my God, I am utterly ashamed; I blush to lift up my face to you. For our sins are piled higher than our heads, and our guilt has reached to the heavens.  
**9:7** From the days of our ancestors until now, we have been steeped in sin. That is why we and our kings and our priests have been at the mercy of the pagan kings of the land. We have been killed, captured, robbed, and disgraced, just as we are today.  
**9:8** “But now we have been given a brief moment of grace, for the LORD our God has allowed a few of us to survive as a remnant. He has given us security in this holy place. Our God has brightened our eyes and granted us some relief from our slavery.  
**9:9** For we were slaves, but in his unfailing love our God did not abandon us in our slavery. Instead, he caused the kings of Persia to treat us favorably. He revived us so we could rebuild the Temple of our God and repair its ruins. He has given us a protective wall in Judah and Jerusalem.  
**9:10** “And now, O our God, what can we say after all of this? For once again we have abandoned your commands!  
**9:11** Your servants the prophets warned us when they said, ‘The land you are entering to possess is totally defiled by the detestable practices of the people living there. From one end to the other, the land is filled with corruption.  
**9:12** Don’t let your daughters marry their sons! Don’t take their daughters as wives for your sons. Don’t ever promote the peace and prosperity of those nations. If you follow these instructions, you will be strong and will enjoy the good things the land produces, and you will leave this prosperity to your children forever.’  
**9:13** “Now we are being punished because of our wickedness and our great guilt. But we have actually been punished far less than we deserve, for you, our God, have allowed some of us to survive as a remnant.  
**9:14** But even so, we are again breaking your commands and intermarrying with people who do these detestable things. Won’t your anger be enough to destroy us, so that even this little remnant no longer survives?  
**9:15** O LORD, God of Israel, you are just. We come before you in our guilt as nothing but an escaped remnant, though in such a condition none of us can stand in your presence.”
