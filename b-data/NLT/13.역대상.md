# 13.역대상 (NLT)



## 1장

**1:1** The descendants of Adam were Seth, Enosh,  
**1:2** Kenan, Mahalalel, Jared,  
**1:3** Enoch, Methuselah, Lamech,  
**1:4** and Noah. The sons of Noah were<sup>①</sup> Shem, Ham, and Japheth.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in Greek version (see also <a name='B:10 5:3-32'>Gen 5:3-32); Hebrew lacks <i>The sons of Noah were.</i>  
**1:5** The descendants of Japheth were Gomer, Magog, Madai, Javan, Tubal, Meshech, and Tiras.  
**1:6** The descendants of Gomer were Ashkenaz, Riphath,<sup>①</sup> and Togarmah.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in some Hebrew manuscripts and Greek version (see also <a name='B:10 10:3'>Gen 10:3); most Hebrew manuscripts read <i>Diphath.</i>  
**1:7** The descendants of Javan were Elishah, Tarshish, Kittim, and Rodanim.  
**1:8** The descendants of Ham were Cush, Mizraim,<sup>①</sup> Put, and Canaan.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>Egypt;</i> also in <a name='B:130 1:11'>1:11.  
**1:9** The descendants of Cush were Seba, Havilah, Sabtah, Raamah, and Sabteca. The descendants of Raamah were Sheba and Dedan.  
**1:10** Cush was also the ancestor of Nimrod, who was the first heroic warrior on earth.  
**1:11** Mizraim was the ancestor of the Ludites, Anamites, Lehabites, Naphtuhites,  
**1:12** Pathrusites, Casluhites, and the Caphtorites, from whom the Philistines came.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>Casluhites, from whom the Philistines came, Caphtorites.</i> See <a name='B:300 47:4'>Jer 47:4; <a name='B:370 9:7'>Amos 9:7.  
**1:13** Canaan’s oldest son was Sidon, the ancestor of the Sidonians. Canaan was also the ancestor of the Hittites,<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>ancestor of Heth.</i>  
**1:14** Jebusites, Amorites, Girgashites,  
**1:15** Hivites, Arkites, Sinites,  
**1:16** Arvadites, Zemarites, and Hamathites.  
**1:17** The descendants of Shem were Elam, Asshur, Arphaxad, Lud, and Aram. The descendants of Aram were<sup>①</sup> Uz, Hul, Gether, and Mash.<sup>②</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in one Hebrew manuscript and some Greek manuscripts (see also <a name='B:10 10:23'>Gen 10:23); most Hebrew manuscripts lack <i>The descendants of Aram were.</i> <sup>②</sup>As in parallel text at <a name='B:10 10:23'>Gen 10:23; Hebrew reads <i>and Meshech.</i>  
**1:18** Arphaxad was the father of Shelah. Shelah was the father of Eber.  
**1:19** Eber had two sons. The first was named Peleg (which means “division”), for during his lifetime the people of the world were divided into different language groups. His brother’s name was Joktan.  
**1:20** Joktan was the ancestor of Almodad, Sheleph, Hazarmaveth, Jerah,  
**1:21** Hadoram, Uzal, Diklah,  
**1:22** Obal,<sup>①</sup> Abimael, Sheba,<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in some Hebrew manuscripts and Syriac version (see also <a name='B:10 10:28'>Gen 10:28); most Hebrew manuscripts read <i>Ebal.</i>  
**1:23** Ophir, Havilah, and Jobab. All these were descendants of Joktan.  
**1:24** So this is the family line descended from Shem: Arphaxad, Shelah,<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Some Greek manuscripts read <i>Arphaxad, Cainan, Shelah.</i> See notes on <a name='B:10 10:24'>Gen 10:24; <a name='B:10 11:12-13'>11:12-13.  
**1:25** Eber, Peleg, Reu,  
**1:26** Serug, Nahor, Terah,  
**1:27** and Abram, later known as Abraham.  
**1:28** The sons of Abraham were Isaac and Ishmael.  
**1:29** These are their genealogical records: The sons of Ishmael were Nebaioth (the oldest), Kedar, Adbeel, Mibsam,  
**1:30** Mishma, Dumah, Massa, Hadad, Tema,  
**1:31** Jetur, Naphish, and Kedemah. These were the sons of Ishmael.  
**1:32** The sons of Keturah, Abraham’s concubine, were Zimran, Jokshan, Medan, Midian, Ishbak, and Shuah. The sons of Jokshan were Sheba and Dedan.  
**1:33** The sons of Midian were Ephah, Epher, Hanoch, Abida, and Eldaah. All these were descendants of Abraham through his concubine Keturah.  
**1:34** Abraham was the father of Isaac. The sons of Isaac were Esau and Israel.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup><i>Israel</i> is the name that God gave to Jacob.  
**1:35** The sons of Esau were Eliphaz, Reuel, Jeush, Jalam, and Korah.  
**1:36** The descendants of Eliphaz were Teman, Omar, Zepho,<sup>①</sup> Gatam, Kenaz, and Amalek, who was born to Timna.<sup>②</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in many Hebrew manuscripts and a few Greek manuscripts (see also <a name='B:10 36:11'>Gen 36:11); most Hebrew manuscripts read <i>Zephi.</i> <sup>②</sup>As in some Greek manuscripts (see also <a name='B:10 36:12'>Gen 36:12); Hebrew reads <i>Kenaz, Timna, and Amalek.</i>  
**1:37** The descendants of Reuel were Nahath, Zerah, Shammah, and Mizzah.  
**1:38** The descendants of Seir were Lotan, Shobal, Zibeon, Anah, Dishon, Ezer, and Dishan.  
**1:39** The descendants of Lotan were Hori and Hemam.<sup>①</sup> Lotan’s sister was named Timna.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in parallel text at <a name='B:10 36:22'>Gen 36:22; Hebrew reads <i>and Homam.</i>  
**1:40** The descendants of Shobal were Alvan,<sup>①</sup> Manahath, Ebal, Shepho,<sup>②</sup> and Onam. The descendants of Zibeon were Aiah and Anah.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in many Hebrew manuscripts and a few Greek manuscripts (see also <a name='B:10 36:23'>Gen 36:23); most Hebrew manuscripts read <i>Alian.</i> <sup>②</sup>As in some Hebrew manuscripts (see also <a name='B:10 36:23'>Gen 36:23); most Hebrew manuscripts read <i>Shephi.</i>  
**1:41** The son of Anah was Dishon. The descendants of Dishon were Hemdan,<sup>①</sup> Eshban, Ithran, and Keran.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in many Hebrew manuscripts and some Greek manuscripts (see also <a name='B:10 36:26'>Gen 36:26); most Hebrew manuscripts read <i>Hamran.</i>  
**1:42** The descendants of Ezer were Bilhan, Zaavan, and Akan.<sup>①</sup> The descendants of Dishan<sup>②</sup> were Uz and Aran.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in many Hebrew and Greek manuscripts (see also <a name='B:10 36:27'>Gen 36:27); most Hebrew manuscripts read <i>Jaakan.</i> <sup>②</sup>Hebrew <i>Dishon;</i> compare <a name='B:130 1:38'>1:38 and parallel text at <a name='B:10 36:28'>Gen 36:28.  
**1:43** These are the kings who ruled in the land of Edom before any king ruled over the Israelites <sup>①</sup>: Bela son of Beor, who ruled from his city of Dinhabah.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>before an Israelite king ruled over them.</i>  
**1:44** When Bela died, Jobab son of Zerah from Bozrah became king in his place.  
**1:45** When Jobab died, Husham from the land of the Temanites became king in his place.  
**1:46** When Husham died, Hadad son of Bedad became king in his place and ruled from the city of Avith. He was the one who destroyed the Midianite army in the land of Moab.  
**1:47** When Hadad died, Samlah from the city of Masrekah became king in his place.  
**1:48** When Samlah died, Shaul from the city of Rehoboth-on-the-River became king in his place.  
**1:49** When Shaul died, Baal-hanan son of Acbor became king in his place.  
**1:50** When Baal-hanan died, Hadad became king in his place and ruled from the city of Pau.<sup>①</sup> His wife was Mehetabel, the daughter of Matred and granddaughter of Me-zahab.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in many Hebrew manuscripts, some Greek manuscripts, Syriac version, and Latin Vulgate (see also <a name='B:10 36:39'>Gen 36:39); most Hebrew manuscripts read <i>Pai.</i>  
**1:51** Then Hadad died. The clan leaders of Edom were Timna, Alvah,<sup>①</sup> Jetheth,<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in an alternate reading of the Masoretic Text (see also <a name='B:10 36:40'>Gen 36:40); the other alternate reads <i>Aliah.</i>  
**1:52** Oholibamah, Elah, Pinon,  
**1:53** Kenaz, Teman, Mibzar,  
**1:54** Magdiel, and Iram. These are the clan leaders of Edom.  

## 2장

**2:1** The sons of Israel<sup>①</sup> were Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Zebulun,<br>→ <sup>①</sup><i>Israel</i> is the name that God gave to Jacob.  
**2:2** Dan, Joseph, Benjamin, Naphtali, Gad, and Asher.  
**2:3** Judah had three sons from Bathshua, a Canaanite woman. Their names were Er, Onan, and Shelah. But the LORD saw that the oldest son, Er, was a wicked man, so he killed him.  
**2:4** Later Judah had twin sons from Tamar, his widowed daughter-in-law. Their names were Perez and Zerah. So Judah had five sons in all.  
**2:5** The sons of Perez were Hezron and Hamul.  
**2:6** The sons of Zerah were Zimri, Ethan, Heman, Calcol, and Darda<sup>①</sup>—five in all.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in many Hebrew manuscripts, some Greek manuscripts, and Syriac version (see also <a name='B:110 4:31'>1 Kgs 4:31); Hebrew reads <i>Dara.</i>  
**2:7** The son of Carmi (a descendant of Zimri) was Achan,<sup>①</sup> who brought disaster on Israel by taking plunder that had been set apart for the LORD.<sup>②</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>Achar;</i> compare <a name='B:60 7:1'>Josh 7:1. <i>Achar</i> means “disaster.” <sup>②</sup>The Hebrew term used here refers to the complete consecration of things or people to the LORD, either by destroying them or by giving them as an offering.  
**2:8** The son of Ethan was Azariah.  
**2:9** The sons of Hezron were Jerahmeel, Ram, and Caleb.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>Kelubai,</i> a variant spelling of Caleb; compare <a name='B:130 2:18'>2:18.  
**2:10** Ram was the father of Amminadab.<br>Amminadab was the father of Nahshon, a leader of Judah.  
**2:11** Nahshon was the father of Salmon.<sup>①</sup><br>Salmon was the father of Boaz.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in Greek version (see also <a name='B:80 4:20'>Ruth 4:20); Hebrew reads <i>Salma.</i>  
**2:12** Boaz was the father of Obed.<br>Obed was the father of Jesse.  
**2:13** Jesse’s first son was Eliab, his second was Abinadab, his third was Shimea,  
**2:14** his fourth was Nethanel, his fifth was Raddai,  
**2:15** his sixth was Ozem, and his seventh was David.  
**2:16** Their sisters were named Zeruiah and Abigail. Zeruiah had three sons named Abishai, Joab, and Asahel.  
**2:17** Abigail married a man named Jether, an Ishmaelite, and they had a son named Amasa.  
**2:18** Hezron’s son Caleb had sons from his wife Azubah and from Jerioth.<sup>①</sup> Her sons were named Jesher, Shobab, and Ardon.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>Caleb had a daughter named Jerioth from his wife, Azubah.</i> The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain.  
**2:19** After Azubah died, Caleb married Ephrathah,<sup>①</sup> and they had a son named Hur.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>Ephrath,</i> a variant spelling of Ephrathah; compare <a name='B:130 2:50'>2:50 and <a name='B:130 4:4'>4:4.  
**2:20** Hur was the father of Uri. Uri was the father of Bezalel.  
**2:21** When Hezron was sixty years old, he married Gilead’s sister, the daughter of Makir. They had a son named Segub.  
**2:22** Segub was the father of Jair, who ruled twenty-three towns in the land of Gilead.  
**2:23** (But Geshur and Aram captured the Towns of Jair<sup>①</sup> and also took Kenath and its sixty surrounding villages.) All these were descendants of Makir, the father of Gilead.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>captured Havvoth-jair.</i>  
**2:24** Soon after Hezron died in the town of Caleb-ephrathah, his wife Abijah gave birth to a son named Ashhur (the father of<sup>①</sup> Tekoa).<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>the founder of;</i> also in <a name='B:130 2:42'>2:42, <a name='B:130 2:45'>45, <a name='B:130 2:49'>49.  
**2:25** The sons of Jerahmeel, the oldest son of Hezron, were Ram (the firstborn), Bunah, Oren, Ozem, and Ahijah.  
**2:26** Jerahmeel had a second wife named Atarah. She was the mother of Onam.  
**2:27** The sons of Ram, the oldest son of Jerahmeel, were Maaz, Jamin, and Eker.  
**2:28** The sons of Onam were Shammai and Jada. The sons of Shammai were Nadab and Abishur.  
**2:29** The sons of Abishur and his wife Abihail were Ahban and Molid.  
**2:30** The sons of Nadab were Seled and Appaim. Seled died without children,  
**2:31** but Appaim had a son named Ishi. The son of Ishi was Sheshan. Sheshan had a descendant named Ahlai.  
**2:32** The sons of Jada, Shammai’s brother, were Jether and Jonathan. Jether died without children,  
**2:33** but Jonathan had two sons named Peleth and Zaza. These were all descendants of Jerahmeel.  
**2:34** Sheshan had no sons, though he did have daughters. He also had an Egyptian servant named Jarha.  
**2:35** Sheshan gave one of his daughters to be the wife of Jarha, and they had a son named Attai.  
**2:36** Attai was the father of Nathan.<br>Nathan was the father of Zabad.  
**2:37** Zabad was the father of Ephlal.<br>Ephlal was the father of Obed.  
**2:38** Obed was the father of Jehu.<br>Jehu was the father of Azariah.  
**2:39** Azariah was the father of Helez.<br>Helez was the father of Eleasah.  
**2:40** Eleasah was the father of Sismai.<br>Sismai was the father of Shallum.  
**2:41** Shallum was the father of Jekamiah.<br>Jekamiah was the father of Elishama.  
**2:42** The descendants of Caleb, the brother of Jerahmeel, included Mesha (the firstborn), who became the father of Ziph. Caleb’s descendants also included the sons of Mareshah, the father of Hebron.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>who founded Hebron.</i> The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain.  
**2:43** The sons of Hebron were Korah, Tappuah, Rekem, and Shema.  
**2:44** Shema was the father of Raham. Raham was the father of Jorkeam. Rekem was the father of Shammai.  
**2:45** The son of Shammai was Maon. Maon was the father of Beth-zur.  
**2:46** Caleb’s concubine Ephah gave birth to Haran, Moza, and Gazez. Haran was the father of Gazez.  
**2:47** The sons of Jahdai were Regem, Jotham, Geshan, Pelet, Ephah, and Shaaph.  
**2:48** Another of Caleb’s concubines, Maacah, gave birth to Sheber and Tirhanah.  
**2:49** She also gave birth to Shaaph (the father of Madmannah) and Sheva (the father of Macbenah and Gibea). Caleb also had a daughter named Acsah.  
**2:50** These were all descendants of Caleb.<br>The sons of Hur, the oldest son of Caleb’s wife Ephrathah, were Shobal (the founder of Kiriath-jearim),  
**2:51** Salma (the founder of Bethlehem), and Hareph (the founder of Beth-gader).  
**2:52** The descendants of Shobal (the founder of Kiriath-jearim) were Haroeh, half the Manahathites,  
**2:53** and the families of Kiriath-jearim—the Ithrites, Puthites, Shumathites, and Mishraites, from whom came the people of Zorah and Eshtaol.  
**2:54** The descendants of Salma were the people of Bethlehem, the Netophathites, Atroth-beth-joab, the other half of the Manahathites, the Zorites,  
**2:55** and the families of scribes living at Jabez—the Tirathites, Shimeathites, and Sucathites. All these were Kenites who descended from Hammath, the father of the family of Recab.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>the founder of Beth-recab.</i>  

## 3장

**3:1** These are the sons of David who were born in Hebron:<br>The oldest was Amnon, whose mother was Ahinoam from Jezreel.<br>The second was Daniel, whose mother was Abigail from Carmel.  
**3:2** The third was Absalom, whose mother was Maacah, the daughter of Talmai, king of Geshur.<br>The fourth was Adonijah, whose mother was Haggith.  
**3:3** The fifth was Shephatiah, whose mother was Abital.<br>The sixth was Ithream, whose mother was Eglah, David’s wife.  
**3:4** These six sons were born to David in Hebron, where he reigned seven and a half years. Then David reigned another thirty-three years in Jerusalem.  
**3:5** The sons born to David in Jerusalem included Shammua,<sup>①</sup> Shobab, Nathan, and Solomon. Their mother was Bathsheba,<sup>②</sup> the daughter of Ammiel.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in Syriac version (see also <a name='B:130 14:4'>14:4; <a name='B:100 5:14'>2 Sam 5:14); Hebrew reads <i>Shimea.</i> <sup>②</sup>Hebrew <i>Bathshua,</i> a variant spelling of Bathsheba.  
**3:6** David also had nine other sons: Ibhar, Elishua,<sup>①</sup> Elpelet,<sup>②</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in some Hebrew and Greek manuscripts (see also <a name='B:130 14:5-7'>14:5-7 and <a name='B:100 5:15'>2 Sam 5:15); most Hebrew manuscripts read <i>Elishama.</i> <sup>②</sup>Hebrew <i>Eliphelet;</i> compare parallel text at <a name='B:130 14:5-7'>14:5-7.  
**3:7** Nogah, Nepheg, Japhia,  
**3:8** Elishama, Eliada, and Eliphelet.  
**3:9** These were the sons of David, not including his sons born to his concubines. Their sister was named Tamar.  
**3:10** The descendants of Solomon were Rehoboam, Abijah, Asa, Jehoshaphat,  
**3:11** Jehoram,<sup>①</sup> Ahaziah, Joash,<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>Joram,</i> a variant spelling of Jehoram.  
**3:12** Amaziah, Uzziah,<sup>①</sup> Jotham,<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>Azariah,</i> a variant spelling of Uzziah.  
**3:13** Ahaz, Hezekiah, Manasseh,  
**3:14** Amon, and Josiah.  
**3:15** The sons of Josiah were Johanan (the oldest), Jehoiakim (the second), Zedekiah (the third), and Jehoahaz<sup>①</sup> (the fourth).<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>Shallum,</i> another name for Jehoahaz.  
**3:16** The successors of Jehoiakim were his son Jehoiachin and his brother Zedekiah.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>The sons of Jehoiakim were his son Jeconiah</i> [a variant spelling of Jehoiachin] <i>and his son Zedekiah.</i>  
**3:17** The sons of Jehoiachin,<sup>①</sup> who was taken prisoner by the Babylonians, were Shealtiel,<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>Jeconiah,</i> a variant spelling of Jehoiachin.  
**3:18** Malkiram, Pedaiah, Shenazzar, Jekamiah, Hoshama, and Nedabiah.  
**3:19** The sons of Pedaiah were Zerubbabel and Shimei. The sons of Zerubbabel were Meshullam and Hananiah. (Their sister was Shelomith.)  
**3:20** His five other sons were Hashubah, Ohel, Berekiah, Hasadiah, and Jushab-hesed.  
**3:21** The sons of Hananiah were Pelatiah and Jeshaiah. Jeshaiah’s son was Rephaiah. Rephaiah’s son was Arnan. Arnan’s son was Obadiah. Obadiah’s son was Shecaniah.  
**3:22** The descendants of Shecaniah were Shemaiah and his sons, Hattush, Igal, Bariah, Neariah, and Shaphat—six in all.  
**3:23** The sons of Neariah were Elioenai, Hizkiah, and Azrikam—three in all.  
**3:24** The sons of Elioenai were Hodaviah, Eliashib, Pelaiah, Akkub, Johanan, Delaiah, and Anani—seven in all.  

## 4장

**4:1** The descendants of Judah were Perez, Hezron, Carmi, Hur, and Shobal.  
**4:2** Shobal’s son Reaiah was the father of Jahath. Jahath was the father of Ahumai and Lahad. These were the families of the Zorathites.  
**4:3** The descendants of<sup>①</sup> Etam were Jezreel, Ishma, Idbash, their sister Hazzelelponi,<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in Greek version; Hebrew reads <i>father of.</i> The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain.  
**4:4** Penuel (the father of<sup>①</sup> Gedor), and Ezer (the father of Hushah). These were the descendants of Hur (the firstborn of Ephrathah), the ancestor of Bethlehem.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>the founder of;</i> also in <a name='B:130 4:5'>4:5, <a name='B:130 4:12'>12, <a name='B:130 4:14'>14, <a name='B:130 4:17'>17, <a name='B:130 4:18'>18, and perhaps other instances where the text reads <i>the father of.</i>  
**4:5** Ashhur (the father of Tekoa) had two wives, named Helah and Naarah.  
**4:6** Naarah gave birth to Ahuzzam, Hepher, Temeni, and Haahashtari.  
**4:7** Helah gave birth to Zereth, Izhar,<sup>①</sup> Ethnan,<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in an alternate reading in the Masoretic Text (see also Latin Vulgate); the other alternate and the Greek version read <i>Zohar.</i>  
**4:8** and Koz, who became the ancestor of Anub, Zobebah, and all the families of Aharhel son of Harum.  
**4:9** There was a man named Jabez who was more honorable than any of his brothers. His mother named him Jabez<sup>①</sup> because his birth had been so painful.<br>→ <sup>①</sup><i>Jabez</i> sounds like a Hebrew word meaning “distress” or “pain.”  
**4:10** He was the one who prayed to the God of Israel, “Oh, that you would bless me and expand my territory! Please be with me in all that I do, and keep me from all trouble and pain!” And God granted him his request.  
**4:11** Kelub (the brother of Shuhah) was the father of Mehir. Mehir was the father of Eshton.  
**4:12** Eshton was the father of Beth-rapha, Paseah, and Tehinnah. Tehinnah was the father of Ir-nahash. These were the descendants of Recah.  
**4:13** The sons of Kenaz were Othniel and Seraiah. Othniel’s sons were Hathath and Meonothai.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in some Greek manuscripts and Latin Vulgate; Hebrew lacks <i>and Meonothai.</i>  
**4:14** Meonothai was the father of Ophrah. Seraiah was the father of Joab, the founder of the Valley of Craftsmen,<sup>①</sup> so called because they were craftsmen.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>Joab, the father of Ge-harashim.</i>  
**4:15** The sons of Caleb son of Jephunneh were Iru, Elah, and Naam. The son of Elah was Kenaz.  
**4:16** The sons of Jehallelel were Ziph, Ziphah, Tiria, and Asarel.  
**4:17** The sons of Ezrah were Jether, Mered, Epher, and Jalon. One of Mered’s wives became<sup>①</sup> the mother of Miriam, Shammai, and Ishbah (the father of Eshtemoa).<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>Jether’s wife became;</i> Hebrew reads <i>She became.</i>  
**4:18** He married a woman from Judah, who became the mother of Jered (the father of Gedor), Heber (the father of Soco), and Jekuthiel (the father of Zanoah). Mered also married Bithia, a daughter of Pharaoh, and she bore him children.  
**4:19** Hodiah’s wife was the sister of Naham. One of her sons was the father of Keilah the Garmite, and another was the father of Eshtemoa the Maacathite.

## 28장

**28:1** David summoned all the officials of Israel to Jerusalem—the leaders of the tribes, the commanders of the army divisions, the other generals and captains,<sup>①</sup> the overseers of the royal property and livestock, the palace officials, the mighty men, and all the other brave warriors in the kingdom.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>the commanders of thousands and commanders of hundreds.</i>  
**28:2** David rose to his feet and said: “My brothers and my people! It was my desire to build a Temple where the Ark of the LORD’s Covenant, God’s footstool, could rest permanently. I made the necessary preparations for building it,  
**28:3** but God said to me, ‘You must not build a Temple to honor my name, for you are a warrior and have shed much blood.’  
**28:4** “Yet the LORD, the God of Israel, has chosen me from among all my father’s family to be king over Israel forever. For he has chosen the tribe of Judah to rule, and from among the families of Judah he chose my father’s family. And from among my father’s sons the LORD was pleased to make me king over all Israel.  
**28:5** And from among my sons—for the LORD has given me many—he chose Solomon to succeed me on the throne of Israel and to rule over the LORD’s kingdom.  
**28:6** He said to me, ‘Your son Solomon will build my Temple and its courtyards, for I have chosen him as my son, and I will be his father.  
**28:7** And if he continues to obey my commands and regulations as he does now, I will make his kingdom last forever.’  
**28:8** “So now, with God as our witness, and in the sight of all Israel—the LORD’s assembly—I give you this charge. Be careful to obey all the commands of the LORD your God, so that you may continue to possess this good land and leave it to your children as a permanent inheritance.  
**28:9** “And Solomon, my son, learn to know the God of your ancestors intimately. Worship and serve him with your whole heart and a willing mind. For the LORD sees every heart and knows every plan and thought. If you seek him, you will find him. But if you forsake him, he will reject you forever.  
**28:10** So take this seriously. The LORD has chosen you to build a Temple as his sanctuary. Be strong, and do the work.”  
**28:11** Then David gave Solomon the plans for the Temple and its surroundings, including the entry room, the storerooms, the upstairs rooms, the inner rooms, and the inner sanctuary—which was the place of atonement.  
**28:12** David also gave Solomon all the plans he had in mind<sup>①</sup> for the courtyards of the LORD’s Temple, the outside rooms, the treasuries, and the rooms for the gifts dedicated to the LORD.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>the plans of the spirit that was with him.</i>  
**28:13** The king also gave Solomon the instructions concerning the work of the various divisions of priests and Levites in the Temple of the LORD. And he gave specifications for the items in the Temple that were to be used for worship.  
**28:14** David gave instructions regarding how much gold and silver should be used to make the items needed for service.  
**28:15** He told Solomon the amount of gold needed for the gold lampstands and lamps, and the amount of silver for the silver lampstands and lamps, depending on how each would be used.  
**28:16** He designated the amount of gold for the table on which the Bread of the Presence would be placed and the amount of silver for other tables.  
**28:17** David also designated the amount of gold for the solid gold meat hooks used to handle the sacrificial meat and for the basins, pitchers, and dishes, as well as the amount of silver for every dish.  
**28:18** He designated the amount of refined gold for the altar of incense. Finally, he gave him a plan for the LORD’s “chariot”—the gold cherubim<sup>①</sup> whose wings were stretched out over the Ark of the LORD’s Covenant.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>for the gold cherub chariot.</i>  
**28:19** “Every part of this plan,” David told Solomon, “was given to me in writing from the hand of the LORD. <sup>①</sup>”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>was written under the direction of the LORD.</i>  
**28:20** Then David continued, “Be strong and courageous, and do the work. Don’t be afraid or discouraged, for the LORD God, my God, is with you. He will not fail you or forsake you. He will see to it that all the work related to the Temple of the LORD is finished correctly.  
**28:21** The various divisions of priests and Levites will serve in the Temple of God. Others with skills of every kind will volunteer, and the officials and the entire nation are at your command.”  

## 29장

**29:1** Then King David turned to the entire assembly and said, “My son Solomon, whom God has clearly chosen as the next king of Israel, is still young and inexperienced. The work ahead of him is enormous, for the Temple he will build is not for mere mortals—it is for the LORD God himself!  
**29:2** Using every resource at my command, I have gathered as much as I could for building the Temple of my God. Now there is enough gold, silver, bronze, iron, and wood, as well as great quantities of onyx, other precious stones, costly jewels, and all kinds of fine stone and marble.  
**29:3** “And now, because of my devotion to the Temple of my God, I am giving all of my own private treasures of gold and silver to help in the construction. This is in addition to the building materials I have already collected for his holy Temple.  
**29:4** I am donating more than 112 tons of gold<sup>①</sup> from Ophir and 262 tons of refined silver<sup>②</sup> to be used for overlaying the walls of the buildings<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>3,000 talents</i> [102 metric tons] <i>of gold.</i> <sup>②</sup>Hebrew <i>7,000 talents</i> [238 metric tons] <i>of silver.</i>  
**29:5** and for the other gold and silver work to be done by the craftsmen. Now then, who will follow my example and give offerings to the LORD today?”  
**29:6** Then the family leaders, the leaders of the tribes of Israel, the generals and captains of the army,<sup>①</sup> and the king’s administrative officers all gave willingly.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>the commanders of thousands and commanders of hundreds.</i>  
**29:7** For the construction of the Temple of God, they gave about 188 tons of gold,<sup>①</sup> 10,000 gold coins,<sup>②</sup> 375 tons of silver,<sup>③</sup> 675 tons of bronze,<sup>④</sup> and 3,750 tons of iron.<sup>⑤</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>5,000 talents</i> [170 metric tons] <i>of gold.</i> <sup>②</sup>Hebrew <i>10,000 darics</i> [a Persian coin] <i>of gold,</i> about 185 pounds or 84 kilograms in weight. <sup>③</sup>Hebrew <i>10,000 talents</i> [340 metric tons] <i>of silver.</i> <sup>④</sup>Hebrew <i>18,000 talents</i> [612 metric tons] <i>of bronze.</i> <sup>⑤</sup>Hebrew <i>100,000 talents</i> [3,400 metric tons] <i>of iron.</i>  
**29:8** They also contributed numerous precious stones, which were deposited in the treasury of the house of the LORD under the care of Jehiel, a descendant of Gershon.  
**29:9** The people rejoiced over the offerings, for they had given freely and wholeheartedly to the LORD, and King David was filled with joy.  
**29:10** Then David praised the LORD in the presence of the whole assembly:<br>“O LORD, the God of our ancestor Israel,<sup>①</sup> may you be praised forever and ever!<br>→ <sup>①</sup><i>Israel</i> is the name that God gave to Jacob.

**29:12** Wealth and honor come from you alone, for you rule over everything. Power and might are in your hand, and at your discretion people are made great and given strength.  
**29:13** “O our God, we thank you and praise your glorious name!  
**29:14** But who am I, and who are my people, that we could give anything to you? Everything we have has come from you, and we give you only what you first gave us!  
**29:15** We are here for only a moment, visitors and strangers in the land as our ancestors were before us. Our days on earth are like a passing shadow, gone so soon without a trace.  
**29:16** “O LORD our God, even this material we have gathered to build a Temple to honor your holy name comes from you! It all belongs to you!  
**29:17** I know, my God, that you examine our hearts and rejoice when you find integrity there. You know I have done all this with good motives, and I have watched your people offer their gifts willingly and joyously.  
**29:18** “O LORD, the God of our ancestors Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, make your people always want to obey you. See to it that their love for you never changes.  
**29:19** Give my son Solomon the wholehearted desire to obey all your commands, laws, and decrees, and to do everything necessary to build this Temple, for which I have made these preparations.”  
**29:20** Then David said to the whole assembly, “Give praise to the LORD your God!” And the entire assembly praised the LORD, the God of their ancestors, and they bowed low and knelt before the LORD and the king.  
**29:21** The next day they brought 1,000 bulls, 1,000 rams, and 1,000 male lambs as burnt offerings to the LORD. They also brought liquid offerings and many other sacrifices on behalf of all Israel.  
**29:22** They feasted and drank in the LORD’s presence with great joy that day. And again they crowned David’s son Solomon as their new king. They anointed him before the LORD as their leader, and they anointed Zadok as priest.  
**29:23** So Solomon took the throne of the LORD in place of his father, David, and he succeeded in everything, and all Israel obeyed him.  
**29:24** All the officials, the warriors, and the sons of King David pledged their loyalty to King Solomon.  
**29:25** And the LORD exalted Solomon in the sight of all Israel, and he gave Solomon greater royal splendor than any king in Israel before him.  
**29:26** So David son of Jesse reigned over all Israel.  
**29:27** He reigned over Israel for forty years, seven of them in Hebron and thirty-three in Jerusalem.  
**29:28** He died at a ripe old age, having enjoyed long life, wealth, and honor. Then his son Solomon ruled in his place.  
**29:29** All the events of King David’s reign, from beginning to end, are written in <i>The Record of Samuel the Seer, The Record of Nathan the Prophet,</i> and <i>The Record of Gad the Seer.</i>
