# 11.열왕기상 (NLT)



## 1장

**1:1** King David was now very old, and no matter how many blankets covered him, he could not keep warm.  
**1:2** So his advisers told him, “Let us find a young virgin to wait on you and look after you, my lord. She will lie in your arms and keep you warm.”  
**1:3** So they searched throughout the land of Israel for a beautiful girl, and they found Abishag from Shunem and brought her to the king.  
**1:4** The girl was very beautiful, and she looked after the king and took care of him. But the king had no sexual relations with her.  
**1:5** About that time David’s son Adonijah, whose mother was Haggith, began boasting, “I will make myself king.” So he provided himself with chariots and charioteers and recruited fifty men to run in front of him.  
**1:6** Now his father, King David, had never disciplined him at any time, even by asking, “Why are you doing that?” Adonijah had been born next after Absalom, and he was very handsome.  
**1:7** Adonijah took Joab son of Zeruiah and Abiathar the priest into his confidence, and they agreed to help him become king.  
**1:8** But Zadok the priest, Benaiah son of Jehoiada, Nathan the prophet, Shimei, Rei, and David’s personal bodyguard refused to support Adonijah.  
**1:9** Adonijah went to the Stone of Zoheleth<sup>①</sup> near the spring of En-rogel, where he sacrificed sheep, cattle, and fattened calves. He invited all his brothers—the other sons of King David—and all the royal officials of Judah.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>to the Serpent’s Stone;</i> Greek version supports reading <i>Zoheleth</i> as a proper name.  
**1:10** But he did not invite Nathan the prophet or Benaiah or the king’s bodyguard or his brother Solomon.  
**1:11** Then Nathan went to Bathsheba, Solomon’s mother, and asked her, “Haven’t you heard that Haggith’s son, Adonijah, has made himself king, and our lord David doesn’t even know about it?  
**1:12** If you want to save your own life and the life of your son Solomon, follow my advice.  
**1:13** Go at once to King David and say to him, ‘My lord the king, didn’t you make a vow and say to me, “Your son Solomon will surely be the next king and will sit on my throne”? Why then has Adonijah become king?’  
**1:14** And while you are still talking with him, I will come and confirm everything you have said.”  
**1:15** So Bathsheba went into the king’s bedroom. (He was very old now, and Abishag was taking care of him.)  
**1:16** Bathsheba bowed down before the king. “What can I do for you?” he asked her.  
**1:17** She replied, “My lord, you made a vow before the LORD your God when you said to me, ‘Your son Solomon will surely be the next king and will sit on my throne.’  
**1:18** But instead, Adonijah has made himself king, and my lord the king does not even know about it.  
**1:19** He has sacrificed many cattle, fattened calves, and sheep, and he has invited all the king’s sons to attend the celebration. He also invited Abiathar the priest and Joab, the commander of the army. But he did not invite your servant Solomon.  
**1:20** And now, my lord the king, all Israel is waiting for you to announce who will become king after you.  
**1:21** If you do not act, my son Solomon and I will be treated as criminals as soon as my lord the king has died.”  
**1:22** While she was still speaking with the king, Nathan the prophet arrived.  
**1:23** The king’s officials told him, “Nathan the prophet is here to see you.” Nathan went in and bowed before the king with his face to the ground.  
**1:24** Nathan asked, “My lord the king, have you decided that Adonijah will be the next king and that he will sit on your throne?  
**1:25** Today he has sacrificed many cattle, fattened calves, and sheep, and he has invited all the king’s sons to attend the celebration. He also invited the commanders of the army and Abiathar the priest. They are feasting and drinking with him and shouting, ‘Long live King Adonijah!’  
**1:26** But he did not invite me or Zadok the priest or Benaiah or your servant Solomon.  
**1:27** Has my lord the king really done this without letting any of his officials know who should be the next king?”  
**1:28** King David responded, “Call Bathsheba!” So she came back in and stood before the king.  
**1:29** And the king repeated his vow: “As surely as the LORD lives, who has rescued me from every danger,  
**1:30** your son Solomon will be the next king and will sit on my throne this very day, just as I vowed to you before the LORD, the God of Israel.”  
**1:31** Then Bathsheba bowed down with her face to the ground before the king and exclaimed, “May my lord King David live forever!”  
**1:32** Then King David ordered, “Call Zadok the priest, Nathan the prophet, and Benaiah son of Jehoiada.” When they came into the king’s presence,  
**1:33** the king said to them, “Take Solomon and my officials down to Gihon Spring. Solomon is to ride on my own mule.  
**1:34** There Zadok the priest and Nathan the prophet are to anoint him king over Israel. Blow the ram’s horn and shout, ‘Long live King Solomon!’  
**1:35** Then escort him back here, and he will sit on my throne. He will succeed me as king, for I have appointed him to be ruler over Israel and Judah.”  
**1:36** “Amen!” Benaiah son of Jehoiada replied. “May the LORD, the God of my lord the king, decree that it happen.  
**1:37** And may the LORD be with Solomon as he has been with you, my lord the king, and may he make Solomon’s reign even greater than yours!”  
**1:38** So Zadok the priest, Nathan the prophet, Benaiah son of Jehoiada, and the king’s bodyguard<sup>①</sup> took Solomon down to Gihon Spring, with Solomon riding on King David’s own mule.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>the Kerethites and Pelethites;</i> also in <a name='B:110 1:44'>1:44.  
**1:39** There Zadok the priest took the flask of olive oil from the sacred tent and anointed Solomon with the oil. Then they sounded the ram’s horn and all the people shouted, “Long live King Solomon!”  
**1:40** And all the people followed Solomon into Jerusalem, playing flutes and shouting for joy. The celebration was so joyous and noisy that the earth shook with the sound.  
**1:41** Adonijah and his guests heard the celebrating and shouting just as they were finishing their banquet. When Joab heard the sound of the ram’s horn, he asked, “What’s going on? Why is the city in such an uproar?”  
**1:42** And while he was still speaking, Jonathan son of Abiathar the priest arrived. “Come in,” Adonijah said to him, “for you are a good man. You must have good news.”  
**1:43** “Not at all!” Jonathan replied. “Our lord King David has just declared Solomon king!  
**1:44** The king sent him down to Gihon Spring with Zadok the priest, Nathan the prophet, and Benaiah son of Jehoiada, protected by the king’s bodyguard. They had him ride on the king’s own mule,  
**1:45** and Zadok and Nathan have anointed him at Gihon Spring as the new king. They have just returned, and the whole city is celebrating and rejoicing. That’s what all the noise is about.  
**1:46** What’s more, Solomon is now sitting on the royal throne as king.  
**1:47** And all the royal officials have gone to King David and congratulated him, saying, ‘May your God make Solomon’s fame even greater than your own, and may Solomon’s reign be even greater than yours!’ Then the king bowed his head in worship as he lay in his bed,  
**1:48** and he said, ‘Praise the LORD, the God of Israel, who today has chosen a successor to sit on my throne while I am still alive to see it.’”  
**1:49** Then all of Adonijah’s guests jumped up in panic from the banquet table and quickly scattered.  
**1:50** Adonijah was afraid of Solomon, so he rushed to the sacred tent and grabbed on to the horns of the altar.  
**1:51** Word soon reached Solomon that Adonijah had seized the horns of the altar in fear, and that he was pleading, “Let King Solomon swear today that he will not kill me!”  
**1:52** Solomon replied, “If he proves himself to be loyal, not a hair on his head will be touched. But if he makes trouble, he will die.”  
**1:53** So King Solomon summoned Adonijah, and they brought him down from the altar. He came and bowed respectfully before King Solomon, who dismissed him, saying, “Go on home.”  

## 2장

**2:1** As the time of King David’s death approached, he gave this charge to his son Solomon:  
**2:2** “I am going where everyone on earth must someday go. Take courage and be a man.  
**2:3** Observe the requirements of the LORD your God, and follow all his ways. Keep the decrees, commands, regulations, and laws written in the Law of Moses so that you will be successful in all you do and wherever you go.  
**2:4** If you do this, then the LORD will keep the promise he made to me. He told me, ‘If your descendants live as they should and follow me faithfully with all their heart and soul, one of them will always sit on the throne of Israel.’  
**2:5** “And there is something else. You know what Joab son of Zeruiah did to me when he murdered my two army commanders, Abner son of Ner and Amasa son of Jether. He pretended that it was an act of war, but it was done in a time of peace,<sup>①</sup> staining his belt and sandals with innocent blood.<sup>②</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>He murdered them during a time of peace as revenge for deaths they had caused in time of war.</i> <sup>②</sup>As in some Greek and Old Latin manuscripts; Hebrew reads <i>with the blood of war.</i>  
**2:6** Do with him what you think best, but don’t let him grow old and go to his grave in peace.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>don’t let his white head go down to Sheol in peace.</i>  
**2:7** “Be kind to the sons of Barzillai of Gilead. Make them permanent guests at your table, for they took care of me when I fled from your brother Absalom.  
**2:8** “And remember Shimei son of Gera, the man from Bahurim in Benjamin. He cursed me with a terrible curse as I was fleeing to Mahanaim. When he came down to meet me at the Jordan River, I swore by the LORD that I would not kill him.  
**2:9** But that oath does not make him innocent. You are a wise man, and you will know how to arrange a bloody death for him. <sup>①</sup>”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>how to bring his white head down to Sheol in blood.</i>  
**2:10** Then David died and was buried with his ancestors in the City of David.  
**2:11** David had reigned over Israel for forty years, seven of them in Hebron and thirty-three in Jerusalem.  
**2:12** Solomon became king and sat on the throne of David his father, and his kingdom was firmly established.  
**2:13** One day Adonijah, whose mother was Haggith, came to see Bathsheba, Solomon’s mother. “Have you come with peaceful intentions?” she asked him. “Yes,” he said, “I come in peace.  
**2:14** In fact, I have a favor to ask of you.” “What is it?” she asked.  
**2:15** He replied, “As you know, the kingdom was rightfully mine; all Israel wanted me to be the next king. But the tables were turned, and the kingdom went to my brother instead; for that is the way the LORD wanted it.  
**2:16** So now I have just one favor to ask of you. Please don’t turn me down.” “What is it?” she asked.  
**2:17** He replied, “Speak to King Solomon on my behalf, for I know he will do anything you request. Ask him to let me marry Abishag, the girl from Shunem.”  
**2:18** “All right,” Bathsheba replied. “I will speak to the king for you.”  
**2:19** So Bathsheba went to King Solomon to speak on Adonijah’s behalf. The king rose from his throne to meet her, and he bowed down before her. When he sat down on his throne again, the king ordered that a throne be brought for his mother, and she sat at his right hand.  
**2:20** “I have one small request to make of you,” she said. “I hope you won’t turn me down.” “What is it, my mother?” he asked. “You know I won’t refuse you.”  
**2:21** “Then let your brother Adonijah marry Abishag, the girl from Shunem,” she replied.  
**2:22** “How can you possibly ask me to give Abishag to Adonijah?” King Solomon demanded. “You might as well ask me to give him the kingdom! You know that he is my older brother, and that he has Abiathar the priest and Joab son of Zeruiah on his side.”  
**2:23** Then King Solomon made a vow before the LORD: “May God strike me and even kill me if Adonijah has not sealed his fate with this request.  
**2:24** The LORD has confirmed me and placed me on the throne of my father, David; he has established my dynasty as he promised. So as surely as the LORD lives, Adonijah will die this very day!”  
**2:25** So King Solomon ordered Benaiah son of Jehoiada to execute him, and Adonijah was put to death.  
**2:26** Then the king said to Abiathar the priest, “Go back to your home in Anathoth. You deserve to die, but I will not kill you now, because you carried the Ark of the Sovereign LORD for David my father and you shared all his hardships.”  
**2:27** So Solomon deposed Abiathar from his position as priest of the LORD, thereby fulfilling the prophecy the LORD had given at Shiloh concerning the descendants of Eli.  
**2:28** Joab had not joined Absalom’s earlier rebellion, but he had joined Adonijah’s rebellion. So when Joab heard about Adonijah’s death, he ran to the sacred tent of the LORD and grabbed on to the horns of the altar.  
**2:29** When this was reported to King Solomon, he sent Benaiah son of Jehoiada to execute him.  
**2:30** Benaiah went to the sacred tent of the LORD and said to Joab, “The king orders you to come out!” But Joab answered, “No, I will die here.” So Benaiah returned to the king and told him what Joab had said.  
**2:31** “Do as he said,” the king replied. “Kill him there beside the altar and bury him. This will remove the guilt of Joab’s senseless murders from me and from my father’s family.  
**2:32** The LORD will repay him<sup>①</sup> for the murders of two men who were more righteous and better than he. For my father knew nothing about the deaths of Abner son of Ner, commander of the army of Israel, and of Amasa son of Jether, commander of the army of Judah.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>will return his blood on his own head.</i>  
**2:33** May their blood be on Joab and his descendants forever, and may the LORD grant peace forever to David, his descendants, his dynasty, and his throne.”  
**2:34** So Benaiah son of Jehoiada returned to the sacred tent and killed Joab, and he was buried at his home in the wilderness.  
**2:35** Then the king appointed Benaiah to command the army in place of Joab, and he installed Zadok the priest to take the place of Abiathar.  
**2:36** The king then sent for Shimei and told him, “Build a house here in Jerusalem and live there. But don’t step outside the city to go anywhere else.  
**2:37** On the day you so much as cross the Kidron Valley, you will surely die; and your blood will be on your own head.”  
**2:38** Shimei replied, “Your sentence is fair; I will do whatever my lord the king commands.” So Shimei lived in Jerusalem for a long time.  
**2:39** But three years later two of Shimei’s slaves ran away to King Achish son of Maacah of Gath. When Shimei learned where they were,  
**2:40** he saddled his donkey and went to Gath to search for them. When he found them, he brought them back to Jerusalem.  
**2:41** Solomon heard that Shimei had left Jerusalem and had gone to Gath and returned.  
**2:42** So the king sent for Shimei and demanded, “Didn’t I make you swear by the LORD and warn you not to go anywhere else or you would surely die? And you replied, ‘The sentence is fair; I will do as you say.’  
**2:43** Then why haven’t you kept your oath to the LORD and obeyed my command?”  
**2:44** The king also said to Shimei, “You certainly remember all the wicked things you did to my father, David. May the LORD now bring that evil on your own head.  
**2:45** But may I, King Solomon, receive the LORD’s blessings, and may one of David’s descendants always sit on this throne in the presence of the LORD.”  
**2:46** Then, at the king’s command, Benaiah son of Jehoiada took Shimei outside and killed him. So the kingdom was now firmly in Solomon’s grip.  

## 3장

**3:1** Solomon made an alliance with Pharaoh, the king of Egypt, and married one of his daughters. He brought her to live in the City of David until he could finish building his palace and the Temple of the LORD and the wall around the city.  
**3:2** At that time the people of Israel sacrificed their offerings at local places of worship, for a temple honoring the name of the LORD had not yet been built.  
**3:3** Solomon loved the LORD and followed all the decrees of his father, David, except that Solomon, too, offered sacrifices and burned incense at the local places of worship.  
**3:4** The most important of these places of worship was at Gibeon, so the king went there and sacrificed 1,000 burnt offerings.  
**3:5** That night the LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream, and God said, “What do you want? Ask, and I will give it to you!”  
**3:6** Solomon replied, “You showed great and faithful love to your servant my father, David, because he was honest and true and faithful to you. And you have continued to show this great and faithful love to him today by giving him a son to sit on his throne.  
**3:7** “Now, O LORD my God, you have made me king instead of my father, David, but I am like a little child who doesn’t know his way around.  
**3:8** And here I am in the midst of your own chosen people, a nation so great and numerous they cannot be counted!  
**3:9** Give me an understanding heart so that I can govern your people well and know the difference between right and wrong. For who by himself is able to govern this great people of yours?”  
**3:10** The Lord was pleased that Solomon had asked for wisdom.  
**3:11** So God replied, “Because you have asked for wisdom in governing my people with justice and have not asked for a long life or wealth or the death of your enemies—  
**3:12** I will give you what you asked for! I will give you a wise and understanding heart such as no one else has had or ever will have!  
**3:13** And I will also give you what you did not ask for—riches and fame! No other king in all the world will be compared to you for the rest of your life!  
**3:14** And if you follow me and obey my decrees and my commands as your father, David, did, I will give you a long life.”  
**3:15** Then Solomon woke up and realized it had been a dream. He returned to Jerusalem and stood before the Ark of the Lord’s Covenant, where he sacrificed burnt offerings and peace offerings. Then he invited all his officials to a great banquet.  
**3:16** Some time later two prostitutes came to the king to have an argument settled.  
**3:17** “Please, my lord,” one of them began, “this woman and I live in the same house. I gave birth to a baby while she was with me in the house.  
**3:18** Three days later this woman also had a baby. We were alone; there were only two of us in the house.  
**3:19** “But her baby died during the night when she rolled over on it.  
**3:20** Then she got up in the night and took my son from beside me while I was asleep. She laid her dead child in my arms and took mine to sleep beside her.  
**3:21** And in the morning when I tried to nurse my son, he was dead! But when I looked more closely in the morning light, I saw that it wasn’t my son at all.”  
**3:22** Then the other woman interrupted, “It certainly was your son, and the living child is mine.” “No,” the first woman said, “the living child is mine, and the dead one is yours.” And so they argued back and forth before the king.  
**3:23** Then the king said, “Let’s get the facts straight. Both of you claim the living child is yours, and each says that the dead one belongs to the other.  
**3:24** All right, bring me a sword.” So a sword was brought to the king.  
**3:25** Then he said, “Cut the living child in two, and give half to one woman and half to the other!”  
**3:26** Then the woman who was the real mother of the living child, and who loved him very much, cried out, “Oh no, my lord! Give her the child—please do not kill him!” But the other woman said, “All right, he will be neither yours nor mine; divide him between us!”  
**3:27** Then the king said, “Do not kill the child, but give him to the woman who wants him to live, for she is his mother!”  
**3:28** When all Israel heard the king’s decision, the people were in awe of the king, for they saw the wisdom God had given him for rendering justice.

**4:28** They also brought the necessary barley and straw for the royal horses in the stables.

**4:30** In fact, his wisdom exceeded that of all the wise men of the East and the wise men of Egypt.  
**4:31** He was wiser than anyone else, including Ethan the Ezrahite and the sons of Mahol—Heman, Calcol, and Darda. His fame spread throughout all the surrounding nations.  
**4:32** He composed some 3,000 proverbs and wrote 1,005 songs.  
**4:33** He could speak with authority about all kinds of plants, from the great cedar of Lebanon to the tiny hyssop that grows from cracks in a wall. He could also speak about animals, birds, small creatures, and fish.  
**4:34** And kings from every nation sent their ambassadors to listen to the wisdom of Solomon.  

## 5장

**5:1** <sup>①</sup>King Hiram of Tyre had always been a loyal friend of David. When Hiram learned that David’s son Solomon was the new king of Israel, he sent ambassadors to congratulate him.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Verses <a name='B:110 5:1-18'>5:1-18 are numbered 5:15-32 in Hebrew text.  
**5:2** Then Solomon sent this message back to Hiram:  
**5:3** “You know that my father, David, was not able to build a Temple to honor the name of the LORD his God because of the many wars waged against him by surrounding nations. He could not build until the LORD gave him victory over all his enemies.  
**5:4** But now the LORD my God has given me peace on every side; I have no enemies, and all is well.  
**5:5** So I am planning to build a Temple to honor the name of the LORD my God, just as he had instructed my father, David. For the LORD told him, ‘Your son, whom I will place on your throne, will build the Temple to honor my name.’  
**5:6** “Therefore, please command that cedars from Lebanon be cut for me. Let my men work alongside yours, and I will pay your men whatever wages you ask. As you know, there is no one among us who can cut timber like you Sidonians!”  
**5:7** When Hiram received Solomon’s message, he was very pleased and said, “Praise the LORD today for giving David a wise son to be king of the great nation of Israel.”  
**5:8** Then he sent this reply to Solomon:<br>“I have received your message, and I will supply all the cedar and cypress timber you need.

## 8장

## 11장

**11:1** Now King Solomon loved many foreign women. Besides Pharaoh’s daughter, he married women from Moab, Ammon, Edom, Sidon, and from among the Hittites.  
**11:2** The LORD had clearly instructed the people of Israel, “You must not marry them, because they will turn your hearts to their gods.” Yet Solomon insisted on loving them anyway.  
**11:3** He had 700 wives of royal birth and 300 concubines. And in fact, they did turn his heart away from the LORD.  
**11:4** In Solomon’s old age, they turned his heart to worship other gods instead of being completely faithful to the LORD his God, as his father, David, had been.  
**11:5** Solomon worshiped Ashtoreth, the goddess of the Sidonians, and Molech,<sup>①</sup> the detestable god of the Ammonites.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>Milcom,</i> a variant spelling of Molech; also in <a name='B:110 11:33'>11:33.  
**11:6** In this way, Solomon did what was evil in the LORD’s sight; he refused to follow the LORD completely, as his father, David, had done.  
**11:7** On the Mount of Olives, east of Jerusalem,<sup>①</sup> he even built a pagan shrine for Chemosh, the detestable god of Moab, and another for Molech, the detestable god of the Ammonites.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>On the mountain east of Jerusalem.</i>  
**11:8** Solomon built such shrines for all his foreign wives to use for burning incense and sacrificing to their gods.  
**11:9** The LORD was very angry with Solomon, for his heart had turned away from the LORD, the God of Israel, who had appeared to him twice.  
**11:10** He had warned Solomon specifically about worshiping other gods, but Solomon did not listen to the LORD’s command.  
**11:11** So now the LORD said to him, “Since you have not kept my covenant and have disobeyed my decrees, I will surely tear the kingdom away from you and give it to one of your servants.  
**11:12** But for the sake of your father, David, I will not do this while you are still alive. I will take the kingdom away from your son.  
**11:13** And even so, I will not take away the entire kingdom; I will let him be king of one tribe, for the sake of my servant David and for the sake of Jerusalem, my chosen city.”  
**11:14** Then the LORD raised up Hadad the Edomite, a member of Edom’s royal family, to be Solomon’s adversary.  
**11:15** Years before, David had defeated Edom. Joab, his army commander, had stayed to bury some of the Israelite soldiers who had died in battle. While there, they killed every male in Edom.  
**11:16** Joab and the army of Israel had stayed there for six months, killing them.  
**11:17** But Hadad and a few of his father’s royal officials escaped and headed for Egypt. (Hadad was just a boy at the time.)  
**11:18** They set out from Midian and went to Paran, where others joined them. Then they traveled to Egypt and went to Pharaoh, who gave them a home, food, and some land.  
**11:19** Pharaoh grew very fond of Hadad, and he gave him his wife’s sister in marriage—the sister of Queen Tahpenes.  
**11:20** She bore him a son named Genubath. Tahpenes raised him<sup>①</sup> in Pharaoh’s palace among Pharaoh’s own sons.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in Greek version; Hebrew reads <i>weaned him.</i>  
**11:21** When the news reached Hadad in Egypt that David and his commander Joab were both dead, he said to Pharaoh, “Let me return to my own country.”  
**11:22** “Why?” Pharaoh asked him. “What do you lack here that makes you want to go home?” “Nothing,” he replied. “But even so, please let me return home.”  
**11:23** God also raised up Rezon son of Eliada as Solomon’s adversary. Rezon had fled from his master, King Hadadezer of Zobah,  
**11:24** and had become the leader of a gang of rebels. After David conquered Hadadezer, Rezon and his men fled to Damascus, where he became king.  
**11:25** Rezon was Israel’s bitter adversary for the rest of Solomon’s reign, and he made trouble, just as Hadad did. Rezon hated Israel intensely and continued to reign in Aram.  
**11:26** Another rebel leader was Jeroboam son of Nebat, one of Solomon’s own officials. He came from the town of Zeredah in Ephraim, and his mother was Zeruah, a widow.  
**11:27** This is the story behind his rebellion. Solomon was rebuilding the supporting terraces<sup>①</sup> and repairing the walls of the city of his father, David.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>the millo.</i> The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain.  
**11:28** Jeroboam was a very capable young man, and when Solomon saw how industrious he was, he put him in charge of the labor force from the tribes of Ephraim and Manasseh, the descendants of Joseph.  
**11:29** One day as Jeroboam was leaving Jerusalem, the prophet Ahijah from Shiloh met him along the way. Ahijah was wearing a new cloak. The two of them were alone in a field,  
**11:30** and Ahijah took hold of the new cloak he was wearing and tore it into twelve pieces.  
**11:31** Then he said to Jeroboam, “Take ten of these pieces, for this is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: ‘I am about to tear the kingdom from the hand of Solomon, and I will give ten of the tribes to you!  
**11:32** But I will leave him one tribe for the sake of my servant David and for the sake of Jerusalem, which I have chosen out of all the tribes of Israel.  
**11:33** For Solomon has<sup>①</sup> abandoned me and worshiped Ashtoreth, the goddess of the Sidonians; Chemosh, the god of Moab; and Molech, the god of the Ammonites. He has not followed my ways and done what is pleasing in my sight. He has not obeyed my decrees and regulations as David his father did.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in Greek, Syriac, and Latin Vulgate; Hebrew reads <i>For they have.</i>

**11:35** But I will take the kingdom away from his son and give ten of the tribes to you.  
**11:36** His son will have one tribe so that the descendants of David my servant will continue to reign, shining like a lamp in Jerusalem, the city I have chosen to be the place for my name.  
**11:37** And I will place you on the throne of Israel, and you will rule over all that your heart desires.

**11:41** The rest of the events in Solomon’s reign, including all his deeds and his wisdom, are recorded in <i>The Book of the Acts of Solomon.</i>  
**11:42** Solomon ruled in Jerusalem over all Israel for forty years.  
**11:43** When he died, he was buried in the City of David, named for his father. Then his son Rehoboam became the next king.  

## 12장

**12:1** Rehoboam went to Shechem, where all Israel had gathered to make him king.  
**12:2** When Jeroboam son of Nebat heard of this, he returned from Egypt,<sup>①</sup> for he had fled to Egypt to escape from King Solomon.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in Greek version and Latin Vulgate (see also <a name='B:140 10:2'>2 Chr 10:2); Hebrew reads <i>he lived in Egypt.</i>  
**12:3** The leaders of Israel summoned him, and Jeroboam and the whole assembly of Israel went to speak with Rehoboam.  
**12:4** “Your father was a hard master,” they said. “Lighten the harsh labor demands and heavy taxes that your father imposed on us. Then we will be your loyal subjects.”  
**12:5** Rehoboam replied, “Give me three days to think this over. Then come back for my answer.” So the people went away.  
**12:6** Then King Rehoboam discussed the matter with the older men who had counseled his father, Solomon. “What is your advice?” he asked. “How should I answer these people?”  
**12:7** The older counselors replied, “If you are willing to be a servant to these people today and give them a favorable answer, they will always be your loyal subjects.”  
**12:8** But Rehoboam rejected the advice of the older men and instead asked the opinion of the young men who had grown up with him and were now his advisers.  
**12:9** “What is your advice?” he asked them. “How should I answer these people who want me to lighten the burdens imposed by my father?”  
**12:10** The young men replied, “This is what you should tell those complainers who want a lighter burden: ‘My little finger is thicker than my father’s waist!  
**12:11** Yes, my father laid heavy burdens on you, but I’m going to make them even heavier! My father beat you with whips, but I will beat you with scorpions!’”  
**12:12** Three days later Jeroboam and all the people returned to hear Rehoboam’s decision, just as the king had ordered.  
**12:13** But Rehoboam spoke harshly to the people, for he rejected the advice of the older counselors  
**12:14** and followed the counsel of his younger advisers. He told the people, “My father laid heavy burdens on you, but I’m going to make them even heavier! My father beat you with whips, but I will beat you with scorpions!”  
**12:15** So the king paid no attention to the people. This turn of events was the will of the LORD, for it fulfilled the LORD’s message to Jeroboam son of Nebat through the prophet Ahijah from Shiloh.  
**12:16** When all Israel realized that the king had refused to listen to them, they responded,<br>“Down with the dynasty of David!<br>We have no interest in the son of Jesse.<br>Back to your homes, O Israel!<br>Look out for your own house, O David!” So the people of Israel returned home.  
**12:17** But Rehoboam continued to rule over the Israelites who lived in the towns of Judah.  
**12:18** King Rehoboam sent Adoniram,<sup>①</sup> who was in charge of forced labor, to restore order, but the people of Israel stoned him to death. **12:20** When the people of Israel learned of Jeroboam’s return from Egypt, they called an assembly and made him king over all Israel. So only the tribe of Judah remained loyal to the family of David.  
**12:21** When Rehoboam arrived at Jerusalem, he mobilized the men of Judah and the tribe of Benjamin—180,000 select troops—to fight against the men of Israel and to restore the kingdom to himself.  
**12:22** But God said to Shemaiah, the man of God,  
**12:23** “Say to Rehoboam son of Solomon, king of Judah, and to all the people of Judah and Benjamin, and to the rest of the people,  
**12:24** ‘This is what the LORD says: Do not fight against your relatives, the Israelites. Go back home, for what has happened is my doing!’” So they obeyed the message of the LORD and went home, as the LORD had commanded.  
**12:25** Jeroboam then built up the city of Shechem in the hill country of Ephraim, and it became his capital. Later he went and built up the town of Peniel.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>Penuel,</i> a variant spelling of Peniel.

**12:28** So on the advice of his counselors, the king made two gold calves. He said to the people,<sup>①</sup> “It is too much trouble for you to worship in Jerusalem. Look, Israel, these are the gods who brought you out of Egypt!”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>to them.</i>

## 13장

**13:5** At the same time a wide crack appeared in the altar, and the ashes poured out, just as the man of God had predicted in his message from the LORD.  
**13:6** The king cried out to the man of God, “Please ask the LORD your God to restore my hand again!” So the man of God prayed to the LORD, and the king’s hand was restored and he could move it again.  
**13:7** Then the king said to the man of God, “Come to the palace with me and have something to eat, and I will give you a gift.”  
**13:8** But the man of God said to the king, “Even if you gave me half of everything you own, I would not go with you. I would not eat or drink anything in this place.  
**13:9** For the LORD gave me this command: ‘You must not eat or drink anything while you are there, and do not return to Judah by the same way you came.’”

**13:14** Then he rode after the man of God and found him sitting under a great tree. The old prophet asked him, “Are you the man of God who came from Judah?” “Yes, I am,” he replied.  
**13:15** Then he said to the man of God, “Come home with me and eat some food.”  
**13:16** “No, I cannot,” he replied. “I am not allowed to eat or drink anything here in this place.

**13:22** You came back to this place and ate and drank where he told you not to eat or drink. Because of this, your body will not be buried in the grave of your ancestors.”

**13:27** Then the prophet said to his sons, “Saddle a donkey for me.” So they saddled a donkey,  
**13:28** and he went out and found the body lying in the road. The donkey and lion were still standing there beside it, for the lion had not eaten the body nor attacked the donkey.  
**13:29** So the prophet laid the body of the man of God on the donkey and took it back to the town to mourn over him and bury him.  
**13:30** He laid the body in his own grave, crying out in grief, “Oh, my brother!”  
**13:31** Afterward the prophet said to his sons, “When I die, bury me in the grave where the man of God is buried. Lay my bones beside his bones.

## 17장

**17:1** Now Elijah, who was from Tishbe in Gilead, told King Ahab, “As surely as the LORD, the God of Israel, lives—the God I serve—there will be no dew or rain during the next few years until I give the word!”

**17:5** So Elijah did as the LORD told him and camped beside Kerith Brook, east of the Jordan.  
**17:6** The ravens brought him bread and meat each morning and evening, and he drank from the brook.  
**17:7** But after a while the brook dried up, for there was no rainfall anywhere in the land.  
**17:8** Then the LORD said to Elijah,  
**17:9** “Go and live in the village of Zarephath, near the city of Sidon. I have instructed a widow there to feed you.”  
**17:10** So he went to Zarephath. As he arrived at the gates of the village, he saw a widow gathering sticks, and he asked her, “Would you please bring me a little water in a cup?”  
**17:11** As she was going to get it, he called to her, “Bring me a bite of bread, too.”  
**17:12** But she said, “I swear by the LORD your God that I don’t have a single piece of bread in the house. **17:13** But Elijah said to her, “Don’t be afraid! Go ahead and do just what you’ve said, but make a little bread for me first. Then use what’s left to prepare a meal for yourself and your son.

**17:15** So she did as Elijah said, and she and Elijah and her family continued to eat for many days.

**17:17** Some time later the woman’s son became sick. He grew worse and worse, and finally he died.  
**17:18** Then she said to Elijah, “O man of God, what have you done to me? Have you come here to point out my sins and kill my son?”

**17:20** Then Elijah cried out to the LORD, “O LORD my God, why have you brought tragedy to this widow who has opened her home to me, causing her son to die?”  
**17:21** And he stretched himself out over the child three times and cried out to the LORD, “O LORD my God, please let this child’s life return to him.”  
**17:22** The LORD heard Elijah’s prayer, and the life of the child returned, and he revived!  
**17:23** Then Elijah brought him down from the upper room and gave him to his mother. “Look!” he said. “Your son is alive!”  
**17:24** Then the woman told Elijah, “Now I know for sure that you are a man of God, and that the LORD truly speaks through you.”  

## 18장

**22:28** But Micaiah replied, “If you return safely, it will mean that the LORD has not spoken through me!” Then he added to those standing around, “Everyone mark my words!”  
**22:29** So King Ahab of Israel and King Jehoshaphat of Judah led their armies against Ramoth-gilead.  
**22:30** The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, “As we go into battle, I will disguise myself so no one will recognize me, but you wear your royal robes.” So the king of Israel disguised himself, and they went into battle.  
**22:31** Meanwhile, the king of Aram had issued these orders to his thirty-two chariot commanders: “Attack only the king of Israel. Don’t bother with anyone else!”  
**22:32** So when the Aramean chariot commanders saw Jehoshaphat in his royal robes, they went after him. “There is the king of Israel!” they shouted. But when Jehoshaphat called out,  
**22:33** the chariot commanders realized he was not the king of Israel, and they stopped chasing him.  
**22:34** An Aramean soldier, however, randomly shot an arrow at the Israelite troops and hit the king of Israel between the joints of his armor. “Turn the horses<sup>①</sup> and get me out of here!” Ahab groaned to the driver of his chariot. “I’m badly wounded!”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>Turn your hand.</i>  
**22:35** The battle raged all that day, and the king remained propped up in his chariot facing the Arameans. The blood from his wound ran down to the floor of his chariot, and as evening arrived he died.  
**22:36** Just as the sun was setting, the cry ran through his troops: “We’re done for! Run for your lives!”  
**22:37** So the king died, and his body was taken to Samaria and buried there.  
**22:38** Then his chariot was washed beside the pool of Samaria, and dogs came and licked his blood at the place where the prostitutes bathed,<sup>①</sup> just as the LORD had promised.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>his blood, and the prostitutes bathed [in it];</i> or <i>his blood, and they washed his armor.</i>  
**22:39** The rest of the events in Ahab’s reign and everything he did, including the story of the ivory palace and the towns he built, are recorded in <i>The Book of the History of the Kings of Israel.</i>  
**22:40** So Ahab died, and his son Ahaziah became the next king.  
**22:41** Jehoshaphat son of Asa began to rule over Judah in the fourth year of King Ahab’s reign in Israel.  
**22:42** Jehoshaphat was thirty-five years old when he became king, and he reigned in Jerusalem twenty-five years. His mother was Azubah, the daughter of Shilhi.  
**22:43** Jehoshaphat was a good king, following the example of his father, Asa. He did what was pleasing in the LORD’s sight. <sup>①</sup>During his reign, however, he failed to remove all the pagan shrines, and the people still offered sacrifices and burned incense there.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Verses <a name='B:110 22:43'>22:43b-<a name='B:110 22:53'>53 are numbered 22:44-54 in Hebrew text.  
**22:44** Jehoshaphat also made peace with the king of Israel.  
**22:45** The rest of the events in Jehoshaphat’s reign, the extent of his power, and the wars he waged are recorded in <i>The Book of the History of the Kings of Judah.</i>  
**22:46** He banished from the land the rest of the male and female shrine prostitutes, who still continued their practices from the days of his father, Asa.  
**22:47** (There was no king in Edom at that time, only a deputy.)  
**22:48** Jehoshaphat also built a fleet of trading ships<sup>①</sup> to sail to Ophir for gold. But the ships never set sail, for they met with disaster in their home port of Ezion-geber.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>fleet of ships of Tarshish.</i>  
**22:49** At one time Ahaziah son of Ahab had proposed to Jehoshaphat, “Let my men sail with your men in the ships.” But Jehoshaphat refused the request.  
**22:50** When Jehoshaphat died, he was buried with his ancestors in the City of David. Then his son Jehoram became the next king.  
**22:51** Ahaziah son of Ahab began to rule over Israel in the seventeenth year of King Jehoshaphat’s reign in Judah. He reigned in Samaria two years.  
**22:52** But he did what was evil in the LORD’s sight, following the example of his father and mother and the example of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who had led Israel to sin.  
**22:53** He served Baal and worshiped him, provoking the anger of the LORD, the God of Israel, just as his father had done.
