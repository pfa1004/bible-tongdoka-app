# 24.예레미야 (NLT)



## 1장

**1:1** These are the words of Jeremiah son of Hilkiah, one of the priests from the town of Anathoth in the land of Benjamin.  
**1:2** The LORD first gave messages to Jeremiah during the thirteenth year of the reign of Josiah son of Amon, king of Judah.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>The thirteenth year of Josiah’s reign was 627 B.C.  
**1:3** The LORD’s messages continued throughout the reign of King Jehoiakim, Josiah’s son, until the eleventh year of the reign of King Zedekiah, another of Josiah’s sons. In August<sup>①</sup> of that eleventh year the people of Jerusalem were taken away as captives.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>In the fifth month,</i> of the ancient Hebrew lunar calendar. A number of events in Jeremiah can be cross-checked with dates in surviving Babylonian records and related accurately to our modern calendar. The fifth month in the eleventh year of Zedekiah’s reign occurred within the months of August and September 586 B.C. Also see 52:12 and the note there.  
**1:4** The LORD gave me this message:  
**1:5** “I knew you before I formed you in your mother’s womb.<br>Before you were born I set you apart<br>and appointed you as my prophet to the nations.”  
**1:6** “O Sovereign LORD,” I said, “I can’t speak for you! I’m too young!”  
**1:7** The LORD replied, “Don’t say, ‘I’m too young,’ for you must go wherever I send you and say whatever I tell you.  
**1:8** And don’t be afraid of the people, for I will be with you and will protect you. I, the LORD, have spoken!”  
**1:9** Then the LORD reached out and touched my mouth and said,<br>“Look, I have put my words in your mouth!  
**1:10** Today I appoint you to stand up<br>against nations and kingdoms.<br>Some you must uproot and tear down,<br>destroy and overthrow.<br>Others you must build up<br>and plant.”  
**1:11** Then the LORD said to me, “Look, Jeremiah! What do you see?” And I replied, “I see a branch from an almond tree.”  
**1:12** And the LORD said, “That’s right, and it means that I am watching,<sup>①</sup> and I will certainly carry out all my plans.”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>The Hebrew word for “watching” <i>(shoqed)</i> sounds like the word for “almond tree” <i>(shaqed).</i>  
**1:13** Then the LORD spoke to me again and asked, “What do you see now?” And I replied, “I see a pot of boiling water, spilling from the north.”  
**1:14** “Yes,” the LORD said, “for terror from the north will boil out on the people of this land.  
**1:15** Listen! I am calling the armies of the kingdoms of the north to come to Jerusalem. I, the LORD, have spoken!<br>“They will set their thrones<br>at the gates of the city.<br>They will attack its walls<br>and all the other towns of Judah.  
**1:16** I will pronounce judgment<br>on my people for all their evil—<br>for deserting me and burning incense to other gods.<br>Yes, they worship idols made with their own hands!  
**1:17** “Get up and prepare for action.<br>Go out and tell them everything I tell you to say.<br>Do not be afraid of them,<br>or I will make you look foolish in front of them.  
**1:18** For see, today I have made you strong<br>like a fortified city that cannot be captured,<br>like an iron pillar or a bronze wall.<br>You will stand against the whole land—<br>the kings, officials, priests, and people of Judah.  
**1:19** They will fight you, but they will fail.<br>For I am with you, and I will take care of you.<br>I, the LORD, have spoken!”  

## 2장

**2:1** The LORD gave me another message. He said,  
**2:2** “Go and shout this message to Jerusalem. This is what the LORD says:<br>“I remember how eager you were to please me<br>as a young bride long ago,<br>how you loved me and followed me<br>even through the barren wilderness.  
**2:3** In those days Israel was holy to the LORD,<br>the first of his children.<sup>①</sup><br>All who harmed his people were declared guilty,<br>and disaster fell on them.<br>I, the LORD, have spoken!”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>the firstfruits of his harvest.</i>  
**2:4** Listen to the word of the LORD, people of Jacob—all you families of Israel!  
**2:5** This is what the LORD says:<br>“What did your ancestors find wrong with me<br>that led them to stray so far from me?<br>They worshiped worthless idols,<br>only to become worthless themselves.  
**2:6** They did not ask, ‘Where is the LORD<br>who brought us safely out of Egypt<br>and led us through the barren wilderness—<br>a land of deserts and pits,<br>a land of drought and death,<br>where no one lives or even travels?’  
**2:7** “And when I brought you into a fruitful land<br>to enjoy its bounty and goodness,<br>you defiled my land and<br>corrupted the possession I had promised you.  
**2:8** The priests did not ask,<br>‘Where is the LORD?’<br>Those who taught my word ignored me,<br>the rulers turned against me,<br>and the prophets spoke in the name of Baal,<br>wasting their time on worthless idols.  
**2:9** Therefore, I will bring my case against you,”<br>says the LORD.<br>“I will even bring charges against your children’s children<br>in the years to come.  
**2:10** “Go west and look in the land of Cyprus<sup>①</sup>;<br>go east and search through the land of Kedar.<br>Has anyone ever heard of anything<br>as strange as this?<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>Kittim.</i>  
**2:11** Has any nation ever traded its gods for new ones,<br>even though they are not gods at all?<br>Yet my people have exchanged their glorious God<sup>①</sup><br>for worthless idols!<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>their glory.</i>  
**2:12** The heavens are shocked at such a thing<br>and shrink back in horror and dismay,”<br>says the LORD.  
**2:13** “For my people have done two evil things:<br>They have abandoned me—<br>the fountain of living water.<br>And they have dug for themselves cracked cisterns<br>that can hold no water at all!  
**2:14** “Why has Israel become a slave?<br>Why has he been carried away as plunder?  
**2:15** Strong lions have roared against him,<br>and the land has been destroyed.<br>The towns are now in ruins,<br>and no one lives in them anymore.  
**2:16** Egyptians, marching from their cities of Memphis<sup>①</sup> and Tahpanhes,<br>have destroyed Israel’s glory and power.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>Noph.</i>  
**2:17** And you have brought this upon yourselves<br>by rebelling against the LORD your God,<br>even though he was leading you on the way!  
**2:18** “What have you gained by your alliances with Egypt<br>and your covenants with Assyria?<br>What good to you are the streams of the Nile<sup>①</sup><br>or the waters of the Euphrates River?<sup>②</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>of Shihor,</i> a branch of the Nile River. <sup>②</sup>Hebrew <i>the river?</i>  
**2:19** Your wickedness will bring its own punishment.<br>Your turning from me will shame you.<br>You will see what an evil, bitter thing it is<br>to abandon the LORD your God and not to fear him.<br>I, the Lord, the LORD of Heaven’s Armies, have spoken!  
**2:20** “Long ago I broke the yoke that oppressed you<br>and tore away the chains of your slavery,<br>but still you said,<br>‘I will not serve you.’<br>On every hill and under every green tree,<br>you have prostituted yourselves by bowing down to idols.  
**2:21** But I was the one who planted you,<br>choosing a vine of the purest stock—the very best.<br>How did you grow into this corrupt wild vine?  
**2:22** No amount of soap or lye can make you clean.<br>I still see the stain of your guilt.<br>I, the Sovereign LORD, have spoken!  
**2:23** “You say, ‘That’s not true!<br>I haven’t worshiped the images of Baal!’<br>But how can you say that?<br>Go and look in any valley in the land!<br>Face the awful sins you have done.<br>You are like a restless female camel<br>desperately searching for a mate.  
**2:24** You are like a wild donkey,<br>sniffing the wind at mating time.<br>Who can restrain her lust?<br>Those who desire her don’t need to search,<br>for she goes running to them!  
**2:25** When will you stop running?<br>When will you stop panting after other gods?<br>But you say, ‘Save your breath.<br>I’m in love with these foreign gods,<br>and I can’t stop loving them now!’  
**2:26** “Israel is like a thief<br>who feels shame only when he gets caught.<br>They, their kings, officials, priests, and prophets—<br>all are alike in this.  
**2:27** To an image carved from a piece of wood they say,<br>‘You are my father.’<br>To an idol chiseled from a block of stone they say,<br>‘You are my mother.’<br>They turn their backs on me,<br>but in times of trouble they cry out to me,<br>‘Come and save us!’  
**2:28** But why not call on these gods you have made?<br>When trouble comes, let them save you if they can!<br>For you have as many gods<br>as there are towns in Judah.  
**2:29** Why do you accuse me of doing wrong?<br>You are the ones who have rebelled,”<br>says the LORD.  
**2:30** “I have punished your children,<br>but they did not respond to my discipline.<br>You yourselves have killed your prophets<br>as a lion kills its prey.  
**2:31** “O my people, listen to the words of the LORD!<br>Have I been like a desert to Israel?<br>Have I been to them a land of darkness?<br>Why then do my people say, ‘At last we are free from God!<br>We don’t need him anymore!’  
**2:32** Does a young woman forget her jewelry,<br>or a bride her wedding dress?<br>Yet for years on end<br>my people have forgotten me.  
**2:33** “How you plot and scheme to win your lovers.<br>Even an experienced prostitute could learn from you!  
**2:34** Your clothing is stained with the blood of the innocent and the poor,<br>though you didn’t catch them breaking into your houses!  
**2:35** And yet you say,<br>‘I have done nothing wrong.<br>Surely God isn’t angry with me!’<br>But now I will punish you severely<br>because you claim you have not sinned.  
**2:36** First here, then there—<br>you flit from one ally to another asking for help.<br>But your new friends in Egypt will let you down,<br>just as Assyria did before.  
**2:37** In despair, you will be led into exile<br>with your hands on your heads,<br>for the LORD has rejected the nations you trust.<br>They will not help you at all.  

## 3장

**3:1** “If a man divorces a woman<br>and she goes and marries someone else,<br>he will not take her back again,<br>for that would surely corrupt the land.<br>But you have prostituted yourself with many lovers,<br>so why are you trying to come back to me?”<br>says the LORD.  
**3:2** “Look at the shrines on every hilltop.<br>Is there any place you have not been defiled<br>by your adultery with other gods?<br>You sit like a prostitute beside the road waiting for a customer.<br>You sit alone like a nomad in the desert.<br>You have polluted the land with your prostitution<br>and your wickedness.  
**3:3** That’s why even the spring rains have failed.<br>For you are a brazen prostitute and completely shameless.  
**3:4** Yet you say to me,<br>‘Father, you have been my guide since my youth.  
**3:5** Surely you won’t be angry forever!<br>Surely you can forget about it!’<br>So you talk,<br>but you keep on doing all the evil you can.”  
**3:6** During the reign of King Josiah, the LORD said to me, “Have you seen what fickle Israel has done? Like a wife who commits adultery, Israel has worshiped other gods on every hill and under every green tree.  
**3:7** I thought, ‘After she has done all this, she will return to me.’ But she did not return, and her faithless sister Judah saw this.  
**3:8** She saw<sup>①</sup> that I divorced faithless Israel because of her adultery. But that treacherous sister Judah had no fear, and now she, too, has left me and given herself to prostitution.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in Dead Sea Scrolls, one Greek manuscript, and Syriac version; Masoretic Text reads <i>I saw.</i>  
**3:9** Israel treated it all so lightly—she thought nothing of committing adultery by worshiping idols made of wood and stone. So now the land has been polluted.  
**3:10** But despite all this, her faithless sister Judah has never sincerely returned to me. She has only pretended to be sorry. I, the LORD, have spoken!”  
**3:11** Then the LORD said to me, “Even faithless Israel is less guilty than treacherous Judah!  
**3:12** Therefore, go and give this message to Israel.<sup>①</sup> This is what the LORD says:<br>“O Israel, my faithless people,<br>come home to me again,<br>for I am merciful.<br>I will not be angry with you forever.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>toward the north.</i>  
**3:13** Only acknowledge your guilt.<br>Admit that you rebelled against the LORD your God<br>and committed adultery against him<br>by worshiping idols under every green tree.<br>Confess that you refused to listen to my voice.<br>I, the LORD, have spoken!  
**3:14** “Return home, you wayward children,”<br>says the LORD,<br>“for I am your master.<br>I will bring you back to the land of Israel<sup>①</sup>—<br>one from this town and two from that family—<br>from wherever you are scattered.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>to Zion.</i>  
**3:15** And I will give you shepherds after my own heart,<br>who will guide you with knowledge and understanding.  
**3:16** “And when your land is once more filled with people,” says the LORD, “you will no longer wish for ‘the good old days’ when you possessed the Ark of the LORD’s Covenant. You will not miss those days or even remember them, and there will be no need to rebuild the Ark.  
**3:17** In that day Jerusalem will be known as ‘The Throne of the LORD.’ All nations will come there to honor the LORD. They will no longer stubbornly follow their own evil desires.  
**3:18** In those days the people of Judah and Israel will return together from exile in the north. They will return to the land I gave your ancestors as an inheritance forever.  
**3:19** “I thought to myself,<br>‘I would love to treat you as my own children!’<br>I wanted nothing more than to give you this beautiful land—<br>the finest possession in the world.<br>I looked forward to your calling me ‘Father,’<br>and I wanted you never to turn from me.  
**3:20** But you have been unfaithful to me, you people of Israel!<br>You have been like a faithless wife who leaves her husband.<br>I, the LORD, have spoken.”  
**3:21** Voices are heard high on the windswept mountains,<br>the weeping and pleading of Israel’s people.<br>For they have chosen crooked paths<br>and have forgotten the LORD their God.  
**3:22** “My wayward children,” says the LORD,<br>“come back to me, and I will heal your wayward hearts.”<br>“Yes, we’re coming,” the people reply,<br>“for you are the LORD our God.  
**3:23** Our worship of idols on the hills<br>and our religious orgies on the mountains<br>are a delusion.<br>Only in the LORD our God<br>will Israel ever find salvation.  
**3:24** From childhood we have watched<br>as everything our ancestors worked for—<br>their flocks and herds, their sons and daughters—<br>was squandered on a delusion.  
**3:25** Let us now lie down in shame<br>and cover ourselves with dishonor,<br>for we and our ancestors have sinned<br>against the LORD our God.<br>From our childhood to this day<br>we have never obeyed him.”  

## 4장

**4:1** “O Israel,” says the LORD,<br>“if you wanted to return to me, you could.<br>You could throw away your detestable idols<br>and stray away no more.  
**4:2** Then when you swear by my name, saying,<br>‘As surely as the LORD lives,’<br>you could do so<br>with truth, justice, and righteousness.<br>Then you would be a blessing to the nations of the world,<br>and all people would come and praise my name.”  
**4:3** This is what the LORD says to the people of Judah and Jerusalem:<br>“Plow up the hard ground of your hearts!<br>Do not waste your good seed among thorns.  
**4:4** O people of Judah and Jerusalem,<br>surrender your pride and power.<br>Change your hearts before the LORD,<sup>①</sup><br>or my anger will burn like an unquenchable fire<br>because of all your sins.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>Circumcise yourselves to the LORD, and take away the foreskins of your heart.</i>  
**4:5** “Shout to Judah, and broadcast to Jerusalem!<br>Tell them to sound the alarm throughout the land:<br>‘Run for your lives!<br>Flee to the fortified cities!’  
**4:6** Raise a signal flag as a warning for Jerusalem <sup>①</sup>:<br>‘Flee now! Do not delay!’<br>For I am bringing terrible destruction upon you<br>from the north.”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>Zion.</i>  
**4:7** A lion stalks from its den,<br>a destroyer of nations.<br>It has left its lair and is headed your way.<br>It’s going to devastate your land!<br>Your towns will lie in ruins,<br>with no one living in them anymore.  
**4:8** So put on clothes of mourning<br>and weep with broken hearts,<br>for the fierce anger of the LORD<br>is still upon us.  
**4:9** “In that day,” says the LORD,<br>“the king and the officials will tremble in fear.<br>The priests will be struck with horror,<br>and the prophets will be appalled.”  
**4:10** Then I said, “O Sovereign LORD,<br>the people have been deceived by what you said,<br>for you promised peace for Jerusalem.<br>But the sword is held at their throats!”  
**4:11** The time is coming when the LORD will say<br>to the people of Jerusalem,<br>“My dear people, a burning wind is blowing in from the desert,<br>and it’s not a gentle breeze useful for winnowing grain.  
**4:12** It is a roaring blast sent by me!<br>Now I will pronounce your destruction!”  
**4:13** Our enemy rushes down on us like storm clouds!<br>His chariots are like whirlwinds.<br>His horses are swifter than eagles.<br>How terrible it will be, for we are doomed!  
**4:14** O Jerusalem, cleanse your heart<br>that you may be saved.<br>How long will you harbor<br>your evil thoughts?  
**4:15** Your destruction has been announced<br>from Dan and the hill country of Ephraim.  
**4:16** “Warn the surrounding nations<br>and announce this to Jerusalem:<br>The enemy is coming from a distant land,<br>raising a battle cry against the towns of Judah.  
**4:17** They surround Jerusalem like watchmen around a field,<br>for my people have rebelled against me,”<br>says the LORD.  
**4:18** “Your own actions have brought this upon you.<br>This punishment is bitter, piercing you to the heart!”  
**4:19** My heart, my heart—I writhe in pain!<br>My heart pounds within me! I cannot be still.<br>For I have heard the blast of enemy trumpets<br>and the roar of their battle cries.  
**4:20** Waves of destruction roll over the land,<br>until it lies in complete desolation.<br>Suddenly my tents are destroyed;<br>in a moment my shelters are crushed.  
**4:21** How long must I see the battle flags<br>and hear the trumpets of war?  
**4:22** “My people are foolish<br>and do not know me,” says the LORD.<br>“They are stupid children<br>who have no understanding.<br>They are clever enough at doing wrong,<br>but they have no idea how to do right!”  
**4:23** I looked at the earth, and it was empty and formless.<br>I looked at the heavens, and there was no light.  
**4:24** I looked at the mountains and hills,<br>and they trembled and shook.  
**4:25** I looked, and all the people were gone.<br>All the birds of the sky had flown away.  
**4:26** I looked, and the fertile fields had become a wilderness.<br>The towns lay in ruins,<br>crushed by the LORD’s fierce anger.  
**4:27** This is what the LORD says:<br>“The whole land will be ruined,<br>but I will not destroy it completely.  
**4:28** The earth will mourn<br>and the heavens will be draped in black<br>because of my decree against my people.<br>I have made up my mind and will not change it.”  
**4:29** At the noise of charioteers and archers,<br>the people flee in terror.<br>They hide in the bushes<br>and run for the mountains.<br>All the towns have been abandoned—<br>not a person remains!  
**4:30** What are you doing,<br>you who have been plundered?<br>Why do you dress up in beautiful clothing<br>and put on gold jewelry?<br>Why do you brighten your eyes with mascara?<br>Your primping will do you no good!<br>The allies who were your lovers<br>despise you and seek to kill you.  
**4:31** I hear a cry, like that of a woman in labor,<br>the groans of a woman giving birth to her first child.<br>It is beautiful Jerusalem<sup>①</sup><br>gasping for breath and crying out,<br>“Help! I’m being murdered!”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>the daughter of Zion.</i>  

## 5장

**5:1** “Run up and down every street in Jerusalem,” says the LORD.<br>“Look high and low; search throughout the city!<br>If you can find even one just and honest person,<br>I will not destroy the city.  
**5:2** But even when they are under oath,<br>saying, ‘As surely as the LORD lives,’<br>they are still telling lies!”  
**5:3** LORD, you are searching for honesty.<br>You struck your people,<br>but they paid no attention.<br>You crushed them,<br>but they refused to be corrected.<br>They are determined, with faces set like stone;<br>they have refused to repent.  
**5:4** Then I said, “But what can we expect from the poor?<br>They are ignorant.<br>They don’t know the ways of the LORD.<br>They don’t understand God’s laws.  
**5:5** So I will go and speak to their leaders.<br>Surely they know the ways of the LORD<br>and understand God’s laws.”<br>But the leaders, too, as one man,<br>had thrown off God’s yoke<br>and broken his chains.  
**5:6** So now a lion from the forest will attack them;<br>a wolf from the desert will pounce on them.<br>A leopard will lurk near their towns,<br>tearing apart any who dare to venture out.<br>For their rebellion is great,<br>and their sins are many.  
**5:7** “How can I pardon you?<br>For even your children have turned from me.<br>They have sworn by gods that are not gods at all!<br>I fed my people until they were full.<br>But they thanked me by committing adultery<br>and lining up at the brothels.  
**5:8** They are well-fed, lusty stallions,<br>each neighing for his neighbor’s wife.  
**5:9** Should I not punish them for this?” says the LORD.<br>“Should I not avenge myself against such a nation?  
**5:10** “Go down the rows of the vineyards and destroy the grapevines,<br>leaving a scattered few alive.<br>Strip the branches from the vines,<br>for these people do not belong to the LORD.  
**5:11** The people of Israel and Judah<br>are full of treachery against me,”<br>says the LORD.  
**5:12** “They have lied about the LORD<br>and said, ‘He won’t bother us!<br>No disasters will come upon us.<br>There will be no war or famine.  
**5:13** God’s prophets are all windbags<br>who don’t really speak for him.<br>Let their predictions of disaster fall on themselves!’”  
**5:14** Therefore, this is what the LORD God of Heaven’s Armies says:<br>“Because the people are talking like this,<br>my messages will flame out of your mouth<br>and burn the people like kindling wood.  
**5:15** O Israel, I will bring a distant nation against you,”<br>says the LORD.<br>“It is a mighty nation,<br>an ancient nation,<br>a people whose language you do not know,<br>whose speech you cannot understand.  
**5:16** Their weapons are deadly;<br>their warriors are mighty.  
**5:17** They will devour the food of your harvest;<br>they will devour your sons and daughters.<br>They will devour your flocks and herds;<br>they will devour your grapes and figs.<br>And they will destroy your fortified towns,<br>which you think are so safe.  
**5:18** “Yet even in those days I will not blot you out completely,” says the LORD.  
**5:19** “And when your people ask, ‘Why did the LORD our God do all this to us?’ you must reply, ‘You rejected him and gave yourselves to foreign gods in your own land. Now you will serve foreigners in a land that is not your own.’  
**5:20** “Make this announcement to Israel,<sup>①</sup><br>and say this to Judah:<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>to the house of Jacob.</i> The names “Jacob” and “Israel” are often interchanged throughout the Old Testament, referring sometimes to the individual patriarch and sometimes to the nation.  
**5:21** Listen, you foolish and senseless people,<br>with eyes that do not see<br>and ears that do not hear.  
**5:22** Have you no respect for me?<br>Why don’t you tremble in my presence?<br>I, the LORD, define the ocean’s sandy shoreline<br>as an everlasting boundary that the waters cannot cross.<br>The waves may toss and roar,<br>but they can never pass the boundaries I set.  
**5:23** But my people have stubborn and rebellious hearts.<br>They have turned away and abandoned me.  
**5:24** They do not say from the heart,<br>‘Let us live in awe of the LORD our God,<br>for he gives us rain each spring and fall,<br>assuring us of a harvest when the time is right.’  
**5:25** Your wickedness has deprived you of these wonderful blessings.<br>Your sin has robbed you of all these good things.  
**5:26** “Among my people are wicked men<br>who lie in wait for victims like a hunter hiding in a blind.<br>They continually set traps<br>to catch people.  
**5:27** Like a cage filled with birds,<br>their homes are filled with evil plots.<br>And now they are great and rich.  
**5:28** They are fat and sleek,<br>and there is no limit to their wicked deeds.<br>They refuse to provide justice to orphans<br>and deny the rights of the poor.  
**5:29** Should I not punish them for this?” says the LORD.<br>“Should I not avenge myself against such a nation?  
**5:30** A horrible and shocking thing<br>has happened in this land—  
**5:31** the prophets give false prophecies,<br>and the priests rule with an iron hand.<br>Worse yet, my people like it that way!<br>But what will you do when the end comes?  

## 6장

**6:1** “Run for your lives, you people of Benjamin!<br>Get out of Jerusalem!<br>Sound the alarm in Tekoa!<br>Send up a signal at Beth-hakkerem!<br>A powerful army is coming from the north,<br>coming with disaster and destruction.  
**6:2** O Jerusalem,<sup>①</sup> you are my beautiful and delicate daughter—<br>but I will destroy you!<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>Daughter of Zion.</i>  
**6:3** Enemies will surround you, like shepherds camped around the city.<br>Each chooses a place for his troops to devour.

**52:13** He burned down the Temple of the LORD, the royal palace, and all the houses of Jerusalem. He destroyed all the important buildings<sup>①</sup> in the city.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>destroyed the houses of all the important people.</i>  
**52:14** Then he supervised the entire Babylonian<sup>①</sup> army as they tore down the walls of Jerusalem on every side.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>Chaldean.</i>  
**52:15** Then Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, took as exiles some of the poorest of the people, the rest of the people who remained in the city, the defectors who had declared their allegiance to the king of Babylon, and the rest of the craftsmen.  
**52:16** But Nebuzaradan allowed some of the poorest people to stay behind to care for the vineyards and fields.  
**52:17** The Babylonians broke up the bronze pillars in front of the LORD’s Temple, the bronze water carts, and the great bronze basin called the Sea, and they carried all the bronze away to Babylon.  
**52:18** They also took all the ash buckets, shovels, lamp snuffers, basins, dishes, and all the other bronze articles used for making sacrifices at the Temple.  
**52:19** The captain of the guard also took the small bowls, incense burners, basins, pots, lampstands, ladles, bowls used for liquid offerings, and all the other articles made of pure gold or silver.  
**52:20** The weight of the bronze from the two pillars, the Sea with the twelve bronze oxen beneath it, and the water carts was too great to be measured. These things had been made for the LORD’s Temple in the days of King Solomon.  
**52:21** Each of the pillars was 27 feet tall and 18 feet in circumference.<sup>①</sup> They were hollow, with walls 3 inches thick.<sup>②</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>18 cubits</i> [8.3 meters] <i>tall and 12 cubits</i> [5.5 meters] <i>in circumference.</i> <sup>②</sup>Hebrew <i>4 fingers thick</i> [8 centimeters].  
**52:22** The bronze capital on top of each pillar was 7 1⁄2 feet<sup>①</sup> high and was decorated with a network of bronze pomegranates all the way around.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>5 cubits</i> [2.3 meters].  
**52:23** There were 96 pomegranates on the sides, and a total of 100 pomegranates on the network around the top.  
**52:24** Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, took with him as prisoners Seraiah the high priest, Zephaniah the priest of the second rank, and the three chief gatekeepers.  
**52:25** And from among the people still hiding in the city, he took an officer who had been in charge of the Judean army; seven of the king’s personal advisers; the army commander’s chief secretary, who was in charge of recruitment; and sixty other citizens.  
**52:26** Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, took them all to the king of Babylon at Riblah.  
**52:27** And there at Riblah, in the land of Hamath, the king of Babylon had them all put to death. So the people of Judah were sent into exile from their land.  
**52:28** The number of captives taken to Babylon in the seventh year of Nebuchadnezzar’s reign<sup>①</sup> was 3,023.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>This exile in the seventh year of Nebuchadnezzar’s reign occurred in 597 B.C.  
**52:29** Then in Nebuchadnezzar’s eighteenth year<sup>①</sup> he took 832 more.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>This exile in the eighteenth year of Nebuchadnezzar’s reign occurred in 586 B.C.  
**52:30** In Nebuchadnezzar’s twenty-third year<sup>①</sup> he sent Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, who took 745 more—a total of 4,600 captives in all.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>This exile in the twenty-third year of Nebuchadnezzar’s reign occurred in 581 B.C.  
**52:31** In the thirty-seventh year of the exile of King Jehoiachin of Judah, Evil-merodach ascended to the Babylonian throne. He was kind to<sup>①</sup> Jehoiachin and released him from prison on March 31 of that year.<sup>②</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>He raised the head of.</i> <sup>②</sup>Hebrew <i>on the twenty-fifth day of the twelfth month,</i> of the ancient Hebrew lunar calendar. This day was March 31, 561 B.C.; also see <a name='C:@300 52:4'>note on <a name='B:300 52:4'>52:4a.  
**52:32** He spoke kindly to Jehoiachin and gave him a higher place than all the other exiled kings in Babylon.  
**52:33** He supplied Jehoiachin with new clothes to replace his prison garb and allowed him to dine in the king’s presence for the rest of his life.  
**52:34** So the Babylonian king gave him a regular food allowance as long as he lived. This continued until the day of his death.
