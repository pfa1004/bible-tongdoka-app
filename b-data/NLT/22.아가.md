# 22.아가 (NLT)



## 1장

**1:1** This is Solomon’s song of songs, more wonderful than any other.  
**1:2** <b>Young Woman</b><sup>①</sup><br>Kiss me and kiss me again,<br>for your love is sweeter than wine.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>The headings identifying the speakers are not in the original text, though the Hebrew usually gives clues by means of the gender of the person speaking.  
**1:3** How pleasing is your fragrance;<br>your name is like the spreading fragrance of scented oils.<br>No wonder all the young women love you!  
**1:4** Take me with you; come, let’s run!<br>The king has brought me into his bedroom. <b>Young Women of Jerusalem</b><br>How happy we are for you, O king.<br>We praise your love even more than wine. <b>Young Woman</b><br>How right they are to adore you.  
**1:5** I am dark but beautiful,<br>O women of Jerusalem—<br>dark as the tents of Kedar,<br>dark as the curtains of Solomon’s tents.  
**1:6** Don’t stare at me because I am dark—<br>the sun has darkened my skin.<br>My brothers were angry with me;<br>they forced me to care for their vineyards,<br>so I couldn’t care for myself—my own vineyard.  
**1:7** Tell me, my love, where are you leading your flock today?<br>Where will you rest your sheep at noon?<br>For why should I wander like a prostitute<sup>①</sup><br>among your friends and their flocks?<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>like a veiled woman.</i>  
**1:8** If you don’t know, O most beautiful woman,<br>follow the trail of my flock,<br>and graze your young goats by the shepherds’ tents.  
**1:9** You are as exciting, my darling,<br>as a mare among Pharaoh’s stallions.  
**1:10** How lovely are your cheeks;<br>your earrings set them afire!<br>How lovely is your neck,<br>enhanced by a string of jewels.  
**1:11** We will make for you earrings of gold<br>and beads of silver.  
**1:12** The king is lying on his couch,<br>enchanted by the fragrance of my perfume.  
**1:13** My lover is like a sachet of myrrh<br>lying between my breasts.  
**1:14** He is like a bouquet of sweet henna blossoms<br>from the vineyards of En-gedi.  
**1:15** How beautiful you are, my darling,<br>how beautiful!<br>Your eyes are like doves.  
**1:16** You are so handsome, my love,<br>pleasing beyond words!<br>The soft grass is our bed;  
**1:17** fragrant cedar branches are the beams of our house,<br>and pleasant smelling firs are the rafters.  

## 2장

**2:1** I am the spring crocus blooming on the Sharon Plain,<sup>①</sup><br>the lily of the valley.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Traditionally rendered <i>I am the rose of Sharon.</i> Sharon Plain is a region in the coastal plain of Palestine.  
**2:2** Like a lily among thistles<br>is my darling among young women.  
**2:3** Like the finest apple tree in the orchard<br>is my lover among other young men.<br>I sit in his delightful shade<br>and taste his delicious fruit.  
**2:4** He escorts me to the banquet hall;<br>it’s obvious how much he loves me.  
**2:5** Strengthen me with raisin cakes,<br>refresh me with apples,<br>for I am weak with love.  
**2:6** His left arm is under my head,<br>and his right arm embraces me.  
**2:7** Promise me, O women of Jerusalem,<br>by the gazelles and wild deer,<br>not to awaken love until the time is right.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>not to awaken love until it is ready.</i>  
**2:8** Ah, I hear my lover coming!<br>He is leaping over the mountains,<br>bounding over the hills.  
**2:9** My lover is like a swift gazelle<br>or a young stag.<br>Look, there he is behind the wall,<br>looking through the window,<br>peering into the room.  
**2:10** My lover said to me,<br>“Rise up, my darling!<br>Come away with me, my fair one!  
**2:11** Look, the winter is past,<br>and the rains are over and gone.  
**2:12** The flowers are springing up,<br>the season of singing birds<sup>①</sup> has come,<br>and the cooing of turtledoves fills the air.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>the season of pruning vines.</i>  
**2:13** The fig trees are forming young fruit,<br>and the fragrant grapevines are blossoming.<br>Rise up, my darling!<br>Come away with me, my fair one!”  
**2:14** My dove is hiding behind the rocks,<br>behind an outcrop on the cliff.<br>Let me see your face;<br>let me hear your voice.<br>For your voice is pleasant,<br>and your face is lovely.  
**2:15** Catch all the foxes,<br>those little foxes,<br>before they ruin the vineyard of love,<br>for the grapevines are blossoming!  
**2:16** My lover is mine, and I am his.<br>He browses among the lilies.  
**2:17** Before the dawn breezes blow<br>and the night shadows flee,<br>return to me, my love, like a gazelle<br>or a young stag on the rugged mountains.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>on the hills of Bether.</i>  

## 3장

**3:1** One night as I lay in bed, I yearned for my lover.<br>I yearned for him, but he did not come.  
**3:2** So I said to myself, “I will get up and roam the city,<br>searching in all its streets and squares.<br>I will search for the one I love.”<br>So I searched everywhere but did not find him.  
**3:3** The watchmen stopped me as they made their rounds,<br>and I asked, “Have you seen the one I love?”  
**3:4** Then scarcely had I left them<br>when I found my love!<br>I caught and held him tightly,<br>then I brought him to my mother’s house,<br>into my mother’s bed, where I had been conceived.  
**3:5** Promise me, O women of Jerusalem,<br>by the gazelles and wild deer,<br>not to awaken love until the time is right.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>not to awaken love until it is ready.</i>  
**3:6** Who is this sweeping in from the wilderness<br>like a cloud of smoke?<br>Who is it, fragrant with myrrh and frankincense<br>and every kind of spice?  
**3:7** Look, it is Solomon’s carriage,<br>surrounded by sixty heroic men,<br>the best of Israel’s soldiers.  
**3:8** They are all skilled swordsmen,<br>experienced warriors.<br>Each wears a sword on his thigh,<br>ready to defend the king against an attack in the night.  
**3:9** King Solomon’s carriage is built<br>of wood imported from Lebanon.  
**3:10** Its posts are silver,<br>its canopy gold;<br>its cushions are purple.<br>It was decorated with love<br>by the young women of Jerusalem.  
**3:11** Come out to see King Solomon,<br>young women of Jerusalem.<sup>①</sup><br>He wears the crown his mother gave him on his wedding day,<br>his most joyous day.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>of Zion.</i>  

## 4장

**4:1** You are beautiful, my darling,<br>beautiful beyond words.<br>Your eyes are like doves<br>behind your veil.<br>Your hair falls in waves,<br>like a flock of goats winding down the slopes of Gilead.  
**4:2** Your teeth are as white as sheep,<br>recently shorn and freshly washed.<br>Your smile is flawless,<br>each tooth matched with its twin.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>Not one is missing; each has a twin.</i>  
**4:3** Your lips are like scarlet ribbon;<br>your mouth is inviting.<br>Your cheeks are like rosy pomegranates<br>behind your veil.  
**4:4** Your neck is as beautiful as the tower of David,<br>jeweled with the shields of a thousand heroes.  
**4:5** Your breasts are like two fawns,<br>twin fawns of a gazelle grazing among the lilies.  
**4:6** Before the dawn breezes blow<br>and the night shadows flee,<br>I will hurry to the mountain of myrrh<br>and to the hill of frankincense.  
**4:7** You are altogether beautiful, my darling,<br>beautiful in every way.  
**4:8** Come with me from Lebanon, my bride,<br>come with me from Lebanon.<br>Come down<sup>①</sup> from Mount Amana,<br>from the peaks of Senir and Hermon,<br>where the lions have their dens<br>and leopards live among the hills.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>Look down.</i>  
**4:9** You have captured my heart,<br>my treasure,<sup>①</sup> my bride.<br>You hold it hostage with one glance of your eyes,<br>with a single jewel of your necklace.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>my sister;</i> also in <a name='B:260 4:10'>4:10, <a name='B:260 4:12'>12.  
**4:10** Your love delights me,<br>my treasure, my bride.<br>Your love is better than wine,<br>your perfume more fragrant than spices.  
**4:11** Your lips are as sweet as nectar, my bride.<br>Honey and milk are under your tongue.<br>Your clothes are scented<br>like the cedars of Lebanon.  
**4:12** You are my private garden, my treasure, my bride,<br>a secluded spring, a hidden fountain.  
**4:13** Your thighs shelter a paradise of pomegranates<br>with rare spices—<br>henna with nard,  
**4:14** nard and saffron,<br>fragrant calamus and cinnamon,<br>with all the trees of frankincense, myrrh, and aloes,<br>and every other lovely spice.  
**4:15** You are a garden fountain,<br>a well of fresh water<br>streaming down from Lebanon’s mountains.  
**4:16** Awake, north wind!<br>Rise up, south wind!<br>Blow on my garden<br>and spread its fragrance all around.<br>Come into your garden, my love;<br>taste its finest fruits.  

## 5장

**5:1** I have entered my garden, my treasure,<sup>①</sup> my bride!<br>I gather myrrh with my spices<br>and eat honeycomb with my honey.<br>I drink wine with my milk. <b>Young Women of Jerusalem</b><br>Oh, lover and beloved, eat and drink!<br>Yes, drink deeply of your love!<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>my sister;</i> also in <a name='B:260 5:2'>5:2.  
**5:2** I slept, but my heart was awake,<br>when I heard my lover knocking and calling:<br>“Open to me, my treasure, my darling,<br>my dove, my perfect one.<br>My head is drenched with dew,<br>my hair with the dampness of the night.”  
**5:3** But I responded,<br>“I have taken off my robe.<br>Should I get dressed again?<br>I have washed my feet.<br>Should I get them soiled?”  
**5:4** My lover tried to unlatch the door,<br>and my heart thrilled within me.  
**5:5** I jumped up to open the door for my love,<br>and my hands dripped with perfume.<br>My fingers dripped with lovely myrrh<br>as I pulled back the bolt.  
**5:6** I opened to my lover,<br>but he was gone!<br>My heart sank.<br>I searched for him<br>but could not find him anywhere.<br>I called to him,<br>but there was no reply.  
**5:7** The night watchmen found me<br>as they made their rounds.<br>They beat and bruised me<br>and stripped off my veil,<br>those watchmen on the walls.  
**5:8** Make this promise, O women of Jerusalem—<br>If you find my lover,<br>tell him I am weak with love.  
**5:9** Why is your lover better than all others,<br>O woman of rare beauty?<br>What makes your lover so special<br>that we must promise this?  
**5:10** My lover is dark and dazzling,<br>better than ten thousand others!  
**5:11** His head is finest gold,<br>his wavy hair is black as a raven.  
**5:12** His eyes sparkle like doves<br>beside springs of water;<br>they are set like jewels<br>washed in milk.  
**5:13** His cheeks are like gardens of spices<br>giving off fragrance.<br>His lips are like lilies,<br>perfumed with myrrh.  
**5:14** His arms are like rounded bars of gold,<br>set with beryl.<br>His body is like bright ivory,<br>glowing with lapis lazuli.  
**5:15** His legs are like marble pillars<br>set in sockets of finest gold.<br>His posture is stately,<br>like the noble cedars of Lebanon.  
**5:16** His mouth is sweetness itself;<br>he is desirable in every way.<br>Such, O women of Jerusalem,<br>is my lover, my friend.  

## 6장

**6:1** Where has your lover gone,<br>O woman of rare beauty?<br>Which way did he turn<br>so we can help you find him?  
**6:2** My lover has gone down to his garden,<br>to his spice beds,<br>to browse in the gardens<br>and gather the lilies.  
**6:3** I am my lover’s, and my lover is mine.<br>He browses among the lilies.  
**6:4** You are beautiful, my darling,<br>like the lovely city of Tirzah.<br>Yes, as beautiful as Jerusalem,<br>as majestic as an army with billowing banners.  
**6:5** Turn your eyes away,<br>for they overpower me.<br>Your hair falls in waves,<br>like a flock of goats winding down the slopes of Gilead.  
**6:6** Your teeth are as white as sheep<br>that are freshly washed.<br>Your smile is flawless,<br>each tooth matched with its twin.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hebrew <i>Not one is missing; each has a twin.</i>  
**6:7** Your cheeks are like rosy pomegranates<br>behind your veil.  
**6:8** Even among sixty queens<br>and eighty concubines<br>and countless young women,  
**6:9** I would still choose my dove, my perfect one—<br>the favorite of her mother,<br>dearly loved by the one who bore her.<br>The young women see her and praise her;<br>even queens and royal concubines sing her praises:  
**6:10** “Who is this, arising like the dawn,<br>as fair as the moon,<br>as bright as the sun,<br>as majestic as an army with billowing banners?”  
**6:11** I went down to the grove of walnut trees<br>and out to the valley to see the new spring growth,<br>to see whether the grapevines had budded<br>or the pomegranates were in bloom.  
**6:12** Before I realized it,<br>my strong desires had taken me to the chariot of a noble man.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>to the royal chariots of my people,</i> or <i>to the chariots of Amminadab.</i> The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain.  
**6:13** <sup>①</sup>Return, return to us, O maid of Shulam.<br>Come back, come back, that we may see you again. <b>Young Man</b><br>Why do you stare at this young woman of Shulam,<br>as she moves so gracefully between two lines of dancers?<sup>②</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Verse <a name='B:260 6:13'>6:13 is numbered 7:1 in Hebrew text. <sup>②</sup>Or <i>as you would at the movements of two armies?</i> or <i>as you would at the dance of Mahanaim?</i> The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain.  

## 7장

**7:1** <sup>①</sup>How beautiful are your sandaled feet,<br>O queenly maiden.<br>Your rounded thighs are like jewels,<br>the work of a skilled craftsman.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Verses <a name='B:260 7:1-13'>7:1-13 are numbered 7:2-14 in Hebrew text.  
**7:2** Your navel is perfectly formed<br>like a goblet filled with mixed wine.<br>Between your thighs lies a mound of wheat<br>bordered with lilies.  
**7:3** Your breasts are like two fawns,<br>twin fawns of a gazelle.  
**7:4** Your neck is as beautiful as an ivory tower.<br>Your eyes are like the sparkling pools in Heshbon<br>by the gate of Bath-rabbim.<br>Your nose is as fine as the tower of Lebanon<br>overlooking Damascus.  
**7:5** Your head is as majestic as Mount Carmel,<br>and the sheen of your hair radiates royalty.<br>The king is held captive by its tresses.  
**7:6** Oh, how beautiful you are!<br>How pleasing, my love, how full of delights!  
**7:7** You are slender like a palm tree,<br>and your breasts are like its clusters of fruit.  
**7:8** I said, “I will climb the palm tree<br>and take hold of its fruit.”<br>May your breasts be like grape clusters,<br>and the fragrance of your breath like apples.  
**7:9** May your kisses be as exciting as the best wine—<b>Young Woman</b><br>Yes, wine that goes down smoothly for my lover,<br>flowing gently over lips and teeth.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>As in Greek and Syriac versions and Latin Vulgate; Hebrew reads <i>over lips of sleepers.</i>  
**7:10** I am my lover’s,<br>and he claims me as his own.  
**7:11** Come, my love, let us go out to the fields<br>and spend the night among the wildflowers.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>in the villages.</i>  
**7:12** Let us get up early and go to the vineyards<br>to see if the grapevines have budded,<br>if the blossoms have opened,<br>and if the pomegranates have bloomed.<br>There I will give you my love.  
**7:13** There the mandrakes give off their fragrance,<br>and the finest fruits are at our door,<br>new delights as well as old,<br>which I have saved for you, my lover.  

## 8장

**8:1** Oh, I wish you were my brother,<br>who nursed at my mother’s breasts.<br>Then I could kiss you no matter who was watching,<br>and no one would criticize me.  
**8:2** I would bring you to my childhood home,<br>and there you would teach me.<sup>①</sup><br>I would give you spiced wine to drink,<br>my sweet pomegranate wine.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>there she will teach me.</i>  
**8:3** Your left arm would be under my head,<br>and your right arm would embrace me.  
**8:4** Promise me, O women of Jerusalem,<br>not to awaken love until the time is right.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>not to awaken love until it is ready.</i>  
**8:5** Who is this sweeping in from the desert,<br>leaning on her lover? <b>Young Woman</b><br>I aroused you under the apple tree,<br>where your mother gave you birth,<br>where in great pain she delivered you.  
**8:6** Place me like a seal over your heart,<br>like a seal on your arm.<br>For love is as strong as death,<br>its jealousy<sup>①</sup> as enduring as the grave.<sup>②</sup><br>Love flashes like fire,<br>the brightest kind of flame.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>its passion.</i> <sup>②</sup>Hebrew <i>as Sheol.</i>  
**8:7** Many waters cannot quench love,<br>nor can rivers drown it.<br>If a man tried to buy love<br>with all his wealth,<br>his offer would be utterly scorned.  
**8:8** We have a little sister<br>too young to have breasts.<br>What will we do for our sister<br>if someone asks to marry her?  
**8:9** If she is a virgin, like a wall,<br>we will protect her with a silver tower.<br>But if she is promiscuous, like a swinging door,<br>we will block her door with a cedar bar.  
**8:10** I was a virgin, like a wall;<br>now my breasts are like towers.<br>When my lover looks at me,<br>he is delighted with what he sees.  
**8:11** Solomon has a vineyard at Baal-hamon,<br>which he leases out to tenant farmers.<br>Each of them pays a thousand pieces of silver<br>for harvesting its fruit.  
**8:12** But my vineyard is mine to give,<br>and Solomon need not pay a thousand pieces of silver.<br>But I will give two hundred pieces<br>to those who care for its vines.  
**8:13** O my darling, lingering in the gardens,<br>your companions are fortunate to hear your voice.<br>Let me hear it, too!  
**8:14** Come away, my love! Be like a gazelle<br>or a young stag on the mountains of spices.
