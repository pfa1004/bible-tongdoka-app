# 30.아모스 (CEV)



## 1장

**1:1**  I am Amos. And I raised sheep near the town of Tekoa<sup>①</sup> when Uzziah was king of Judah and Jeroboam<sup>②</sup> son of Jehoash<sup>③</sup> was king of Israel. Two years before the earthquake,<sup>④</sup> the LORD gave me several messages<sup>⑤</sup> about Israel,<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Tekoa: </i>In the hill country of Judah about eight kilometers south of Bethlehem. <sup>②</sup><i>Uzziah … Jeroboam: </i>Uzziah was king of Judah 781–740 B.C., and Jeroboam II was king of Israel 783–743 B.C. <sup>③</sup><i>Jehoash: </i>The Hebrew text has “Joash,” another spelling of the name. <sup>④</sup><i>Two years … earthquake: </i>Possibly the earthquake of 760 B.C., which seems to have been especially violent. <sup>⑤</sup><i>messages: </i>Or “visions.”  
**1:2**  and I said:<br>When the LORD roars<br>from Jerusalem,<br>pasturelands and Mount Carmel<br>dry up and turn brown.  
**1:3**  The LORD said:<br>I will punish Syria<sup>①</sup><br>for countless crimes,<br>and I won't change my mind.<br>They dragged logs with spikes<sup>②</sup><br>over the people of Gilead.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Syria: </i>The Hebrew text has “Damascus,” the leading city of Syria. <sup>②</sup><i>logs with spikes: </i>These were dragged over grain to thresh it.  
**1:4** Now I will burn down the palaces<br>and fortresses of King Hazael<br>and of King Benhadad.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Hazael … Benhadad: </i>Two Syrian kings.  
**1:5** I will break through<br>the gates of Damascus.<br>I will destroy the people<sup>①</sup><br>of Wicked Valley<sup>②</sup><br>and the ruler of Beth-Eden.<sup>③</sup><br>Then the Syrians will be dragged<br>as prisoners to Kir.<sup>④</sup><br>I, the LORD, have spoken!<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Wicked Valley: </i>The Hebrew text has “Aven Valley,” probably the fertile valley between the Lebanon and the Anti-Lebanon mountains. <sup>②</sup><i>I will … Beth-Eden: </i>Or “I will destroy the people of Wicked Valley and the king who rules from Beth-Eden.” Beth-Eden was a city-state on the banks of the Euphrates River. <sup>③</sup><i>Kir: </i>The exact location of this country is not known; in <a name='B:370 9:7'>9.7 Amos refers to Kir as the original home of the Syrians, and so the verse probably means that the Syrians will lose everything they have gained as a people. <sup>④</sup><i>people: </i>Or “king.”  
**1:6**  The LORD said:<br>I will punish Philistia<sup>①</sup><br>for countless crimes,<br>and I won't change my mind.<br>They dragged off my people<sup>②</sup><br>from town after town<br>to sell them as slaves<br>to the Edomites.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Philistia: </i>The Hebrew text has “Gaza,” one of the main Philistine cities. <sup>②</sup><i>my people: </i>The people of Israel.  
**1:7** That's why I will burn down<br>the walls and fortresses<br>of the city of Gaza.  
**1:8** I will destroy the king<sup>①</sup> of Ashdod<br>and the ruler of Ashkelon.<br>I will strike down Ekron,<sup>②</sup><br>and that will be the end<br>of the Philistines.<br>I, the LORD, have spoken!<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>king: </i>Or “people.” <sup>②</sup><i>Ashdod … Ashkelon … Ekron: </i>Philistine cities.  
**1:9**  The LORD said:<br>I will punish Phoenicia<sup>①</sup><br>for countless crimes,<br>and I won't change my mind.<br>They broke their treaty<br>and dragged off my people<sup>②</sup><br>from town after town<br>to sell them as slaves<br>to the Edomites.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Phoenicia: </i>The Hebrew text has “Tyre,” which was one of the two Phoenician cities; the other was Sidon, which is not mentioned by Amos. <sup>②</sup><i>my people: </i>See the <a name='C370 1:6'>note at <a name='B:370 1:6'>1.6.  
**1:10** That's why I will send flames<br>to burn down the city of Tyre<br>along with its fortresses.  
**1:11**  The LORD said:<br>I will punish Edom<br>for countless crimes,<br>and I won't change my mind.<br>They killed their own relatives<sup>①</sup><br>and were so terribly furious<br>that they showed no mercy.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>their own relatives: </i>The Edomites were descendants of Esau, the brother of Jacob, the ancestor of the Israelites.  
**1:12** Now I will send fire to wipe out<br>the fortresses of Teman<br>and Bozrah.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Teman and Bozrah: </i>These stand for all of Edom; Teman may have been a city or a district. Bozrah, the chief city of northern Edom, was 48 kilometers southeast of the Dead Sea.  
**1:13**  The LORD said:<br>I will punish Ammon<br>for countless crimes,<br>and I won't change my mind.<br>In Gilead they ripped open<br>pregnant women,<br>just to take the land.   
**1:14** Now I will send fire to destroy<br>the walls and fortresses<br>of Rabbah.<sup>①</sup><br>Enemies will shout and attack<br>like a whirlwind.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Rabbah: </i>The capital city of Ammon.  
**1:15** Ammon's king and leaders<br>will be dragged away.<br>I, the LORD, have spoken!  

## 2장

**2:1**  The LORD said:<br>I will punish Moab<br>for countless crimes,<br>and I won't change my mind.<br>They made lime from the bones<sup>①</sup><br>of the king of Edom.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>They … bones: </i>They dug up the bodies of kings and made lime out of them to use as whitewash on their houses and walls.  
**2:2** Now I will send fire to destroy<br>the fortresses of Kerioth.<sup>①</sup><br>Battle shouts and trumpet blasts<br>will be heard as I destroy Moab<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Kerioth: </i>A leading city of Moab and a center for the worship of Chemosh, the chief god of Moab.  
**2:3** with its king and leaders.<br>I, the LORD, have spoken!  
**2:4** The LORD said:<br>I will punish Judah<br>for countless crimes,<br>and I won't change my mind.<br>They have rejected my teachings<br>and refused to obey me.<br>They were led astray<br>by the same false gods<br>their ancestors worshiped.  
**2:5** Now I will send fire on Judah<br>and destroy the fortresses<br>of Jerusalem.  
**2:6** The LORD said:<br>I will punish Israel<br>for countless crimes,<br>and I won't change my mind.<br>They sell honest people for money,<br>and the needy are sold<br>for the price of sandals.  
**2:7** They smear the poor in the dirt<br>and push aside<br>those who are helpless. My holy name is dishonored,<br>because fathers and sons sleep<br>with the same young women.  
**2:8** They lie down beside altars<br>on clothes taken<br>as security for loans.<br>And they drink wine in my temple,<br>wine bought with the money<br>they received from fines.   
**2:9**  Israel, the Amorites<sup>①</sup> were there<br>when you entered Canaan.<br>They were tall as cedars<br>and strong as oaks.<br>But I wiped them out—<br>I destroyed their branches<br>and their roots.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Amorites: </i>This word is used for all the people who lived in Canaan at the time Israel took over the land.  
**2:10** I had rescued you from Egypt,<br>and for forty years I had led you<br>through the desert.<br>Then I gave you the land<br>of the Amorites.   
**2:11**  I chose some of you<br>to be prophets<br>and others to be Nazirites.<sup>①</sup><br>People of Israel,<br>you know this is true.<br>I, the LORD, have spoken!<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Nazirites: </i>People who promised the LORD that they would never drink wine or cut their hair or come in contact with a dead body.  
**2:12** But you commanded the prophets<br>not to speak their message,<br>and you pressured the Nazirites<br>into drinking wine.   
**2:13** And so I will crush you,<br>just as a wagon full of grain<br>crushes the ground.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>ground: </i>One possible meaning for the difficult Hebrew text of verse <a name='B:370 2:13'>13.  
**2:14** No matter how fast you run,<br>you won't escape.<br>No matter how strong you are,<br>you will lose your strength<br>and your life.  
**2:15** Even if you are an expert<br>with a bow and arrow,<br>you will retreat.<br>And you won't get away alive,<br>not even if you run fast<br>or ride a horse.  
**2:16** You may be brave and strong,<br>but you will run away,<br>stripped naked.<br>I, the LORD, have spoken!  

## 3장

**3:1** People of Israel,<br>I rescued you from Egypt.<br>Now listen to my judgment<br>against you.  
**3:2** Of all nations on earth,<br>you are the only one<br>I have chosen.<br>That's why I will punish you<br>because of your sins.  
**3:3** Can two people walk together<br>without agreeing to meet?  
**3:4** Does a lion roar in the forest<br>unless it has caught<br>a victim?<br>Does it growl in its den<br>unless it is eating?  
**3:5** How can anyone catch a bird<br>without using a net?<br>Does a trap spring shut<br>unless something is caught?   
**3:6** Isn't the whole city frightened<br>when the trumpet<br>signals an attack?<br>Isn't the LORD the one who brings<br>disaster on a city?  
**3:7** Whatever the LORD God<br>plans to do,<br>he tells his servants,<br>the prophets.  
**3:8** Everyone is terrified<br>when a lion roars—<br>and ordinary people<br>become prophets<br>when the LORD God speaks.  
**3:9** Here is a message<br>for the leaders<br>of Philistia<sup>①</sup> and Egypt—<br>tell everyone to come together<br>on the hills of Samaria.<br>Let them see the injustice<br>and the lawlessness<br>in that city.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Philistia: </i>The Hebrew text has “Ashdod,” one of the leading cities of Philistia.  
**3:10** The LORD has said<br>that they don't even know how<br>to do right.<br>They have become rich<br>from violence and robbery.  
**3:11** And so the LORD God has sworn<br>that they will be surrounded.<br>Enemies will break through<br>their defenses<br>and steal their treasures.   
**3:12** The LORD has promised<br>that only a few from Samaria<br>will escape with their lives<br>and with some broken pieces<br>of their beds and couches.<sup>①</sup><br>It will be like when a shepherd<br>rescues two leg bones<br>and part of a sheep's ear<br>from the jaws of a lion.<sup>②</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>some … couches: </i>One possible meaning for the difficult Hebrew text. <sup>②</sup><i>lion: </i>When a wild animal attacked and killed a sheep, the shepherd had to rescue part of the sheep and take it to the owner as proof that it had been killed by an animal. Otherwise, the shepherd had to pay the owner the cost of the sheep.  
**3:13** The LORD God All-Powerful<br>told me to speak this message<br>against Jacob's descendants:  
**3:14**  When I, the LORD, punish Israel<br>for their sins,<br>I will destroy the altars<br>at Bethel.<br>Even the corners of the altar<sup>①</sup><br>will be left in the dirt.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>altar: </i>Altars were places of worship but also places of protection. People whose lives were in danger could grab hold of the corners of an altar, and no one was allowed to kill them.  
**3:15** I will tear down winter homes<br>and summer homes.<br>Houses decorated with ivory<br>and all other mansions<br>will be gone forever.<br>I, the LORD, have spoken!  

## 4장

**4:1** You women of Samaria<br>are fat cows!<sup>①</sup><br>You mistreat and abuse<br>the poor and needy,<br>then you say to your husbands,<br>“Bring us more drinks!”<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>fat cows: </i>The Hebrew text has “cows of Bashan,” a fertile plain famous for its rich pastures and well-fed cattle.  
**4:2** I, the LORD God, have sworn<br>by my own name<br>that your time is coming.<br>Not one of you will be left—<br>you will be taken away<br>by sharp hooks.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>taken … hooks: </i>One possible meaning for the difficult Hebrew text.  
**4:3** You will be dragged through holes<br>in your city walls,<br>and you will be thrown<br>toward Harmon.<sup>①</sup><br>I, the LORD, have spoken!<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Harmon: </i>Hebrew; some manuscripts of one ancient translation “Mount Hermon,” a mountain in the north of Palestine, on the way to Assyria.  
**4:4** Come to Bethel and Gilgal.<sup>①</sup><br>Sin all you want!<br>Offer sacrifices the next morning<br>and bring a tenth of your crops<br>on the third day.<sup>②</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Bethel and Gilgal: </i>These were two of the most important centers of worship in northern Israel. Amos mentions these together again in <a name='B:370 5:5'>5.5. <sup>②</sup><i>Offer … day: </i>Or “Offer sacrifices each morning and bring a tenth of your crops every three days.” In verses 4,5 God is condemning the people for meaningless acts of worship.  
**4:5** Bring offerings to show me<br>how thankful you are.<br>Gladly bring more offerings<br>than I have demanded.<br>You really love to do this.<br>I, the LORD God, have spoken!  
**4:6**  I, the LORD, took away the food<br>from every town and village,<br>but still you rejected me.  
**4:7** Three months before harvest,<br>I kept back the rain.<br>Sometimes I would let it fall<br>on one town or field<br>but not on another,<br>and pastures dried up.  
**4:8** People from two or three towns<br>would go to a town<br>that still had water,<br>but it wasn't enough.<br>Even then you rejected me.<br>I, the LORD, have spoken!   
**4:9** I dried up your grain fields;<br>your gardens and vineyards<br>turned brown.<br>Locusts<sup>①</sup> ate your fig trees<br>and olive orchards,<br>but even then you rejected me.<br>I, the LORD, have spoken!<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Locusts: </i>A type of grasshopper that comes in swarms and causes great damage to plant life.  
**4:10** I did terrible things to you,<br>just as I did to Egypt—<br>I killed your young men in war;<br>I let your horses be stolen,<br>and I made your camp stink<br>with dead bodies.<br>Even then you rejected me.<br>I, the LORD, have spoken!   
**4:11**  I destroyed many of you,<br>just as I did the cities<br>of Sodom and Gomorrah.<br>You were a burning stick<br>I rescued from the fire.<br>But even then you rejected me.<br>I, the LORD, have spoken!   
**4:12** Now, Israel, I myself<br>will deal with you.<br>Get ready to face your God!   
**4:13** I created the mountains<br>and the wind.<br>I let humans know<br>what I am thinking.<sup>①</sup><br>I bring darkness at dawn<br>and step over hills.<br>I am the LORD God All-Powerful!<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>I let … thinking: </i>Or “No one's secret thoughts are hidden from me.”  

## 5장

**5:1** Listen, nation of Israel,<br>to my mournful message:  
**5:2** You, dearest Israel, have fallen,<br>never to rise again—<br>you lie deserted in your own land,<br>with no one to help you up.   
**5:3** The LORD God has warned,<br>“From every ten soldiers<br>only one will be left;<br>from a thousand troops,<br>only a hundred will survive.”   
**5:4** The LORD keeps saying,<br>“Israel, turn back to me<br>and you will live!  
**5:5** Don't go to Gilgal or Bethel<br>or even to Beersheba.<sup>①</sup><br>Gilgal will be dragged away,<br>and Bethel will end up<br>as nothing.”<sup>②</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Gilgal … Bethel … Beersheba: </i>These were ancient places of worship, but the LORD had warned his people to stay away from them. <sup>②</sup><i>Gilgal … nothing: </i>In Hebrew “Gilgal” and “dragged away” sound something alike. Bethel (meaning “house of God”) is sometimes called “house of nothing” or “house of sin” by the prophets (see <a name='B:350 4:15'>Hosea 4.15; <a name='B:350 5:8'>5.8; <a name='B:350 10:5-8'>10.5-8).  
**5:6** Turn back to the LORD,<br>you descendants of Joseph,<sup>①</sup><br>and you will live.<br>If you don't, the LORD<br>will attack like fire.<br>Bethel will burn to the ground,<br>and no one can save it.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>descendants of Joseph: </i>Another name for the people of the northern kingdom of Israel.  
**5:7** You people are doomed!<br>You twist the truth<br>and trample on justice.   
**5:8**  But the LORD created the stars<br>and put them in place.<sup>①</sup><br>He turns darkness to dawn<br>and daylight to darkness;<br>he scoops up the ocean<br>and empties it on the earth.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>the stars … place: </i>The Hebrew text mentions two groups of stars, Pleiades and Orion. Since the LORD is the Creator of the stars, he controls the seasons that are signaled by the different positions of the stars. Moreover, the stars are created objects and should not be worshiped.  
**5:9** God destroys mighty soldiers<br>and strong fortresses.   
**5:10** You people hate judges<br>and honest witnesses;  
**5:11** you abuse the poor and demand<br>heavy taxes from them.<br>You have built expensive homes,<br>but you won't enjoy them;<br>you have planted vineyards,<br>but you will get no wine.  
**5:12** I am the LORD, and I know<br>your terrible sins.<br>You cheat honest people<br>and take bribes;<br>you rob the poor of justice.  
**5:13** Times are so evil<br>that anyone with good sense<br>will keep quiet.   
**5:14** If you really want to live,<br>you must stop doing wrong<br>and start doing right.<br>I, the LORD God All-Powerful,<br>will then be on your side,<br>just as you claim I am.  
**5:15** Choose good instead of evil!<br>See that justice is done.<br>Maybe I, the LORD All-Powerful,<br>will be kind to what's left<br>of your people.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>your people: </i>Hebrew “Joseph's descendants” (see the <a name='C370 5:6'>note at <a name='B:370 5:6'>5.6).  
**5:16** This is what the LORD has sworn:<br>Noisy crying will be heard<br>in every town and street.<br>Even farmers will be told<br>to mourn for the dead,<br>together with those<br>who are paid to mourn.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>paid to mourn: </i>In ancient times some people were paid to mourn and make loud cries at funerals.  
**5:17** Your vineyards will be filled<br>with crying and weeping,<sup>①</sup><br>because I will punish you.<br>I, the LORD, have spoken!<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Your vineyards … weeping: </i>Instead of happy celebrations that were often held in vineyards after the harvest.  
**5:18** You look forward to the day<br>when the LORD comes to judge.<br>But you are in for trouble!<br>It won't be a time of sunshine;<br>all will be darkness.  
**5:19** You will run from a lion,<br>only to meet a bear.<br>You will escape to your house,<br>rest your hand on the wall,<br>and be bitten by a snake.  
**5:20** The day when the LORD judges<br>will be dark, very dark,<br>without a ray of light.  
**5:21**  I, the LORD, hate and despise<br>your religious celebrations<br>and your times of worship.  
**5:22** I won't accept your offerings<br>or animal sacrifices—<br>not even your very best.  
**5:23** No more of your noisy songs!<br>I won't listen<br>when you play your harps.  
**5:24** But let justice and fairness<br>flow like a river<br>that never runs dry.   
**5:25**  Israel, for forty years<br>you wandered in the desert,<br>without bringing offerings<br>or sacrifices to me.  
**5:26** Now you will have to carry<br>the two idols you made—<br>Sakkuth, the one you call king,<br>and Kaiwan, the one you built<br>in the shape of a star.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>star: </i>One possible meaning for the difficult Hebrew text of verse <a name='B:370 5:26'>26.  
**5:27** I will force you to march<br>as captives beyond Damascus.<br>I, the LORD God All-Powerful,<br>have spoken!<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>I, the LORD … spoken: </i>Israel did not offer sacrifices and gifts to the LORD during the time they wandered through the desert. But now they have made idols to carry during their ceremonies. So the LORD warns that he will make them “march” away as captives beyond Damascus, where Israel had extended its borders by victories in war (see <a name='B:120 14:28'>2 Kings 14.28).  

## 6장

**6:1** Do you rulers in Jerusalem<br>and in the city of Samaria<br>feel safe and at ease?<br>Everyone bows down to you,<br>and you think you are better<br>than any other nation.<br>But you are in for trouble!  
**6:2** Look what happened<br>to the cities of Calneh,<br>powerful Hamath,<br>and Gath<sup>①</sup> in Philistia.<br>Are you greater than any<br>of those kingdoms?<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Calneh … Hamath … Gath: </i>City-states captured by the Assyrians: Calneh in 738 B.C., Hamath in 720, and Gath in 711.  
**6:3** You are cruel, and you forget<br>the coming day of judgment.   
**6:4** You rich people lounge around<br>on beds with ivory posts,<br>while dining on the meat<br>of your lambs and calves.  
**6:5** You sing foolish songs<br>to the music of harps,<br>and you make up new tunes,<br>just as David used to do.  
**6:6** You drink all the wine you want<br>and wear expensive perfume,<br>but you don't care about<br>the ruin of your nation.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>your nation: </i>Hebrew “Joseph's descendants” (see the <a name='C370 5:6'>note at <a name='B:370 5:6'>5.6).  
**6:7** So you will be the first<br>to be dragged off as captives;<br>your good times will end.   
**6:8** The LORD God All-Powerful<br>has sworn by his own name:<br>“You descendants of Jacob<br>make me angry by your pride,<br>and I hate your fortresses.<br>And so I will surrender your city<br>and possessions<br>to your enemies.”   
**6:9** If only ten of you survive<br>by hiding in a house<br>you will still die.  
**6:10** As you carry out a corpse<br>to prepare it for burial,<sup>①</sup><br>your relative in the house<br>will ask, “Are there others?”<br>You will answer, “No!”<br>Then your relative will reply,<br>“Be quiet! Don't dare mention<br>the name of the LORD.”<sup>②</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>prepare … burial: </i>Or “burn it” or “burn incense for it.” <sup>②</sup><i>the name of the LORD: </i>Two relatives seem to be carrying out corpses for burial. One of them warns the other to be careful not even to say “Thank the LORD!” for fear that the mention of his name may cause something worse to happen.  
**6:11** At the LORD's command,<br>houses great and small<br>will be smashed to pieces.   
**6:12** Horses can't gallop on rocks;<br>oceans<sup>①</sup> can't be plowed.<br>But you have turned justice<br>and fairness<br>into bitter poison.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>oceans: </i>Or “rocky fields.”  
**6:13** You celebrate the defeat<br>of Lo-Debar and Karnaim,<sup>①</sup><br>and you boast by saying,<br>“We did it on our own.”<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Lo-Debar and Karnaim: </i>Two cities east of the Jordan River that were captured by Jeroboam II (see <a name='B:120 14:25'>2 Kings 14.25). In Hebrew “Lo-Debar” can mean “nothing,” and “Karnaim” means “two horns (of a bull).” Horns were symbols of strength, and so the people are bragging about their military power (defeat of “two horns”), which Amos says is “nothing” (Lo-Debar).  
**6:14** But the LORD God All-Powerful<br>will send a nation to attack<br>you people of Israel.<br>They will capture Lebo-Hamath<br>in the north,<br>Arabah Creek<sup>①</sup> in the south,<br>and everything in between.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Lebo-Hamath … Arabah Creek: </i>The northern and southern boundaries of the northern kingdom.  

## 7장

**7:1** The LORD God showed me that he is going to send locusts<sup>①</sup> to attack your crops. It will happen after the king has already been given his share of the grain and before the rest of the grain has been harvested.<sup>②</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>locusts: </i>See the <a name='C370 4:9'>note at <a name='B:370 4:9'>4.9. <sup>②</sup><i>harvested: </i>This would have been an especially bad time for a locust attack. The non-grain crops such as vegetables and onions were just beginning to sprout, and the grain crops were almost ready to be harvested.  
**7:2** In my vision the locusts ate every crop in the land, and I said to the LORD, “Please forgive your nation. It's so weak. How can it survive?”  
**7:3** Then the LORD felt sorry and answered, “I won't let it be destroyed.”
