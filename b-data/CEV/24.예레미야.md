# 24.예레미야 (CEV)



## 1장

**1:1** My name is Jeremiah. I am a priest, and my father Hilkiah and everyone else in my family are from Anathoth in the territory of the Benjamin tribe. This book contains the things that the LORD told me to say.  
**1:2**  The LORD first spoke to me in the thirteenth year that Josiah<sup>①</sup> was king of Judah,<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Josiah: </i>Ruled 640–609 B.C.  
**1:3**  and he continued to speak to me during the rule of Josiah's son Jehoiakim.<sup>①</sup> The last time the LORD spoke to me was in the fifth month<sup>②</sup> of the eleventh year that Josiah's son Zedekiah<sup>③</sup> was king. That was also when the people of Jerusalem were taken away as prisoners.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Jehoiakim: </i>Ruled 609–598 B.C. <sup>②</sup><i>fifth month: </i>Ab, the fifth month of the Hebrew calendar, from about mid-July to mid-August. <sup>③</sup><i>Zedekiah: </i>Ruled 598–586 B.C.  
**1:4** The LORD said:  
**1:5** “Jeremiah, I am your Creator,<br>and before you were born,<br>I chose you to speak for me<br>to the nations.”  
**1:6** I replied, “I'm not a good speaker, LORD, and I'm too young.”  
**1:7** “Don't say you're too young,” the LORD answered. “If I tell you to go and speak to someone, then go! And when I tell you what to say, don't leave out a word!  
**1:8** I promise to be with you and keep you safe, so don't be afraid.”  
**1:9** The LORD reached out his hand, then he touched my mouth and said, “I am giving you the words to say,  
**1:10** and I am sending you with authority to speak to the nations for me. You will tell them of doom and destruction, and of rising and rebuilding again.”  
**1:11** The LORD showed me something in a vision. Then he asked, “What do you see, Jeremiah?” I answered, “A branch of almonds that ripen early.”<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>almonds … rise early: </i>In Hebrew “almonds that ripen early” sounds like “always rise early.”  
**1:12** “That's right,” the LORD replied, “and I always rise early<sup>①</sup> to keep a promise.”  
**1:13** Then the LORD showed me something else and asked, “What do you see now?” I answered, “I see a pot of boiling water in the north, and it's about to spill out toward us.”  
**1:14** The LORD said:<br>I will pour out destruction<br>all over the land.  
**1:15** Just watch while I send<br>for the kings of the north.<br>They will attack and capture<br>Jerusalem and other towns,<br>then set up their thrones<br>at the gates of Jerusalem.   
**1:16** I will punish my people,<br>because they are guilty<br>of turning from me<br>to worship idols.   
**1:17** Jeremiah, get ready!<br>Go and tell the people<br>what I command you to say.<br>Don't be frightened by them,<br>or I will make you terrified<br>while they watch.   
**1:18** My power will make you strong<br>like a fortress<br>or a column of iron<br>or a wall of bronze.<br>You will oppose all of Judah,<br>including its kings and leaders,<br>its priests and people.  
**1:19** They will fight back,<br>but they won't win.<br>I, the LORD, give my word—<br>I won't let them harm you.  

## 2장

**2:1** The LORD told me  
**2:2** to go to Jerusalem and tell everyone that he had said:<br>When you were my young bride,<br>you loved me and followed me<br>through the barren desert.  
**2:3** You belonged to me alone,<br>like the first part of the harvest,<br>and I severely punished<br>those who mistreated you.   
**2:4** Listen, people of Israel,<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Israel: </i>After the nation was divided, the northern kingdom was called “Israel,” and the southern kingdom was called “Judah” (see <a name='B:110 12:1-20'>1 Kings 12.1-20). In 722 B.C. the Assyrians conquered the northern kingdom, and Judah was all that was left. And so in the book of Jeremiah the name “Israel” is most often used of the southern kingdom.  
**2:5** and I, the LORD, will speak.<br>I was never unfair<br>to your ancestors,<br>but they left me<br>and became worthless<br>by following worthless idols.  
**2:6** Your ancestors refused<br>to ask for my help,<br>though I had rescued them<br>from Egypt<br>and led them through<br>a treacherous, barren desert,<br>where no one lives<br>or dares to travel.   
**2:7** I brought you here to my land,<br>where food is abundant,<br>but you made my land filthy<br>with your sins.  
**2:8** The priests who teach my laws<br>don't care to know me.<br>Your leaders rebel against me;<br>your prophets<br>give messages from Baal<br>and worship false gods.  
**2:9** I will take you to court<br>and accuse you<br>and your descendants<br>*  
**2:10** of a crime that no nation<br>has ever committed before.<br>Just ask anyone, anywhere,<br>from the eastern deserts<br>to the islands in the west.  
**2:11** You will find that no nation<br>has ever abandoned its gods<br>even though they were false.<br>I am the true and glorious God,<br>but you have rejected me<br>to worship idols.  
**2:12** Tell the heavens<br>to tremble with fear!  
**2:13** You, my people, have sinned<br>in two ways—<br>you have rejected me, the source<br>of life-giving water,<br>and you've tried to collect water<br>in cracked and leaking pits<br>dug in the ground.   
**2:14** People of Israel,<br>you weren't born slaves;<br>you were captured in war.  
**2:15** Enemies roared like lions<br>and destroyed your land;<br>towns lie burned and empty.  
**2:16** Soldiers from the Egyptian towns<br>of Memphis and Tahpanhes<br>have cracked your skulls.  
**2:17** It's all your own fault!<br>You stopped following me,<br>the LORD your God,  
**2:18** and you trusted the power<br>of Egypt and Assyria.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>trusted … Assyria: </i>Hebrew “went to Egypt and drank from the Shihor River, and you went to Assyria and drank from the Euphrates River.”  
**2:19** Your own sins will punish you,<br>because it was a bitter mistake<br>for you to reject me<br>without fear of punishment.<br>I, the LORD All-Powerful,<br>have spoken.   
**2:20** Long ago you left me<br>and broke all ties between us,<br>refusing to be my servant.<br>Now you worship other gods<br>by having sex<br>on hilltops or in the shade<br>of large trees.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>having sex … trees: </i>In some Canaanite religions, worshipers had sex with temple prostitutes, who represented their gods; many of the Canaanite places of worship were on hilltops or under large trees.  
**2:21** You were a choice grapevine,<br>but you have become<br>a wild, useless vine.  
**2:22** The LORD said:<br>People of Israel,<br>you are stained with guilt,<br>and no soap or bleach<br>can wash it away.  
**2:23** You deny your sins<br>and say, “We aren't unclean.<br>We haven't worshiped Baal.”<sup>①</sup><br>But think about what you do<br>in Hinnom Valley.<sup>②</sup><br>You run back and forth<br>like young camels,<br>as you rush to worship one idol<br>after another.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Hinnom Valley: </i>Hebrew “the valley” (see <a name='B:300 7:31-32'>7.31-32; <a name='B:300 19:1-6'>19.1-6). <sup>②</sup><i>Baal: </i>The Hebrew text has “the Baals,” probably because the god Baal was believed to be present in different forms at different places of worship.  
**2:24** You are a female donkey<br>sniffing the desert air,<br>wanting to mate<br>with just anyone.<br>You are an easy catch!  
**2:25** Your shoes are worn out,<br>and your throat is parched<br>from running here and there<br>to worship foreign gods.<br>“Stop!” I shouted,<br>but you replied, “No!<br>I love those gods too much.”   
**2:26** You and your leaders<br>are more disgraceful<br>than thieves—<br>you and your kings,<br>your priests and prophets  
**2:27** worship stone idols<br>and sacred poles<br>as if they had created you<br>and had given you life.<br>You have rejected me,<br>but when you're in trouble,<br>you cry to me for help.  
**2:28** Go cry to the gods you made!<br>There should be enough of them<br>to save you,<br>because Judah has as many gods<br>as it has towns.  
**2:29** The LORD said to Israel:<br>You accuse me of not saving you,<br>but I say you have rebelled.  
**2:30** I tried punishing you,<br>but you refused<br>to come back to me,<br>and like fierce lions<br>you killed my prophets.   
**2:31** Now listen to what I say!<br>Did I abandon you in the desert<br>or surround you with darkness?<br>You are my people,<br>yet you have told me,<br>“We'll do what we want,<br>and we refuse<br>to worship you!”  
**2:32** A bride could not forget<br>to wear her jewelry<br>to her wedding,<br>but you have forgotten me<br>day after day.  
**2:33** You are so clever<br>at finding lovers<br>that you could give lessons<br>to a prostitute.  
**2:34** You killed innocent people<br>for no reason at all.<br>And even though their blood<br>can be seen on your clothes,  
**2:35** you claim to be innocent,<br>and you want me to stop<br>being angry with you.<br>So I'll take you to court,<br>and we'll see who is right.   
**2:36** When Assyria let you down,<br>you quickly ran to Egypt,<br>but you'll find no help there,  
**2:37** and you will leave<br>in great sadness.<sup>①</sup><br>I won't let you find help<br>from those you trust.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>in great sadness: </i>Or “as prisoners.”  

## 3장

**3:3** So I, the LORD, refused<br>to let the spring rains fall.<br>But just like a prostitute,<br>you still have no shame<br>for what you have done.  
**3:4** You call me your father<br>or your long-lost friend;  
**3:5** you beg me to stop being angry,<br>but you won't stop sinning.  
**3:6**  When Josiah<sup>①</sup> was king, the LORD said: Jeremiah, the kingdom of Israel<sup>②</sup> was like an unfaithful wife who became a prostitute on the hilltops and in the shade of large trees.<sup>③</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Josiah: </i>Ruled 640–609 B.C. <sup>②</sup><i>Israel: </i>The northern kingdom (see the <a name='C300 2:4'>note at <a name='B:300 2:4'>2.4). <sup>③</sup><i>prostitute … trees: </i>See the <a name='C300 2:20'>note at <a name='B:300 2:20'>2.20.  
**3:7** I knew that the kingdom of Israel had been unfaithful and committed many sins, yet I still hoped she might come back to me. But she didn't, so I divorced her and sent her away. Her sister, the kingdom of Judah, saw what happened, but she wasn't worried in the least, and I watched her become unfaithful like her sister.  
**3:8**   
**3:9** The kingdom of Judah wasn't sorry for being a prostitute, and she didn't care that she had made both herself and the land unclean by worshiping idols of stone and wood.  
**3:10** And worst of all, the people of Judah pretended to come back to me.  
**3:11** Even the people of Israel were honest enough not to pretend.  
**3:12** Jeremiah, shout toward the north:<br>Israel, I am your LORD—<br>come back to me!<br>You were unfaithful<br>and made me furious,<br>but I am merciful,<br>and so I will forgive you.  
**3:13** Just admit that you rebelled<br>and worshiped foreign gods<br>under large trees everywhere.  
**3:14** You are unfaithful children,<br>but you belong to me.<br>Come home!<br>I'll take one or two of you<br>from each town and clan<br>and bring you to Zion.  
**3:15** Then I'll appoint wise rulers<br>who will obey me,<br>and they will care for you<br>like shepherds.   
**3:16** You will increase in numbers,<br>and there will be no need<br>to remember the sacred chest<br>or to make a new one.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>make a new one: </i>The sacred chest was probably destroyed or taken away by the Babylonians when they captured Jerusalem in 586 B.C. <sup>②</sup><i>sacred chest … throne: </i>The sacred chest was thought to be God's throne on earth.  
**3:17** The whole city of Jerusalem<br>will be my throne.<sup>①</sup><br>All nations will come here<br>to worship me,<br>and they will no longer follow<br>their stubborn, evil hearts.  
**3:18** Then, in countries to the north,<br>you people of Judah and Israel<br>will be reunited,<br>and you will return to the land<br>I gave your ancestors.  
**3:19** I have always wanted<br>to treat you as my children<br>and give you the best land,<br>the most beautiful on earth.<br>I wanted you to call me “Father”<br>and not turn from me.  
**3:20** But instead, you are like a wife<br>who broke her wedding vows.<br>You have been unfaithful to me.<br>I, the LORD, have spoken.   
**3:21** Listen to the noise<br>on the hilltops!<br>It's the people of Israel,<br>weeping and begging me<br>to answer their prayers.<br>They forgot about me<br>and chose the wrong path.  
**3:22** I will tell them, “Come back,<br>and I will cure you<br>of your unfaithfulness.” They will answer,<br>“We will come back, because you<br>are the LORD our God.  
**3:23** On hilltops, we worshiped idols<br>and made loud noises,<br>but it was all for nothing—<br>only you can save us.  
**3:24** Since the days of our ancestors<br>when our nation was young,<br>that shameful god Baal<sup>①</sup> has taken<br>our crops and livestock,<br>our sons and daughters.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>that shameful god Baal: </i>The Hebrew text has “The Shame,” which was sometimes used as a way of making fun of the Canaanite god Baal.  
**3:25** We have rebelled against you<br>just like our ancestors,<br>and we are ashamed of our sins.”  

## 4장

**4:1** The LORD said:<br>Israel, if you really want<br>to come back to me, get rid<br>of those disgusting idols.  
**4:2** Make promises only in my name,<br>and do what you promise!<br>Then all nations will praise me,<br>and I will bless them.  
**4:3**  People of Jerusalem and Judah,<br>don't be so stubborn!<br>Your hearts have become hard,<br>like unplowed ground<br>where thornbushes grow.  
**4:4** With all your hearts,<br>keep the agreement<br>I made with you.<br>But if you are stubborn<br>and keep on sinning,<br>my anger will burn like a fire<br>that cannot be put out. *  
**4:5** “Sound the trumpets, my people.<br>Warn the people of Judah,<sup>①</sup><br>‘Run for your lives!<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Judah: </i>Hebrew “Judah and Jerusalem.”  
**4:6** Head for Jerusalem<br>or another walled town!’ “Jeremiah, tell them I'm sending<br>disaster from the north.  
**4:7** An army will come out,<br>like a lion from its den.<br>It will destroy nations<br>and leave your towns empty<br>and in ruins.”   
**4:8** Then I told the people<br>of Israel,<br>“Put on sackcloth!<sup>①</sup><br>Mourn and cry out,<br>‘The LORD is still angry<br>with us.’ ”<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>sackcloth: </i>A rough, dark-colored cloth made from goat or camel hair and used to make grain sacks. It was worn in times of trouble or sorrow.  
**4:9** The LORD said,<br>“When all this happens,<br>the king and his officials,<br>the prophets and the priests<br>will be shocked and terrified.”  
**4:10** I said, “You are the LORD God. So why have you fooled everyone, especially the people of Jerusalem? Why did you promise peace, when a knife is at our throats?”  
**4:11** When disaster comes, the LORD will tell you people of Jerusalem,<br>“I am sending a windstorm<br>from the desert—<br>not a welcome breeze.<sup>①</sup><br>And it will sweep you away<br>as punishment for your sins.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>a welcome breeze: </i>Hebrew “a wind to blow away the husks.” Farmers used a special shovel to pitch grain and husks into the air. Wind would blow away the light husks, and the grain would fall back to the ground, where it could be gathered up.  
**4:12**   
**4:13** Look! The enemy army<br>swoops down like an eagle;<br>their cavalry and chariots<br>race faster than storm clouds<br>blown by the wind.” Then you will answer,<br>“We are doomed!”   
**4:14** But Jerusalem, there is still time<br>for you to be saved.<br>Wash the evil from your hearts<br>and stop making sinful plans,  
**4:15** before a message of disaster<br>arrives from the hills of Ephraim<br>and the town of Dan.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Ephraim … Dan: </i>The hills of Ephraim were to the north of Jerusalem, and Dan was even farther north. They would be reached by the invading army first.  
**4:16** The LORD said,<br>“Tell the nations that my people<br>have rebelled against me.<br>And so an army will come<br>from far away<br>to surround Jerusalem<br>and the towns of Judah.<br>I, the LORD, have spoken.   
**4:17**   
**4:18** “People of Judah,<br>your hearts will be in pain,<br>but it's your own fault<br>that you will be punished.”  
**4:19** I can't stand the pain!<br>My heart pounds,<br>as I twist and turn in agony.<br>I hear the signal trumpet<br>and the battle cry of the enemy,<br>and I cannot be silent.  
**4:20** I see the enemy defeating us<br>time after time,<br>leaving everything in ruins.<br>Even my own home<br>is destroyed in a moment.  
**4:21** How long will I see enemy flags<br>and hear their trumpets?   
**4:22** I heard the LORD say,<br>“My people ignore me.<br>They are foolish children<br>who do not understand<br>that they will be punished.<br>All they know is how to sin.”   
**4:23** After this, I looked around.<br>The earth was barren,<br>with no form of life.<br>The sun, moon, and stars<br>had disappeared.  
**4:24** The mountains were shaking;  
**4:25** no people could be seen,<br>and all the birds<br>had flown away.  
**4:26** Farmland had become a desert,<br>and towns were in ruins.<br>The LORD's fierce anger<br>had done all of this.  
**4:27** The LORD said:<br>I have made my decision,<br>and I won't change my mind.<br>This land will be destroyed,<br>although not completely.<br>The sky will turn dark,<br>and the earth will mourn.   
**4:28**   
**4:29** Enemy cavalry and archers<br>shout their battle cry.<br>People run for their lives<br>and try to find safety<br>among trees and rocks.<br>Every town is empty.   
**4:30** Jerusalem, your land<br>has been wiped out.<br>But you act like a prostitute<br>and try to win back your lovers,<br>who now hate you.<br>You can put on a red dress,<br>gold jewelry, and eye shadow,<br>but it's no use—<br>your lovers are out to kill you!

## 5장

**5:21**  You fools! Why don't you listen<br>when I speak?<br>Why can't you understand

**5:23** You stubborn people have rebelled<br>and turned your backs on me.

## 6장

**6:4** Kings will tell their troops,<br>“If we reach Jerusalem<br>in the morning,<br>we'll attack at noon.<br>But if we arrive later,
