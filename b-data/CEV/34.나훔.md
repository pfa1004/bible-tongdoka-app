# 34.나훔 (CEV)



## 1장

**1:1**  I am Nahum from Elkosh.<sup>①</sup> And this is the message<sup>②</sup> that I wrote down about Nineveh.<sup>③</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Elkosh: </i>The location of Elkosh is not known. <sup>②</sup><i>message: </i>Or “vision.” <sup>③</sup><i>Nineveh: </i>The capital of Assyria, the hated enemy of Israel.  
**1:2** The LORD God demands loyalty.<br>In his anger, he takes revenge<br>on his enemies.  
**1:3** The LORD is powerful,<br>yet patient;<br>he makes sure that the guilty<br>are always punished.<br>He can be seen in storms<br>and in whirlwinds;<br>clouds are the dust from his feet.   
**1:4** At the LORD's command,<br>oceans and rivers dry up.<br>Bashan, Mount Carmel,<br>and Lebanon<sup>①</sup> wither,<br>and their flowers fade.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Bashan, Mount Carmel, and Lebanon: </i>Three regions noted for their trees and flowers.  
**1:5** At the sight of the LORD,<br>mountains and hills<br>tremble and melt;<br>the earth and its people<br>shudder and shake.  
**1:6** Who can stand the heat<br>of his furious anger?<br>It flashes out like fire<br>and shatters stones.  
**1:7** The LORD is good.<br>He protects those who trust him<br>in times of trouble.  
**1:8** But like a roaring flood,<br>the LORD chases his enemies<br>into dark places<br>and destroys them.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>the LORD chases his enemies … and destroys them: </i>Or “the LORD chases Nineveh … and destroys her.”  
**1:9** So don't plot against the LORD!<br>He wipes out his enemies,<br>and they never revive.  
**1:10** They are like drunkards<br>overcome by wine,<br>or like twisted thornbushes<br>burning in a fire.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>fire: </i>One possible meaning for the difficult Hebrew text of verse <a name='B:410 1:10'>10.  
**1:11** Assyria, one of your rulers<br>has made evil plans<br>against the LORD.   
**1:12** But the LORD says, “Assyria,<br>no matter how strong you are,<br>you will be cut down!<br>My people Judah,<br>I have troubled you before,<br>but I won't do it again.  
**1:13** I'll snap your chains<br>and set you free<br>from the Assyrians.”   
**1:14** Assyria, this is what else<br>the LORD says to you:<br>“Your name will be forgotten.<br>I will destroy every idol<br>in your temple,<br>and I will send you to the grave,<br>because you are worthless.”   
**1:15**  Look toward the mountains,<br>people of Judah!<br>Here comes a messenger<br>with good news of peace.<br>Celebrate your festivals.<br>Keep your promises to God.<br>Your evil enemies are destroyed<br>and will never again<br>invade your country.  

## 2장

**2:1** Nineveh, someone is coming<br>to attack and scatter you.<br>Guard your fortresses!<br>Watch the road! Be brave!<br>Prepare for battle!  
**2:2** Judah and Israel are like trees<br>with branches broken<br>by their enemies.<br>But the LORD is going to restore<br>their power and glory. *  
**2:3** Nineveh, on this day of attack,<br>your enemies' shields are red;<br>their uniforms are crimson.  
**2:4** Their horses<sup>①</sup> prance,<br>and their armored<sup>②</sup> chariots<br>dart around like lightning<br>or flaming torches.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>horses: </i>Two ancient translations; Hebrew “spears.” <sup>②</sup><i>armored: </i>One possible meaning for the difficult Hebrew text.  
**2:5** An officer gives a command.<br>But his soldiers stumble,<br>as they hasten to build<br>a shelter to protect themselves<br>against rocks thrown down<br>from the city wall.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>to build … city wall: </i>One possible meaning for the difficult Hebrew text.  
**2:6** The river gates<sup>①</sup> fly open,<br>and panic floods the palace.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>river gates: </i>Nineveh was protected by a moat filled with water from the nearby Tigris River.  
**2:7** Nineveh is disgraced.<br>The queen is dragged off.<br>Her servant women mourn;<br>they moan like doves,<br>and they beat their breasts<br>in sorrow.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>sorrow: </i>One possible meaning for the difficult Hebrew text of verse <a name='B:410 2:7'>7.  
**2:8** Nineveh is like a pond<br>with leaking water.<br>Shouts of “Stop! Don't go!”<br>can be heard everywhere.<br>But everyone is leaving.   
**2:9** Enemy soldiers shout,<br>“The city is full of treasure<br>and all kinds of wealth.<br>Steal her silver! Grab her gold!”   
**2:10** Nineveh is doomed! Destroyed!<br>Her people tremble with fear;<br>their faces turn pale.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>faces turn pale: </i>Or “ashes cover their faces.”  
**2:11** What happened to this city?<br>They were safer there<br>than powerful lions in a den,<br>with no one to disturb them.  
**2:12** These are the same lions<br>that ferociously attacked<br>their victims,<br>then dragged away the flesh<br>to feed their young.   
**2:13** The LORD All-Powerful<br>is against you, Nineveh.<br>God will burn your chariots<br>and send an army to kill<br>those young lions of yours.<br>You will never again<br>make victims of others<br>or send messengers to threaten<br>everyone on this earth.  

## 3장

**3:1** Doom to the crime capital!<br>Nineveh, city of murder<br>and treachery,  
**3:2** here is your fate—<br>cracking whips,<br>churning wheels;<br>galloping horses,<br>roaring chariots;  
**3:3** cavalry attacking,<br>swords and spears flashing;<br>soldiers stumbling<br>over piles of dead bodies.  
**3:4** You were nothing more<br>than a prostitute<br>using your magical charms<br>and witchcraft<br>to attract and trap nations.   
**3:5** But I, the LORD All-Powerful,<br>am now your enemy.<br>I will pull up your skirt<br>and let nations and kingdoms<br>stare at your nakedness.  
**3:6** I will cover you with garbage,<br>treat you like trash,<br>and rub you in the dirt.  
**3:7** Everyone who sees you<br>will turn away and shout,<br>“Nineveh is done for!<br>Is anyone willing to mourn<br>or to give her comfort?”  
**3:8** Nineveh, do you feel safer<br>than the city of Thebes?<sup>①</sup><br>The Nile River<br>was its wall of defense.<sup>②</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Thebes: </i>In 663 B.C., the Assyrian King Ashurbanipal captured this Egyptian city, which seems to have been built with protection similar to that of Nineveh. <sup>②</sup><i>was its … defense: </i>One possible meaning for the difficult Hebrew text.  
**3:9** Thebes trusted the mighty power<br>of Ethiopia<sup>①</sup> and Egypt;<br>the nations of Put<sup>②</sup> and Libya<br>were her allies.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Ethiopia: </i>The Hebrew text has “Cush,” which was a region south of Egypt that included parts of the present countries of Ethiopia and Sudan. <sup>②</sup><i>Put: </i>A region in Africa, possibly part of the present country of Libya.  
**3:10** But she was captured and taken<br>to a foreign country.<br>Her children were murdered<br>at every street corner.<br>The members of her royal family<br>were auctioned off,<br>and her high officials<br>were bound in chains.   
**3:11** Nineveh, now it's your turn!<br>You will get drunk and try to hide<br>from your enemy.  
**3:12** Your fortresses are fig trees<br>with ripe figs.<br>Merely shake the trees,<br>and fruit will fall<br>into every open mouth.  
**3:13** Your army is weak.<br>Fire has destroyed the crossbars<br>on your city gates;<br>now they stand wide open<br>to your enemy.   
**3:14** Your city is under attack.<br>Haul in extra water!<br>Strengthen your defenses!<br>Start making bricks!<br>Stir the mortar!  
**3:15** You will still go up in flames<br>and be cut down by swords<br>that will wipe you out like a field<br>attacked by grasshoppers.<br>So, go ahead and increase<br>like a swarm of locusts!<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>locusts: </i>A type of grasshopper that comes in swarms and causes great damage to plant life.  
**3:16** More merchants are in your city<br>than there are stars<br>in the sky—<br>but they are like locusts<br>that eat everything,<br>then fly away.  
**3:17** Your guards and your officials<br>are swarms of locusts.<br>On a chilly day<br>they settle on a fence,<br>but when the sun comes out,<br>they take off<br>to who-knows-where.   
**3:18** King of Assyria,<br>your officials and leaders<br>are sound asleep,<br>while your people are scattered<br>in the mountains.<br>Yes, your people are sheep<br>without a shepherd.  
**3:19** You're fatally wounded.<br>There's no hope for you.<br>But everyone claps<br>when they hear this news,<br>because your constant cruelty<br>has caused them pain.
