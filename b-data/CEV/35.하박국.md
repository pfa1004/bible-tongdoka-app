# 35.하박국 (CEV)



## 1장

**1:1** I am Habakkuk the prophet. And this is the message<sup>①</sup> that the LORD gave me.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>message: </i>Or “vision.”  
**1:2** Our LORD, how long must I beg<br>for your help<br>before you listen?<br>How long before you save us<br>from all this violence?  
**1:3** Why do you make me watch<br>such terrible injustice?<br>Why do you allow violence,<br>lawlessness, crime, and cruelty<br>to spread everywhere?  
**1:4** Laws cannot be enforced;<br>justice is always the loser;<br>criminals crowd out honest people<br>and twist the laws around.  
**1:5**  Look and be amazed<br>at what's happening<br>among the nations!<br>Even if you were told,<br>you would never believe<br>what's taking place now.  
**1:6**  I am sending the Babylonians.<br>They are fierce and cruel—<br>marching across the land,<br>conquering cities and towns.   
**1:7** How fearsome and frightening.<br>Their only laws and rules<br>are the ones they make up.  
**1:8** Their cavalry troops are faster<br>than leopards,<br>more ferocious than wolves<br>hunting at sunset,<br>and swifter than hungry eagles<br>suddenly swooping down.   
**1:9** They are eager to destroy,<sup>①</sup><br>and they gather captives<br>like handfuls of sand.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>eager to destroy: </i>One possible meaning for the difficult Hebrew text.  
**1:10** They make fun of rulers<br>and laugh at fortresses,<br>while building dirt mounds<br>so they can capture cities.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>dirt mounds … cities: </i>Attacking armies often build dirt mounds against city walls to make it easier for them to climb the wall and capture the city.  
**1:11** Then suddenly they disappear<br>like a gust of wind—<br>those sinful people who worship<br>their own strength.  
**1:12** Holy LORD God, mighty rock,<sup>①</sup><br>you are eternal,<br>and we<sup>②</sup> are safe from death.<br>You are using those Babylonians<br>to judge and punish others.<sup>③</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>mighty rock: </i>The Hebrew text has “rock,” which is sometimes used in poetry to compare the LORD to a mountain where his people can run for protection from their enemies. <sup>②</sup><i>we: </i>Hebrew; one ancient Jewish tradition “you.” <sup>③</sup><i>You … others: </i>Or “You will judge and punish those Babylonians.”  
**1:13** But you can't stand sin or wrong.<br>So don't sit by in silence<br>while they gobble up people<br>who are better than they are.   
**1:14** The people you put on this earth<br>are like fish or reptiles<br>without a leader.  
**1:15** Then an enemy comes along<br>and takes them captive<br>with hooks and nets.<br>It makes him so happy  
**1:16** that he offers sacrifices<br>to his fishing nets,<br>because they make him rich<br>and provide choice foods.  
**1:17** Will he keep hauling in his nets<br>and destroying nations<br>without showing mercy?  

## 2장

**2:1** While standing guard<br>on the watchtower,<br>I waited for the LORD's answer,<br>before explaining the reason<br>for my complaint.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>I … complaint: </i>One possible meaning for the difficult Hebrew text.  
**2:2** Then the LORD told me:<br>“I will give you my message<br>in the form of a vision.<br>Write it clearly enough<br>to be read at a glance.  
**2:3**  At the time I have decided,<br>my words will come true.<br>You can trust what I say<br>about the future.<br>It may take a long time,<br>but keep on waiting—<br>it will happen!   
**2:4**  “I, the LORD, refuse to accept<br>anyone who is proud.<br>Only those who live by faith<br>are acceptable to me.”<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Only … me: </i>Or “But those who are acceptable to me will live because of their faithfulness.”  
**2:5** Wine<sup>①</sup> is treacherous,<br>and arrogant people<br>are never satisfied.<br>They are no less greedy<br>than death itself—<br>they open their mouths as wide<br>as the world of the dead<br>and swallow everyone.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Wine: </i>The Standard Hebrew Text; the Dead Sea Scrolls “Wealth.”  
**2:6** But they will be mocked<br>with these words:<br>You're doomed!<br>You stored up stolen goods<br>and cheated others<br>of what belonged to them.  
**2:7** But without warning,<br>those you owe<br>will demand payment.<br>Then you will become<br>a frightened victim.  
**2:8** You robbed cities and nations<br>everywhere on earth<br>and murdered their people.<br>Now those who survived<br>will be as cruel to you.   
**2:9** You're doomed!<br>You made your family rich<br>at the expense of others.<br>You even said to yourself,<br>“I'm above the law.”  
**2:10** But you will bring shame<br>on your family<br>and ruin to yourself<br>for what you did to others.  
**2:11** The very stones and wood<br>in your home<br>will testify against you.   
**2:12** You're doomed! You built a city<br>on crime and violence.  
**2:13**  But the LORD All-Powerful<br>sends up in flames<br>what nations and people<br>work so hard to gain.   
**2:14**  Just as water fills the sea,<br>the land will be filled<br>with people who know<br>and honor the LORD.   
**2:15** You're doomed!<br>You get your friends drunk,<br>just to see them naked.  
**2:16** Now you will be disgraced<br>instead of praised.<br>The LORD will make you drunk,<br>and when others see you naked,<br>you will lose their respect.  
**2:17** You destroyed trees and animals<br>on Mount Lebanon;<br>you were ruthless to towns<br>and people everywhere.<br>Now you will be terrorized.  
**2:18** What is an idol worth?<br>It's merely a false god.<br>Why trust a speechless image<br>made from wood or metal<br>by human hands?  
**2:19** What can you learn from idols<br>covered with silver or gold?<br>They can't even breathe.<br>Pity anyone who says to an idol<br>of wood or stone,<br>“Get up and do something!”   
**2:20** Let all the world be silent—<br>the LORD is present<br>in his holy temple.  

## 3장

**3:1** This is my prayer:<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>prayer: </i>The Hebrew text adds “according to the shigionoth,” which may mean a prayer of request or a prayer to be accompanied by a special musical instrument.  
**3:2** I know your reputation, LORD,<br>and I am amazed<br>at what you have done.<br>Please turn from your anger<br>and be merciful;<br>do for us what you did<br>for our ancestors.   
**3:3** You are the same Holy God<br>who came from Teman<br>and Paran<sup>①</sup> to help us.<br>The brightness of your glory<br>covered the heavens,<br>and your praises were heard<br>everywhere on earth.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Teman … Paran: </i>Teman is a district in Edom, but the name is sometimes used of the whole country of Edom; Paran is the hill country along the western border of the Gulf of Aqaba. In <a name='B:70 5:4'>Judges 5.4, the LORD is said to have marched from Edom to help his people; in <a name='B:50 33:2'>Deuteronomy 33.2, Paran is mentioned in connection with the LORD's appearance at Sinai.  
**3:4** Your glory shone like the sun,<br>and light flashed from your hands,<br>hiding your mighty power.  
**3:5** Dreadful diseases and plagues<br>marched in front<br>and followed behind.  
**3:6** When you stopped,<br>the earth shook;<br>when you stared,<br>nations trembled;<br>when you walked<br>along your ancient paths,<br>eternal mountains and hills<br>crumbled and collapsed.  
**3:7** The tents of desert tribes<br>in Cushan and Midian<sup>①</sup><br>were ripped apart.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Cushan and Midian: </i>Tribes of the Arabian desert who were enemies of Israel.  
**3:8** Our LORD, were you angry<br>with the monsters<br>of the deep?<sup>①</sup><br>You attacked in your chariot<br>and wiped them out.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>monsters of the deep: </i>The Hebrew text has “rivers and oceans,” which may stand for the powerful monsters that were thought to have lived there before the LORD defeated them.  
**3:9** Your arrows were ready<br>and obeyed your commands.<sup>①</sup> You split the earth apart<br>with rivers and streams;<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>obeyed your commands: </i>One possible meaning for the difficult Hebrew text.  
**3:10** mountains trembled<br>at the sight of you;<br>rain poured from the clouds;<br>ocean waves roared and rose.  
**3:11** The sun and moon stood still,<br>while your arrows and spears<br>flashed like lightning.   
**3:12** In your furious anger,<br>you trampled on nations  
**3:13** to rescue your people<br>and save your chosen one.<sup>①</sup><br>You crushed a nation's ruler<br>and stripped his evil kingdom<br>of its power.<sup>②</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>chosen one: </i>Or “chosen ones.” <sup>②</sup><i>You crushed … power: </i>One possible meaning for the difficult Hebrew text.  
**3:14** His troops had come like a storm,<br>hoping to scatter us<br>and glad to gobble us up.<br>To them we were refugees<br>in hiding—<br>but you smashed their heads<br>with their own weapons.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>but you … weapons: </i>One possible meaning for the difficult Hebrew text.  
**3:15** Then your chariots churned<br>the waters of the sea.  
**3:16** When I heard this message,<sup>①</sup><br>I felt weak from fear,<br>and my lips quivered.<br>My bones seemed to melt,<br>and I stumbled around.<br>But I will patiently wait.<br>Someday those vicious enemies<br>will be struck by disaster.<sup>②</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>heard this message: </i>Or “saw this vision.” <sup>②</sup><i>I will … disaster: </i>One possible meaning for the difficult Hebrew text.  
**3:17** Fig trees may no longer bloom,<br>or vineyards produce grapes;<br>olive trees may be fruitless,<br>and harvest time a failure;<br>sheep pens may be empty,<br>and cattle stalls vacant—  
**3:18** but I will still celebrate<br>because the LORD God<br>is my Savior.  
**3:19**  The LORD gives me strength.<br>He makes my feet as sure<br>as those of a deer,<br>and he helps me stand<br>on the mountains.<sup>①</sup> To the music director:<br>Use stringed instruments.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>stand on the mountains: </i>One possible meaning for the difficult Hebrew text.
