# 33.미가 (CEV)



## 1장

**1:1**  I am Micah from Moresheth.<sup>①</sup> And this is the message about Samaria and Jerusalem<sup>②</sup> that the LORD gave to me when Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah<sup>③</sup> were the kings of Judah.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Moresheth: </i>A town in southern Judah not far from Gath. In verse 14 it is called Moresheth-Gath. <sup>②</sup><i>Samaria and Jerusalem: </i>Samaria was the capital of the northern kingdom (Israel), and Jerusalem was the capital of the southern kingdom (Judah). <sup>③</sup><i>Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah: </i>Jotham, the son of Uzziah, ruled Judah 740–736 B.C.; Ahaz, the son of Jotham, ruled 736–716 B.C.; Hezekiah, the son of Ahaz, ruled 716–687 B.C.  
**1:2** Listen, all of you!<br>Earth and everything on it,<br>pay close attention.<br>The LORD God accuses you<br>from his holy temple.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>holy temple: </i>Possibly the one in heaven, though it may be the Jerusalem temple.  
**1:3** And he will come down<br>to crush underfoot<br>every pagan altar.  
**1:4** Mountains will melt<br>beneath his feet<br>like wax beside a fire.<br>Valleys will vanish like water<br>rushing down a ravine.  
**1:5** This will happen because of<br>the terrible sins of Israel,<br>the descendants of Jacob.<br>Samaria has led Israel to sin,<br>and pagan altars at Jerusalem<br>have made Judah sin.   
**1:6** So the LORD will leave Samaria<br>in ruins—<br>merely an empty field<br>where vineyards are planted.<br>He will scatter its stones<br>and destroy its foundations.  
**1:7** Samaria's idols will be smashed,<br>and the wages<br>of temple prostitutes<sup>①</sup><br>will be destroyed by fire.<br>Silver and gold from those idols<br>will then be used by foreigners<br>as payment for prostitutes.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>wages of temple prostitutes: </i>At pagan temples, people had sex with prostitutes as a way of worshiping the idols, and the money earned in this way was used to support the pagan religion.  
**1:8** Because of this tragedy,<sup>①</sup><br>I go barefoot and naked.<br>My crying and weeping<br>sound like howling wolves<br>or ostriches.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>this tragedy: </i>Either the destruction of Samaria (verses 6,7) or the coming destruction of Judah and Jerusalem.  
**1:9** The nation is fatally wounded.<br>Judah is doomed.<br>Jerusalem will fall.   
**1:10** Don't tell it in Gath!<br>Don't even cry.<br>Instead, roll in the dust<br>at Beth-Leaphrah.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Gath … Beth-Leaphrah: </i>Gath was a Philistine city; Beth-Leaphrah is unknown, but in Hebrew it sounds like “House of Dust.”  
**1:11** Depart naked and ashamed,<br>you people of Shaphir.<sup>①</sup><br>The town of Bethezel<sup>②</sup> mourns<br>because no one from Zaanan<sup>③</sup><br>went out to help.<sup>④</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Zaanan: </i>Mentioned only here in the Old Testament; in Hebrew “Zaanan” means “one who goes out.” <sup>②</sup><i>The town … help: </i>Or “No one from Zaanan refused to desert their town, and Bethezel mourns because it is left undefended.” <sup>③</sup><i>Shaphir: </i>Mentioned only here in the Old Testament; in Hebrew “Shaphir” means “beautiful.” <sup>④</sup><i>Bethezel: </i>Mentioned only here in the Old Testament; in Hebrew “Bethezel” means “house next door.”  
**1:12** Everyone in Maroth<sup>①</sup><br>hoped for the best,<br>but the LORD sent disaster<br>down on Jerusalem.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Maroth: </i>Mentioned only here in the Old Testament; in Hebrew “Maroth” means “bitter.”  
**1:13** Get the war chariots ready,<br>you people of Lachish.<sup>①</sup><br>You led Jerusalem into sin,<br>just as Israel did.<sup>②</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Lachish: </i>The chief city of southwest Judah, about 48 kilometers from Jerusalem. <sup>②</sup><i>led … sin … did: </i>Or “You led Jerusalem and Israel into sin.” In Hebrew “Lachish” sounds like “a team of horses (that pulls a war chariot).” And the sin may be that Lachish led the nation to trust the power of war chariots instead of the LORD. But the sin could be idolatry or some false teachings that were brought in from Egypt by way of Lachish.  
**1:14** Now you will have to give<br>a going-away gift<sup>①</sup><br>to Moresheth.<sup>②</sup><br>Israel's kings will discover<br>that they cannot trust<br>the town of Achzib.<sup>③</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>going-away gift: </i>The gift (dowry) that a bride's father gave her when she left the home of her parents to live with the family of her husband. In Hebrew the word for “bride” or “fiancee” sounds like “Moresheth.” <sup>②</sup><i>Moresheth: </i>Hebrew “Moresheth-Gath”; the home of Micah (see verse <a name='B:400 1:1'>1). <sup>③</sup><i>Achzib: </i>Meaning “lie” or “deception” was near Adullam Cave (verse 15), where David hid from King Saul (see <a name='B:90 22:1'>1 Samuel 22.1,<a name='B:90 22:2'>2). Micah probably means that the people of Israel (including their king) will have to run for their lives, but will find that all hope for escape is merely a “lie” (see verse 15).  
**1:15** People of Mareshah,<sup>①</sup><br>the LORD will send someone<br>to capture your town.<br>Then Israel's glorious king<br>will be forced to hide<br>in Adullam Cave.<sup>②</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Mareshah: </i>Sounds something like the Hebrew word for “conqueror” and was only a few kilometers northeast of Lachish. <sup>②</sup><i>Adullam Cave: </i>See the <a name='C400 1:14'>note at <a name='B:400 1:14'>1.14.  
**1:16** Judah, shave your head<br>as bald as a vulture<br>and start mourning.<br>Your precious children<sup>①</sup><br>will be dragged off<br>to a foreign country.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>precious children: </i>The towns mentioned in verses 10-15.  

## 2장

**2:1** Doomed! You're doomed!<br>At night you lie in bed,<br>making evil plans.<br>And when morning comes,<br>you do what you've planned<br>because you have the power.  
**2:2** You grab any field or house<br>that you want;<br>you cheat families<br>out of homes and land.   
**2:3** But here is what the LORD says:<br>“I am planning trouble for you.<br>Your necks will be caught<br>in a noose,<br>and you will be disgraced<br>in this time of disaster.”   
**2:4** When that happens,<br>this sorrowful song<br>will be sung about you:<br>“Ruined! Completely ruined!<br>The LORD has taken our land<br>and given it to traitors.”<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>The LORD … traitors: </i>One possible meaning for the difficult Hebrew text.  
**2:5** And so you will never again<br>own property<br>among the LORD's people.   
**2:6** “Enough of your preaching!”<br>That's what you tell me.<br>“We won't be disgraced,<br>so stop preaching!”   
**2:7** Descendants of Jacob,<br>is it right for you to claim<br>that the LORD did what he did<br>because he was angry?<br>Doesn't he always bless<br>those who do right?  
**2:8** My people, you have turned against<br>one another!<br>You have even stolen<br>clothes right off the backs<br>of innocent neighbors<br>who pass by in peace.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>of innocent neighbors … peace: </i>Or “of your unsuspecting soldiers returning home from battle.”  
**2:9** You take over lovely homes<br>that belong to the women<br>of my nation.<br>Then you cheat their children<br>out of the inheritance<br>that comes from the LORD.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>inheritance … LORD: </i>The Hebrew text has “my glory,” which refers to the inheritance of land that the LORD had promised his people.  
**2:10** Get out of here, you crooks!<br>You'll find no rest here.<br>You're not fit to belong<br>to the LORD's people,<br>and you will be destroyed.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>destroyed: </i>One possible meaning for the difficult Hebrew text.  
**2:11** The only prophet you want<br>is a liar who will say,<br>“Drink and get drunk!”  
**2:12** I, the LORD, promise<br>to bring together<br>the people of Israel<br>who have survived.<br>I will gather them,<br>just as a shepherd<br>brings sheep together,<br>and there will be many.  
**2:13** I will break down the gate<br>and lead them out—<br>then I will be their king.  

## 3장

**3:1** Listen to me,<br>you rulers of Israel!<br>You know right from wrong,  
**3:2** but you prefer to do evil<br>instead of what is right.<br>You skin my people alive.<br>You strip off their flesh,  
**3:3** break their bones,<br>cook it all in a pot,<br>and gulp it down.   
**3:4** Someday you will beg the LORD<br>to help you,<br>but he will turn away<br>because of your sins.   
**3:5** You lying prophets promise<br>security for anyone<br>who gives you food,<br>but disaster for anyone<br>who refuses to feed you.<br>Here is what the LORD says<br>to you prophets:  
**3:6** “You will live in the dark,<br>far from the sight of the sun,<br>with no message from me.  
**3:7** You prophets and fortunetellers<br>will all be disgraced,<br>with no message from me.”   
**3:8** But the LORD has filled me<br>with power and his Spirit.<br>I have been given the courage<br>to speak about justice<br>and to tell you people of Israel<br>that you have sinned.  
**3:9** So listen to my message,<br>you rulers of Israel!<br>You hate justice<br>and twist the truth.  
**3:10** You make cruelty and murder<br>a way of life in Jerusalem.  
**3:11** You leaders accept bribes<br>for dishonest decisions.<br>You priests and prophets<br>teach and preach,<br>but only for money. Then you say,<br>“The LORD is on our side.<br>No harm will come to us.”  
**3:12**  And so, because of you,<br>Jerusalem will be plowed under<br>and left in ruins.<br>Thorns will cover the mountain<br>where the temple now stands.  

## 4장

**4:1** In the future, the mountain<br>with the LORD's temple<br>will be the highest of all.<br>It will reach above the hills,<br>and every nation<br>will rush to it.  
**4:2** People of many nations<br>will come and say,<br>“Let's go up to the mountain<br>of the LORD God of Jacob<br>and worship in his temple.” The LORD will teach us his Law<br>from Jerusalem,<br>and we will obey him.  
**4:3**  He will settle arguments<br>between distant<br>and powerful nations.<br>They will pound their swords<br>and their spears<br>into rakes and shovels;<br>they will never again make war<br>or attack one another.  
**4:4**  Everyone will find rest<br>beneath their own fig trees<br>or grape vines,<br>and they will live in peace.<br>This is a solemn promise<br>of the LORD All-Powerful.   
**4:5** Others may follow their gods,<br>but we will always follow<br>the LORD our God.  
**4:6** The LORD said:<br>At that time<br>I will gather my people—<br>the lame and the outcasts,<br>and all others into whose lives<br>I have brought sorrow.  
**4:7** Then the lame and the outcasts<br>will belong to my people<br>and become a strong nation.<br>I, the LORD, will rule them<br>from Mount Zion forever.  
**4:8** Mount Zion in Jerusalem,<br>guardian of my people,<br>you will rule again.   
**4:9** Jerusalem, why are you crying?<br>Don't you have a king?<br>Have your advisors gone?<br>Are you suffering<br>like a woman in childbirth?  
**4:10** Keep on groaning with pain,<br>you people of Jerusalem!<br>If you escape from your city<br>to the countryside,<br>you will still be taken<br>as prisoners to Babylonia.<br>But later I will rescue you<br>from your enemies.   
**4:11** Zion, because of your sins<br>you are surrounded<br>by many nations who say,<br>“We can hardly wait<br>to see you disgraced.”<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>We … disgraced: </i>Or “We'll pull up your skirt and expose your nakedness!”  
**4:12** But they don't know<br>that I, the LORD,<br>have gathered them here<br>to grind them like grain.  
**4:13** Smash them to pieces, Zion!<br>I'll let you be like a bull<br>with iron horns<br>and bronze hoofs.<br>Crush those nations<br>and bring their wealth to me,<br>the LORD of the earth.  

## 5장

**5:1** Jerusalem, enemy troops<br>have surrounded you;<sup>①</sup><br>they have struck Israel's ruler<br>in the face with a stick.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Jerusalem … you: </i>Or “Jerusalem, you are slashing yourself in sorrow, because of the enemy troops.”  
**5:2**  Bethlehem Ephrath,<br>you are one of the smallest towns<br>in the nation of Judah.<br>But the LORD will choose<br>one of your people<br>to rule the nation—<br>someone whose family<br>goes back to ancient times.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>family … times: </i>Or “kingdom is eternal.”  
**5:3** The LORD will abandon Israel<br>only until this ruler is born,<br>and the rest of his family<sup>①</sup><br>returns to Israel.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>family: </i>Or “people.”  
**5:4** Like a shepherd<br>taking care of his sheep,<br>this ruler will lead<br>and care for his people<br>by the power and glorious name<br>of the LORD his God.<br>His people will live securely,<br>and the whole earth will know<br>his true greatness,  
**5:5** because he will bring peace.<br>Let Assyria attack our country<br>and our palaces.<br>We will counterattack,<br>led by a number of rulers  
**5:6**  whose strong army will defeat<br>the nation of Assyria.<sup>①</sup><br>Yes, our leaders will rescue us,<br>if those Assyrians<br>dare to invade our land.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>the nation of Assyria: </i>The Hebrew text uses both “land of Assyria” and “land of Nimrod,” which was a poetic name for Assyria.  
**5:7** A few of Jacob's descendants<br>survived and are scattered<br>among the nations.<br>But the LORD will let them<br>cover the earth like dew and rain<br>that refreshes the soil.  
**5:8** At present they are scattered,<br>but later they will attack,<br>as though they were fierce lions<br>pouncing on sheep.<br>Their enemies will be torn<br>to shreds,<br>with no one to save them;  
**5:9** they will be helpless,<br>completely destroyed.  
**5:10** The LORD said:<br>At that time I will wipe out<br>your cavalry and chariots,  
**5:11** as well as your cities<br>and your fortresses.  
**5:12** I will stop you<br>from telling fortunes<br>and practicing witchcraft.  
**5:13** You will no longer worship<br>the idols or stone images<br>you have made—<br>I will destroy them,  
**5:14** together with the sacred poles<sup>①</sup><br>and even your towns.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>sacred poles: </i>Used in the worship of Asherah, the fertility goddess.  
**5:15** I will become furious<br>and take revenge on the nations<br>that refuse to obey me.  

## 6장

**6:1** The LORD said to his people:<br>Come and present your case<br>to the hills and mountains.  
**6:2** Israel, I am bringing charges<br>against you—<br>I call upon the mountains<br>and the earth's firm foundation<br>to be my witnesses.   
**6:3** My people, have I wronged you<br>in any way at all?<br>Please tell me.  
**6:4**  I rescued you from Egypt,<br>where you were slaves.<br>I sent Moses, Aaron, and Miriam<br>to be your leaders.  
**6:5**  Don't forget the evil plans<br>of King Balak of Moab<br>or what Balaam son of Beor<sup>①</sup><br>said to him.<br>Remember how I, the LORD,<br>saved you many times<br>on your way from Acacia<br>to Gilgal.<sup>②</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Balak … Beor: </i>See <a name='B:40 22:1-24:1'>Numbers 22-24. <sup>②</sup><i>Acacia to Gilgal: </i>Acacia was where the Israelites camped after the experience with Balaam (see <a name='B:40 25:1'>Numbers 25.1; <a name='B:60 2:1'>Joshua 2.1; <a name='B:60 3:1'>3.1); Gilgal was where they camped while waiting to attack Jericho (see <a name='B:60 4:19-5:12'>Joshua 4.19-5.12).  
**6:6** What offering should I bring<br>when I bow down to worship<br>the LORD God Most High?<br>Should I try to please him<sup>①</sup><br>by sacrificing<br>calves a year old?<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>try to please him: </i>This refers to what are traditionally called “burnt sacrifices,” which were offered as a way of pleasing the LORD.  
**6:7** Will thousands of sheep<br>or rivers of olive oil<br>make God satisfied with me?<br>Should I sacrifice to the LORD<br>my first-born child as payment<br>for my terrible sins?  
**6:8** The LORD God has told us<br>what is right<br>and what he demands:<br>“See that justice is done,<br>let mercy be your first concern,<br>and humbly obey your God.”  
**6:9** I am the LORD,<br>and you are wise to respect<br>my power to punish.<br>So listen to my message<br>for the city of Jerusalem:<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Jerusalem: </i>One possible meaning for the difficult Hebrew text of verse <a name='B:400 6:9'>9.  
**6:10** You store up stolen treasures<br>and use dishonest scales.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>scales: </i>One possible meaning for the difficult Hebrew text of verse <a name='B:400 6:10'>10.  
**6:11** But I, the LORD, will punish you<br>for cheating with weights<br>and with measures.  
**6:12** You rich people are violent,<br>and everyone tells lies.   
**6:13** Because of your sins,<br>I will wound you and leave you<br>ruined and defenseless.  
**6:14** You will eat,<br>but still be hungry;<br>you will store up goods,<br>but lose everything—<br>I, the LORD, will let it all<br>be captured in war.  
**6:15** You won't harvest what you plant<br>or use the oil<br>from your olive trees<br>or drink the wine<br>from grapes you grow.   
**6:16**  Jerusalem, this will happen<br>because you followed<br>the sinful example<br>of kings Omri and Ahab.<sup>①</sup><br>Now I will destroy you<br>and your property.<br>Then the people of every nation<br>will make fun and insult you.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Omri and Ahab: </i>King Ahab was the son of Omri and the husband of the evil Jezebel. Almost two centuries before Micah, the prophet Elijah had spoken against the idolatry and the other sinful practices that Ahab had encouraged in Israel (see <a name='B:110 16:21-34'>1 Kings 16.21-34; <a name='B:110 18:1-18'>18.1-18; <a name='B:110 21:1-26'>21.1-26).  

## 7장

**7:1** I feel so empty inside—<br>like someone starving<br>for grapes or figs,<br>after the vines and trees<br>have all been picked clean.  
**7:2** No one is loyal to God;<br>no one does right.<br>Everyone is brutal<br>and eager to deceive<br>everyone else.  
**7:3** People cooperate to commit crime.<br>Judges and leaders demand bribes,<br>and rulers cheat in court.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>court: </i>One possible meaning for the difficult Hebrew text of verse <a name='B:400 7:3'>3.  
**7:4** The most honest of them<br>is worse than a thorn patch. Your doom has come!<br>Lookouts sound the warning,<br>and everyone panics.  
**7:5** Don't trust anyone,<br>not even your best friend,<br>and be careful what you say<br>to the one you love.   
**7:6**  Sons refuse to respect<br>their own fathers,<br>daughters rebel against<br>their own mothers,<br>and daughters-in-law despise<br>their mothers-in-law.<br>Your family is now your enemy.  
**7:7** But I trust the LORD God<br>to save me,<br>and I will wait for him<br>to answer my prayer.  
**7:8** My enemies, don't be glad<br>because of my troubles!<br>I may have fallen,<br>but I will get up;<br>I may be sitting in the dark,<br>but the LORD is my light.  
**7:9** I have sinned against the LORD.<br>And so I must endure his anger,<br>until he comes to my defense.<br>But I know that I will see him<br>making things right for me<br>and leading me to the light.   
**7:10** You, my enemies, said,<br>“The LORD God is helpless.”<br>Now each of you<br>will be disgraced<br>and put to shame.<br>I will see you trampled<br>like mud in the street.  
**7:11** Towns of Judah, the day is coming<br>when your walls will be rebuilt,<br>and your boundaries enlarged.  
**7:12** People will flock to you<br>from Assyria and Egypt,<br>from Babylonia<sup>①</sup><br>and everywhere else.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Babylonia: </i>The Hebrew text has “the river,” meaning the Euphrates River, which stood for Babylonia.  
**7:13** Those nations will suffer disaster<br>because of what they did.  
**7:14** Lead your people, LORD!<br>Come and be our shepherd.<br>Grasslands surround us,<br>but we live in a forest.<br>So lead us to Bashan and Gilead,<sup>①</sup><br>and let us find pasture<br>as we did long ago.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Bashan and Gilead: </i>Two regions east of the Jordan River, known for their fertile pasturelands.  
**7:15** I, the LORD, will work miracles<br>just as I did when I led you<br>out of Egypt.  
**7:16** Nations will see this<br>and be ashamed because<br>of their helpless armies.<br>They will be in shock,<br>unable to speak or hear,  
**7:17** because of their fear of me,<br>your LORD and God.<br>Then they will come trembling,<br>crawling out of their fortresses<br>like insects or snakes,<br>lapping up the dust.  
**7:18** Our God, no one is like you.<br>We are all that is left<br>of your chosen people,<br>and you freely forgive<br>our sin and guilt.<br>You don't stay angry forever;<br>you're glad to have pity  
**7:19** and pleased to be merciful.<br>You will trample on our sins<br>and throw them in the sea.  
**7:20** You will keep your word<br>and be faithful to Jacob<br>and to Abraham,<br>as you promised our ancestors<br>many years ago.
