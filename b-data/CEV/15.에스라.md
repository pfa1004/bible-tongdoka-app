## 1장

**1:1**  Years ago the LORD sent Jeremiah with a message about a promise<sup>①</sup> for the people of Israel. Then in the first year that Cyrus was king of Persia,<sup>②</sup> the LORD kept his promise by telling Cyrus to send this official message to all parts of his kingdom:<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>a promise: </i>That the people of Israel would be set free from Babylonia after 70 years (see <a name='B:300 25:11'>Jeremiah 25.11; <a name='B:300 29:10'>29.10). <sup>②</sup><i>the first year that Cyrus was king of Persia: </i>Probably 539 B.C., when Cyrus captured Babylonia. **1:2**  I am King Cyrus of Persia. The LORD God of heaven, who is also the God of Israel, has made me the ruler of all nations on earth. And he has chosen me to build a temple for him in Jerusalem, which is in Judah. The LORD God will watch over and encourage any of his people who want to go back to Jerusalem and help build the temple.  
**1:3**   
**1:4** Everyone else must provide what is needed. They must give money, supplies, and animals, as well as gifts for rebuilding God's temple.  
**1:5** Many people felt that the LORD God wanted them to help rebuild his temple, and they made plans to go to Jerusalem. Among them were priests, Levites, and leaders of the tribes of Judah and Benjamin.  
**1:6** The others helped by giving silver articles, gold, personal possessions, cattle, and other valuable gifts, as well as offerings for the temple.  
**1:7** King Cyrus gave back the things that Nebuchadnezzar<sup>①</sup> had taken from the LORD's temple in Jerusalem and had put in the temple of his own gods.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Nebuchadnezzar: </i>Known as Nebuchadnezzar II, who ruled Babylonia from 605 to 562 B.C. **1:8** Cyrus placed Mithredath, his chief treasurer, in charge of these things. Mithredath counted them and gave a list to Sheshbazzar, the governor of Judah.  
**1:9** Included among them were: 30 large gold dishes; 1,000 large silver dishes; 29 other dishes;<sup>①</sup> 30 gold bowls; 410 silver bowls; and 1,000 other articles.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>other dishes: </i>Or “knives.”  
**1:10**   
**1:11** Altogether, there were 5,400 gold and silver dishes, bowls, and other articles. Sheshbazzar took them with him when he and the others returned to Jerusalem from Babylonia.

## 3장

**3:1** During the seventh month<sup>①</sup> of the year, the Israelites who had settled in their towns went to Jerusalem.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>seventh month: </i>Tishri (also called Ethanim), the seventh month of the Hebrew calendar, from about mid-September to mid-October.  
**3:2**  The priest Joshua son of Jozadak, together with the other priests, and Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel and his relatives rebuilt the altar of Israel's God. Then they were able to offer sacrifices there by following the instructions God had given to Moses, the man of God.  
**3:3**  And they built the altar where it had stood before,<sup>①</sup> even though they were afraid of the people who were already living around there. Then every morning and evening they burned sacrifices and offerings to the LORD.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>where it had stood before: </i>One possible meaning for the difficult Hebrew text.  
**3:4**  The people followed the rules for celebrating the Festival of Shelters and offered the proper sacrifices each day.  
**3:5**  They offered sacrifices to please the LORD,<sup>①</sup> sacrifices at each New Moon Festival, and sacrifices at the rest of the LORD's festivals. Every offering the people had brought voluntarily was also presented to the LORD.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>sacrifices to please the LORD: </i>In traditional translations these sacrifices are usually called “whole burnt offerings” (see <a name='B:30 1:1-16'>Leviticus 1.1-16).  
**3:6** Although work on the temple itself had not yet begun, the people started offering sacrifices on the LORD's altar on the first day of the seventh month of that year.  
**3:7** King Cyrus of Persia had said the Israelites could have cedar trees brought from Lebanon to Joppa by sea. So they sent grain, wine, and olive oil to the cities of Tyre and Sidon as payment for these trees, and they gave money to the stoneworkers and carpenters.  
**3:8** During the second month<sup>①</sup> of the second year after the people had returned from Babylonia, they started rebuilding the LORD's temple. Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel, Joshua son of Jozadak, the priests, the Levites, and everyone else who had returned started working. Every Levite over 20 years of age was put in charge of some part of the work.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>second month: </i>Ziv, the second month of the Hebrew calendar, from about mid-April to mid-May.  
**3:9** The Levites in charge of the whole project were Joshua and his sons and relatives and Kadmiel and his sons from the family of Hodaviah.<sup>①</sup> The family of Henadad worked along with them.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Hodaviah: </i>Or “Yehudah” or “Hodiah.”  
**3:10**  When the builders had finished laying the foundation of the temple, the priests put on their robes and blew trumpets in honor of the LORD, while the Levites from the family of Asaph praised God with cymbals. All of them followed the instructions given years before by King David.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>King David: </i>Ruled from about 1010 to 970 B.C.  
**3:11**  They praised the LORD and gave thanks as they took turns singing:<br>“The LORD is good!<br>His faithful love for Israel<br>will last forever.” Everyone started shouting and praising the LORD because work on the foundation of the temple had begun.  
**3:12**  Many of the older priests and Levites and the heads of families wept bitterly because they remembered seeing the first temple years before. But others were so happy that they celebrated with joyful shouts.  
**3:13** Their shouting and crying were so noisy that it all sounded alike and could be heard a long way off.

**4:1** <sup>①</sup> The enemies of the tribes of Judah and Benjamin heard that the people had come back to rebuild the temple of the LORD God of Israel.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Foreigners: </i>People from foreign countries who had been captured by Assyrian and Babylonian kings and forced to settle in Palestine.  
**4:2**  So they went to Zerubbabel and to the family leaders and said, “Let us help! **4:3** But Zerubbabel, Joshua, and the family leaders answered, “You cannot take part in building a temple for the LORD our God! We will build it ourselves, just as King Cyrus of Persia commanded us.”

**4:22** Do this at once, so that no harm will come to the kingdom.

**6:1** King Darius ordered someone to go through the old records kept in Babylonia.

**6:6** King Darius sent this message: Governor Tattenai of Western Province and Shethar Bozenai, you and your advisors must stay away from the temple.  
**6:7** Let the Jewish governor and leaders rebuild it where it stood before. And stop slowing them down!  
**6:8** Starting at once, I am ordering you to help the leaders by paying their expenses from the tax money collected in Western Province.  
**6:9** And don't fail to let the priests in Jerusalem have whatever they need each day so they can offer sacrifices to the God of heaven. Give them young bulls, rams, sheep, as well as wheat, salt, wine, and olive oil.

**6:14**  With great success the Jewish leaders continued working on the temple, while Haggai and Zechariah encouraged them by their preaching. ## 7장

## 9장

**9:1** Later the Jewish leaders came to me<sup>①</sup> and said: Many Israelites, including priests and Levites, are living just like the people around them. They are even guilty of some of the horrible sins of the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Perizzites, the Jebusites, the Ammonites, the Moabites, the Egyptians, and the Amorites.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>me: </i>Ezra.  
**9:2** Some Israelite men have married foreign women and have let their sons do the same thing. Our own officials and leaders were the first to commit this disgusting sin, and now God's holy people are mixed with foreigners.  
**9:3** This news made me so angry that I ripped my clothes and tore hair from my head and beard. Then I just sat in shock  
**9:4** until the time for the evening sacrifice. Many of our people were greatly concerned and gathered around me, because the God of Israel had warned us to stay away from foreigners.  
**9:5** At the time of the evening sacrifice, I was still sitting there in sorrow with my clothes all torn. So I got down on my knees, then lifted my arms,  
**9:6** and prayed: I am much too ashamed to face you, LORD God. Our sins and our guilt have swept over us like a flood that reaches up to the heavens.  
**9:7** Since the time of our ancestors, all of us have sinned. That's why we, our kings, and our priests have often been defeated by other kings. They have killed some of us and made slaves of others; they have taken our possessions and made us ashamed, just as we are today.  
**9:8** But for now, LORD God, you have shown great kindness to us. You made us truly happy by letting some of us settle in this sacred place and by helping us in our time of slavery.  
**9:9** We are slaves, but you have never turned your back on us. You love us, and because of you, the kings of Persia have helped us. It's as though you have given us new life! You let us rebuild your temple and live safely in Judah and Jerusalem.  
**9:10** Our God, what can we say now? Even after all this, we have disobeyed the commands  
**9:11** that were given to us by your servants the prophets. They said the land you are giving us is full of sinful and wicked people, who never stop doing disgusting things.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>doing disgusting things: </i>Probably worshiping idols.  
**9:12**  And we were warned not to let our daughters and sons marry their sons and daughters. Your prophets also told us never to help those foreigners or even let them live in peace. You wanted us to become strong and to enjoy the good things in the land, then someday to leave it to our children forever.  
**9:13** You punished us because of our terrible sins. But you did not punish us nearly as much as we deserve, and you have brought some of us back home.  
**9:14** Why should we disobey your commands again by letting our sons and daughters marry these foreigners who do such disgusting things? That would make you angry enough to destroy us all!  
**9:15** LORD God of Israel, you have been more than fair by letting a few of us survive. But once again, our sins have made us ashamed to face you.
