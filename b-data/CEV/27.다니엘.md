# 27.다니엘 (CEV)



## 1장

**1:1**  In the third year that Jehoiakim was king of Judah,<sup>①</sup> King Nebuchadnezzar of Babylonia attacked Jerusalem.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Jehoiakim … king of Judah: </i>Ruled 609–598 B.C.  
**1:2**  The Lord let Nebuchadnezzar capture Jehoiakim and take away some of the things used in God's temple. And when the king returned to Babylonia,<sup>①</sup> he put these things in the temple of his own god.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Babylonia: </i>The Hebrew text has “Shinar,” another name for Babylonia.  
**1:3** One day the king ordered Ashpenaz, his highest palace official, to choose some young men from the royal family of Judah and from other leading Jewish families.  
**1:4** The king said, “They must be healthy, handsome, smart, wise, educated, and fit to serve in the royal palace. Teach them how to speak and write our language  
**1:5** and give them the same food and wine that I am served. Train them for three years, and then they can become court officials.”  
**1:6** Four of the young Jews chosen were Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah, all from the tribe of Judah.  
**1:7** But the king's chief official gave them Babylonian names: Daniel became Belteshazzar, Hananiah became Shadrach, Mishael became Meshach, and Azariah became Abednego.  
**1:8** Daniel made up his mind to eat and drink only what God had approved for his people to eat. And he asked the king's chief official for permission not to eat the food and wine served in the royal palace.  
**1:9** God had made the official friendly and kind to Daniel.  
**1:10** But the man still told him, “The king has decided what you must eat and drink. And I am afraid he will kill me, if you eat something else and end up looking worse than the other young men.”  
**1:11** The king's official had put a guard in charge of Daniel and his three friends. So Daniel said to the guard,  
**1:12** “For the next ten days, let us have only vegetables and water at mealtime.  
**1:13** When the ten days are up, compare how we look with the other young men, and decide what to do with us.”  
**1:14** The guard agreed to do what Daniel had asked.  
**1:15** Ten days later, Daniel and his friends looked healthier and better than the young men who had been served food from the royal palace.  
**1:16** After this, the guard let them eat vegetables instead of the rich food and wine.  
**1:17** God made the four young men smart and wise. They read a lot of books and became well educated. Daniel could also tell the meaning of dreams and visions.  
**1:18** At the end of the three-year period set by King Nebuchadnezzar, his chief palace official brought all the young men to him.  
**1:19** The king interviewed them and discovered that none of the others were as outstanding as Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah. So they were given positions in the royal court.  
**1:20** From then on, whenever the king asked for advice, he found their wisdom was ten times better than that of any of his other advisors and magicians.  
**1:21** Daniel served there until the first year of King Cyrus.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>first year of King Cyrus: </i>539 B.C.  

## 2장

**2:1** During the second year that Nebuchadnezzar was king, he had such horrible nightmares that he could not sleep.  
**2:2** So he called in his counselors, advisors, magicians, and wise men,  
**2:3** and said, “I am disturbed by a dream that I don't understand, and I want you to explain it.”  
**2:4** They answered in Aramaic,<sup>①</sup> “Your Majesty, we hope you live forever! We are your servants. Please tell us your dream, and we will explain what it means.”<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Aramaic: </i>Chapter <a name='B:340 2:4-7:28'>2.4-7.28 is written in Aramaic, a language closely related to Hebrew.  
**2:5** But the king replied, “No! I have made up my mind. If you don't tell me both the dream and its meaning, you will be chopped to pieces and your houses will be torn down.  
**2:6** However, if you do tell me both the dream and its meaning, you will be greatly rewarded and highly honored. Now tell me the dream and explain what it means.”  
**2:7** “Your Majesty,” they said, “if you will only tell us your dream, we will interpret it for you.”  
**2:8** The king replied, “You're just stalling for time,  
**2:9** because you know what's going to happen if you don't come up with the answer. You've decided to make up a bunch of lies, hoping I might change my mind. Now tell me the dream, and that will prove that you can interpret it.”  
**2:10** His advisors explained, “Your Majesty, you are demanding the impossible! No king, not even the most famous and powerful, has ever ordered his advisors, magicians, or wise men to do such a thing.  
**2:11** It can't be done, except by the gods, and they don't live here on earth.”  
**2:12** This made the king so angry that he gave orders for every wise man in Babylonia to be put to death, including Daniel and his three friends.  
**2:13**   
**2:14** Arioch was the king's official in charge of putting the wise men to death. He was on his way to have it done, when Daniel very wisely went to him  
**2:15** and asked, “Why did the king give such cruel<sup>①</sup> orders?” After Arioch explained what had happened,<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>cruel: </i>Or “urgent.”  
**2:16** Daniel rushed off and said to the king, “If you will just give me some time, I'll explain your dream.”  
**2:17** Daniel returned home and told his three friends.  
**2:18** Then he said, “Pray that the God who rules from heaven will be merciful and explain this mystery, so that we and the others won't be put to death.”  
**2:19** In a vision one night, Daniel was shown the dream and its meaning. Then he praised the God who rules from heaven:  
**2:20** “Our God, your name<br>will be praised<br>forever and forever.<br>You are all-powerful,<br>and you know everything.  
**2:21** You control human events—<br>you give rulers their power<br>and take it away,<br>and you are the source<br>of wisdom and knowledge.   
**2:22** “You explain deep mysteries,<br>because even the dark<br>is light to you.  
**2:23** You are the God<br>who was worshiped<br>by my ancestors.<br>Now I thank you and praise you<br>for making me wise<br>and telling me the king's dream,<br>together with its meaning.”  
**2:24** Daniel went back to Arioch, the official in charge of executing the wise men. Daniel said, “Don't kill those men! Take me to the king, and I will explain the meaning of his dream.”  
**2:25** Arioch rushed Daniel to the king and announced, “Your Majesty, I have found out that one of the men brought here from Judah can explain your dream.”  
**2:26** The king asked Daniel,<sup>①</sup> “Can you tell me my dream and what it means?”<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Daniel: </i>Aramaic “Daniel whose name was Belteshazzar” (see <a name='B:340 1:7'>1.7).  
**2:27** Daniel answered: Your Majesty, not even the smartest person in all the world can do what you are demanding.  
**2:28** But the God who rules from heaven can explain mysteries. And while you were sleeping, he showed you what will happen in the future.  
**2:29**   
**2:30** However, you must realize that these mysteries weren't explained to me because I am smarter than everyone else. Instead, it was done so that you would understand what you have seen.  
**2:31** Your Majesty, what you saw standing in front of you was a huge and terrifying statue, shining brightly.  
**2:32** Its head was made of gold, its chest and arms were silver, and from its waist down to its knees, it was bronze.  
**2:33** From there to its ankles it was iron, and its feet were a mixture of iron and clay.  
**2:34** As you watched, a stone was cut from a mountain—but not by human hands. The stone struck the feet, completely shattering the iron and clay.  
**2:35** Then the iron, the clay, the bronze, the silver, and the gold were crushed and blown away without a trace, like husks of wheat at threshing time. But the stone became a tremendous mountain that covered the entire earth.  
**2:36** That was the dream, and now I'll tell you what it means.  
**2:37** Your Majesty, you are the greatest of kings, and God has highly honored you with power  
**2:38** over all humans, animals, and birds. You are the head of gold.  
**2:39** After you are gone, another kingdom will rule, but it won't be as strong. Then it will be followed by a kingdom of bronze that will rule the whole world.  
**2:40** Next, a kingdom of iron will come to power, crushing and shattering everything.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>crushing … everything: </i>Three ancient translations; Aramaic adds “and like iron crushing.”  
**2:41** This fourth kingdom will be divided—it will be both strong and brittle, just as you saw that the feet and toes were a mixture of iron and clay.  
**2:42**   
**2:43** This kingdom will be the result of a marriage between kingdoms, but it will crumble, just as iron and clay don't stick together.  
**2:44** During the time of those kings, the God who rules from heaven will set up an eternal kingdom that will never fall. It will be like the stone that was cut from the mountain, but not by human hands—the stone that crushed the iron, bronze, clay, silver, and gold. Your Majesty, in your dream the great God has told you what is going to happen, and you can trust this interpretation.  
**2:45**   
**2:46** King Nebuchadnezzar bowed low to the ground and worshiped Daniel. Then he gave orders for incense to be burned and a sacrifice of grain to be offered in honor of Daniel.  
**2:47** The king said, “Now I know that your God is above all other gods and kings, because he gave you the power to explain this mystery.”  
**2:48** The king then presented Daniel with a lot of gifts; he promoted him to governor of Babylon Province and put him in charge of the other wise men.  
**2:49** At Daniel's request, the king appointed Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego to high positions in Babylon Province, and he let Daniel stay on as a palace official.  

## 3장

**3:1**  King Nebuchadnezzar ordered a gold statue to be built 27 meters high and nearly 3 meters wide. He had it set up in Dura Valley near the city of Babylon,  
**3:2** and he commanded his governors, advisors, treasurers, judges, and his other officials to come from everywhere in his kingdom to the dedication of the statue.  
**3:3** So all of them came and stood in front of it.  
**3:4** Then an official stood up and announced: People of every nation and race, now listen to the king's command!  
**3:5** Trumpets, flutes, harps, and all other kinds of musical instruments will soon start playing. When you hear the music, you must bow down and worship the statue that King Nebuchadnezzar has set up.  
**3:6** Anyone who refuses will at once be thrown into a flaming furnace.  
**3:7** As soon as the people heard the music, they bowed down and worshiped the gold statue that the king had set up.  
**3:8** Some Babylonians used this as a chance to accuse the Jews to King Nebuchadnezzar.  
**3:9** They said, “Your Majesty, we hope you live forever!  
**3:10** You commanded everyone to bow down and worship the gold statue when the music played.  
**3:11** And you said that anyone who did not bow down and worship it would be thrown into a flaming furnace.  
**3:12** Sir, you appointed three men to high positions in Babylon Province, but they have disobeyed you. Those Jews, Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, refuse to worship your gods and the statue you have set up.”  
**3:13** King Nebuchadnezzar was furious. So he sent for the three young men and said,  
**3:14** “I hear that you refuse to worship my gods and the gold statue I have set up.  
**3:15** Now I am going to give you one more chance. If you bow down and worship the statue when you hear the music, everything will be all right. But if you don't, you will at once be thrown into a flaming furnace. No god can save you from me.”  
**3:16** The three men replied, “Your Majesty, we don't need to defend ourselves.  
**3:17** The God we worship can save us from you and your flaming furnace.  
**3:18** But even if he doesn't, we still won't worship your gods and the gold statue you have set up.”  
**3:19** Nebuchadnezzar's face twisted with anger at the three men. And he ordered the furnace to be heated seven times hotter than usual.  
**3:20** Next, he commanded some of his strongest soldiers to tie up the men and throw them into the flaming furnace.  
**3:21**  The king wanted it done at that very moment. So the soldiers tied up Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego and threw them into the flaming furnace with all of their clothes still on, including their turbans. The fire was so hot that flames leaped out and killed the soldiers.  
**3:22**   
**3:23**   
**3:24** Suddenly the king jumped up and shouted, “Weren't only three men tied up and thrown into the fire?” “Yes, Your Majesty,” his officers answered.  
**3:25** “But I see four men walking around in the fire,” the king replied. “None of them is tied up or harmed, and the fourth one looks like a god.”<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>a god: </i>Aramaic “a son of the gods” or “a son of God.”  
**3:26** Nebuchadnezzar went closer to the flaming furnace and said to the three young men, “You servants of the Most High God, come out at once!” They came out,  
**3:27** and the king's high officials, governors, and advisors all crowded around them. The men were not burned, their hair wasn't scorched, and their clothes didn't even smell like smoke.  
**3:28** King Nebuchadnezzar said: Praise their God for sending an angel to rescue his servants! They trusted their God and refused to obey my commands. Yes, they chose to die rather than to worship or serve any god except their own.  
**3:29** And I won't allow people of any nation or race to say anything against their God. Anyone who does will be chopped up and their houses will be torn down, because no other god has such great power to save.  
**3:30** After this happened, the king appointed Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego to even higher positions in Babylon Province.

**4:2** I am glad to tell about<br>the wonderful miracles<br>God Most High<br>has done for me.

**4:5** when suddenly I had some horrifying dreams and visions.

**4:7** After they arrived, I told them my dream, but they were not able to say what it meant.

## 5장

**5:1** One evening, King Belshazzar gave a great banquet for a thousand of his highest officials, and he drank wine with them.  
**5:2** He got drunk and ordered his servants to bring in the gold and silver cups his father Nebuchadnezzar<sup>①</sup> had taken from the temple in Jerusalem. Belshazzar wanted the cups, so that he and all his wives and officials could drink from them.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>his father Nebuchadnezzar: </i>Belshazzar was actually the son of King Nabonidus, who was from another family. But in ancient times, it was possible to refer to a previous king as the “father” of the present king.  
**5:3**  When the gold cups were brought in, everyone at the banquet drank from them and praised their idols made of gold, silver, bronze, iron, wood, and stone.  
**5:4**   
**5:5** Suddenly a human hand was seen writing on the plaster wall of the palace. The hand was just behind the lampstand, and the king could see it writing.  
**5:6** He was so frightened that his face turned pale, his knees started shaking, and his legs became weak.  
**5:7** The king called in his advisors, who claimed they could talk with the spirits of the dead and understand the meanings found in the stars. He told them, “The man who can read this writing and tell me what it means will become the third most powerful man in my kingdom. He will wear robes of royal purple and a gold chain around his neck.”  
**5:8** All of King Belshazzar's highest officials came in, but not one of them could read the writing or tell what it meant,  
**5:9** and they were completely puzzled. Now the king was more afraid than ever before, and his face turned white as a ghost.  
**5:10** When the queen heard the king and his officials talking, she came in and said: Your Majesty, I hope you live forever! Don't be afraid or look so pale.  
**5:11** In your kingdom there is a man who has been given special powers by the holy gods. When your father Nebuchadnezzar was king, this man was known to be as smart, intelligent, and wise as the gods themselves. Your father put him in charge of all who claimed they could talk with the spirits or understand the meanings in the stars or tell about the future.  
**5:12** He also changed the man's name from Daniel to Belteshazzar. Not only is he wise and intelligent, but he can explain dreams and riddles and solve difficult problems. Send for Daniel, and he will tell you what the writing means.  
**5:13** When Daniel was brought in, the king said: So you are Daniel, one of the captives my father brought back from Judah!  
**5:14** I was told that the gods have given you special powers and that you are intelligent and very wise.  
**5:15** Neither my advisors nor the men who talk with the spirits of the dead could read this writing or tell me what it means.  
**5:16** But I have been told that you understand everything and that you can solve difficult problems. Now then, if you can read this writing and tell me what it means, you will become the third most powerful man in my kingdom. You will wear royal purple robes and have a gold chain around your neck.  
**5:17** Daniel answered: Your Majesty, I will read the writing and tell you what it means. But you may keep your gifts or give them to someone else.  
**5:18** Sir, the Most High God made your father a great and powerful man and brought him much honor and glory.  
**5:19** God did such great things for him that people of all nations and races shook with fear. Your father had the power of life or death over everyone, and he could honor or ruin anyone he chose.  
**5:20** But when he became proud and stubborn, his glorious kingdom was taken from him.  
**5:21** His mind became like that of an animal, and he was forced to stay away from people and live with wild donkeys. Your father ate grass like an ox, and he slept outside where his body was soaked with dew. He was forced to do this until he learned that the Most High God rules all kingdoms on earth and chooses their kings.  
**5:22** King Belshazzar, you knew all of this, but you still refused to honor the Lord who rules from heaven.  
**5:23** Instead, you turned against him and ordered the cups from his temple to be brought here, so that you and your wives and officials could drink wine from them. You praised idols made of silver, gold, bronze, iron, wood, and stone, even though they cannot see or hear or think. You refused to worship the God who gives you breath and controls everything you do.  
**5:24** That's why he sent the hand to write this message on the wall.  
**5:25** The words written there are <i>mene</i>, which means “numbered,” <i>tekel</i>, which means “weighed,” and <i>parsin</i>,<sup>①</sup> which means “divided.” God has numbered the days of your kingdom and has brought it to an end. He has weighed you on his balance scales, and you fall short of what it takes to be king. So God has divided your kingdom between the Medes and the Persians.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>mene … tekel … parsin: </i>In the Aramaic text of verse <a name='B:340 5:25'>25, the words “mene, tekel, parsin,” are used, and in verses 26-28 the words “mene, tekel, peres” (the singular of “parsin”) are used. “Parsin” means “divided,” but “peres” can mean either “divided” or “Persia.”  
**5:26**   
**5:27**   
**5:28**   
**5:29** Belshazzar gave a command for Daniel to be made the third most powerful man in his kingdom and to be given a purple robe and a gold chain.  
**5:30** That same night, the king was killed.  
**5:31** Then Darius the Mede, who was 62 years old, took over his kingdom.  

## 6장

**6:1** Darius divided his kingdom into 120 states and placed a governor in charge of each one.  
**6:2** In order to make sure that his government was run properly, Darius put three other officials in charge of the governors. One of these officials was Daniel.  
**6:3** And he did his work so much better than the other governors and officials that the king decided to let him govern the whole kingdom.  
**6:4** The other men tried to find something wrong with the way Daniel did his work for the king. But they could not accuse him of anything wrong, because he was honest and faithful and did everything he was supposed to do.  
**6:5** Finally, they said to one another, “We will never be able to bring any charge against Daniel, unless it has to do with his religion.”  
**6:6** They all went to the king and said: “Your Majesty, we hope you live forever!  
**6:7**  All of your officials, leaders, advisors, and governors agree that you should make a law forbidding anyone to pray to any god or human except you for the next 30 days. Everyone who disobeys this law must be thrown into a pit of lions.  
**6:8** Order this to be written and then sign it, so it cannot be changed, just as no written law of the Medes and Persians can be changed.”  
**6:9** So King Darius made the law and had it written down.  
**6:10** Daniel heard about the law, but when he returned home, he went upstairs and prayed in front of the window that faced Jerusalem. In the same way that he had always done, he knelt down in prayer three times a day, giving thanks to God.  
**6:11** The men who had spoken to the king watched Daniel and saw him praying to his God for help.  
**6:12** They went back to the king and said, “Didn't you make a law that forbids anyone to pray to any god or human except you for the next 30 days? **11:21** The successor of this king of the north will be a worthless nobody, who doesn't even come from a royal family. He will suddenly appear and gain control of the kingdom by treachery.  
**11:22** Then he will destroy armies and remove God's chosen high priest.  
**11:23** He will make a treaty, but he will be deceitful and break it, even though he has only a few followers.  
**11:24** Without warning, he will successfully invade a wealthy province, which is something his ancestors never did. Then he will divide among his followers all of its treasures and property. But none of this will last very long.  
**11:25** He will gather a large and powerful army, and with great courage he will attack the king of the south. The king of the south will meet him with a much stronger army, but he will lose the battle, because he will be betrayed  
**11:26** by members of the royal court. He will be ruined, and most of his army will be slaughtered.  
**11:27** The two kings will meet around a table and tell evil lies to each other. But their plans will fail, because God has already decided what will happen.  
**11:28** Then the king of the north will return to his country with great treasures. But on the way, he will attack the religion of God's people and do whatever else he pleases.  
**11:29** At the time God has decided, the king of the north will invade the southern kingdom again, but this time, things will be different.  
**11:30**  Ships from the west will come to attack him, and he will be discouraged. Then he will start back to his own country and take out his anger on the religion of God's faithful people, while showing kindness to those who are unfaithful.  
**11:31**  He will send troops to pollute the temple and the fortress, and he will stop the daily sacrifices. Then he will set up that “Horrible Thing” that causes destruction.  
**11:32** The king will use deceit to win followers from those who are unfaithful to God, but those who remain faithful will do everything possible to oppose him.  
**11:33** Wise leaders will instruct many of the people. But for a while, some of these leaders will either be killed with swords or burned alive, or else robbed of their possessions and thrown into prison.  
**11:34** They will receive only a little help in their time of trouble, and many of their followers will be treacherous.  
**11:35** Some of those who are wise will suffer, so that God will make them pure and acceptable until the end, which will still come at the time he has decided.  
**11:36**  This king will do as he pleases. He will proudly claim to be greater than any god and will insult the only true God. Indeed, he will be successful until God is no longer angry with his people.  
**11:37** This king will reject the gods his ancestors worshiped and the god preferred by women.<sup>①</sup> In fact, he will put himself above all gods<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>god preferred by women: </i>Perhaps Tammuz or Adonis, which were popular among the women of that time.  
**11:38** and worship only the so-called god of fortresses, who was unknown to his ancestors. And he will honor it with gold, silver, precious stones, and other costly gifts.  
**11:39** With the help of this foreign god, he will capture the strongest fortresses. Everyone who worships this god will be put in a position of power and rewarded with wealth and land.  
**11:40** At the time of the end, the king of the south will attack the kingdom of the north. But its king will rush out like a storm with war chariots, cavalry, and many ships. Indeed, his forces will flood one country after another,  
**11:41** and when they reach the holy land,<sup>①</sup> tens of thousands will be killed. But the countries of Edom and Moab and the ruler of Ammon<sup>②</sup> will escape.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>the holy land: </i>See the <a name='C340 8:9'>note at <a name='B:340 8:9'>8.9. <sup>②</sup><i>the ruler of Ammon: </i>Or “what is left of Ammon.”  
**11:42** The king of the north will invade many countries, including Egypt,  
**11:43** and he will take its rich treasures of gold and silver. He will also conquer Libya and Ethiopia.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Ethiopia: </i>The Hebrew text has “Cush,” which was a region south of Egypt that included parts of the present countries of Ethiopia and Sudan.  
**11:44** But he will be alarmed by news from the east and the north, and he will become furious and cause great destruction.  
**11:45** After this, he will set up camp between the Mediterranean Sea and Mount Zion. Then he will be destroyed, and no one will be able to save him.

**12:3**  Everyone who has been wise will shine as bright as the sky above, and everyone who has led others to please God will shine forever like the stars.  
**12:4**  Daniel, I now command you to keep the message of this book secret until the end of time, even though many people will go everywhere, searching for the knowledge to be found in it.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>even though … in it: </i>One possible meaning for the difficult Hebrew text.
