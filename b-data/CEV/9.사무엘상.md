# 9.사무엘상 (CEV)



## 1장

**1:1** Elkanah lived in Ramah,<sup>①</sup> a town in the hill country of Ephraim. His great-great-grandfather was Zuph, so Elkanah was a member of the Zuph clan of the Ephraim tribe. Elkanah's father was Jeroham, his grandfather was Elihu, and his great-grandfather was Tohu.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Ramah: </i>The Hebrew has “Ramathaim,” a longer form of “Ramah” (see verse 19).  
**1:2** Elkanah had two wives,<sup>①</sup> Hannah and Peninnah. Although Peninnah had children, Hannah did not have any.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>two wives: </i>Having more than one wife was allowed in those times.  
**1:3** Once a year Elkanah traveled from his hometown to Shiloh, where he worshiped the LORD All-Powerful and offered sacrifices. Eli was the LORD's priest there, and his two sons Hophni and Phinehas served with him as priests.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Eli … priests: </i>One ancient translation; Hebrew “Hophni and Phinehas, the two sons of Eli, served the LORD as priests.”  
**1:4** Whenever Elkanah offered a sacrifice, he gave some of the meat<sup>①</sup> to Peninnah and some to each of her sons and daughters.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>meat: </i>For some sacrifices, like this one, only part of the meat was burned. Some was given to the priest, and the rest was eaten by the family and guests of the worshiper (see <a name='B:30 3:1-17'>Leviticus 3.1-17; <a name='B:30 7:11-18'>7.11-18).  
**1:5** But he gave Hannah even more, because<sup>①</sup> he loved Hannah very much, even though the LORD had kept her from having children of her own.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>even more, because: </i>One ancient translation; Hebrew “only one; he.”  
**1:6** Peninnah liked to make Hannah feel miserable about not having any children,  
**1:7** especially when the family went to the house of the LORD<sup>①</sup> each year. One day, Elkanah was there offering a sacrifice, when Hannah began crying and refused to eat.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>house of the LORD: </i>Another name for the place of worship at Shiloh, which still may have been the sacred tent at this time.  
**1:8** So Elkanah asked, “Hannah, why are you crying? Why won't you eat? Why do you feel so bad? Don't I mean more to you than ten sons?”  
**1:9** When the sacrifice had been offered, and they had eaten the meal, Hannah got up and went to pray. Eli was sitting in his chair near the door to the place of worship.  
**1:10** Hannah was heartbroken and was crying as she prayed,  
**1:11**  “LORD All-Powerful, I am your servant, but I am so miserable! Please let me have a son. I promise to give him to you for as long as he lives, and his hair will never be cut.”<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>his hair … cut: </i>Never cutting the child's hair would be a sign that he would belong to the LORD (see <a name='B:40 6:1'>Numbers 6.1,<a name='B:40 6:21'>21, especially verse <a name='B:90 1:5'>5).  
**1:12** Hannah prayed silently to the LORD for a long time. But her lips were moving, and Eli thought she was drunk.  
**1:13**   
**1:14** “How long are you going to stay drunk?” he asked. “Sober up!”  
**1:15** “Sir, please don't think I'm no good!” Hannah answered. “I'm not drunk, and I haven't been drinking. But I do feel miserable and terribly upset. I've been praying all this time, telling the LORD about my problems.”  
**1:16**   
**1:17** Eli replied, “Go home. Everything will be fine. The God of Israel will answer your prayer.”  
**1:18** “Sir, thank you for being so kind to me,” Hannah said. Then she left, and after eating something, she felt much better.  
**1:19** Elkanah and his family got up early the next morning and worshiped the LORD. Then they went back home to Ramah. Later the LORD blessed Elkanah and Hannah  
**1:20** with a son. She named him Samuel because she had asked the LORD for him.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>him: </i>In Hebrew “Samuel” sounds something like “Someone from God” or “The name of God” or “His name is God.”  
**1:21** The next time Elkanah and his family went to offer their yearly sacrifice, he took along a gift that he had promised to give to the LORD.  
**1:22** But Hannah stayed home, because she had told Elkanah, “Samuel and I won't go until he's old enough for me to stop nursing him. Then I'll give him to the LORD, and he can stay there at Shiloh for the rest of his life.”  
**1:23** “You know what's best,” Elkanah said. “Stay here until it's time to stop nursing him. I'm sure the LORD will help you do what you have promised.”<sup>①</sup> Hannah did not go to Shiloh until she stopped nursing Samuel.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>the LORD … promised: </i>The Dead Sea Scrolls and two ancient translations; the Standard Hebrew Text “the LORD will do what he said.”  
**1:24** When it was the time of year to go to Shiloh again, Hannah and Elkanah<sup>①</sup> took Samuel to the LORD's house. They brought along a three-year-old bull,<sup>②</sup> a sack containing about nine kilograms of flour, and a clay jar full of wine. Hannah and Elkanah offered the bull as a sacrifice, then brought the little boy to Eli.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>When it was the time of year to go to Shiloh again, Hannah and Elkanah: </i>The Dead Sea Scrolls and one ancient translation; the Standard Hebrew Text “she.” <sup>②</sup><i>a three-year-old bull: </i>The Dead Sea Scrolls and two ancient translations; the Standard Hebrew Text “three bulls.”  
**1:25**   
**1:26** “Sir,” Hannah said, “a few years ago I stood here beside you and asked the LORD  
**1:27** to give me a child. Here he is! The LORD gave me just what I asked for.  
**1:28** Now I am giving him to the LORD, and he will be the LORD's servant for as long as he lives.” Elkanah<sup>①</sup> worshiped the LORD there at Shiloh, and<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Elkanah: </i>Or “They” or “Samuel.”  

## 2장

**2:5** People who once<br>had plenty to eat<br>must now hire themselves out<br>for only a piece of bread.<br>But you give the hungry more<br>than enough to eat.<br>A woman did not have a child,<br>and you gave her seven,<br>but a woman who had many<br>was left with none.

**2:11** Elkanah and Hannah went back home to Ramah, but the boy Samuel stayed to help Eli serve the LORD.  
**2:12** Eli's sons were priests, but they were dishonest and refused to obey the LORD. So, while people were boiling the meat from their sacrifices, these priests would send over a servant with a large, three-pronged fork.  
**2:13**   
**2:14** The servant would stick the fork into the cooking pot, and whatever meat came out on the fork was taken back to Eli's two sons. That was how they treated every Israelite who came to offer sacrifices in Shiloh.  
**2:15** Sometimes, when people were offering sacrifices, the servant would come over, even before the fat had been cut off and sacrificed to the LORD.<sup>①</sup> Then the servant would tell them, “The priest doesn't want his meat boiled! Give him some raw meat that he can roast!”<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>sacrificed to the LORD: </i>The fat belonged to the LORD and was supposed to be burned as a sacrifice before the rest of the animal was cooked and eaten (see <a name='B:30 3:3'>Leviticus 3.3,<a name='B:30 3:4'>4,<a name='B:30 3:9'>9,<a name='B:30 3:10'>10,<a name='B:30 3:14'>14,<a name='B:30 3:15'>15).  
**2:16** Usually the people answered, “Take what you want. But first, let us sacrifice the fat to the LORD.” “No,” the servant would reply. “If you don't give it to me now, I'll take it by force.”  
**2:17** Eli's sons did not show any respect for the sacrifices that the people offered. This was a terrible sin, and it made the LORD very angry.  
**2:18** The boy Samuel served the LORD and wore a special linen garment<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>a special linen garment: </i>Either a loin cloth or a jacket or a vest worn only by priests.  
**2:19** and the clothes<sup>①</sup> his mother made for him. She brought new clothes every year, when she and her husband came to offer sacrifices at Shiloh.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>clothes: </i>The Hebrew word means a sleeveless coat or robe that was worn by priests. Samuel was a small child, but his mother made him clothes just like those worn by priests.  
**2:20** Eli always blessed Elkanah and his wife and said, “Samuel was born in answer to your prayers. Now you have given him to the LORD. I pray that the LORD will bless you with more children to take his place.” After Eli had blessed them, Elkanah and Hannah would return home.  
**2:21** The LORD was kind to Hannah, and she had three more sons and two daughters. But Samuel grew up at the LORD's house in Shiloh.  
**2:22** Eli was now very old, and he heard what his sons were doing to the people of Israel.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Israel: </i>The Dead Sea Scrolls and one ancient translation; the Standard Hebrew Text adds “He heard that his sons were even sleeping with the women who worked at the entrance to the sacred tent.”  
**2:23** “Why are you doing these awful things?” he asked them. “I've been hearing nothing but complaints about you from all of the LORD's people.  
**2:24**   
**2:25** If you harm another person, God can help make things right between the two of you. But if you commit a crime against the LORD, no one can help you!” But the LORD had already decided to kill them. So he kept them from listening to their father.  
**2:26**  Each day, as Samuel grew older, the LORD was pleased with him, and so were the people.  
**2:27** One day a prophet came to Eli and gave him this message from the LORD: When your ancestors were slaves of the king of Egypt, I came and showed them who I am.  
**2:28**  Out of all the tribes of Israel, I chose your family to be my priests. I wanted them to offer sacrifices and burn incense to me and to find out from me what I want my people to do. I commanded everyone to bring their sacrifices here where I live, and I allowed you and your family to keep those that were not offered to me on the altar. But you honor your sons instead of me! You don't respect<sup>①</sup> the sacrifices and offerings that are brought to me, and you've all grown fat from eating the best parts.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>don't respect: </i>The Standard Hebrew Text; the Dead Sea Scrolls and one ancient translation “are greedy for.”  
**2:29**   
**2:30** I am the LORD, the God of Israel. I promised to always let your family serve me as priests, but now I tell you that I cannot do this any longer! I honor anyone who honors me, but I put a curse on anyone who hates me.  
**2:31** The time will come when I will kill you and everyone else in your family. Not one of you will live to an old age.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Not one … to Israel: </i>The Standard Hebrew Text; the Dead Sea Scrolls and one ancient translation do not have these words.  
**2:32** Your family<sup>①</sup> will have a lot of trouble. I will be kind to Israel,<sup>②</sup> but everyone in your family will die young.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Your family: </i>Or “My house of worship.”  
**2:33** If I let anyone from your family be a priest, his<sup>①</sup> life will be full of sadness and sorrow. But most of the men in your family will die a violent death!<sup>②</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>his: </i>The Dead Sea Scrolls and one ancient translation; the Standard Hebrew Text “your.” <sup>②</sup><i>die a violent death: </i>The Dead Sea Scrolls and one ancient translation; the Standard Hebrew Text “die.”  
**2:34**  To prove to you that I will do these things, your two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, will die on the same day.  
**2:35** I have chosen someone else to be my priest, someone who will be faithful and obey me. I will always let his family serve as priests and help my chosen king.  
**2:36** But if anyone is left from your family, he will come to my priest and beg for money or a little bread. He may even say to my priest, “Please let me be a priest, so I will at least have something to eat.”  

## 3장

**3:1** Samuel served the LORD by helping Eli the priest, who was by that time almost blind. In those days, the LORD hardly ever spoke directly to people, and he did not appear to them in dreams very often. But one night, Eli was asleep in his room,  
**3:2**   
**3:3** and Samuel was sleeping on a mat near the sacred chest in the LORD's house. They had not been asleep very long<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>They … long: </i>The Hebrew text has “The lamp of God was still burning.” An olive oil lamp would go out after a few hours if the wick was not adjusted.  
**3:4** when the LORD called out Samuel's name. “Here I am!” Samuel answered.  
**3:5** Then he ran to Eli and said, “Here I am. What do you want?” “I didn't call you,” Eli answered. “Go back to bed.” Samuel went back.  
**3:6** Again the LORD called out Samuel's name. Samuel got up and went to Eli. “Here I am,” he said. “What do you want?” Eli told him, “Son, I didn't call you. Go back to sleep.”  
**3:7** The LORD had not spoken to Samuel before, and Samuel did not recognize the voice.  
**3:8** When the LORD called out his name for the third time, Samuel went to Eli again and said, “Here I am. What do you want?” Eli finally realized that it was the LORD who was speaking to Samuel.  
**3:9** So he said, “Go back and lie down! If someone speaks to you again, answer, ‘I'm listening, LORD. What do you want me to do?’ ” Once again Samuel went back and lay down.  
**3:10** The LORD then stood beside Samuel and called out as he had done before, “Samuel! Samuel!” “I'm listening,” Samuel answered. “What do you want me to do?”  
**3:11** The LORD said: Samuel, I am going to do something in Israel that will shock everyone who hears about it!  
**3:12** I will punish Eli and his family, just as I promised.  
**3:13** He knew that his sons refused to respect me,<sup>①</sup> and he let them get away with it, even though I said I would punish his family forever.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>refused … me: </i>Or “were insulting everyone.”  
**3:14** I warned Eli that sacrifices or offerings could never make things right! His family has done too many disgusting things.  
**3:15** The next morning, Samuel got up and opened the doors to the LORD's house. He was afraid to tell Eli what the LORD had said.  
**3:16** But Eli told him, “Samuel, my boy, come here!” “Yes, sir!” Samuel answered.  
**3:17** Eli said, “What did God say to you? Tell me everything. I'll ask God to punish you terribly if you don't tell me every word he said!”  
**3:18** Samuel told Eli everything. Then Eli said, “He is the LORD, and he will do what's right.”  
**3:19** As Samuel grew up, the LORD helped him and made everything Samuel said come true.  
**3:20** From the town of Dan in the north to the town of Beersheba in the south, everyone in the country knew that Samuel was truly the LORD's prophet.  
**3:21** The LORD often appeared to Samuel at Shiloh and told him what to say.  

## 4장

**4:1** Then Samuel would speak to the whole nation of Israel. One day the Israelites went out to fight the Philistines. They set up camp near Ebenezer, and the Philistines camped at Aphek.  
**4:2** The Philistines made a fierce attack. They defeated the Israelites and killed about 4,000 of them.  
**4:3** The Israelite army returned to their camp, and the leaders said, “Why did the LORD let us lose to the Philistines today? Let's get the sacred chest where the LORD's agreement with Israel is kept. Then the LORD<sup>①</sup> will help us and rescue us from our enemies.”<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>LORD: </i>Or “chest.”  
**4:4**  The army sent some soldiers to bring back the sacred chest from Shiloh, because the LORD All-Powerful has his throne on the winged creatures on top of the chest. As Eli's two sons, Hophni and Phinehas,  
**4:5** brought the chest into camp, the army cheered so loudly that the ground shook.  
**4:6** The Philistines heard the noise and said, “What are those Hebrews shouting about?” When the Philistines learned that the sacred chest had been brought into the camp,  
**4:7** they were scared to death and said: The gods have come into their camp. Now we're in real trouble! Nothing like this has ever happened to us before.  
**4:8** We're in big trouble! Who can save us from these powerful gods? They're the same gods who made all those horrible things happen to the Egyptians in the desert.  
**4:9** Philistines, be brave and fight hard! If you don't, those Hebrews will rule us, just as we've been ruling them. Fight and don't be afraid.  
**4:10** The Philistines did fight. They killed 30,000 Israelite soldiers, and all the rest ran off to their homes.  
**4:11** Hophni and Phinehas were killed, and the sacred chest was captured.  
**4:12** That same day a soldier from the tribe of Benjamin ran from the battlefront to Shiloh. He had torn his clothes and put dirt on his head to show his sorrow.  
**4:13** He went into town and told the news about the battle, and everyone started crying. Eli was afraid that something might happen to the sacred chest. So he was sitting on his chair beside the road, just waiting.  
**4:14** He was 98 years old and blind, but he could hear everyone crying, and he asked, “What's all that noise?” The soldier hurried over and told Eli,  
**4:15**   
**4:16** “I escaped from the fighting today and ran here.” “Young man, what happened?” Eli asked.  
**4:17** “Israel ran away from the Philistines,” the soldier answered. “Many of our people were killed, including your two sons, Hophni and Phinehas. But worst of all, the sacred chest was captured.”  
**4:18** Eli was still sitting on a chair beside the wall of the town gate. And when the man said that the Philistines had taken the sacred chest, Eli fell backwards. He was a very heavy old man, and the fall broke his neck and killed him. He had been a leader<sup>①</sup> of Israel for 40 years.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>leader: </i>The Hebrew word means that Eli may have been an army commander, a judge, and a priest.  
**4:19** The wife of Phinehas was about to give birth. And soon after she heard that the sacred chest had been captured and that her husband and his father had died, her baby came. The birth was very hard,  
**4:20** and she was dying. But the women taking care of her said, “Don't be afraid—it's a boy!” She didn't pay any attention to them.  
**4:21** Instead she kept thinking about losing her husband and her father-in-law. So she said, “My son will be named Ichabod,<sup>①</sup> because the glory of Israel left our country when the sacred chest was captured.”<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Ichabod: </i>Ichabod means “where is the glory?” or “there is no glory.”  

## 5장

**5:1** The Philistines took the sacred chest from near Ebenezer to the town of Ashdod.  
**5:2** They brought it into the temple of their god Dagon and put it next to the statue of Dagon, which they worshiped.  
**5:3** When the people of Ashdod got up early the next morning, they found the statue lying facedown on the floor in front of the sacred chest. They put the statue back where it belonged.  
**5:4** But early the next morning, it had fallen over again and was lying facedown on the floor in front of the chest. The body of the statue was still in one piece, but its head and both hands had broken off and were lying on the stone floor in the doorway.  
**5:5** This is why the priests and everyone else step over that part of the doorway when they enter the temple of Dagon in Ashdod.  
**5:6** The LORD caused a lot of trouble for the people of Ashdod and their neighbors. He made sores break out all over their bodies,<sup>①</sup> and everyone was in a panic.<sup>②</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>sores … bodies: </i>Or “He struck them with bubonic plague.” <sup>②</sup><i>panic: </i>Two ancient translations add “Rats came from their ships, and people were dying right and left.”  
**5:7** Finally, they said, “The God of Israel did this. He is the one who caused all this trouble for us and our god Dagon. We've got to get rid of this chest.”  
**5:8** The people of Ashdod invited all the Philistine rulers to come to Ashdod, and they asked them, “What can we do with the sacred chest that belongs to the God of Israel?” “Send it to Gath,” the rulers answered. But after they took it there,  
**5:9** the LORD made sores break out on everyone in town. The people of Gath were frightened,  
**5:10** so they sent the sacred chest to Ekron. But before they could take it through the town gates, the people of Ekron started screaming, “They've brought the sacred chest that belongs to the God of Israel! It will kill us and our families too!”  
**5:11** The people of Ekron called for another meeting of the Philistine rulers and told them, “Send this chest back where it belongs. Then it won't kill us.” Everyone was in a panic, because God was causing a lot of people to die,  
**5:12** and those who had survived were suffering from the sores. They all cried to their gods for help.  

## 6장

**6:1** After the sacred chest had been in Philistia for seven months,<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>months: </i>One ancient translation adds “and rats were everywhere” or “and rats ate the crops.”  
**6:2** the Philistines called in their priests and fortunetellers, and asked, “What should we do with this sacred chest? Tell us how to send it back where it belongs!”  
**6:3** “Don't send it back without a gift,” the priests and fortunetellers answered. “Send along something to Israel's God to make up for taking the chest in the first place. Then you will be healed, and you will find out why the LORD was causing you so much trouble.”  
**6:4** “What should we send?” the Philistines asked. The priests and fortunetellers answered: There are five Philistine rulers, and they all have the same disease that you have.  
**6:5** So make five gold models of the sores and five gold models of the rats that are wiping out your crops. If you honor the God of Israel with this gift, maybe he will stop causing trouble for you and your gods and your crops.  
**6:6** Don't be like the Egyptians and their king. They were stubborn, but when Israel's God was finished with them, they had to let Israel go.  
**6:7** Get a new cart and two cows that have young calves and that have never pulled a cart. Hitch the cows to the cart, but take the calves back to their barn.  
**6:8** Then put the chest on the cart. Put the gold rats and sores into a bag and put it on the cart next to the chest. Then send it on its way.  
**6:9** Watch to see if the chest goes on up the road to the Israelite town of Beth-Shemesh. If it goes back to its own country, you will know that it was the LORD who made us suffer so badly. But if the chest doesn't go back to its own country, then the LORD had nothing to do with the disease that hit us—it was simply bad luck.  
**6:10** The Philistines followed their advice. They hitched up the two cows to the cart, but they kept their calves in a barn.  
**6:11** Then they put the chest on the cart, along with the bag that had the gold rats and sores in it.  
**6:12** The cows went straight up the road toward Beth-Shemesh, mooing as they went. The Philistine rulers followed them until they got close to Beth-Shemesh.  
**6:13** The people of Beth-Shemesh were harvesting their wheat<sup>①</sup> in the valley. When they looked up and saw the chest, they were so happy that they stopped working and started celebrating.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>wheat: </i>The wheat harvest took place in May and June.  
**6:14** The cows left the road and pulled the cart into a field that belonged to Joshua from Beth-Shemesh, and they stopped beside a huge rock. Some men from the tribe of Levi were there. So they took the chest off the cart and placed it on the rock, and then they did the same thing with the bag of gold rats and sores. A few other people chopped up the cart and made a fire. They killed the cows and burned them as sacrifices to the LORD. After that, they offered more sacrifices.  
**6:15**   
**6:16** When the five rulers of the Philistines saw what had happened, they went back to Ekron that same day.  
**6:17** That is how the Philistines sent gifts to the LORD to make up for taking the sacred chest. They sent five gold sores, one each for their towns of Ashdod, Gaza, Ashkelon, Gath, and Ekron.  
**6:18** They also sent one gold rat for each walled town and for every village that the five Philistine rulers controlled. The huge stone<sup>①</sup> where the Levites set the chest is still there in Joshua's field as a reminder of what happened.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>stone: </i>A few Hebrew manuscripts; most Hebrew manuscripts “meadow” or “stream.”  
**6:19** Some of the men of Beth-Shemesh looked inside the sacred chest, and the LORD God killed 70<sup>①</sup> of them. This made the people of Beth-Shemesh very sad,<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>70: </i>A few Hebrew manuscripts; most Hebrew manuscripts “70 men, 50,000 men.”  
**6:20** and they started saying, “No other God is like the LORD! Who can go near him and stay alive? We'll have to send the chest away from here. But where can we send it?”  
**6:21** They sent messengers to tell the people of Kiriath-Jearim, “The Philistines have sent back the sacred chest. Why don't you take it and keep it there with you?”  

## 7장

**7:1**  The people of Kiriath-Jearim got the chest and took it to Abinadab's house, which was on a hill in their town. They chose his son Eleazar to take care of it,

**28:23** “No, I won't eat!” But his officers and the woman kept on urging Saul, until he finally agreed. He got up off the floor and sat on the bed.  
**28:24** At once the woman killed a calf that she had been fattening up. She cooked part of the meat and baked some thin bread.<sup>①</sup><br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>thin bread: </i>Bread made without yeast, since there was no time for the bread to rise.  
**28:25** Then she served the food to Saul and his officers, who ate and left before daylight.

## 30장

**30:1** It took David and his men three days to reach Ziklag. But while they had been away, the Amalekites had been raiding in the desert around there. They had attacked Ziklag, burned it to the ground,  
**30:2** and had taken away the women and children.  
**30:3** When David and his men came to Ziklag, they saw the burned-out ruins and learned that their families had been taken captive.  
**30:4** They started crying and kept it up until they were too weak to cry any more.  
**30:5**  David's two wives, Ahinoam and Abigail, had been taken captive with everyone else.  
**30:6** David was desperate. His soldiers were so upset over what had happened to their sons and daughters that they were thinking about stoning David to death. But he felt the LORD God giving him strength,  
**30:7**  and he said to the priest, “Abiathar, let's ask God what to do.” Abiathar brought everything he needed to get answers from God, and he went over to David.  
**30:8** Then David asked the LORD, “Should I go after the people who raided our town? Can I catch up with them?” “Go after them,” the LORD answered. “You will catch up with them, and you will rescue your families.”  
**30:9** David led his 600 men to Besor Gorge, but 200 of them were too tired to go across. So they stayed behind, while David and the other 400 men crossed the gorge.  
**30:10**   
**30:11** Some of David's men found an Egyptian out in a field and took him to David. They gave the Egyptian some bread, and he ate it. Then they gave him a drink of water,  
**30:12** some dried figs, and two handfuls of raisins. This was the first time in three days he had tasted food or water. Now he felt much better.  
**30:13** “Who is your master?” David asked. “And where do you come from?” “I'm from Egypt,” the young man answered. “I'm the servant of an Amalekite, but he left me here three days ago because I was sick.  
**30:14** We had attacked some towns in the desert where the Cherethites live, in the area that belongs to Judah, and in the desert where the Caleb clan lives. And we burned down Ziklag.”  
**30:15** “Will you take me to those Amalekites?” David asked. “Yes, I will, if you promise with God as a witness that you won't kill me or hand me over to my master.”  
**30:16** He led David to the Amalekites. They were eating and drinking everywhere, celebrating because of what they had taken from Philistia and Judah.  
**30:17** David attacked just before sunrise the next day and fought until sunset.<sup>①</sup> Four hundred Amalekites rode away on camels, but they were the only ones who escaped.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>just … sunset: </i>Or “at dusk, and fought until sunset on the next day.”  
**30:18** David rescued his two wives and everyone else the Amalekites had taken from Ziklag.  
**30:19** No one was missing—young or old, sons or daughters. David brought back everything that had been stolen,  
**30:20** including their livestock. David also took the sheep and cattle that the Amalekites had with them, but he kept these separate from the others. Everyone agreed that these would be David's reward.  
**30:21** On the way back, David went to the 200 men he had left at Besor Gorge, because they had been too tired to keep up with him. They came toward David and the people who were with him. When David was close enough, he greeted the 200 men and asked how they were doing.  
**30:22** Some of David's men were good-for-nothings, and they said, “Those men didn't go with us to the battle, so they don't get any of the things we took back from the Amalekites. Let them take their wives and children and go!”  
**30:23** But David said: My friends, don't be so greedy with what the LORD has given us! The LORD protected us and gave us victory over the people who attacked.  
**30:24** Who would pay attention to you, anyway? Soldiers who stay behind to guard the camp get as much as those who go into battle.  
**30:25** David made this a law for Israel, and it has been the same ever since.  
**30:26** David went back to Ziklag with everything they had taken from the Amalekites. He sent some of these things as gifts to his friends who were leaders of Judah, and he told them, “We took these things from the LORD's enemies. Please accept them as a gift.”  
**30:27** This is a list of the towns where David sent gifts: Bethel,<sup>①</sup> Ramoth in the Southern Desert, Jattir, Aroer, Siphmoth, Eshtemoa, Racal, the towns belonging to the Jerahmeelites and the Kenites, Hormah, Bor-Ashan, Athach, and Hebron. He also sent gifts to the other towns where he and his men had traveled.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Bethel: </i>Or “Bethuel” (see <a name='B:60 19:4'>Joshua 19.4).  

## 31장

**31:1** Meanwhile, the Philistines were fighting Israel at Mount Gilboa. Israel's soldiers ran from the Philistines, and many of them were killed.  
**31:2** The Philistines closed in on Saul and his sons, and they killed his sons Jonathan, Abinadab, and Malchishua.  
**31:3** The fighting was fierce around Saul, and he was badly wounded by enemy arrows.  
**31:4** Saul told the soldier who carried his weapons, “Kill me with your sword! I don't want these worthless Philistines to torture and make fun of me.” But the soldier was afraid to kill him. Saul then took out his own sword; he stuck the blade into his stomach, and fell on it.  
**31:5** When the soldier knew that Saul was dead, he killed himself in the same way.  
**31:6** Saul was dead, his three sons were dead, and the soldier who carried his weapons was dead. They and all his soldiers died on that same day.  
**31:7** The Israelites on the other side of Jezreel Valley<sup>①</sup> and the other side of the Jordan learned that Saul and his sons were dead. They saw that the Israelite army had run away. So they ran away too, and the Philistines moved into the towns the Israelites had left behind.<br>▷ <sup>①</sup><i>Jezreel Valley: </i>Hebrew “valley.” Shunem (see <a name='B:90 28:4'>28.4) and Gilboa (see verse <a name='B:90 31:1'>1) were across the Jezreel Valley from each other.

**31:11** The people who lived in Jabesh in Gilead heard what the Philistines had done to Saul's body.

**31:13** They buried the bones under a small tree in Jabesh, and for seven days, they went without eating to show their sorrow.
