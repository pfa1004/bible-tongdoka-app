# 18.욥기 (CSB)



## 1장

**1:1** There was a man in the country of Uz named Job. He was a man of complete integrity, who feared God and turned away from evil.  
**1:2** He had seven sons and three daughters.  
**1:3** His estate included seven thousand sheep and goats, three thousand camels, five hundred yoke of oxen, five hundred female donkeys, and a very large number of servants. Job was the greatest man among all the people of the east.  
**1:4** His sons used to take turns having banquets at their homes. They would send an invitation to their three sisters to eat and drink with them.  
**1:5** Whenever a round of banqueting was over, Job would send for his children and purify them, rising early in the morning to offer burnt offerings for<sup>①</sup> all of them. For Job thought, “Perhaps my children have sinned, having cursed God in their hearts.” This was Job’s regular practice.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>for the number of</i>  
**1:6** One day the sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, and Satan<sup>①</sup> also came with them.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>the adversary</i>  
**1:7** The LORD asked Satan, “Where have you come from?” “From roaming through the earth,” Satan answered him, “and walking around on it.”  
**1:8** Then the LORD said to Satan, “Have you considered my servant Job? No one else on earth is like him, a man of perfect integrity, who fears God and turns away from evil.”  
**1:9** Satan answered the LORD, “Does Job fear God for nothing?  
**1:10** Haven’t you placed a hedge around him, his household, and everything he owns? You have blessed the work of his hands, and his possessions have increased in the land.  
**1:11** But stretch out your hand and strike everything he owns, and he will surely curse you to your face.”  
**1:12** “Very well,” the LORD told Satan, “everything he owns is in your power. However, do not lay a hand on Job himself.” So Satan left the LORD’s presence.  
**1:13** One day when Job’s sons and daughters were eating and drinking wine in their oldest brother’s house,  
**1:14** a messenger came to Job and reported, “While the oxen were plowing and the donkeys grazing nearby,  
**1:15** the Sabeans swooped down and took them away. They struck down the servants with the sword, and I alone have escaped to tell you!”  
**1:16** He was still speaking when another messenger came and reported, “God’s fire fell from heaven. It burned the sheep and the servants and devoured them, and I alone have escaped to tell you!”  
**1:17** That messenger was still speaking when yet another came and reported, “The Chaldeans formed three bands, made a raid on the camels, and took them away. They struck down the servants with the sword, and I alone have escaped to tell you!”  
**1:18** He was still speaking when another messenger came and reported: “Your sons and daughters were eating and drinking wine in their oldest brother’s house.  
**1:19** Suddenly a powerful wind swept in from the desert and struck the four corners of the house. It collapsed on the young people so that they died, and I alone have escaped to tell you!”  
**1:20** Then Job stood up, tore his robe, and shaved his head. He fell to the ground and worshiped,  
**1:21** saying:<br>Naked I came from my mother’s womb,<br>and naked I will leave this life.<sup>①</sup><br>The LORD gives, and the LORD takes away.<br>Blessed be the name of the LORD.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>will return there;</i> Ps <a name='B:230 139:13'>139:13,<a name='B:230 139:15'>15  
**1:22** Throughout all this Job did not sin or blame God for anything.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>or ascribe blame to God</i>  

## 2장

**2:1** One day the sons of God came again to present themselves before the LORD, and Satan also came with them to present himself before the LORD.  
**2:2** The LORD asked Satan, “Where have you come from?” “From roaming through the earth,” Satan answered him, “and walking around on it.”  
**2:3** Then the LORD said to Satan, “Have you considered my servant Job? No one else on earth is like him, a man of perfect integrity, who fears God and turns away from evil. He still retains his integrity, even though you incited me against him, to destroy him for no good reason.”  
**2:4** “Skin for skin!” Satan answered the LORD. “A man will give up everything he owns in exchange for his life.  
**2:5** But stretch out your hand and strike his flesh and bones, and he will surely curse you to your face.”  
**2:6** “Very well,” the LORD told Satan, “he is in your power; only spare his life.”  
**2:7** So Satan left the LORD’s presence and infected Job with terrible boils from the soles of his feet to the top of his head.  
**2:8** Then Job took a piece of broken pottery to scrape himself while he sat among the ashes.  
**2:9** His wife said to him, “Are you still holding on to your integrity? Curse God and die!”  
**2:10** “You speak as a foolish woman speaks,” he told her. “Should we accept only good from God and not adversity?” Throughout all this Job did not sin in what he said.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>sin with his lips</i>  
**2:11** Now when Job’s three friends—Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite, and Zophar the Naamathite—heard about all this adversity that had happened to him, each of them came from his home. They met together to go and sympathize with him and comfort him.  
**2:12** When they looked from a distance, they could barely recognize him. They wept aloud, and each man tore his robe and threw dust into the air and on his head.  
**2:13** Then they sat on the ground with him seven days and nights, but no one spoke a word to him because they saw that his suffering was very intense.  

## 3장

**3:1** After this, Job began to speak and cursed the day he was born.  
**3:2** He said:  
**3:3** May the day I was born perish,<br>and the night that said,<br>“A boy is conceived.”  
**3:4** If only that day had turned to darkness!<br>May God above not care about it,<br>or light shine on it.  
**3:5** May darkness and gloom reclaim it,<br>and a cloud settle over it.<br>May what darkens the day terrify it.  
**3:6** If only darkness had taken that night away!<br>May it not appear<sup>①</sup> among the days of the year<br>or be listed in the calendar.<sup>②</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>LXX, Syr, Tg, Vg; MT reads <i>rejoice</i> <sup>②</sup>Lit <i>or enter the number of months</i>  
**3:7** Yes, may that night be barren;<br>may no joyful shout be heard in it.  
**3:8** Let those who curse days<br>condemn it,<br>those who are ready to rouse Leviathan.  
**3:9** May its morning stars grow dark.<br>May it wait for daylight but have none;<br>may it not see the breaking<sup>①</sup> of dawn.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>the eyelids</i>  
**3:10** For that night did not shut<br>the doors of my mother’s womb,<br>and hide sorrow from my eyes.  
**3:11** Why was I not stillborn;<br>why didn’t I die as I came from the womb?  
**3:12** Why did the knees receive me,<br>and why were there breasts for me to nurse?  
**3:13** Now I would certainly be lying down in peace;<br>I would be asleep.<br>Then I would be at rest  
**3:14** with the kings and counselors of the earth,<br>who rebuilt ruined cities for themselves,  
**3:15** or with princes who had gold,<br>who filled their houses with silver.  
**3:16** Or why was I not hidden like a miscarried child,<br>like infants who never see daylight?  
**3:17** There the wicked cease to make trouble,<br>and there the weary find rest.  
**3:18** The captives are completely at rest;<br>they do not hear a taskmaster’s voice.  
**3:19** Both small and great are there,<br>and the slave is set free from his master.  
**3:20** Why is light given to one burdened with grief,<br>and life to those whose existence is bitter,  
**3:21** who wait for death, but it does not come,<br>and search for it more than for hidden treasure,  
**3:22** who are filled with much joy<br>and are glad when they reach the grave?  
**3:23** Why is life given to a man whose path is hidden,<br>whom God has hedged in?  
**3:24** I sigh when food is put before me,<sup>①</sup><br>and my groans pour out like water.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>My sighing serves as my food</i>  
**3:25** For the thing I feared has overtaken me,<br>and what I dreaded has happened to me.  
**3:26** I cannot relax or be calm;<br>I have no rest, for turmoil has come.  

## 4장

**4:1** Then Eliphaz the Temanite replied:  
**4:2** Should anyone try to speak with you<br>when you are exhausted?<br>Yet who can keep from speaking?  
**4:3** Indeed, you have instructed many<br>and have strengthened weak hands.  
**4:4** Your words have steadied the one who was stumbling<br>and braced the knees that were buckling.  
**4:5** But now that this has happened to you,<br>you have become exhausted.<br>It strikes you, and you are dismayed.  
**4:6** Isn’t your piety your confidence,<br>and the integrity of your life<sup>①</sup> your hope?<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>ways</i>  
**4:7** Consider: Who has perished when he was innocent?<br>Where have the honest<sup>①</sup> been destroyed?<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>the upright</i>, or <i>those with integrity</i>  
**4:8** In my experience, those who plow injustice<br>and those who sow trouble reap the same.  
**4:9** They perish at a single blast from God<br>and come to an end by the breath of his nostrils.  
**4:10** The lion may roar and the fierce lion growl,<br>but the teeth of young lions are broken.  
**4:11** The strong lion dies if it catches no prey,<br>and the cubs of the lioness are scattered.  
**4:12** A word was brought to me in secret;<br>my ears caught a whisper of it.  
**4:13** Among unsettling thoughts from visions in the night,<br>when deep sleep comes over men,  
**4:14** fear and trembling came over me<br>and made all my bones shake.  
**4:15** I felt a draft<sup>①</sup> on my face,<br>and the hair on my body stood up.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>a spirit</i>  
**4:16** A figure stood there,<br>but I could not recognize its appearance;<br>a form loomed before my eyes.<br>I heard a whispering voice:  
**4:17** “Can a mortal be righteous before God?<br>Can a man be more pure than his Maker?”  
**4:18** If God puts no trust in his servants<br>and he charges his angels with foolishness,<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>error;</i> Hb obscure  
**4:19** how much more those who dwell in clay houses,<br>whose foundation is in the dust,<br>who are crushed like a moth!  
**4:20** They are smashed to pieces from dawn to dusk;<br>they perish forever while no one notices.  
**4:21** Are their tent cords not pulled up?<br>They die without wisdom.  

## 5장

**5:2** For anger kills a fool,<br>and jealousy slays the gullible.  
**5:3** I have seen a fool taking root,<br>but I immediately pronounced a curse on his home.  
**5:4** His children are far from safety.<br>They are crushed at the city gate,<br>with no one to rescue them.

**5:15** He saves the needy from their sharp words<sup>①</sup><br>and from the clutches of the powerful.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>from the sword of their mouth;</i> Ps <a name='B:230 55:21'>55:21; <a name='B:230 59:7'>59:7

**5:27** We have investigated this, and it is true!<br>Hear it and understand it for yourself.  

## 6장

**6:1** Then Job answered:  
**6:2** If only my grief could be weighed<br>and my devastation placed with it on the scales.  
**6:3** For then it would outweigh the sand of the seas!<br>That is why my words are rash.  
**6:4** Surely the arrows of the Almighty have pierced<sup>①</sup> me;<br>my spirit drinks their poison.<br>God’s terrors are arrayed against me.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>Almighty are in</i>  
**6:5** Does a wild donkey bray over fresh grass<br>or an ox low over its fodder?  
**6:6** Is bland food eaten without salt?<br>Is there flavor in an egg white?<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hb obscure  
**6:7** I refuse to touch them;<br>they are like contaminated food.  
**6:8** If only my request would be granted<br>and God would provide what I hope for:  
**6:9** that he would decide to crush me,<br>to unleash his power and cut me off!  
**6:10** It would still bring me comfort,<br>and I would leap for joy in unrelenting pain<br>that I have not denied<sup>①</sup> the words of the Holy One.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>hidden</i>  
**6:11** What strength do I have, that I should continue to hope?<br>What is my future, that I should be patient?  
**6:12** Is my strength that of stone,<br>or my flesh made of bronze?  
**6:13** Since I cannot help myself,<br>the hope for success has been banished from me.  
**6:14** A despairing man should receive loyalty from his friends,<sup>①</sup><br>even if he abandons the fear of the Almighty.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>To the despairing his friend loyalty</i>  
**6:15** My brothers are as treacherous as a wadi,<br>as seasonal streams that overflow  
**6:16** and become darkened<sup>①</sup> because of ice,<br>and the snow melts into them.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>turbid</i>  
**6:17** The wadis evaporate in warm weather;<br>they disappear from their channels in hot weather.  
**6:18** Caravans turn away from their routes,<br>go up into the desert, and perish.  
**6:19** The caravans of Tema look for these streams.<br>The traveling merchants of Sheba hope for them.  
**6:20** They are ashamed because they had been confident of finding water.<br>When they arrive there, they are disappointed.  
**6:21** So this is what you have now become to me.<sup>①</sup><br>When you see something dreadful, you are afraid.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Alt Hb tradition reads <i>So you have now become nothing</i>  
**6:22** Have I ever said, “Give me something”<br>or “Pay a bribe for me from your wealth”  
**6:23** or “Deliver me from the enemy’s hand”<br>or “Redeem me from the hand of the ruthless”?  
**6:24** Teach me, and I will be silent.<br>Help me understand what I did wrong.  
**6:25** How painful honest words can be!<br>But what does your rebuke prove?  
**6:26** Do you think that you can disprove my words<br>or that a despairing man’s words are mere wind?  
**6:27** No doubt you would cast lots for a fatherless child<br>and negotiate a price to sell your friend.  
**6:28** But now, please look at me;<br>I will not lie to your face.  
**6:29** Reconsider; don’t be unjust.<br>Reconsider; my righteousness is still the issue.  
**6:30** Is there injustice on my tongue<br>or can my palate not taste disaster?  

## 7장

**7:1** Isn’t each person consigned to forced labor on earth?<br>Are not his days like those of a hired worker?  
**7:2** Like a slave he longs for shade;<br>like a hired worker he waits for his pay.  
**7:3** So I have been made to inherit months of futility,<br>and troubled nights have been assigned to me.  
**7:4** When I lie down I think,<br>“When will I get up?”<br>But the evening drags on endlessly,<br>and I toss and turn until dawn.  
**7:5** My flesh is clothed with maggots and encrusted with dirt.<sup>①</sup><br>My skin forms scabs<sup>②</sup> and then oozes.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>and dirty scabs</i> <sup>②</sup>Lit <i>skin hardens</i>  
**7:6** My days pass more swiftly than a weaver’s shuttle;<br>they come to an end without hope.  
**7:7** Remember that my life is but a breath.<br>My eye will never again see anything good.  
**7:8** The eye of anyone who looks on me<br>will no longer see me.<br>Your eyes will look for me, but I will be gone.  
**7:9** As a cloud fades away and vanishes,<br>so the one who goes down to Sheol will never rise again.  
**7:10** He will never return to his house;<br>his hometown will no longer remember<sup>①</sup> him.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>know</i>  
**7:11** Therefore I will not restrain my mouth.<br>I will speak in the anguish of my spirit;<br>I will complain in the bitterness of my soul.  
**7:12** Am I the sea<sup>①</sup> or a sea monster,<br>that you keep me under guard?<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>the sea god</i>  
**7:13** When I say, “My bed will comfort me,<br>and my couch will ease my complaint,”  
**7:14** then you frighten me with dreams,<br>and terrify me with visions,  
**7:15** so that I prefer strangling<sup>①</sup>—<br>death rather than life in this body.<sup>②</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>suffocation</i> <sup>②</sup>Lit <i>than my bones</i>  
**7:16** I give up! I will not live forever.<br>Leave me alone, for my days are a breath.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>are futile</i>  
**7:17** What is a mere human, that you think so highly of him<br>and pay so much attention to him?  
**7:18** You inspect him every morning,<br>and put him to the test every moment.  
**7:19** Will you ever look away from me,<br>or leave me alone long enough to swallow?<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>swallow my saliva?</i>  
**7:20** If I have sinned, what have I done to you,<br>Watcher of humanity?<br>Why have you made me your target,<br>so that I have become a burden to you?<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Alt Hb tradition, LXX; MT, Vg read <i>myself</i>  
**7:21** Why not forgive my sin<br>and pardon my iniquity?<br>For soon I will lie down in the grave.<br>You will eagerly seek me, but I will be gone.  

## 8장

**8:1** Then Bildad the Shuhite replied:  
**8:2** How long will you go on saying these things?<br>Your words are a blast of wind.  
**8:3** Does God pervert justice?<br>Does the Almighty pervert what is right?  
**8:4** Since your children sinned against him,<br>he gave them over to their rebellion.  
**8:5** But if you earnestly seek God<br>and ask the Almighty for mercy,  
**8:6** if you are pure and upright,<br>then he will move even now on your behalf<br>and restore the home where your righteousness dwells.  
**8:7** Then, even if your beginnings were modest,<br>your final days will be full of prosperity.  
**8:8** For ask the previous generation,<br>and pay attention to what their ancestors discovered,  
**8:9** since we were born only yesterday and know nothing.<br>Our days on earth are but a shadow.  
**8:10** Will they not teach you and tell you<br>and speak from their understanding?  
**8:11** Does papyrus grow where there is no marsh?<br>Do reeds flourish without water?  
**8:12** While still uncut shoots,<br>they would dry up quicker than any other plant.  
**8:13** Such is the destiny<sup>①</sup> of all who forget God;<br>the hope of the godless will perish.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>Such are the ways</i>  
**8:14** His source of confidence is fragile;<sup>①</sup><br>what he trusts in is a spider’s web.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>cut off;</i> Hb obscure  
**8:15** He leans on his web, but it doesn’t stand firm.<br>He grabs it, but it does not hold up.  
**8:16** He is a well-watered plant in the sunshine;<br>his shoots spread out over his garden.  
**8:17** His roots are intertwined around a pile of rocks.<br>He looks for a home among the stones.  
**8:18** If he is uprooted<sup>①</sup> from his place,<br>it will deny knowing him, saying, “I never saw you.”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>destroyed</i>

**8:20** Look, God does not reject a person of integrity,<br>and he will not support<sup>①</sup> evildoers.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>grasp the hand of</i>  
**8:21** He will yet fill your mouth with laughter<br>and your lips with a shout of joy.  
**8:22** Your enemies will be clothed with shame;<br>the tent of the wicked will no longer exist.  

## 9장

**9:1** Then Job answered:  
**9:2** Yes, I know what you’ve said is true,<br>but how can a person be justified before God?  
**9:3** If one wanted to take him to court,<br>he could not answer God<sup>①</sup> once in a thousand times.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>court, God would not answer him</i>

**9:5** He removes mountains without their knowledge,<br>overturning them in his anger.  
**9:6** He shakes the earth from its place<br>so that its pillars tremble.  
**9:7** He commands the sun not to shine<br>and seals off the stars.  
**9:8** He alone stretches out the heavens<br>and treads on the waves of the sea.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>and walks on the back of the sea god</i>

**9:22** It is all the same. Therefore I say,<br>“He destroys both the blameless and the wicked.”

**42:6** Therefore, I reject my words and am sorry for them;<br>I am dust and ashes.<sup>①</sup><sup>②</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>LXX reads <i>I despise myself and melt; I consider myself dust and ashes</i> <sup>②</sup>Lit <i>I reject and I relent, concerning dust and ashes</i>  
**42:7** After the LORD had finished speaking<sup>①</sup> to Job, he said to Eliphaz the Temanite, “I am angry with you and your two friends, for you have not spoken the truth about me, as my servant Job has.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>speaking these words</i>  
**42:8** Now take seven bulls and seven rams, go to my servant Job, and offer a burnt offering for yourselves. Then my servant Job will pray for you. I will surely accept his prayer and not deal with you as your folly deserves. For you have not spoken the truth about me, as my servant Job has.”  
**42:9** Then Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite, and Zophar the Naamathite went and did as the LORD had told them, and the LORD accepted Job’s prayer.  
**42:10** After Job had prayed for his friends, the LORD restored his fortunes and doubled his previous possessions.  
**42:11** All his brothers, sisters, and former acquaintances came to him and dined with him in his house. They sympathized with him and comforted him concerning all the adversity the LORD had brought on him. Each one gave him a piece of silver<sup>①</sup> and a gold earring.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>a qesitah;</i> the value of this currency is unknown  
**42:12** So the LORD blessed the last part of Job’s life more than the first. He owned fourteen thousand sheep and goats, six thousand camels, one thousand yoke of oxen, and one thousand female donkeys.  
**42:13** He also had seven sons and three daughters.  
**42:14** He named his first daughter Jemimah, his second Keziah, and his third Keren-happuch.  
**42:15** No women as beautiful as Job’s daughters could be found in all the land, and their father granted them an inheritance with their brothers.  
**42:16** Job lived 140 years after this and saw his children and their children to the fourth generation.  
**42:17** Then Job died, old and full of days.
