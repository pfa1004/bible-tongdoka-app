# 7.사사기 (CSB)



## 1장

**1:1** After the death of Joshua, the Israelites inquired of the LORD, “Who will be the first to fight for us against the Canaanites?”  
**1:2** The LORD answered, “Judah is to go. I have handed the land over to him.”  
**1:3** Judah said to his brother Simeon, “Come with me to my allotted territory, and let’s fight against the Canaanites. I will also go with you to your allotted territory.” So Simeon went with him.  
**1:4** When Judah attacked, the LORD handed the Canaanites and Perizzites over to them. They struck down ten thousand men in Bezek.  
**1:5** They found Adoni-bezek in Bezek, fought against him, and struck down the Canaanites and Perizzites.  
**1:6** When Adoni-bezek fled, they pursued him, caught him, and cut off his thumbs and big toes.  
**1:7** Adoni-bezek said, “Seventy kings with their thumbs and big toes cut off used to pick up scraps<sup>①</sup> under my table. God has repaid me for what I have done.” They brought him to Jerusalem, and he died there.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>toes cut off are gathering</i>  
**1:8** The men of Judah fought against Jerusalem, captured it, put it to the sword, and set the city on fire.  
**1:9** Afterward, the men of Judah marched down to fight against the Canaanites who were living in the hill country, the Negev, and the Judean foothills.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>the Shephelah</i>  
**1:10** Judah also marched against the Canaanites who were living in Hebron (Hebron was formerly named Kiriath-arba). They struck down Sheshai, Ahiman, and Talmai.  
**1:11** From there they marched against the residents of Debir (Debir was formerly named Kiriath-sepher).  
**1:12** Caleb said, “Whoever attacks and captures Kiriath-sepher, I will give my daughter Achsah to him as a wife.”  
**1:13** So Othniel son of Kenaz, Caleb’s youngest brother, captured it, and Caleb gave his daughter Achsah to him as his wife.  
**1:14** When she arrived, she persuaded Othniel to ask her father for a field. As she got off her donkey, Caleb asked her, “What do you want?”  
**1:15** She answered him, “Give me a blessing. Since you have given me land in the Negev, give me springs also.” So Caleb gave her both the upper and lower springs.  
**1:16** The descendants of the Kenite, Moses’s father-in-law, had gone up with the men of Judah from the City of Palms<sup>①</sup> to the Wilderness of Judah, which was in the Negev of Arad. They went to live among the people.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>= Jericho; Dt <a name='B:50 34:3'>34:3; Jdg <a name='B:70 3:13'>3:13; 2Ch <a name='B:140 28:15'>28:15  
**1:17** Judah went with his brother Simeon, struck the Canaanites who were living in Zephath, and completely destroyed the town. So they named the town Hormah.  
**1:18** Judah captured Gaza and its territory, Ashkelon and its territory, and Ekron and its territory.  
**1:19** The LORD was with Judah and enabled them to take possession of the hill country, but they could not drive out the people who were living in the plain because those people had iron chariots.  
**1:20** Judah gave Hebron to Caleb, just as Moses had promised. Then Caleb drove out the three sons of Anak who lived there.  
**1:21** At the same time the Benjaminites did not drive out the Jebusites who were living in Jerusalem. The Jebusites have lived among the Benjaminites in Jerusalem to this day.  
**1:22** The house of Joseph also attacked Bethel, and the LORD was with them.  
**1:23** They sent spies to Bethel (the town was formerly named Luz).  
**1:24** The spies saw a man coming out of the town and said to him, “Please show us how to get into town, and we will show you kindness.”  
**1:25** When he showed them the way into the town, they put the town to the sword but released the man and his entire family.  
**1:26** Then the man went to the land of the Hittites, built a town, and named it Luz. That is its name still today.  
**1:27** At that time Manasseh failed to take possession of Beth-shean and Taanach and their surrounding villages, or the residents of Dor, Ibleam, and Megiddo and their surrounding villages; the Canaanites were determined to stay in this land.  
**1:28** When Israel became stronger, they made the Canaanites serve as forced labor but never drove them out completely.  
**1:29** At that time Ephraim failed to drive out the Canaanites who were living in Gezer, so the Canaanites have lived among them in Gezer.  
**1:30** Zebulun failed to drive out the residents of Kitron or the residents of Nahalol, so the Canaanites lived among them and served as forced labor.  
**1:31** Asher failed to drive out the residents of Acco or of Sidon, or Ahlab, Achzib, Helbah, Aphik, or Rehob.  
**1:32** The Asherites lived among the Canaanites who were living in the land, because they failed to drive them out.  
**1:33** Naphtali did not drive out the residents of Beth-shemesh or the residents of Beth-anath. They lived among the Canaanites who were living in the land, but the residents of Beth-shemesh and Beth-anath served as their forced labor.  
**1:34** The Amorites forced the Danites into the hill country and did not allow them to go down into the valley.  
**1:35** The Amorites were determined to stay in Har-heres, Aijalon, and Shaalbim. When the house of Joseph got the upper hand, the Amorites were made to serve as forced labor.  
**1:36** The territory of the Amorites extended from the Scorpions’ Ascent, that is from Sela upward.  

## 2장

**2:1** The angel of the LORD went up from Gilgal to Bochim and said, “I brought you out of Egypt and led you into the land I had promised to your ancestors. I also said: I will never break my covenant with you.  
**2:2** You are not to make a covenant with the inhabitants of this land. You are to tear down their altars. But you have not obeyed me. What have you done?  
**2:3** Therefore, I now say: I will not drive out these people before you. They will be thorns<sup>①</sup> in your sides, and their gods will be a trap for you.”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>traps</i>  
**2:4** When the angel of the LORD had spoken these words to all the Israelites, the people wept loudly.  
**2:5** So they named that place Bochim<sup>①</sup> and offered sacrifices there to the LORD.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>Weeping</i>  
**2:6** Previously, when Joshua had sent the people away, the Israelites had gone to take possession of the land, each to his own inheritance.  
**2:7** The people worshiped the LORD throughout Joshua’s lifetime and during the lifetimes of the elders who outlived Joshua. They had seen all the LORD’s great works he had done for Israel.  
**2:8** Joshua son of Nun, the servant of the LORD, died at the age of 110.  
**2:9** They buried him in the territory of his inheritance, in Timnath-heres, in the hill country of Ephraim, north of Mount Gaash.  
**2:10** That whole generation was also gathered to their ancestors. After them another generation rose up who did not know the LORD or the works he had done for Israel.  
**2:11** The Israelites did what was evil in the LORD’s sight. They worshiped the Baals  
**2:12** and abandoned the LORD, the God of their ancestors, who had brought them out of Egypt. They followed other gods from the surrounding peoples and bowed down to them. They angered the LORD,  
**2:13** for they abandoned him and worshiped Baal and the Ashtoreths.  
**2:14** The LORD’s anger burned against Israel, and he handed them over to marauders who raided them. He sold them to the enemies around them, and they could no longer resist their enemies.  
**2:15** Whenever the Israelites went out, the LORD was against them and brought disaster on them, just as he had promised and sworn to them. So they suffered greatly.  
**2:16** The LORD raised up judges, who saved them from the power of their marauders,  
**2:17** but they did not listen to their judges. Instead, they prostituted themselves with other gods, bowing down to them. They quickly turned from the way of their ancestors, who had walked in obedience to the LORD’s commands. They did not do as their ancestors did.  
**2:18** Whenever the LORD raised up a judge for the Israelites, the LORD was with him and saved the people from the power of their enemies while the judge was still alive. The LORD was moved to pity whenever they groaned because of those who were oppressing and afflicting them.  
**2:19** Whenever the judge died, the Israelites would act even more corruptly than their ancestors, following other gods to serve them and bow in worship to them. They did not turn from their evil practices or their obstinate ways.  
**2:20** The LORD’s anger burned against Israel, and he declared, “Because this nation has violated my covenant that I made with their ancestors and disobeyed me,  
**2:21** I will no longer drive out before them any of the nations Joshua left when he died.  
**2:22** I did this to test Israel and to see whether or not they would keep the LORD’s way by walking in it, as their ancestors had.”  
**2:23** The LORD left these nations and did not drive them out immediately. He did not hand them over to Joshua.  

## 3장

**3:1** These are the nations the LORD left in order to test all those in Israel who had experienced none of the wars in Canaan.  
**3:2** This was to teach the future generations of the Israelites how to fight in battle, especially those who had not fought before.  
**3:3** These nations included the five rulers of the Philistines and all of the Canaanites, the Sidonians, and the Hivites who lived in the Lebanese mountains from Mount Baal-hermon as far as the entrance to Hamath.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>as Lebo-hamath</i>  
**3:4** The LORD left them to test Israel, to determine if they would keep the LORD’s commands he had given their ancestors through Moses.  
**3:5** But they settled among the Canaanites, Hethites, Amorites, Perizzites, Hivites, and Jebusites.  
**3:6** The Israelites took their daughters as wives for themselves, gave their own daughters to their sons, and worshiped their gods.  
**3:7** The Israelites did what was evil in the LORD’s sight; they forgot the LORD their God and worshiped the Baals and the Asherahs.  
**3:8** The LORD’s anger burned against Israel, and he sold them to King Cushan-rishathaim<sup>①</sup> of Aram-naharaim,<sup>②</sup> and the Israelites served him eight years.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>Doubly-Evil</i> <sup>②</sup>= Mesopotamia  
**3:9** The Israelites cried out to the LORD. So the LORD raised up Othniel son of Kenaz, Caleb’s youngest brother, as a deliverer to save the Israelites.  
**3:10** The Spirit of the LORD came on him, and he judged Israel. Othniel went out to battle, and the LORD handed over King Cushan-rishathaim of Aram to him, so that Othniel overpowered him.  
**3:11** Then the land had peace for forty years, and Othniel son of Kenaz died.  
**3:12** The Israelites again did what was evil in the LORD’s sight. He gave King Eglon of Moab power over Israel, because they had done what was evil in the LORD’s sight.  
**3:13** After Eglon convinced the Ammonites and the Amalekites to join forces with him, he attacked and defeated Israel and took possession of the City of Palms.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>= Jericho; Dt <a name='B:50 34:3'>34:3; Jdg <a name='B:70 1:16'>1:16; 2Ch <a name='B:140 28:15'>28:15  
**3:14** The Israelites served King Eglon of Moab eighteen years.  
**3:15** Then the Israelites cried out to the LORD, and he raised up Ehud son of Gera, a left-handed Benjaminite,<sup>①</sup> as a deliverer for them. The Israelites sent him with the tribute for King Eglon of Moab.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>= son of the right hand  
**3:16** Ehud made himself a double-edged sword eighteen inches long.<sup>①</sup> He strapped it to his right thigh under his clothes<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>sword a gomed in length</i>  
**3:17** and brought the tribute to King Eglon of Moab, who was an extremely fat man.  
**3:18** When Ehud had finished presenting the tribute, he dismissed the people who had carried it.  
**3:19** At the carved images near Gilgal he returned and said, “King Eglon, I have a secret message for you.” The king said, “Silence!” and all his attendants left him.  
**3:20** Then Ehud approached him while he was sitting alone in his upstairs room where it was cool. Ehud said, “I have a message from God for you,” and the king stood up from his throne.  
**3:21** Ehud reached with his left hand, took the sword from his right thigh, and plunged it into Eglon’s belly.  
**3:22** Even the handle went in after the blade, and Eglon’s fat closed in over it, so that Ehud did not withdraw the sword from his belly. And the waste came out.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>And Eglon’s bowels discharged</i>  
**3:23** Ehud escaped by way of the porch, closing and locking the doors of the upstairs room behind him.  
**3:24** Ehud was gone when Eglon’s servants came in. They looked and found the doors of the upstairs room locked and thought he was relieving himself<sup>①</sup> in the cool room.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>was covering his feet</i>  
**3:25** The servants waited until they became embarrassed and saw that he had still not opened the doors of the upstairs room. So they took the key and opened the doors—and there was their lord lying dead on the floor!  
**3:26** Ehud escaped while the servants waited. He passed the Jordan near the carved images and reached Seirah.  
**3:27** After he arrived, he sounded the ram’s horn throughout the hill country of Ephraim. The Israelites came down with him from the hill country, and he became their leader.  
**3:28** He told them, “Follow me, because the LORD has handed over your enemies, the Moabites, to you.” So they followed him, captured the fords of the Jordan leading to Moab, and did not allow anyone to cross over.  
**3:29** At that time they struck down about ten thousand Moabites, all stout and able-bodied men. Not one of them escaped.  
**3:30** Moab became subject to Israel that day, and the land had peace for eighty years.  
**3:31** After Ehud, Shamgar son of Anath became judge. He also delivered Israel, striking down six hundred Philistines with a cattle prod.  

## 4장

**4:1** The Israelites again did what was evil in the sight of the LORD after Ehud had died.  
**4:2** So the LORD sold them to King Jabin of Canaan, who reigned in Hazor. The commander of his army was Sisera who lived in Harosheth of the Nations.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>Harosheth-ha-goiim</i>, also in vv. <a name='B:70 4:13'>13,<a name='B:70 4:16'>16  
**4:3** Then the Israelites cried out to the LORD, because Jabin had nine hundred iron chariots, and he harshly oppressed them twenty years.  
**4:4** Deborah, a prophetess and the wife of Lappidoth, was judging Israel at that time.  
**4:5** She would sit under the palm tree of Deborah between Ramah and Bethel in the hill country of Ephraim, and the Israelites went up to her to settle disputes.  
**4:6** She summoned Barak son of Abinoam from Kedesh in Naphtali and said to him, “Hasn’t the LORD, the God of Israel, commanded you, ‘Go, deploy the troops on Mount Tabor, and take with you ten thousand men from the Naphtalites and Zebulunites?  
**4:7** Then I will lure Sisera commander of Jabin’s army, his chariots, and his infantry at the Wadi Kishon to fight against you, and I will hand him over to you.’”  
**4:8** Barak said to her, “If you will go with me, I will go. But if you will not go with me, I will not go.”  
**4:9** “I will gladly go with you,” she said, “but you will receive no honor on the road you are about to take, because the LORD will sell Sisera to a woman.” So Deborah got up and went with Barak to Kedesh.  
**4:10** Barak summoned Zebulun and Naphtali to Kedesh; ten thousand men followed him, and Deborah also went with him.  
**4:11** Now Heber the Kenite had moved away from the Kenites, the sons of Hobab, Moses’s father-in-law, and pitched his tent beside the oak tree of Zaanannim, which was near Kedesh.  
**4:12** It was reported to Sisera that Barak son of Abinoam had gone up Mount Tabor.  
**4:13** Sisera summoned all his nine hundred iron chariots and all the troops who were with him from Harosheth of the Nations to the Wadi Kishon.  
**4:14** Then Deborah said to Barak, “Go! This is the day the LORD has handed Sisera over to you. Hasn’t the LORD gone before you?” So Barak came down from Mount Tabor with ten thousand men following him.  
**4:15** The LORD threw Sisera, all his charioteers, and all his army into a panic before Barak’s assault. Sisera left his chariot and fled on foot.  
**4:16** Barak pursued the chariots and the army as far as Harosheth of the Nations, and the whole army of Sisera fell by the sword; not a single man was left.  
**4:17** Meanwhile, Sisera had fled on foot to the tent of Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite, because there was peace between King Jabin of Hazor and the family of Heber the Kenite.  
**4:18** Jael went out to greet Sisera and said to him, “Come in, my lord. Come in with me. Don’t be afraid.” So he went into her tent, and she covered him with a blanket.  
**4:19** He said to her, “Please give me a little water to drink for I am thirsty.” She opened a container of milk, gave him a drink, and covered him again.  
**4:20** Then he said to her, “Stand at the entrance to the tent. If a man comes and asks you, ‘Is there a man here? ’ say, ‘No.’”  
**4:21** While he was sleeping from exhaustion, Heber’s wife, Jael, took a tent peg, grabbed a hammer, and went silently to Sisera. She hammered the peg into his temple and drove it into the ground, and he died.  
**4:22** When Barak arrived in pursuit of Sisera, Jael went out to greet him and said to him, “Come and I will show you the man you are looking for.” So he went in with her, and there was Sisera lying dead with a tent peg through his temple!  
**4:23** That day God subdued King Jabin of Canaan before the Israelites.  
**4:24** The power of the Israelites continued to increase against King Jabin of Canaan until they destroyed him.

## 6장

**6:1** The Israelites did what was evil in the sight of the LORD. So the LORD handed them over to Midian seven years,  
**6:2** and they oppressed Israel. Because of Midian, the Israelites made hiding places for themselves in the mountains, caves, and strongholds.  
**6:3** Whenever the Israelites planted crops, the Midianites, Amalekites, and the the people of the east came and attacked them.  
**6:4** They encamped against them and destroyed the produce of the land, even as far as Gaza. They left nothing for Israel to eat, as well as no sheep, ox, or donkey.  
**6:5** For the Midianites came with their cattle and their tents like a great swarm of locusts. They and their camels were without number, and they entered the land to lay waste to it.  
**6:6** So Israel became poverty-stricken because of Midian, and the Israelites cried out to the LORD.  
**6:7** When the Israelites cried out to him because of Midian,  
**6:8** the LORD sent a prophet to them. He said to them, “This is what the LORD God of Israel says: ‘I brought you out of Egypt and out of the place of slavery.  
**6:9** I rescued you from the power of Egypt and the power of all who oppressed you. I drove them out before you and gave you their land.  
**6:10** I said to you: I am the LORD your God. Do not fear the gods of the Amorites whose land you live in. But you did not obey me.’”  
**6:11** The angel of the LORD came, and he sat under the oak that was in Ophrah, which belonged to Joash, the Abiezrite. His son Gideon was threshing wheat in the winepress in order to hide it from the Midianites.  
**6:12** Then the angel of the LORD appeared to him and said, “The LORD is with you, valiant warrior.”  
**6:13** Gideon said to him, “Please, my lord, if the LORD is with us, why has all this happened? And where are all his wonders that our ancestors told us about? They said, ‘Hasn’t the LORD brought us out of Egypt? ’ But now the LORD has abandoned us and handed us over to Midian.”  
**6:14** The LORD turned to him and said, “Go in the strength you have and deliver Israel from the grasp of Midian. I am sending you!”  
**6:15** He said to him, “Please, Lord, how can I deliver Israel? Look, my family is the weakest in Manasseh, and I am the youngest in my father’s family.”  
**6:16** “But I will be with you,” the LORD said to him. “You will strike Midian down as if it were one man.”  
**6:17** Then he said to him, “If I have found favor with you, give me a sign that you are speaking with me.  
**6:18** Please do not leave this place until I return to you. Let me bring my gift and set it before you.” And he said, “I will stay until you return.”

## 18장

**18:1** In those days, there was no king in Israel, and the Danite tribe was looking for territory to occupy. Up to that time no territory had been captured by them among the tribes of Israel.  
**18:2** So the Danites sent out five brave men from all their clans, from Zorah and Eshtaol, to scout out the land and explore it. They told them, “Go and explore the land.” They came to the hill country of Ephraim as far as the home of Micah and spent the night there.  
**18:3** While they were near Micah’s home, they recognized the accent of the young Levite. So they went over to him and asked, “Who brought you here? What are you doing in this place? What is keeping you here?”  
**18:4** He told them, “This is what Micah has done for me: He has hired me, and I became his priest.”  
**18:5** Then they said to him, “Please inquire of God for us to determine if we will have a successful journey.”  
**18:6** The priest told them, “Go in peace. The LORD is watching over the journey you are going on.”  
**18:7** The five men left and came to Laish. They saw that the people who were there were living securely, in the same way as the Sidonians, quiet and unsuspecting. There was nothing lacking<sup>①</sup> in the land and no oppressive ruler. They were far from the Sidonians, having no alliance with anyone.<sup>②</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Hb obscure <sup>②</sup>MT; some LXX mss, Sym, Old Lat, Syr read <i>Aram</i>  
**18:8** When the men went back to their relatives at Zorah and Eshtaol, their relatives asked them, “What did you find out?”  
**18:9** They answered, “Come on, let’s attack them, for we have seen the land, and it is very good. Why wait? Don’t hesitate to go and invade and take possession of the land!  
**18:10** When you get there, you will come to an unsuspecting people and a spacious land, for God has handed it over to you. It is a place where nothing on earth is lacking.”  
**18:11** Six hundred Danites departed from Zorah and Eshtaol armed with weapons of war.  
**18:12** They went up and camped at Kiriath-jearim in Judah. This is why the place is still called the Camp of Dan<sup>①</sup> today; it is west of Kiriath-jearim.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>called Mahaneh-dan</i>  
**18:13** From there they traveled to the hill country of Ephraim and arrived at Micah’s house.  
**18:14** The five men who had gone to scout out the land of Laish told their brothers, “Did you know that there are an ephod, household gods, and a carved image and a silver idol<sup>①</sup> in these houses? Now think about what you should do.”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>image, the cast image</i>  
**18:15** So they detoured there and went to the house of the young Levite at the home of Micah and greeted him.  
**18:16** The six hundred Danite men were standing by the entrance of the city gate, armed with their weapons of war.  
**18:17** Then the five men who had gone to scout out the land went in and took the carved image, the ephod, the household idols, and the silver idol,<sup>①</sup> while the priest was standing by the entrance of the city gate with the six hundred men armed with weapons of war.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Or <i>the cast image</i>, also in v. <a name='B:70 18:18'>18  
**18:18** When they entered Micah’s house and took the carved image, the ephod, the household idols, and the silver idol, the priest said to them, “What are you doing?”  
**18:19** They told him, “Be quiet. Keep your mouth shut.<sup>①</sup> Come with us and be a father and a priest to us. Is it better for you to be a priest for the house of one person or for you to be a priest for a tribe and family in Israel?”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>Put your hand on your mouth</i>  
**18:20** So the priest was pleased and took the ephod, household idols, and carved image, and went with the people.  
**18:21** They prepared to leave, putting their dependents, livestock, and possessions in front of them.  
**18:22** After they were some distance from Micah’s house, the men who were in the houses near it were mustered and caught up with the Danites.  
**18:23** They called to the Danites, who turned to face them, and said to Micah, “What’s the matter with you that you mustered the men?”  
**18:24** He said, “You took the gods I had made and the priest, and went away. What do I have left? How can you say to me, ‘What’s the matter with you? ’”  
**18:25** The Danites said to him, “Don’t raise your voice against us, or angry men will attack you, and you and your family will lose your lives.”  
**18:26** The Danites went on their way, and Micah turned to go back home, because he saw that they were stronger than he was.  
**18:27** After they had taken the gods Micah had made and the priest that belonged to him, they went to Laish, to a quiet and unsuspecting people. They killed them with their swords and burned the city.  
**18:28** There was no one to rescue them because it was far from Sidon and they had no alliance with anyone. It was in a valley that belonged to Beth-rehob. They rebuilt the city and lived in it.  
**18:29** They named the city Dan, after the name of their ancestor Dan, who was born to Israel. The city was formerly named Laish.  
**18:30** The Danites set up the carved image for themselves. Jonathan son of Gershom, son of Moses,<sup>①</sup> and his sons were priests for the Danite tribe until the time of the exile from the land.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Some Hb mss, LXX, Vg; other Hb mss read <i>Manasseh</i>  
**18:31** So they set up for themselves Micah’s carved image that he had made, and it was there as long as the house of God was in Shiloh.  

## 19장

**19:1** In those days, when there was no king in Israel, a Levite staying in a remote part of the hill country of Ephraim acquired a woman from Bethlehem in Judah as his concubine.  
**19:2** But she was unfaithful to<sup>①</sup> him and left him for her father’s house in Bethlehem in Judah. She was there for four months.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>LXX reads <i>was angry with</i>  
**19:3** Then her husband got up and followed her to speak kindly to her and bring her back. He had his servant with him and a pair of donkeys. So she brought him to her father’s house, and when the girl’s father saw him, he gladly welcomed him.  
**19:4** His father-in-law, the girl’s father, detained him, and he stayed with him for three days. They ate, drank, and spent the nights there.  
**19:5** On the fourth day, they got up early in the morning and prepared to go, but the girl’s father said to his son-in-law, “Have something to eat to keep up your strength and then you can go.”  
**19:6** So they sat down and the two of them ate and drank together. Then the girl’s father said to the man, “Please agree to stay overnight and enjoy yourself.”  
**19:7** The man got up to go, but his father-in-law persuaded him, so he stayed and spent the night there again.  
**19:8** He got up early in the morning of the fifth day to leave, but the girl’s father said to him, “Please keep up your strength.” So they waited until late afternoon and the two of them ate.  
**19:9** The man got up to go with his concubine and his servant, when his father-in-law, the girl’s father, said to him, “Look, night is coming. Please spend the night. See, the day is almost over. Spend the night here, enjoy yourself, then you can get up early tomorrow for your journey and go home.”  
**19:10** But the man was unwilling to spend the night. He got up, departed, and arrived opposite Jebus (that is, Jerusalem). The man had his two saddled donkeys and his concubine with him.  
**19:11** When they were near Jebus and the day was almost gone, the servant said to his master, “Please, why not let us stop at this Jebusite city and spend the night here?”  
**19:12** But his master replied to him, “We will not stop at a foreign city where there are no Israelites. Let’s move on to Gibeah.”  
**19:13** “Come on,” he said,<sup>①</sup> “let’s try to reach one of these places and spend the night in Gibeah or Ramah.”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>said to his servant</i>  
**19:14** So they continued on their journey, and the sun set as they neared Gibeah in Benjamin.  
**19:15** They stopped<sup>①</sup> to go in and spend the night in Gibeah. The Levite went in and sat down in the city square, but no one took them into their home to spend the night.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>stopped there</i>  
**19:16** In the evening, an old man came in from his work in the field. He was from the hill country of Ephraim, but he was residing in Gibeah where the people were Benjaminites.  
**19:17** When he looked up and saw the traveler in the city square, the old man asked, “Where are you going, and where do you come from?”  
**19:18** He answered him, “We’re traveling from Bethlehem in Judah to the remote hill country of Ephraim, where I am from. I went to Bethlehem in Judah, and now I’m going to the house of the LORD.<sup>①</sup> No one has taken me into his home,<br>→ <sup>①</sup>LXX reads <i>to my house</i>  
**19:19** although there’s straw and feed for the donkeys, and I have bread and wine for me, my concubine, and the servant<sup>①</sup> with us. There is nothing we lack.”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Some Hb mss, Syr, Tg, Vg; other Hb mss read <i>servants</i>  
**19:20** “Welcome!” said the old man. “I’ll take care of everything you need. Only don’t spend the night in the square.”  
**19:21** So he brought him to his house and fed the donkeys. Then they washed their feet and ate and drank.  
**19:22** While they were enjoying themselves, all of a sudden, wicked men of the city surrounded the house and beat on the door. They said to the old man who was the owner of the house, “Bring out the man who came to your house so we can have sex with him!”  
**19:23** The owner of the house went out and said to them, “Please don’t do this evil, my brothers. After all, this man has come into my house. Don’t commit this horrible outrage.  
**19:24** Here, let me bring out my virgin daughter and the man’s concubine now. Abuse them and do whatever you want to them. But don’t commit this outrageous thing against this man.”  
**19:25** But the men would not listen to him, so the man seized his concubine and took her outside to them. They raped her and abused her all night until morning. At daybreak they let her go.  
**19:26** Early that morning, the woman made her way back, and as it was getting light, she collapsed at the doorway of the man’s house where her master was.  
**19:27** When her master got up in the morning, opened the doors of the house, and went out to leave on his journey, there was the woman, his concubine, collapsed near the doorway of the house with her hands on the threshold.  
**19:28** “Get up,” he told her. “Let’s go.” But there was no response. So the man put her on his donkey and set out for home.  
**19:29** When he entered his house, he picked up a knife, took hold of his concubine, cut her into twelve pieces, limb by limb, and then sent her throughout the territory of Israel.  
**19:30** Everyone who saw it said, “Nothing like this has ever happened or has been seen since the day the Israelites came out of the land of Egypt until now.<sup>①</sup> Think it over, discuss it, and speak up!”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>LXX reads <i>until now.” He commanded the men he sent out, saying, “You will say this to all the men of Israel: Has anything like this happened since the day the Israelites came out of Egypt until this day?</i>  

## 20장

**20:1** All the Israelites from Dan to Beer-sheba and from the land of Gilead came out, and the community assembled as one body before the LORD at Mizpah.  
**20:2** The leaders of all the people and of all the tribes of Israel presented themselves in the assembly of God’s people: four hundred thousand armed foot soldiers.  
**20:3** The Benjaminites heard that the Israelites had gone up to Mizpah. The Israelites asked, “Tell us, how did this evil act happen?”  
**20:4** The Levite, the husband of the murdered woman, answered, “I went to Gibeah in Benjamin with my concubine to spend the night.  
**20:5** Citizens of Gibeah came to attack me and surrounded the house at night. They intended to kill me, but they raped my concubine, and she died.  
**20:6** Then I took my concubine and cut her in pieces, and sent her throughout Israel’s territory, because they have committed a wicked outrage in Israel.  
**20:7** Look, all of you are Israelites. Give your judgment and verdict here and now.”  
**20:8** Then all the people stood united and said, “None of us will go to his tent or return to his house.  
**20:9** Now this is what we will do to Gibeah: we will attack it. By lot  
**20:10** we will take ten men out of every hundred from all the tribes of Israel, and one hundred out of every thousand, and one thousand out of every ten thousand to get provisions for the troops when they go to Gibeah in Benjamin to punish them for all the outrage they committed in Israel.”  
**20:11** So all the men of Israel gathered united against the city.  
**20:12** Then the tribes of Israel sent men throughout the tribe of Benjamin, saying, “What is this evil act that has happened among you?  
**20:13** Hand over the wicked men in Gibeah so we can put them to death and purge evil from Israel.” But the Benjaminites would not listen to their fellow Israelites.  
**20:14** Instead, the Benjaminites gathered together from their cities to Gibeah to go out and fight against the Israelites.  
**20:15** On that day the Benjaminites mobilized twenty-six thousand armed men from their cities, besides seven hundred fit young men rallied by the inhabitants of Gibeah.  
**20:16** There were seven hundred fit young men who were left-handed among all these troops; all could sling a stone at a hair and not miss.  
**20:17** The Israelites, apart from Benjamin, mobilized four hundred thousand armed men, every one an experienced warrior.  
**20:18** They set out, went to Bethel, and inquired of God. The Israelites asked, “Who is to go first to fight for us against the Benjaminites?” And the LORD answered, “Judah will be first.”  
**20:19** In the morning, the Israelites set out and camped near Gibeah.  
**20:20** The men of Israel went out to fight against Benjamin and took their battle positions against Gibeah.  
**20:21** The Benjaminites came out of Gibeah and slaughtered twenty-two thousand men of Israel on the field that day.  
**20:22** But the Israelite troops rallied and again took their battle positions in the same place where they positioned themselves on the first day.  
**20:23** They went up, wept before the LORD until evening, and inquired of him, “Should we again attack our brothers the Benjaminites?” And the LORD answered, “Fight against them.”  
**20:24** On the second day the Israelites advanced against the Benjaminites.  
**20:25** That same day the Benjaminites came out from Gibeah to meet them and slaughtered an additional eighteen thousand Israelites on the field; all were armed.  
**20:26** The whole Israelite army went to Bethel where they wept and sat before the LORD. They fasted that day until evening and offered burnt offerings and fellowship offerings to the LORD.  
**20:27** Then the Israelites inquired of the LORD. In those days, the ark of the covenant of God was there,  
**20:28** and Phinehas son of Eleazar, son of Aaron, was serving before it. The Israelites asked, “Should we again fight against our brothers the Benjaminites or should we stop?” The LORD answered, “Fight, because I will hand them over to you tomorrow.”  
**20:29** So Israel set up an ambush around Gibeah.  
**20:30** On the third day the Israelites fought against the Benjaminites and took their battle positions against Gibeah as before.  
**20:31** Then the Benjaminites came out against the troops and were drawn away from the city. They began to attack the troops as before, killing about thirty men of Israel on the highways, one of which goes up to Bethel and the other to Gibeah through the open country.  
**20:32** The Benjaminites said, “We are defeating them as before.” But the Israelites said, “Let’s flee and draw them away from the city to the highways.”  
**20:33** So all the men of Israel got up from their places and took their battle positions at Baal-tamar, while the Israelites in ambush charged out of their places west of<sup>①</sup> Geba.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>LXX, Syr, Vg; MT reads <i>places in the plain of</i>, or <i>places in the cave of</i>  
**20:34** Then ten thousand fit young men from all Israel made a frontal assault against Gibeah, and the battle was fierce, but the Benjaminites did not know that disaster was about to strike them.  
**20:35** The LORD defeated Benjamin in the presence of Israel, and on that day the Israelites slaughtered 25,100 men of Benjamin; all were armed.  
**20:36** Then the Benjaminites realized they had been defeated. The men of Israel had retreated before Benjamin, because they were confident in the ambush they had set against Gibeah.  
**20:37** The men in ambush had rushed quickly against Gibeah; they advanced and put the whole city to the sword.  
**20:38** The men of Israel had a prearranged signal with the men in ambush: when they sent up a great cloud of smoke from the city,  
**20:39** the men of Israel would return to the battle. When Benjamin had begun to strike them down, killing about thirty men of Israel, they said, “They’re defeated before us, just as they were in the first battle.”  
**20:40** But when the column of smoke began to go up from the city, Benjamin looked behind them, and the whole city was going up in smoke.<sup>①</sup><br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>up to the sky</i>  
**20:41** Then the men of Israel returned, and the men of Benjamin were terrified when they realized that disaster had struck them.  
**20:42** They retreated before the men of Israel toward the wilderness, but the battle overtook them, and those who came out of the cities<sup>①</sup> slaughtered those between them.<br>→ <sup>①</sup>LXX, Vg read <i>city</i>  
**20:43** They surrounded the Benjaminites, pursued them, and easily overtook them near Gibeah toward the east.  
**20:44** There were eighteen thousand men who died from Benjamin; all were warriors.  
**20:45** Then Benjamin turned and fled toward the wilderness to Rimmon Rock, and Israel killed five thousand men on the highways. They overtook them at Gidom and struck two thousand more dead.  
**20:46** All the Benjaminites who died that day were twenty-five thousand armed men; all were warriors.  
**20:47** But six hundred men escaped into the wilderness to Rimmon Rock and stayed there four months.  
**20:48** The men of Israel turned back against the other Benjaminites and killed them with their swords—the entire city, the animals, and everything that remained. They also burned all the cities that remained.  

## 21장

**21:1** The men of Israel had sworn an oath at Mizpah: “None of us will give his daughter to a Benjaminite in marriage.”  
**21:2** So the people went to Bethel and sat there before God until evening. They wept loudly and bitterly,  
**21:3** and cried out, “Why, LORD God of Israel, has it occurred<sup>①</sup> that one tribe is missing in Israel today?”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>has this occurred in Israel</i>  
**21:4** The next day the people got up early, built an altar there, and offered burnt offerings and fellowship offerings.  
**21:5** The Israelites asked, “Who of all the tribes of Israel didn’t come to the LORD with the assembly?” For a great oath had been taken that anyone who had not come to the LORD at Mizpah would certainly be put to death.  
**21:6** But the Israelites had compassion on their brothers, the Benjaminites, and said, “Today a tribe has been cut off from Israel.  
**21:7** What should we do about wives for the survivors? We’ve sworn to the LORD not to give them any of our daughters as wives.”  
**21:8** They asked, “Which city among the tribes of Israel didn’t come to the LORD at Mizpah?” It turned out that no one from Jabesh-gilead had come to the camp and the assembly.  
**21:9** For when the roll was called, no men were there from the inhabitants of Jabesh-gilead.  
**21:10** The congregation sent twelve thousand brave warriors there and commanded them, “Go and kill the inhabitants of Jabesh-gilead with the sword, including women and dependents.  
**21:11** This is what you should do: Completely destroy every male, as well as every woman who has gone to bed with a man.”  
**21:12** They found among the inhabitants of Jabesh-gilead four hundred young virgins, who had not been intimate with a man, and they brought them to the camp at Shiloh in the land of Canaan.  
**21:13** The whole congregation sent a message of peace to the Benjaminites who were at Rimmon Rock.  
**21:14** Benjamin returned at that time, and Israel gave them the women they had kept alive from Jabesh-gilead. But there were not enough for them.  
**21:15** The people had compassion on Benjamin, because the LORD had made this gap in the tribes of Israel.  
**21:16** The elders of the congregation said, “What should we do about wives for those who are left, since the women of Benjamin have been destroyed?”  
**21:17** They said, “There must be heirs for the survivors of Benjamin, so that a tribe of Israel will not be wiped out.  
**21:18** But we can’t give them our daughters as wives.” For the Israelites had sworn, “Anyone who gives a wife to a Benjaminite is cursed.”  
**21:19** They also said, “Look, there’s an annual festival to the LORD in Shiloh, which is north of Bethel, east of the highway that goes up from Bethel to Shechem, and south of Lebonah.”  
**21:20** Then they commanded the Benjaminites, “Go and hide in the vineyards.  
**21:21** Watch, and when you see the young women of Shiloh come out to perform the dances, each of you leave the vineyards and catch a wife for yourself from the young women of Shiloh, and go to the land of Benjamin.  
**21:22** When their fathers or brothers come to us and protest, we will tell them, ‘Show favor to them, since we did not get enough wives for each of them in the battle. You didn’t actually give the women to them, so<sup>①</sup> you are not guilty of breaking your oath.’”<br>→ <sup>①</sup>Lit <i>at this time</i>  
**21:23** The Benjaminites did this and took the number of women they needed from the dancers they caught. They went back to their own inheritance, rebuilt their cities, and lived in them.  
**21:24** At that time, each of the Israelites returned from there to his own tribe and family. Each returned from there to his own inheritance.  
**21:25** In those days there was no king in Israel; everyone did whatever seemed right to him.
